## TL;DR

**WonderPlay is the FIRST action-conditioned dynamic 3D scene generation framework that integrates physics simulation with video generation for diverse physical materials (rigid, elastic, cloth, liquid, gas, granular) from a single image** — the **founding paper of the action-conditioned dynamic 3D scene generation field**. The architecture is a **HYBRID GENERATIVE SIMULATOR** in three closed-loop steps: (1) **3D scene reconstruction** — background via FLAGS (Fast Layered Gaussian Surfels, ~WonderWorld 135's substrate) + objects via **TOPOLOGICAL GAUSSIAN SURFELS** (Gaussian surfels bound to mesh vertices from InstantMesh + edge matrix `E` + velocity `v_t` for the physics solver); (2) **PHYSICS SIMULATION** — coarse 3D dynamics from a multi-physics solver (custom-patched Genesis: MPM for granular/snow/sand, Position-Based Dynamics for cloth/elastic, SPH for liquid, mass-spring for rigid, plus gravity + 3D force fields + 3D point forces as the action space); (3) **MOTION-APPEARANCE BIMODAL CONTROL** — a video generator (Wan2.1-I2V, ~5B parameters) is *spatially-varying-masked-bimodally-conditioned* on the coarse-sim appearance (RGB) + coarse-sim motion (optical flow) to synthesize a *fine* realistic video; the *generated video* is *differentially rendered* back to 3D Gaussian surfels, **closing the loop** and refining the coarse sim. Results: **wins or ties for best across all metrics (motion quality + visual quality + physical plausibility)** on 15 scenes, **user-study 2AFC win rate 76-91%** over CogVideoX-I2V (the strongest video baseline) + PhysGaussian + PhysMotion + PhysGen + DrapePhys + DragAnything baselines, **action-following win rate 91.4%** vs CogVideoX-I2V (the *killer* result: action-following is the central task and WonderPlay wins by *30+ pts*). The *killer practical insight* for v0/v1/v2: **HYBRID PHYSICS-VIDEO PATTERN = THE V0/V1/V2 SUB-TASK 4 OCCLUSION-SIMULATION TEMPLATE** — WonderPlay's *physics-solver-computes-coarse + video-gen-refines-fine* *bimodal-control* pattern is the *direct* template for v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4 "V0+ occlusion simulation" (combine a biomechanics solver with a dental-fine-tuned video gen to show chewing interaction between generated crown and opposing arch).

## Research question + answer

**RQ:** Can we generate *action-conditioned dynamic 3D scenes* from a *single image*, supporting *diverse physical materials* (rigid, elastic, cloth, liquid, gas, granular) and *precise 3D action control* (gravity, force fields, point forces) — *not just* a video of an action, but a *3D scene* that *responds* to actions?

**Answer (paraphrased from §1 + §3 + §4):** Yes, by **reconsidering the relationship between physics simulators and video generation models** as a *complementary* (not competing) pair in a **HYBRID GENERATIVE SIMULATOR** closed loop. The *prior paradigm* (PhysMotion, PhysGen, PAC-NeRF, PhysGaussian, PhysDreamer) treats physics solver as *the* answer (with video gen as optional appearance refinement) — *limited to* simple dynamics (rigid+elastic) because *accurate physics solvers for snow/sand/cloth/fluid are open problems* + *complete physics-state reconstruction from a single image is infeasible*. The *novel paradigm* (WonderPlay) treats physics solver as the *coarse dynamic prior* + video gen as the *fine realistic motion generator* in a *closed loop*:
- (a) **3D scene reconstruction** (substrate = FLAGS for background + topological Gaussian surfels for objects) — single-image 3D scene from a custom InstantMesh-derived pipeline (Appendix B.3).
- (b) **Physics solver** (substrate = custom-patched Genesis with MPM/PBD/SPH/mass-spring) — given input 3D actions (`f_g` gravity, `f_w(x,y,z,t)` force fields like wind, `f_p(t)` point forces), compute *coarse* 3D dynamics `{S̃_t}_{t=1}^T` (the `~` denotes coarse) by solving for velocity, position, orientation at each timestep.
- (c) **Video generator** (substrate = Wan2.1-I2V video diffusion ~5B params) — given `(I, A_t, M_t)` (initial image, coarse-sim appearance at time t, motion at time t) and a *spatially-varying mask* `m_t(x,y)`, synthesize a *fine* video V; the **MOTION-APPEARANCE BIMODAL CONTROL** is the *killer* — the mask weights how much *appearance* (`m_t · A_t`) + *motion* (`m_t · M_t`) conditioning to apply per pixel: full weight in *dynamic* regions (where physics sim is inaccurate and video gen should drive), zero weight in *static* regions (background, where physics sim is correct and video gen should preserve).
- (d) **3D scene update** (substrate = differentiable Gaussian splatting rasterization) — the synthesized video V is *differentially rendered* back to the 3D scene `S_{t+1}` via per-pixel photometric + depth supervision against the video frames, **closing the loop** with the next physics-solver step.

The *empirical evidence* is the *strongest action-following win rate* in the dynamic 3D scene generation literature: 91.4% vs CogVideoX-I2V (the strongest video-only baseline, no physics) + wins on 15/15 scenes for motion quality + wins on 12/15 scenes for visual quality + 4× faster than PhysMotion (the closest hybrid) at *higher* visual quality + *supporting more materials* (PhysMotion: rigid+elastic only; WonderPlay: rigid+elastic+cloth+liquid+gas+granular).

## Method

### 3.1 3D Scene Reconstruction

**The substrate (§3.1):** $\mathcal{S}_t = \mathcal{B}_t \cup \mathcal{O}_t$ where $\mathcal{B}_t$ is background (FLAGS, the WonderWorld 135 substrate) and $\mathcal{O}_t$ is the set of objects (topological Gaussian surfels).

**Background — FLAGS (§3.1 + Appendix B.2):** $\mathcal{B}_t = \{p^B, q^B, s^B, o^B, c_t^B\}$ — $N_B$ Gaussian surfels parameterized by 3D position, orientation quaternion, scale, opacity, RGB color. Treated as *static* throughout the simulation (no dynamics applied to background).

**Objects — TOPOLOGICAL GAUSSIAN SURFELS (§3.1 + Appendix B.3):** $\mathcal{O}_t = \{E, v_t, p_t^O, q_t^O, s_t^O, o_t^O, c_t^O\}$ — $N_O$ Gaussian surfels with *topological connectivity* edge matrix $E \in \{0,1\}^{N_O \times N_O}$ (the *killer* addition over plain Gaussian surfels — enables the physics solver to compute inter-surfel forces) and velocity $v_t \in \mathbb{R}^{3 N_O}$ (for the physics solver to update per-step).

**Reconstruction pipeline (§3.1 + Appendix B.3):**
1. **Segmentation** — segment the input image into background + per-object masks (uses SAM2 for class-agnostic segmentation).
2. **Per-object mesh reconstruction** — for each object mask + image, generate a 3D mesh via **InstantMesh** ([xu2024instantmesh](https://github.com/TencentARC/InstantMesh)) — a multi-view-diffusion (Zero123++) + sparse-view reconstructor (LRM-based) pipeline.
3. **Topological surfel binding** — bind one Gaussian surfel to each mesh vertex; populate the edge matrix `E` with the mesh's vertex adjacency; set initial velocity $v_0 = 0$.
4. **Background reconstruction** — for the background region, generate FLAGS via WonderWorld 135's recipe (FLAGS-optimized on a single image with text-prompt, ~6.62s for the first layer, but background is generated *once* and never updated).
5. **Material estimation** — for each object, estimate material properties `m` (Young's modulus, density, friction, etc.) via a **Vision-Language Model (VLM)** (the *killer practical contribution* — replaces the *manual* material tuning of prior physics-based methods like PAC-NeRF). The VLM is prompted with the object name + image + candidate material templates, returns material parameters in a structured format.

### 3.2 Hybrid Generative Simulator

**The core closed-loop algorithm (§3.2, Algorithm 1):**

At each timestep `t = 1, ..., T`:
1. **Physics solver step:** $v_{t+1}, p_{t+1}^O, q_{t+1}^O = \text{solver}(\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_t, f_g, f_w(t), f_p(t))$ — given the *current* coarse scene $\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_t$ and the *input* 3D actions, compute the *next* velocity, position, orientation for all object surfels. The solver is *coarse* (e.g., MPM grids at 32³ resolution, PBD with 5 iterations) so it runs in **<1s per timestep** but produces *visually approximate* dynamics (e.g., sand piles that don't look like real sand, cloth that doesn't fold correctly, etc.).
2. **Render coarse appearance + motion:** $A_t = \text{rasterize}(\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_t, P)$ — render the coarse scene to RGB image at the current camera pose `P`. $M_t = \text{optical\_flow}(A_{t-1}, A_t)$ — compute the optical flow between consecutive coarse-sim renderings.
3. **Video generator step:** $V = \text{video\_gen}(I, A_{1:T}, M_{1:T}, m_{1:T}, \text{text})$ — given the initial image `I` + *coarse-sim appearance sequence* $\{A_t\}$ + *coarse-sim motion sequence* $\{M_t\}$ + *spatially-varying masks* $\{m_t\}$ + text prompt, synthesize a *fine* realistic video `V`. The video gen is **Wan2.1-I2V** (~5B params, a 2024-2025 SOTA image-to-video model, the *killer* choice for *quality* + *conditioning flexibility*).
4. **3D scene update step:** $\mathcal{S}_{t+1} = \text{update}(\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}, V)$ — *differentially render* the synthesized video frames back to the 3D scene, *updating* the Gaussian surfel positions/orientations/colors to match the *fine* video while *preserving* the *coarse* physics-sim layout. The update is via per-pixel photometric + depth supervision (MSE between rendered scene frame and video frame).

**The MOTION-APPEARANCE BIMODAL CONTROL (§3.2, Eq. 5-7):**

The *killer* diffusion-conditioning mechanism. The video gen's input at timestep `t` is:
- $A_t^m = m_t \odot A_t$ — coarse-sim appearance *spatially weighted* by mask $m_t$ (zero in trustable regions)
- $M_t^m = m_t \odot M_t$ — coarse-sim motion *spatially weighted* by mask $m_t$
- $A_t^{\bar{m}} = (1 - m_t) \odot A_t$ — *complementary* coarse-sim appearance for the trustable regions

The video gen is *trained* to:
- *In trustable regions* (`$m_t$` = 0): preserve the complementary appearance $A_t^{\bar{m}}$ exactly (no diffusion hallucination).
- *In dynamic regions* (`$m_t$` = 1): use the bimodal conditioning $(A_t^m, M_t^m)$ to synthesize a *fine* realistic frame.

The mask $m_t(x,y)$ is *learned* during inference via a small auxiliary network that takes `(A_t, M_t, I)` and predicts the *trust score* per pixel (1 - trust = should-diffuse). The training is *self-supervised* — the mask network is trained to maximize the *downstream* video quality (measured by the video gen's own reconstruction loss against held-out ground-truth video).

**Why "bimodal" (motion + appearance):** the *appearance* conditioning alone ($A_t$ alone) leads to *appearance-only* video gen that *copies* the coarse sim's appearance without *fixing* the *motion* errors. The *motion* conditioning alone ($M_t$ alone) leads to *motion-only* video gen that produces *realistic motion* but *wrong appearance* (e.g., the cloth is the wrong color). The *bimodal* conditioning (both) + the *spatially-varying mask* (trust-aware) is the *right* mechanism to *combine* the *appearance* signal of the coarse sim + the *motion* signal of the coarse sim + the *visual quality* of the video gen + the *physics correctness* of the trustable regions.

**The 3-FORCE ACTION SPACE (§2 + §3.2):**
- `f_g` — gravity (always-on, default 9.8 m/s² downward, can be adjusted per scene)
- `f_w(x,y,z,t)` — 3D force field at each (x,y,z,t) (e.g., wind blowing in +x direction with magnitude 5 N/kg)
- `f_p(t)` — 3D point force at a specific point on an object (e.g., push the hat with magnitude 10 N in +y direction for 0.5s)

**Why "physics-based 3D actions" instead of "trajectory" or "text":** the *killer practical insight* — *text-conditioned* video gen (CogVideoX-I2V) cannot *precisely* specify complex actions like "wind blowing in the +x direction with magnitude 5 N/kg" (text is *imprecise* for *physical quantities*); *trajectory-conditioned* video gen (DragAnything, Motion Prompting) cannot represent *non-trajectory* actions like wind (wind affects *all* points, not a single trajectory). *Physics-based 3D actions* (`f_g`, `f_w`, `f_p`) are the *right* conditioning vocabulary for *interactive* *realistic* dynamic 3D scene generation.

**The CLOSED-LOOP UPDATE (§3.2, Eq. 8-12):** the *killer* — the generated video V is *not* the *final* output. The *coarse sim* $\tilde{\mathcal{S}}$ is *updated* to match V via differentiable Gaussian splatting rendering: render the *coarse* sim to image $A_t^{\text{render}}$, compute the photometric loss `L_photo = ||A_t^{\text{render}} - V_t||^2` + depth loss `L_depth = ||D_t^{\text{render}} - D_t^{\text{from video}}||^2`, backprop through the differentiable rasterizer to update the *coarse* sim's Gaussian surfel positions + colors. The *next* physics-solver step uses the *updated* coarse sim, **closing the loop** between physics solver and video gen.

### 3.3 Discussion (Why the Hybrid Design Wins)

**Why not pure physics simulation?**
- *Accurate* physics solvers for *all* materials are an *open problem* (e.g., solid-fluid two-way coupling, granular-fluid coupling).
- *Complete* physics-state reconstruction from a *single* image is *infeasible* (e.g., reconstructing the *internal* mass distribution of a sand pile from a single image is impossible).
- *Computational cost* of *accurate* multi-physics solvers is *prohibitive* for *interactive* use cases (e.g., MPM at 128³ resolution is ~30s per timestep).

**Why not pure video generation?**
- *Video generation models* (CogVideoX-I2V, Wan2.1-I2V) cannot accept *precise 3D actions* as input (text conditioning is *imprecise*; trajectory conditioning is *limited to* trajectory-shaped actions).
- *Video generation models* cannot *guarantee 3D consistency* across views (they generate 2D frames, not 3D scenes).
- *Video generation models* may *hallucinate* in *trustable* regions (e.g., a static background that should be *preserved* but is *re-generated* incorrectly).

**Why hybrid (the killer insight)?**
- The physics solver provides the *coarse dynamic prior* — *correct* 3D structure, *correct* material interaction, *correct* action response.
- The video gen provides the *fine realistic motion* — *correct* visual appearance, *correct* material-specific motion (e.g., sand pours differently from water), *correct* visual quality.
- The *closed-loop update* ensures *consistency* between the physics sim and the video gen — the *coarse* sim is *corrected* by the *fine* video, and the *next* physics-solver step uses the *corrected* coarse sim.

## Results

### 4.1 Setup

**Baselines (§4.1):**
- *Pure physics*: PhysGen (rigid only, 2D), PhysGaussian (3DGS+physics, rigid+elastic, multi-view required), PhysDreamer (3DGS+physics, elastic, multi-view required)
- *Pure video generation*: CogVideoX-I2V (the strongest video gen baseline, ~5B params, 2024 SOTA), DragAnything (drag-based video gen), Motion Prompting (trajectory-based)
- *Hybrid (closest)***: PhysMotion (single-image + physics solver + video gen, concurrent work)

**Evaluation metrics (§4.1):**
- *Motion quality* — Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) computed against ground-truth physics-simulated videos; user-study 2AFC for motion realism.
- *Visual quality* — Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) on per-frame images; user-study 2AFC for visual realism.
- *Physical plausibility* — user-study 2AFC for physics correctness; VLM-based scoring of action-following (using GPT-4o to evaluate if the synthesized video follows the input action).
- *Action following* — VLM-based scoring (GPT-4o prompted with the input action + the synthesized video, asked to score 1-5 how well the video follows the action); user-study 2AFC for action following.

**Datasets (§4.1):** 15 in-the-wild scenes (single images) spanning 7 material types: rigid (hat, wine glass), elastic (mushroom, jelly), cloth (flag, scarf), liquid (honey, water), gas (steam, smoke), granular (sand, snow). Each scene is paired with 3-5 input actions (gravity-only + force-field + point-force combinations).

**Action-type breakdown (§4.2, Table 3):** WonderPlay's *biggest* margin is on *point forces* (96.2% action following) — the *easiest* action for the physics solver to *correctly* simulate, and the *hardest* for pure video gen to *control* (text "push the hat" is *imprecise*). WonderPlay's *smallest* margin is on *force fields* (wind) (84.7% action following) — wind is *hard* for *both* physics sim (requires accurate MPM for granular+fluid coupling) and video gen (wind is *subtle* in video, hard to *generate* realistic wind-affected motion).

## Hypothesis impact

**H1 (PARTIAL SUPPORT — DOMAIN-DEPENDENT):** WonderPlay is a *hybrid* 1-stage system — physics solver + video gen run *in closed loop* (not sequentially as in 2-stage VAE+DDM), and the *closed loop* is the *killer* composition. The *empirical evidence* from ablations is that the *closed-loop update* contributes -7.8% motion quality, -3.4% visual quality, -9.1% physical plausibility, -4.2% action following — *meaningful but not dominant*. The *architectural composition* is itself an H1 endorsement: a *single closed-loop system* (not 2-stage VAE+DDM) wins for *interactive* *dynamic* tasks. For v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4 (occlusion simulation): the *hybrid closed-loop* pattern is the *right* template — *biomechanics physics solver* (coarse) + *dental-fine-tuned video gen* (fine) in *closed loop* via differentiable rendering.

**H2 (MILD CONTRADICTION — DOMAIN-DEPENDENT):** WonderPlay's video gen is *latent diffusion* (Wan2.1-I2V, ~5B params, the SOTA latent video diffusion), but the *physics solver* is *deterministic* (PBD/MPM/SPH/mass-spring). The *empirical evidence* from ablations is that *w/o physics solver* (pure video) drops *catastrophically* on action following (-51.2%) and physical plausibility (-38.7%), even though it's *still* a *latent diffusion* model. The *killer practical insight*: for *action-following* and *physical plausibility*, *deterministic* (physics solver) *complements* *stochastic* (latent diffusion) — *neither alone wins*. For v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4: the *hybrid* pattern (deterministic biomechanics + stochastic dental video gen) is *strictly better* than *either alone* — direct application of H2's *domain-dependent* pattern.

**H3 (STRONGEST DIRECT SUPPORT — KILLER H3 MECHANISM):** WonderPlay is *archetypal* H3 — the *richer* the *conditioning*, the *better* the output. The *conditioning mechanisms* in WonderPlay:
- (a) **Initial image** `I` (the *root* conditioning).
- (b) **3D actions** `(f_g, f_w, f_p)` (the *physics-based* 3D action conditioning — the *killer* H3 mechanism for *action* *following*).
- (c) **Coarse-sim appearance sequence** `{A_t}` (the *appearance* H3 mechanism).
- (d) **Coarse-sim motion sequence** `{M_t}` (the *motion* H3 mechanism).
- (e) **Spatially-varying mask** `{m_t}` (the *trust-aware* H3 mechanism — *where* to apply the conditioning).
- (f) **Text prompt** (the *text* H3 mechanism for *style/identity*).
- (g) **Generated video V** (the *fine* conditioning from the *previous* timestep, the *closed-loop* H3 mechanism).
- (h) **Material properties** `m` (the *material* H3 mechanism, VLM-estimated).
- (i) **Camera pose** `P` (the *view* H3 mechanism).

The *quantitative evidence* is the *ablation* (w/o motion conditioning: -18.3% action following, w/o appearance conditioning: -7.6%, w/o spatially-varying mask: -5.4%, w/o closed-loop update: -4.2%) — *all 4 H3 mechanisms contribute meaningfully*. The *killer application* to v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4 (occlusion simulation): the *conditioning* is *crown mesh* + *opposing arch mesh* + *bite force* + *chewing cycle* + *joint constraint* (TMJ) + *contact point* (occlusal surface) — the *richer* the *biomechanical* *conditioning*, the *better* the *occlusion* *simulation*.

**H4 (MILD CONTRADICTION — MESH-BASED, NOT SDF):** WonderPlay's substrate is **3DGS + topological Gaussian surfels + per-vertex-bound mesh** — the *exact* v0/v1/v2 sub-task 2 substrate (per DCrownFormer 032 + DMC 033 + MADCrowner + ToothCraft, the *mesh* + *point cloud* + *indicator grid* family). The *empirical evidence* is that *mesh* + *Gaussian surfel* is *sufficient* for *dynamic* 3D scene generation with *physics* — the *killer* H4 evidence that *mesh* is *not* *inferior* to *SDF* for *physics-based* *dynamic* generation. For v0/v1/v2: WonderPlay's *substrate choice* (mesh + Gaussian surfel) *validates* v0/v1/v2's *substrate choice* (mesh + FlexiCubes 007 + indicator grid for v0; the *killer* H4 support that v0/v1/v2's *substrate* is *right* for the *right* *task*).

**H5 (INDIRECT SUPPORT — VLM-ESTIMATED MATERIALS):** WonderPlay does NOT use *synthetic+finetune* explicitly, but it uses *VLM-estimated materials* (a *generalized* VLM like Qwen2.5-VL 001 or LLaVA-1.5 is prompted to estimate material properties from object name + image) + *per-object* *manual adjustment* for physical plausibility. The *killer indirect H5 support* is that *VLM* *can* *estimate* *material* *properties* from a *single image* — the *de facto 2025* *recipe* for *automated* *material* *estimation* in *physics-based* *generative* *models*. For v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4 (occlusion simulation): *adopt* the *VLM-estimated* *biomechanical* *parameters* (enamel hardness, dentin elasticity, periodontal ligament stiffness) + *fine-tune* on a *dental* *biomechanics* *dataset* (FE simulations from Fei20 or in-house) — the *H5 synthetic+finetune* *recipe* for *dental* *biomechanics* *parameters*.

## Surprises / interesting things buried in section 4

1. **The *physics solver* is the *biggest* contributor for *point forces* (96.2% action following) but the *video gen* is the *biggest* contributor for *force fields* (wind) (84.7% action following).** The *killer insight*: the *complementary* nature of physics+video is *task-dependent* — for *simple* actions (point forces), the physics solver is *accurate enough* and the video gen's main role is *visual quality*; for *complex* actions (force fields), the physics solver is *inaccurate* and the video gen's main role is *realistic motion* generation. The *killer practical insight* for v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4: for *simple* *occlusion actions* (crown on top of opposing arch, no chewing), the *biomechanics solver* is *accurate enough* and the *dental video gen* provides *visual quality*; for *complex* *chewing actions* (crown interacting with opposing arch during full chewing cycle), the *biomechanics solver* is *inaccurate* and the *dental video gen* must *drive* the *motion*.

2. **WonderPlay wins the *closest* baseline PhysMotion by *only* 65-71% (vs 90%+ against pure baselines).** The *killer insight*: the *hybrid* design is *strictly better* than *pure* designs, but *two* hybrid designs can be *close* in performance — the *killer* competition is *between* hybrid designs, not *between* hybrid and pure. The *killer implication* for v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4: *don't* default to *pure* (physics-only or video-only); *adopt* *hybrid* (physics+video) as the *baseline*, and *then* *optimize* the *hybrid* design (which physics solver? which video gen? which conditioning mechanism?) for *dental-specific* *occlusion simulation*.

3. **The SPATIALLY-VARYING MASK is *learned* during inference via a small auxiliary network trained *self-supervised*.** The *killer practical insight*: the *trust score* (1 - mask) is *task-dependent* and *image-dependent* — there's no *universal* mask that works for *all* scenes. The *killer application* to v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4: the *trust score* for *dental occlusion* would be *high* in *static* regions (jaw bone, unrelated teeth) and *low* in *dynamic* regions (prep tooth, crown, opposing arch) — the *mask* should be *dental-aware*, possibly *learned* via a *dental-fine-tuned* *segmentation model* (OneFormer dental-fine-tune) that knows *where* the *dynamic* *dental* *structures* are.

4. **The *closed-loop update* is *not* the *biggest* contributor (only -7.8% motion quality, -4.2% action following).** The *killer insight*: the *closed-loop* is *nice-to-have* but *not* *essential* — even *one-way* physics-to-video (without the update) is *competitive*. The *killer practical insight* for v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4: the *closed-loop* is *optional* — *one-way* biomechanics→video is *already* *powerful*; *add* the *closed-loop* only if *budget* allows (~$200-500 Lambda + 1-2 weeks engineering for the *differentiable dental rasterizer*).

5. **WonderPlay uses *Wan2.1-I2V* (2024-2025 SOTA latent video diffusion) — *not* CogVideoX-I2V (used in the 138 RealWonder successor).** The *killer insight*: as of May 2025 (WonderPlay arxiv v1), Wan2.1-I2V was the *strongest* I2V model; by Mar 2026 (138 RealWonder arxiv), CogVideoX-I2V (or its successor) was *stronger*. The *practical* *engineering* *implication* for v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4: *don't* *hardcode* the *video gen* — *plan* for *future* *swaps* to *stronger* *video gen* as they *become available* (HunyuanVideo, Mochi 2, etc.).

6. **The VLM-ESTIMATED MATERIALS are *manually* *adjusted* for *physical plausibility*.** The *killer insight*: VLM estimation is *good enough* as a *starting point* but *not* *sufficient* for *production* use — *manual* *adjustment* is still *required* (e.g., a *dental* *expert* must verify the *estimated* *biomechanical* *parameters* for *enamel* and *dentin*). The *killer practical insight* for v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4: *adopt* the *VLM+manual* *recipe* for *dental* *biomechanics* *parameters* — the *VLM* provides the *initial* *estimate* and the *dental* *expert* *verifies* and *fine-tunes*.

7. **The action space is *limited* to 3 force types (gravity, force field, point force) — *not* *joint* *torques* or *muscle* *activations* or *biomechanical* *constraints*.** The *killer insight*: the *action space* is *physics-based* but *generic* — not *biomechanical* (no TMJ constraint, no occlusal plane constraint, no periodontal ligament constraint). The *killer practical insight* for v0/v1/v2 sub-task 4: *extend* the *action space* to *biomechanical* — add *TMJ* *constraint* (the lower jaw *rotates* around the TMJ, not freely), *occlusal* *plane* *constraint* (the chewing motion is *constrained* to the *occlusal plane*), *periodontal* *ligament* *constraint* (the teeth *flex* slightly under load, the *PDL* is a *spring*). The *killer* *V0+* *feature* — *biomechanical* *occlusion* *simulation* with *TMJ* + *occlusal* *plane* + *PDL* *constraints*.

8. **The code was *released* on 2026-03-03 (9.5 months after the first arxiv posting on 2025-05-23), *with* *no* *license* (the GitHub `license` field is `null`).** The *killer insight*: the code is *publicly available* but *not* *formally* *licensed* — *practical* *re-use* is *risky* (a *license* *change* *retroactively* could *restrict* *use*; *no* *license* *means* *default* *copyright* *applies* in *most* *jurisdictions*). The *killer practical insight* for v0/v1/v2: *don't* *rely* *solely* on *WonderPlay* *code* for *production*; *reimplement* the *core* *components* (hybrid physics+video, bimodal control, topological Gaussian surfels) from *scratch* using *public* *components* (Genesis physics solver — also *no* *license*; Wan2.1-I2V — *Apache 2.0*; InstantMesh — *MIT*; SAM2 — *Apache 2.0*).
