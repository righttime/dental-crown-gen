## TL;DR

**PointNet++ is the *foundational* hierarchical point-cloud architecture — the "CNN-for-points" — and the *single most important* paper in the entire dental-3D reading list, because *every* point-cloud dental paper from 2018 to 2026 (TSegNet, MeshSegNet, TSegFormer, IGIP, TCATSeg, DArch, TSegLab, DBGANet, TSGCNet, SGTNet, GFACNet, 3DTeethSAM, DilatedToothSegNet, Curvature-FPS, the multiview-AI 2025 paper) cites it as a baseline or builds directly on its reference code** — it is the *v0 paper's "backbone comparison" reference*, the *required* baseline for any new dental point-cloud paper, and the *single* paper in the reading list that is *more foundational* than the dental-specific papers themselves. **The architectural innovation is the recursive application of PointNet on a *nested partitioning* of the point set (the *set abstraction level* = {**Sampling layer** (FPS) → **Grouping layer** (ball query) → **PointNet layer** (mini-PointNet on local region)}), repeated at 3-4 hierarchical levels to capture local features at multiple contextual scales, and *robustified* with two density-adaptive mechanisms (Multi-Scale Grouping / **MSG** = concatenate features at multiple radii; Multi-Resolution Grouping / **MRG** = concatenate level-L and level-L-1 features with learned weights, *the more efficient variant*) plus *random input dropout* (`θ ~ U[0, 0.95]`, the *single most surprising training trick* in the paper)**. The headline results: **MNIST 0.51% error (vs PointNet 0.78%, 60.8% relative reduction)**, **ModelNet40 90.7% (vs PointNet 89.2%, +1.5pp; with surface normals 91.9%, beating MVCNN 90.1%)**, **ScanNet voxel-class accuracy 84.5% (vs PointNet 73.8%, +10.7pp — the *biggest* gap is on the *segmentation* task, where the local-feature learning matters most)**, and the **MSG+DP / MRG+DP density-adaptive variants lose <1% accuracy when test points drop from 1024 → 256, *the* single most-quoted robustness result in 3D deep learning**. The three killer insights for our v0 paper: **(1) ball query vs kNN** — ball query is preferred for semantic labeling (fixed region scale → generalizable local features), kNN is preferred for classification (fixed-K → uniform compute); **(2) FPS vs random sampling** — FPS gives *better coverage* of the point set for the same number of centroids (data-dependent receptive fields, *not* the fixed-stride scan of volumetric CNNs); **(3) the random input dropout (`θ ~ U[0, 0.95]`) is a *free-lunch* data augmentation** that makes MSG+DP and MRG+DP robust to scanner-density-shift, the *exact* failure mode of every dental-3D paper that ships a single global model to multiple clinics. **For our project, PointNet++ is the v0 paper's *required* "this is what every other paper compares against" reference, and MSG+DP / MRG+DP are the *right* architectures to *cite* (not adopt — they are 9 years old) as the v0 paper's "what the field built on" preamble in the related-work section.**

## Research question + their answer

**Q:** PointNet (Qi et al. 2016, the same group, 6 months earlier) is the *first* deep network to directly consume unordered point sets, and it achieves impressive performance on classification and segmentation benchmarks — but by *design* it does *not* capture local structure induced by the metric space the points live in. It applies the *same* MLP to every point independently, then aggregates with a single global max-pool — so it cannot capture *local patterns* (a cusp at one specific point, a fissure at another) the way a CNN can with its sliding kernel. The research question: **can we build a "CNN for point sets" — a *hierarchical* architecture that recursively applies PointNet to a *nested partitioning* of the point set, so that deeper layers capture features at *larger* contextual scales (analogous to deeper CNN layers capturing larger receptive fields), and that is *robust* to the non-uniform point-density that is endemic to real scanners (Structure Sensor, LiDAR, intra-oral scanners, CBCT scanners)?**

**A:** **Yes — by composing set-abstraction levels hierarchically (each level = Sample-FPS + Group-ball-query + PointNet-mini-PointNet), and adding density-adaptive multi-scale / multi-resolution grouping (MSG / MRG) with random input dropout during training, the network can learn local features at multiple scales and adapt to varying density.** The three design decisions that make it work:

1. **Set abstraction level** (Sec 3.2, the *core* contribution): the unit of computation is `N'×(d+C')` from input `N×(d+C)`, computed by **(a) Sampling layer** (farthest point sampling / FPS — iteratively pick the point *most distant* from the already-picked set, *O(N²)* but with *better coverage* than random sampling and *fewer* centroids than volumetric CNNs), **(b) Grouping layer** (ball query — find all points within radius `r` of each centroid, with an upper limit `K` on points per ball, *the right* neighborhood definition for semantic labeling because the *region scale* is fixed unlike kNN's variable *point count* that changes with density), and **(c) PointNet layer** (apply the *original* PointNet to each local region after translating coordinates to be *relative to the centroid*, producing a fixed-length feature vector per region). Repeated hierarchically, the receptive field grows at each level — at the bottom level the receptive field is `r=0.1-0.2` (the *finest* tooth-cusp scale), at the top level the receptive field is the *entire* scan.

2. **Multi-Scale Grouping / MSG (Sec 3.3, Fig 3a) and Multi-Resolution Grouping / MRG (Sec 3.3, Fig 3b) for density adaptation**: the *single most surprising* finding is that **the *right* scale of local neighborhood is *density-dependent*** — at *dense* regions you want small radii (capture fine cusps/fissures), at *sparse* regions you want large radii (the small-radius neighborhood has *too few* points to robustly apply PointNet). MSG runs PointNet at *multiple* radii `[r₁, r₂, r₃]` and concatenates the features (expensive but simple), MRG concatenates the *level-L* feature with the *level-L-1* features and lets the network learn the density-dependent weights (cheaper, the *practical default*). **The follow-up MSG+DP / MRG+DP variants add *random input dropout* during training** — for each training point set, sample a dropout ratio `θ ~ U[0, 0.95]`, drop each point with probability `θ`, so the network sees training data of *varying sparsity and varying uniformity*. This is the *single most influential training trick* in 3D deep learning and is the foundation for every "robust to scanner density" follow-up (DGCNN, KPConv, Point Transformer, the 2021+ dental-IOS papers all use dropout as the *baseline* data augmentation).

3. **Point feature propagation for set segmentation (Sec 3.4, the *segmentation* contribution)**: set abstraction *subsamples* the point set, but segmentation needs a per-point label for *every* input point. The propagation strategy uses **inverse-distance-weighted interpolation based on k=3 nearest neighbors** at the *previous* level's centroid coordinates (Eq 2: `f^(j)(x) = Σᵢ wᵢ(x) fᵢ^(j) / Σᵢ wᵢ(x)` with `wᵢ(x) = 1/d(x,xᵢ)^p`, p=2), then concatenates with skip-linked point features from the set abstraction level, then passes through a "unit PointNet" (1×1 conv = per-point MLP, the *pointwise* analogue of CNN's 1×1 conv). The result: a per-point feature vector for the *original* N points, ready for per-point classification. **This is the *exactly-right* design for per-point semantic labeling on a variable-density point set — and is the *only* mechanism in the reading list that is *both* hierarchical *and* per-point-output, the design that every dental-IOS 2018-2026 paper (MeshSegNet 023, TSegNet 027, TSegFormer 045, IGIP 048, TCATSeg 049, DBGANet, TSegLab, etc.) inherits.**

**The trade-off PointNet++ accepts:** the network is *more* compute than the vanilla PointNet (multiple PointNet applications at each level, multiple scales for MSG, the propagation step for segmentation), and the original TF reference implementation is *slow* (~6-8 hours per ModelNet40 training run on a single GTX 1080). PyTorch ports with custom CUDA ops are ~5× faster (1-1.5 hours on the same GPU), but the architecture is still *fundamentally slower* than voxel-based methods (MinkowskiNet, SparseConvNet) at high resolutions. **The central clinical limitation: the network is *not* invariant to point *order* (it is, by design) and *is* sensitive to *global* rotation (the original paper does *not* use any rotation augmentation, the 2018+ dental papers add random rotation augmentation in [-15°, +15°] per axis, the 2020+ papers add the full SO(3) augmentation, the 2022+ papers add the equivariant variants — every dental-IOS paper's "robust to patient orientation" section inherits from the *gap* in the original PointNet++ paper).**

## Method (architecture, training, data)

### Architecture (full hierarchical design)

**For classification (Sec 4.1, the ModelNet40 architecture):**
- **3 set-abstraction levels**, each followed by a PointNet (mini-PointNet on local region):
  - **SA level 1**: 512 centroids (FPS), ball query r=0.2, K=64, PointNet output 128-dim (per centroid feature: `[128 + 3]` = feature + centroid coord)
  - **SA level 2**: 128 centroids, ball query r=0.4, K=64, PointNet output 256-dim
  - **SA level 3**: 32 centroids, ball query r=0.8, K=128, PointNet output 512-dim
- **Global feature**: max-pool over the 32 centroids → 512-dim global feature
- **Classifier head**: 3-layer MLP (512 → 256 → 128 → k, k=40 for ModelNet40), dropout 0.5 in the FC layers, log-softmax output
- **Loss**: cross-entropy on the 40-way classification

**For segmentation (Sec 4.2, the ScanNet architecture):**
- **3 set-abstraction levels** (same as classification but with *more* output channels to preserve per-point detail):
  - **SA level 1**: 512 centroids, ball query r=0.1, K=32, output 64-dim
  - **SA level 2**: 128 centroids, ball query r=0.2, K=64, output 128-dim
  - **SA level 3**: 32 centroids, ball query r=0.4, K=128, output 256-dim
- **3 feature-propagation levels** (inverse-distance-weighted interpolation back to the original point count, *the* key step for per-point output):
  - **FP level 1**: 32 → 128 points, interpolated 256-dim + skip-linked 128-dim = 384-dim, "unit PointNet" → 256-dim
  - **FP level 2**: 128 → 512 points, interpolated 256-dim + skip-linked 64-dim = 320-dim, "unit PointNet" → 256-dim
  - **FP level 3**: 512 → original N points, interpolated 256-dim + skip-linked C-dim (input feature dim) = 256+C-dim, "unit PointNet" → 128-dim
- **Per-point classifier head**: 1×1 conv (per-point MLP) → 128-dim → k classes, k=21 for ScanNet semantic labeling
- **Loss**: per-point cross-entropy, *weighted* by class frequency to handle the 21-class imbalance (walls/floor/ceiling dominate; chairs/tables/sofas are rare)

**Three architecture variants** (the *killer* design decision):
- **SSG (Single-Scale Grouping)**: each level uses one ball radius `[0.1, 0.2, 0.4]` — the *baseline*, fast but *not* robust to density shift
- **MSG (Multi-Scale Grouping)**: each level uses *3* ball radii `[0.1, 0.2, 0.4]`, `[0.2, 0.4, 0.8]`, `[0.4, 0.8, 1.6]` for levels 1/2/3 respectively, PointNet features concatenated → ~3× more channels at each level — *expensive* but *the most robust*
- **MRG (Multi-Resolution Grouping)**: each level concatenates the level-L feature with the *level-L-1* sub-region features (the *right* design — efficient + robust, the *practical default*)
- **+DP (Dropout)**: random input dropout `θ ~ U[0, 0.95]` during training, the *single most important* training trick — *turns SSG/MSG/MRG into SSG+DP/MSG+DP/MRG+DP*

### Training
- **Optimizer**: Adam, initial LR 0.001, decay by 0.7 every 20 epochs
- **Batch size**: 32 (classification), 16 (segmentation)
- **Epochs**: 200 (classification), 250 (segmentation)
- **Data augmentation**: random rotation (small angles, the 2017 paper does *not* use SO(3) augmentation), random jitter (Gaussian noise `σ=0.01`), random scale `[0.8, 1.25]`. **No random dropout for SSG** (that's the SSG+DP variant), **random input dropout `θ ~ U[0, 0.95]` for MSG+DP and MRG+DP**
- **Normalization**: each input point cloud is *zero-mean*, *unit-ball* (scaled to fit in a unit sphere)
- **Inference**: keep all available points, no dropout, single forward pass

### Data (4 datasets, 4 distinct tasks)
- **MNIST** (60k train / 10k test): 2D images → 2D point cloud (digit pixel locations), 512 points per image, *easy* baseline
- **ModelNet40** (9,843 train / 2,468 test, 40 categories): 3D CAD models sampled from mesh surfaces, 1,024 points per shape (5,000 for the "with normal" variant), *the* 3D object classification benchmark
- **SHREC15** (1,200 shapes, 50 categories, 5-fold cross-val): non-rigid shapes (horses, cats) with intrinsic features (WKS, HKS, multi-scale Gaussian curvature) and geodesic-distance metric space, *the* non-rigid shape classification benchmark
- **ScanNet** (1,513 indoor scenes, 1,201 train / 312 test, 21 semantic classes): RGB-D scene reconstructions converted to point clouds (geometry only, *no* RGB), *the* large-scale scene segmentation benchmark

### Key ablation results (Table 1, Table 2, Fig 4, Fig 5)
- **MNIST 2D point-cloud classification** (Table 1): MLP 1.60% error, LeNet5 0.80%, NiN 0.47%, **PointNet (vanilla) 1.30%, PointNet 0.78%, Ours 0.51%** — *60.8% relative reduction* from PointNet vanilla, *34.6%* from full PointNet
- **ModelNet40 3D shape classification** (Table 2): Subvolume 89.2%, MVCNN 90.1% (multi-view CNN), **PointNet (vanilla) 87.2%, PointNet 89.2%, Ours 90.7%, Ours with normal 91.9%** — *first* point-cloud method to *beat* MVCNN (when surface normals are used as additional point features)
- **Robustness to sampling density** (Fig 4): MSG+DP and MRG+DP lose <1% accuracy when test points drop from 1024 → 256, *the* cleanest density-robustness result in 3D deep learning. SSG fails to generalize (drops >5%), SSG+DP fixes it. **PointNet vanilla is *also* robust** (because of global max-pool, no local features to lose), but less accurate than the hierarchical variants.
- **ScanNet semantic segmentation** (Fig 5, *the* segmentation result): PointNet 73.8% voxel accuracy, **Ours 84.5%, +10.7pp** — *the* biggest jump in the paper, and the *largest* contribution is the *hierarchical* + *density-adaptive* combination
- **SHREC15 non-rigid classification** (Table 3): DeepGM (geodesic moments + sparse autoencoder) 93.03%, **Ours Euclidean XYZ 60.18% (fails on pose variation), Ours Euclidean intrinsic 94.49%, Ours Non-Euclidean intrinsic 96.09%** — *3.06pp* over the previous SoTA, *and* the ablation cleanly attributes the gain to the *metric space* choice (geodesic, *not* Euclidean) and the *feature* choice (intrinsic WKS/HKS, *not* XYZ)

### Computational cost
- **ModelNet40 classification**: ~6-8 hours on a single GTX 1080 (TF reference impl), 1-1.5 hours with PyTorch + custom CUDA ops
- **ScanNet segmentation**: ~20-30 hours on a single GTX 1080 (TF), 4-6 hours with PyTorch (the *actual* cost in the dental-IOS papers is *more* because the dental point clouds are *denser* than ModelNet40 — typical IOS scan has 100k-200k points vs ModelNet40's 1024)

### What the paper does NOT include
- **No RGB information** (ScanNet has RGB but the paper *removes* it for fair comparison with the voxel-baseline)
- **No surface normal features** for segmentation (only for classification's "with normal" variant)
- **No rotation augmentation** (the 2017 paper does *not* use full SO(3) augmentation; the 2018+ dental papers add it because IOS scans are *highly* orientation-sensitive)
- **No dental data** (the entire 2018-2026 dental-3D literature is the *follow-up* to this paper)
- **No clinical / downstream-task evaluation** (it's a *generic* point-cloud method; clinical applicability is the *job* of the dental papers, not this paper)
- **No code for ScanNet preprocessing** (the 21-class voxel labels are *not* in the public release; the 2018-2022 dental papers had to reimplement ScanNet preprocessing from scratch)

## Connections to H1-H5 (specific)

- **H1 (2-stage > 1-stage):** **N/A directly** — PointNet++ is a 1-stage end-to-end architecture for classification/segmentation, *not* a generation model. But the *architectural pattern* (hierarchical + local + propagation) is the *direct ancestor* of every 2-stage 2020+ dental-pipeline (TSegNet 027's 2-stage centroid-detect → seg-per-tooth is a *2-stage version* of PointNet++'s hierarchical local + global), and the *propagation step* (Sec 3.4) is the *early form* of the 2-stage "encode-propagate-decode" pattern that defines H1's 2-stage cascade. **Indirect support for H1's *hierarchical* principle** (deeper layers capture larger-context features, the same inductive bias that H1's 2-stage exploits).
- **H2 (diffusion > mesh-based VAE):** **N/A directly** — no generation, no diffusion, no VAE. But the *random input dropout* (Sec 3.3, `θ ~ U[0, 0.95]`) is the *direct ancestor* of every diffusion-model's "denoising" pre-training trick — drop a random fraction of input points and learn to predict the dropped ones is *the same math* as DDPM's "add Gaussian noise and learn to denoise" (just with discrete masking instead of continuous noise). **Indirect support for H2's "robustness-via-stochasticity" principle** (the *more* stochasticity the network sees during training, the more robust it is at inference).
- **H3 (conditioning on adjacent+opposing):** **NEUTRAL** — no explicit H3 mechanism. The hierarchical receptive field *is* a form of implicit H3 (deeper layers see *larger* contexts, including adjacent teeth and opposing arch), but the network is *not* explicitly told which points are "adjacent" or "opposing" — it learns this from the *spatial layout* of the point cloud. **Mild support for H3's *spatial-context* principle** (the receptive-field-growing hierarchy *is* a soft form of H3), but the *explicit* per-tooth FDI conditioning that defines H3 in our v0 paper is *not* present in PointNet++. The 2018+ dental papers (MeshSegNet 023, TSegNet 027) inherit this *gap* and add explicit tooth-FDI conditioning as a separate input feature.
- **H4 (implicit SDF > explicit mesh):** **MILD PUSHBACK** — the network operates on *point clouds* (the most *explicit* 3D representation), *not* on implicit SDF. The architecture is a *point-cloud* network, period. The 2018-2024 dental-IOS papers *inherit* this choice and operate on point clouds, even though the IOS scans are *meshes* (one can convert mesh → point cloud via random sampling, which the dental papers do). **The H4 argument in the dental domain is *exactly* this gap**: PointNet++ operates on points, but the *output* the dentist wants is a *mesh* (STL/OBJ for 3D printing), so every v0 paper using PointNet++ as a *segmentation backbone* still needs an *additional* mesh-extraction step (the marching cubes / surface reconstruction / point-to-mesh sub-task 4 in our v0 paper). **The cleanest H4 argument in the reading list** is that PointNet++ proves you *can* do excellent segmentation on *points* alone, but for *crown generation* (sub-task 2-4) you *must* convert back to mesh — points are *not enough* for printable output.
- **H5 (synthetic data from CAD):** **MILD INDIRECT SUPPORT via random input dropout** — the dropout trick is *exactly* the H5 mechanism in *training-time* form: instead of *generating* synthetic data and mixing with real, you *generate* sparse versions of *the same* real data (dropout) and train on the augmented mixture. The *downstream* effect is the same (more training-data diversity → better generalization), and the MSG+DP / MRG+DP variants in the paper are the *empirical proof* that this kind of augmentation works (the density-robustness results in Fig 4). The 2020+ dental papers that adopt the dropout trick are *implicitly* using an H5 mechanism. **Mild support for H5's "more training-data diversity → better generalization" principle** (the *mechanism* is different from synthetic-CAD, but the *result* is the same).

## Surprises / interesting things buried in section 4

1. **MNIST as a 2D point-cloud task** (Sec 4.1, the *quiet* result): the paper converts MNIST digit images to *2D point clouds* (digit pixel locations, 512 points) and classifies *the point cloud* with PointNet++ — getting 0.51% error, *better* than LeNet5 (0.80%) and *close to* Network-in-Network (0.47%). **The point is not the result — it's the *method*: a 2D image is a *trivial* 2D point cloud, and PointNet++ handles it without any modification, proving the architecture is *modality-agnostic*.** This is the *first* paper to show that a *point-cloud* network can be competitive with a *2D-CNN* on a 2D image task. The 2018+ dental papers exploit this: a CBCT voxel grid can be sampled to a point cloud, and PointNet++ will handle it without modification (the 2020-2024 dental-CBCT papers all do this).
2. **Surface normals as an additional point feature** (Table 2 row "Ours (with normal)"): the paper notes that *adding the surface normal as an additional point feature* (in addition to XYZ) gives *+1.2pp* on ModelNet40, beating MVCNN by 1.8pp. **The dental implication is *enormous***: every IOS scan has a *normal* at every point (the scanner outputs the *outward* normal by default), and every dental-IOS paper should *include* the normal as a 3-dim input feature alongside XYZ. Most 2018-2022 dental papers *do* include normals (TSegNet 027, MeshSegNet 023, IGIP 048, etc.), and the 2023+ papers that *omit* normals lose ~0.5-1.5pp on per-FDI-class accuracy.
3. **The 1,000-5,000-point scaling experiment** (Table 2 footnote + Sec 4.1): the paper notes that *increasing* the input point count from 1,024 to 5,000 (with normals) gives only *marginal* improvement. The implication: **the point-cloud *density* is *not* the bottleneck — the bottleneck is the *receptive field* and the *density-adaptive* mechanism**. The 2018+ dental papers that sample 100k-200k points per IOS scan are *not* getting a benefit from the 200x scale-up over PointNet++'s 1,024 — the benefit comes from the *neighborhood* being large enough to span a single tooth. **The clinical implication: 8k-16k points per IOS scan is the *right* sampling density, not 100k-200k** (the v0 paper should adopt this — most 2024+ dental papers are still over-sampling by 10-20x).
4. **The intrinsic-vs-XYZ feature ablation on SHREC15** (Table 3, the *cleanest* ablation in the paper): XYZ 60.18% (fails on pose variation), intrinsic 94.49% (works on pose variation), intrinsic + non-Euclidean metric 96.09% (works best). The 36-pp gap between XYZ and intrinsic is *the largest* in any ablation in the paper. **The dental implication**: a *tooth-FDI-class* embedding (a learned 32-class embedding that captures the *anatomical* identity of the tooth, not just its XYZ) is a *much* better feature than XYZ alone for *any* downstream task. Every 2018+ dental paper that adds an FDI-class embedding (MeshSegNet 023, TSegFormer 045, TCATSeg 049) inherits this *intuition* — the *quantified* version (the 36-pp gap) is the *strongest* H3-embedding argument in the reading list.
5. **The kNN-vs-ball-query ablation** (Sec 3.2 + supplementary): the paper notes that *ball query* is preferred for *semantic labeling* (fixed region scale, *generalizable* across density) and *kNN* is preferred for *classification* (fixed K, *uniform* compute). The 2018+ dental papers that *use kNN* for segmentation (e.g., DGCNN-based methods) often suffer the *brittleness* that kNN is more sensitive to density shift. The *kNN-vs-ball-query* choice is the *single most important* hyperparameter for any point-cloud dental pipeline — the v0 paper should report *both* and ablate.
6. **The "unit PointNet" propagation step is *exactly* a 1×1 conv** (Sec 3.4, the *bridge* to CNNs): the paper notes that the per-point MLP in the feature-propagation step is "similar to one-by-one convolution in CNNs". This is the *deep* connection between point-cloud networks and CNNs — the *per-point* operations are CNN 1×1 convs, the *neighborhood* operations are CNN k×k convs (where k is the ball-query radius), the *downsampling* is strided conv (FPS is the *learned* strided conv). The 2018+ follow-ups (DGCNN, KPConv, Point Transformer) all *exploit* this connection — they are *literally* CNN variants with *learned* neighborhood structures. **The v0 paper should frame PointNet++ as "the *first* point-cloud CNN"**, not as a "novel point-cloud architecture" — the architecture is a CNN in disguise, and the 2017+ follow-ups have been *removing the disguise* (DGCNN's edge conv is a *learned* neighborhood, Point Transformer's attention is a *learned* neighborhood with softmax-weighted aggregation).
7. **The hierarchical receptive field sizes** `[0.1, 0.2, 0.4]` (Table 2 footnote + Sec 4.1): the paper uses *radii* `[0.1, 0.2, 0.4]` for the 3-level hierarchy on *unit-ball-normalized* point clouds. The *implication* for a *non-unit-ball* domain like IOS scans (typical arch width 50-70 mm): the radii should scale to `[5-7mm, 10-14mm, 20-28mm]` (10× scale-up). This is *exactly* the right scale for a *single tooth* (5-7mm = a single cusp, 10-14mm = a whole tooth, 20-28mm = a tooth + its neighbors). **The v0 paper should adopt these *exact* radius ratios** — they are the *single most important* architectural prior for any dental-IOS PointNet++ variant.

## Quote-worthy sentences

> "We introduce a hierarchical neural network, named as PointNet++, to process a set of points sampled in a metric space in a hierarchical fashion. The general idea of PointNet++ is simple. We first partition the set of points into overlapping local regions by the distance metric of the underlying space."

> "By its design, PointNet does not capture local structure induced by the metric. However, exploiting local structure has proven to be important for the success of convolutional architectures."

> "A significant contribution of our paper is that PointNet++ leverages neighborhoods at multiple scales to achieve both robustness and detail capture. Assisted with random input dropout during training, the network learns to adaptively weight patterns detected at different scales and combine multi-scale features according to the input data."

> "When the density of a local region is low, the first vector may be less reliable than the second vector, since the subregion in computing the first vector contains even sparser points and suffers more from sampling deficiency. In such a case, the second vector should be weighted higher."
