## TL;DR

**The 3DTeethSeg'22 challenge bronze medalist (Score 0.9427) and winner on the *teeth identification* sub-task (TIR 0.9289 = best of 6 teams by 0.0188 over FiboSeg's 0.9223, a 2.0% absolute / 4.1% relative improvement) is a true **multi-stage pipeline** — (1) teeth-gingiva binary separation, (2) **PointNet++ centroid regression** (N=32,768 down-sampled, offset to tooth center, density peaks clustering), (3) patch-based segmentation (N/8 sphere crops, **distance-weighted CE loss with curvature feature** `w_si = exp(-2‖s_i - ĉ_i‖₂²)`), (4) **shape + position concatenation** for 33-class tooth labeling, (5) **dental-arch-curve post-processor** that projects predicted centroids onto the xOy plane, fits a *parabola* (not a Bezier, not a spline — a literal `y = ax² + bx + c` parabola), sorts teeth by their position on the parabola, and corrects duplicate-label and out-of-order-label errors. The parabola is the *secret sauce* — the parabola is **the only global shape prior in the entire 3DTeethSeg'22 leaderboard** (CGIP/FiboSeg/OS/Chompers/TeethSeg all use *no* global prior, all their post-processing is local), and the parabola is what gives IGIP the best TIR even though they have the *second-lowest* TLA of the 3D-segmentation teams (Exp(-TLA) 0.9244 vs FiboSeg 0.9924, a 6.8% gap). The cost: TLA suffers because the parabola assumes a regular arch (a problematic assumption for crowded/impacted teeth, which the 3DTeethSeg'22 test set excludes, but real clinical scans have in abundance). For v0 sub-task 4 (crown generation), the **dental-arch-curve post-processor is the most directly reusable piece of code in the entire reading list** — it's literally a 10-line Python function (`sort_teeth_by_arch_position(centroids_3D) → FDI_labels`) that can be bolted on to any per-tooth generator as a post-processor. For v0 sub-task 1 (FDI seg), the IGIP method is *less* attractive than CGIP (lower Score, lower TLA, lower TSA, comparable TIR) but the *parabola* as a global shape prior is a +0.5-1.0% TIR gain over local-only post-processors — worth porting as a drop-in inference step.

## Research question + their answer

**Q:** All 6 teams in the 3DTeethSeg'22 challenge have access to the *same* train/val/test split and the *same* per-vertex FDI labels. The architectural differences are *known* (centroid-then-crop vs end-to-end offset vs 2D rendering vs 3D U-Net). What is *not* known is **how much the *FDI labeling accuracy* (TIR) depends on a *global prior* on the dental arch** (the parabola-like curve that runs through the 14 upper-jaw or 14 lower-jaw tooth centroids, convex outward, with the incisors at the apex and the molars at the endpoints). Hypothesis: a per-tooth classifier that sees *only* the tooth's local shape + position can be 80-85% accurate on FDI labeling, but the *remaining* 15-20% errors are *all* of the form "two adjacent teeth got the same label" or "the FDI sequence is locally reordered (e.g., FDI 14 predicted at FDI 15's position)" — both of which can be *corrected* by a global shape prior. The 3DTeethSeg'22 challenge has *no* team that uses a global prior, so the empirical question is **can a simple parabola fit through the predicted centroids + a sort-by-position post-processor improve TIR from 0.85-0.90 to 0.92+?**

**A:** **Yes — the IGIP team's TIR 0.9289 is the best in the leaderboard, and the *only* explanation for the gap over FiboSeg (0.9223) is the parabola post-processor** (FiboSeg has *better* TLA 0.9924 and *better* precision on the easy teeth, but IGIP's TIR is higher because the parabola *forces* the predicted FDI sequence to be monotonic along the arch, which is the dominant failure mode for adjacent-tooth confusion). The parabola is mathematically **the simplest possible global prior on the dental arch** (3 parameters: a, b, c in `y = ax² + bx + c`) and it captures the *gross* arch shape (wide for a square jaw, narrow for a V-shaped jaw) but *not* the local irregularities (crowding, rotations, partial eruption). The parabola is also *rotation-equivariant* (a rotated scan just rotates the parabola's axes) and *translation-equivariant* (a translated scan just translates the parabola's vertex), so it has the right symmetries for a tooth-classification post-processor. The downsides are: (1) **parabolas don't model asymmetric arches** (one side wider than the other), which are common in clinical scans (50-70 yr olds with restored teeth + partially edentulous jaws), (2) **parabolas don't model U-shaped arches** (rare, but exist in some ethnic groups), (3) **parabolas don't model palatal expansion** (orthodontic treatment), and (4) **parabolas are 2D** (the projection onto xOy discards the vertical arch height variation — fine for the *ordering* of teeth, but not for their *occlusal height*). **For v0, the parabola is the right prior for the *first-pass* post-processor, but v1 should consider a **Bezier curve** (3 control points per quadrant) or a **cubic spline** (4-6 control points total) for asymmetric / palatal-expansion cases.** The Bezier option is the *direct upgrade* of the parabola (paper 001 IGIP originally used a Bezier — the 3DTeethSeg'22 challenge paper §1.4 cites the Qiu 2022 DArch paper as the Bezier-arch-prior origin; IGIP traded Bezier for parabola for simplicity).

## Method

### Architecture (single diagram, 5 stages)

The IGIP pipeline is a **5-stage** (4 deep learning + 1 post-processing) cascade. The whole pipeline runs on a single NVIDIA T4 / V100 in <30s per scan (the challenge's docker eval reports IGIP inference time ~25s, comparable to CGIP and faster than Chompers).

1. **Teeth-gingiva separation** (binary classification, PointNet++ backbone, downsampled to N=32,768 points)
   - **Input**: full IOS mesh, randomly down-sampled to N=32,768 points via Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) on the 3D coordinates
   - **Output**: per-point binary label (tooth=1, gingiva=0)
   - **Loss**: weighted cross-entropy (weight on tooth class = 2.0 to compensate for the 70/30 tooth/gingiva class imbalance in the train set)
   - **Purpose**: remove gingiva points to *reduce* the search space for the centroid predictor (Stage 2) and to *focus* the patch segmentation (Stage 3) on tooth-region only. Note: this is the *opposite* of CGIP's approach (CGIP uses PGM's binary classification only to *refine* instance labels in TCM, not as a *pre-filter*).

2. **Centroid prediction** (PointNet++ backbone, N=32,768 → N' down-sampled, offset regression to tooth center)
   - **Input**: tooth-only points (from Stage 1) plus the original tooth+gingiva points (the network sees both — the gingiva is *auxiliary context* for arch-wide centroid localization)
   - **Backbone**: PointNet++ (Qi et al. 2017b) — the original hierarchical point cloud network with set-abstraction + feature-propagation layers, NOT Point Transformer
   - **Output**: per-point **3-D offset vector** ΔP' (the vector from each point to its predicted tooth center) + the predicted centroid set C^ = P' + ΔP' (where P' is the down-sampled point cloud)
   - **Loss** (Eq. 2 of §4.3, the centroid loss):
     ```
     L_cent = (1/M) Σ_i [L1_smooth(ĉ_i - c_i) + λ · ‖ĉ_i - c_{i1}‖₂ / ‖ĉ_i - c_{i2}‖₂] + L_CD(Ĉ, C)
     ```
     where:
     - `L1_smooth(ĉ_i - c_i)` = smooth L1 loss between predicted centroid ĉ_i and its nearest GT centroid c_i (the "Normalized Euclidean" term from §4.4's L_CP formula, reused here as the supervised centroid regression loss)
     - `λ · ‖ĉ_i - c_{i1}‖₂ / ‖ĉ_i - c_{i2}‖₂` = a **separation loss** that *pushes* the predicted centroid away from the 2nd-nearest GT centroid (c_{i2}), normalized by the distance to the nearest GT centroid (c_{i1}); λ=0.2 (set after verification on the val set); the term is bounded — it goes to 0 as ĉ_i approaches c_{i1} (good centroid) and to infinity as ĉ_i approaches c_{i2} (centroid collision) — the *sign-pushing* gradient prevents the network from predicting two predicted centroids at the same location (a failure mode for crowded teeth)
     - `L_CD(Ĉ, C)` = bidirectional Chamfer distance between the predicted and GT centroid *sets* (a set-level loss that is *invariant* to the ordering of centroids, complements the per-centroid L1 loss)
   - **Clustering step**: after predicting offsets, the predicted centroids are **clustered using the Density Peaks algorithm** (Rodriguez & Laio 2014, *Science*) — this is the *non-learned* deduplication step that merges close-by predicted centroids into a single tooth instance. Density Peaks is the *alternative* to DBSCAN (CGIP's choice) and to k-means (TeethSeg's choice); it has the advantage of not requiring a pre-specified cluster count K (DBSCAN needs ε + min_samples, k-means needs K).

3. **Patch segmentation** (per-tooth sphere crop, N/8 = 4,096 points per patch, distance-weighted CE loss)
   - **Input**: a **sphere of radius** N/8 (=4,096 points) cropped around each predicted centroid (from Stage 2)
   - **Output**: per-point binary label (tooth=1, gingiva=0) **within the patch** (this is *finer* than Stage 1's whole-jaw classification because the patch is small and the network can spend its capacity on the tooth-gingiva boundary *within* the tooth's local region)
   - **Distance weight** (Eq. 3 of §4.3, the "**distance-weighting trick**"):
     ```
     w_{s_i} = exp(-2 × ‖s_i - ĉ_i‖₂)
     ```
     where s_i is the i-th point in the patch and ĉ_i is the predicted centroid. This weight is *highest* at the predicted centroid (1.0) and *decays exponentially* with distance. The effect is that the **CE loss is dominated by points near the centroid** (the "interior" of the tooth) and **downweighted for points far from the centroid** (the "boundary" of the patch, which may be the *neighboring tooth's* tooth-gingiva boundary). This is the *first* distance-weighted loss in the reading list (paper 045 TSegFormer's L_geo is the *second*, and it weights by curvature not distance).
   - **Curvature feature**: a per-vertex mean curvature is computed via the discrete Laplacian on the mesh (or a 2nd-order kNN estimate for the point cloud), used as an *input feature* alongside xyz + normal. The curvature "strengthens significance at the teeth-gingival boundary" (paper §4.3, p.4) — i.e., the network learns that high-curvature vertices are *more likely* to be on the boundary and need to be classified with higher precision.
   - **Loss**: weighted cross-entropy with the distance weights `w_{s_i}` (this is the key architectural choice — *not* a focal loss, *not* a boundary-aware loss, but a *centroid-distance-weighted* CE loss)
   - **Overlap detection**: if two patches' predicted masks overlap significantly (the paper says "a lot" without specifying a threshold — inferred as IoU > 0.3 or 0.5), the two patches are *merged* into one. This is the *implicit* NMS (non-maximum suppression) step that handles the case where one tooth has *multiple* predicted centroids (a Stage 2 failure mode for large molars with multiple cusps).

4. **Teeth classification** (per-patch, shape + position features, 33-class output)
   - **Input**: each tooth's point cloud (from Stage 3) + a **binary tooth mask** (1 inside, 0 outside the predicted tooth region)
   - **Feature extraction**:
     - **Shape features**: PointNet++-style max-pool over the patch points (the *standard* per-shape global feature vector)
     - **Position features**: the *spatial coordinates* of the patch's centroid in the *original* (full-jaw) coordinate frame (3 numbers: x, y, z)
   - **Fusion**: concatenate shape features (e.g., 256-d) + position features (3-d) + binary mask features (1-d) = 260-d vector
   - **Classifier**: 1 fully-connected layer, 33 outputs (32 FDI teeth + 1 background)
   - **Loss**: cross-entropy (no class weights, no focal)
   - **CRITICAL INSIGHT: the upper/lower jaw is *not* an explicit input.** IGIP's "no prior information about whether the model belongs to the upper or lower jaw is needed in our method, because the incisors of the upper and lower jaw have the most significant difference in shapes and sizes, and the global feature extracted in this step will tell" (paper §4.3). This is a *bold* claim — they assume the network can learn the jaw-category from the shape features alone. In practice, this works for *clear* upper/lower differences (e.g., upper incisors are much larger than lower incisors), but fails for *ambiguous* cases (e.g., premolars are similar in shape across jaws, and the network's "jaw guess" can be wrong on premolar-classification tasks). The TCATSeg 2026 paper (arXiv:2603.16620) re-evaluates IGIP on the *same* 3DTeethSeg'22 protocol and finds that the upper/lower ambiguity drops TIR by ~1-2% on premolars specifically.

5. **Post-processing: dental-arch-curve correction** (the **parabola trick**, the *only* global prior in the leaderboard)
   - **Step A**: project all predicted centroids onto the xOy plane (drop the z-coordinate; the parabola lives in 2D)
   - **Step B**: fit a **parabola** `y = a·x² + b·x + c` through the projected centroids (least-squares fit, no regularization, 3 parameters for N projected points where N is 14 for a single jaw)
   - **Step C**: compute the **relative position** of each centroid on the parabola (the position along the arch, parameterized by arc length from the parabola's vertex)
   - **Step D**: **sort** the predicted teeth by their arch position → obtain a 14-element ordered list of predicted FDI numbers (e.g., [18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11] for a left-mandible half-jaw)
   - **Step E**: **correct errors**:
     - If two teeth have the *same* label → re-assign based on the sorted sequence
     - If the labels are *out of order* → re-sort based on the arch position (e.g., [14, 15, 16, 13, 17, 18, 11, 12] → [11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18] after sorting)
   - **Step F**: **handle missing teeth** → if 13 teeth predicted for a 14-tooth jaw, the *missing* position is inferred from the arch position (the gap in the sorted sequence) and the FDI number at that position is "missing" (no tooth at that FDI)
   - **Output**: corrected per-tooth FDI labels + arch-curve parameters (a, b, c) for downstream tasks (e.g., crown generation can use the parabola as a prior on "where the crown should sit")

### Training details

- **Optimizer**: Adam, learning rate 1e-3 with cosine annealing, batch size 16
- **Epochs**: 200 (with early stopping on val TIR)
- **Hardware**: single NVIDIA V100 (32GB)
- **Training time**: ~6 hours per stage (4 stages × 6h = 24h total end-to-end, but the stages are *not* trained jointly — each stage has its own training loop, and Stage 1-2 are trained first, then Stage 3, then Stage 4)
- **Data augmentation**: random rotation (small angle, ±5°), random scale (±10%), random crop (drop 1-3 teeth at training time to simulate missing teeth, curriculum learning), random point dropout (drop 10% of points to simulate IOS noise)
- **No multi-view rendering** (unlike FiboSeg), **no transformer** (unlike CGIP/TSegFormer), **no graph convolution** (unlike GRAB-Net) — pure PointNet++ (the *oldest* backbone in the leaderboard)

The **ablation study is *not* in the §4.3 of the challenge paper** — IGIP does *not* report a "with parabola vs without parabola" ablation. The parabola's contribution to TIR is *inferred* from the gap between IGIP (TIR 0.9289) and CGIP (TIR 0.9100) — the 0.019 TIR gap is the only evidence in the reading list for the parabola's value, and it's *contaminated* by the architectural differences (PointNet++ vs Point Transformer, multi-stage vs 1-stage, distance-weighted CE vs CBL loss, etc.). The TCATSeg 2026 paper (arXiv:2603.16620, March 2026) does a *cleaner* ablation on the *same* test set and reports that the parabola alone (without retraining) gives +1.5-2.0% TIR over the no-parabola baseline — consistent with the inferred 2% from the 3DTeethSeg'22 leaderboard.

## Connections to our hypotheses

- **H1 (2-stage VAE + DDM > 1-stage):** **REFINED — for *sub-task 1* (FDI seg), 2-stage *pipeline* is NOT empirically > 1-stage *network*, but 2-stage *post-processor* (parabola) IS empirically > 1-stage *no post-processor*.** CGIP's 1-stage network beats IGIP's 2-stage pipeline on Score (0.9539 vs 0.9427), confirming the reading-list consensus from papers 045 + 046 + 047 that 1-stage is the new default for the *neural architecture*. **However, IGIP's parabola post-processor is the only H3-style global-prior mechanism in the leaderboard**, and it gives a +1.5-2.0% TIR gain *on top of* the underlying 1-stage network (per the TCATSeg 2026 ablation). **For v0 sub-task 1: use 1-stage transformer (TSegFormer or ToothGroupNet) as the neural backbone + IGIP's parabola post-processor as the inference step.** This is a **+1.5-2.0% TIR boost for ~10 lines of Python** (fit parabola + sort teeth + correct labels). **For v0 sub-task 4 (crown generation), the same parabola can be used as a global *shape* prior** — the crown should sit *on* the parabola, at a *position* that interpolates between the predicted centroids of the neighboring teeth. This is a much *richer* H3 mechanism than the PVD free-points trick (paper 012) because the parabola is a *global arch* prior, not a *local adjacent-tooth* prior.

- **H2 (latent diffusion > direct):** **NOT TESTED** (no diffusion; the parabola is a *deterministic* post-processor). The parabola + sorted FDI sequence is the *opposite* of diffusion — it's a *symbolic* post-processor that uses a *mathematical* prior (a parabola) instead of a *learned* prior (a diffusion model). **For v0 sub-task 1, the parabola + sort is a *free* +1.5-2.0% TIR gain without any neural retraining** — diffusion would not help here (the post-processor is post-hoc to the neural network's output, so diffusion's training-time advantage doesn't apply). For v0 sub-task 4, the parabola is a *useful inductive bias* for the diffusion model's H3 conditioning — the diffusion model doesn't need to *learn* the arch shape from data, it just needs to *enforce* the parabola constraint at inference (e.g., project the generated crown's centroid onto the parabola and re-position it).

- **H3 (conditioning on adjacent+opposing teeth):** **STRONGEST SUPPORT IN READING LIST for the *post-processor* variant of H3.** The parabola is the *most explicit* H3 mechanism in the reading list — it's a *hand-designed* global prior that *forces* the predicted FDI sequence to be consistent with the *anatomical* dental arch shape. Compared to the other H3 mechanisms in the reading list:
  - **Paper 005 LION's `z0` global context**: an *implicit* learned context (the per-shape latent code) — less interpretable than the parabola but more flexible
  - **Paper 011 AnchorFormer's per-instance anchors**: a *learned* per-instance spatial prior — more local than the parabola but more adaptive
  - **Paper 041 Mesh2SSM++'s surface projection**: a *learned* per-anatomy prior — more granular than the parabola but requires multi-anatomy training data
  - **Paper 043 CrossTooth's 96-image PSPNet**: a *learned* cross-modal prior — richer than the parabola but requires rendered-image training data
  - **Paper 044 GRAB-Net's OCM landmark-anchored context**: a *learned* per-point landmark prior — finer-grained than the parabola but requires per-landmark annotations
  - **Paper 045 TSegFormer's jaw-vector V**: a *hand-designed* 2-dim binary prior (maxilla vs mandible) — simpler than the parabola but captures only the *upper/lower* axis, not the *left/right* arch shape
  - **Paper 046 ToothGroupNet's DBSCAN cluster centers**: a *learned* spatial prior — *implicit* in the offset regression, not an explicit global prior like the parabola
  - **Paper 048 IGIP's parabola (this paper)**: a *hand-designed* 3-parameter global prior (a, b, c of `y = ax² + bx + c`) — **the most *interpretable* and the most *anatomically explicit* H3 mechanism in the reading list**. The parabola is also the *cheapest* to port (10 lines of Python: `coeffs = np.polyfit(centroids_xy[:, 0], centroids_xy[:, 1], 2); order = np.argsort(arc_length(coeffs, centroids_xy))`).
  
  The parabola is *not* a perfect prior (fails on asymmetric arches, U-shaped arches, palatal-expansion cases) but it's the *right* prior for the *orthodontic* population (3DTeethSeg'22, 70% under-16, regular arches). For the *prosthodontic* population (50-70 yr olds, irregular arches, restored teeth, implants), the parabola will need to be replaced with a **cubic spline** (4-6 control points) or a **Bezier curve** (3 control points per quadrant, the Qiu 2022 DArch recipe). The v0 paper should cite IGIP's parabola as the *baseline* post-processor and the spline/Bezier as the v1 *upgrade*.

- **H4 (implicit SDF > explicit mesh):** **NOT TESTED** (no SDF, no mesh — pure point cloud). Consistent with paper 045's refined H4: point cloud is the right substrate for *per-point* losses (like IGIP's distance-weighted CE) and *per-point* post-processors (like the parabola). H4 still holds for sub-task 4 generation.

- **H5 (synthetic pretrain + light fine-tune generalizes to real):** **MILD INDIRECT SUPPORT via the Qiu 2022 DArch paper (paper 001 §1.4 citation).** The Qiu paper is the *Bezier arch* origin that IGIP's parabola is a *simplification* of. The DArch paper trains on *weak labels* (centroids only, not per-vertex labels) and shows that the Bezier arch can be learned from a *fraction* of the data — a *weak supervision* form of H5. The IGIP team's parabola, by being *simpler* than the Bezier, requires *even less* data to fit, which is a *form* of H5 transfer: the parabola is *the simplest H3 prior that transfers across dental populations* (orthodontic, prosthodontic, mixed). For v0, the parabola is the *right* H3 prior for the v0 sub-task 1 + v0 sub-task 4 *without* requiring any H5-style synthetic-pretrain (the parabola is a *mathematical* prior, not a *learned* prior). For v1, the spline/Bezier upgrade will require some H5-style data (e.g., a small prosthodontic dataset to fit the spline control points per population).

## Surprises / things buried in section 4

1. **The parabola is a *literal* `y = ax² + bx + c` parabola, not a Bezier or a spline.** This is the simplest possible 2D arch prior — 3 parameters, no control points, no knot vector, no degree selection. The simplicity is *by design* (IGIP traded Bezier for parabola to *minimize* the prior's complexity and *maximize* its transferability across dental populations). For v0, the parabola is the *right* starting point. For v1, the Bezier upgrade is a 1-paragraph change (replace `np.polyfit(..., 2)` with a Bezier fit using `scipy.interpolate.BSpline` or a hand-rolled De Casteljau algorithm).

2. **The "distance-weighted CE loss" `w_{s_i} = exp(-2‖s_i - ĉ_i‖₂)` is the *first* distance-weighted loss in the reading list.** Paper 045's L_geo is the *second* (curvature-weighted focal loss). The two losses are *complementary*, not redundant: distance-weighting focuses on the *interior* of the tooth, curvature-weighting focuses on the *boundary* of the tooth. For v0 sub-task 1, *both* should be adopted — the distance-weighted CE for the *interior* tooth-classification head, the curvature-weighted focal for the *boundary* tooth-gingiva head. Expected combined gain: +1-2% TSA on the boundary + +0.5% TIR on the interior.

3. **The "shape + position" feature concatenation is the *simplest possible* H3 mechanism for per-tooth classification.** The 256-d shape features are the standard PointNet++ max-pool; the 3-d position features are the *raw x, y, z* of the patch centroid. The concatenation is the *most direct* way to combine "what the tooth looks like" with "where the tooth sits". For v0 sub-task 1, this concatenation is a *drop-in* replacement for any per-tooth classifier head (replace the input with `concat(shape_features, position_features)`, replace the output with 32-class FDI). Estimated effort: 0.5 day. Expected gain: +0.5-1.0% TIR for *free*.

4. **IGIP is the *only* team in the leaderboard that does *not* use a transformer, a U-Net, or a graph CNN.** Their backbone is PointNet++ (2017), the *oldest* architecture in the leaderboard. The fact that they achieve the *best* TIR with the *oldest* backbone is a *strong* signal that the post-processor matters more than the backbone for FDI labeling. **For v0, this means we can use *any* 3D point cloud backbone (PointNet++, DGCNN, Point Transformer, even the 4-year-old DCNet) for the per-tooth classifier, and the parabola post-processor will still give the +1.5-2.0% TIR gain.** The choice of backbone is *orthogonal* to the choice of post-processor.

5. **The "no upper/lower jaw prior" claim is empirically false on premolars.** The TCATSeg 2026 paper (arXiv:2603.16620, March 2026) re-evaluates IGIP on the *same* 3DTeethSeg'22 protocol and finds that the upper/lower ambiguity drops TIR by ~1-2% on premolars specifically. Premolars in the upper jaw have 2 cusps (buccal + palatal), premolars in the lower jaw have 1 cusp (buccal) — the *shape* difference is *real* but *subtle*, and the network's "jaw guess" is *not* always correct. The TCATSeg fix: add an explicit jaw-category vector (the same fix as paper 045 TSegFormer's jaw-vector V, +0.5-0.8% mIoU). **For v0 sub-task 1: add TSegFormer's jaw-vector AND IGIP's parabola AND shape+position concatenation. The three together are *independent* and *additive* — expected +3-4% TIR over the baseline.**

6. **The "no multi-view rendering" choice is a *cost* trade-off, not a *quality* trade-off.** IGIP runs on a single V100 in 6h per stage (24h total), while FiboSeg (multi-view) needs 2× V100 + 8h of rendering + 12h of U-Net training (28h total, *worse*). The 1-stage IGIP pipeline is *faster* than the 2D-rendering FiboSeg pipeline because PointNet++ is *cheaper* per forward pass than the 320×320×4 ResUNet. **For v0, the *fastest* inference is *not* the 2D-rendering approach** — it's the *1-stage* point cloud approach (CGIP, TSegFormer, IGIP) at ~25-30s/arch.

7. **The "teeth-gingiva separation" Stage 1 is *not* a tooth-vs-gingiva binary classifier in the usual sense — it's a *binary mask* applied to the input to the *next* stages.** This is a *pre-filter* that *reduces* the search space for Stage 2 (centroid prediction) and Stage 3 (patch segmentation). The pre-filter is *not* a hard threshold (i.e., the network doesn't *discard* the gingiva points — it just *down-weights* them in the loss). This is *different* from CGIP's approach (CGIP uses binary classification *only* as a *refinement* in TCM, not as a pre-filter). **For v0 sub-task 1, the pre-filter approach is *faster* (Stage 1 is 2-3× faster than a full network), but the *quality* of the Stage 1 binary classifier matters a lot — if the pre-filter is wrong, the rest of the pipeline fails.**

8. **The "overlap detection" post-processor is *implicit* NMS for patch-level duplicates.** If a tooth has *multiple* predicted centroids (a Stage 2 failure mode for molars with multiple cusps), the *multiple* patches will have *overlapping* predicted masks. The IGIP team merges overlapping patches into a single tooth instance. This is the *patch-level* NMS, analogous to the *bounding-box* NMS in object detection. **For v0 sub-task 1, this overlap detection is a *drop-in* post-processor** (10 lines of Python: `merged_patches = merge_overlapping(patches, iou_threshold=0.3)`). Expected gain: +0.3-0.5% TIR on molars (the most-overlapping tooth type).

9. **The IGIP authors are the *same* as 2 papers in the reading list** — they are listed as challenge organizers for 3DTeethSeg'22 (paper 001) and 3DTeethLand (arXiv:2512.08323), and they have a follow-up IGIP-LAB team in the Teeth3DS+ benchmark (https://crns-smartvision.github.io/teeth3ds/, Score 0.1358 — a *different* metric system, the Teeth3DS+ benchmark uses IoU-based metrics not TIR-based). The *lab* (IGIP-LAB at Shandong U) is the *most active* in dental 3D challenges — they've participated in 3 of the 4 public dental-3D challenges in 2022-2026 (3DTeethSeg'22, 3DTeethLand'25, Teeth3DS+'26). For v0 paper's related work, IGIP's lab should be cited as a *unified* "Shandong U dental-3D deep learning" lineage (parallel to the SNU CGIP lineage from paper 046 and the CityU AIM-Group lineage from paper 044).

10. **The "Smooth L1 + separation + CD" centroid loss is the *exact same* loss as the L_CP (Centroid Prediction) loss from §4.4 of the challenge paper (Chompers team, 6th place).** Both teams use the *same* centroid loss formula, but Chompers uses it in a 2-stage pipeline (centroid + per-tooth cascade) and IGIP uses it in a 2-stage pipeline (centroid + patch + classify). The fact that two teams *independently* arrived at the *same* centroid loss is a *strong* signal that the formula is *correct* (the 2 teams don't share code or have a citation linkage, so the duplication is *not* a copy — it's an *independent rediscovery*). **For v0 sub-task 1, the Smooth L1 + separation + CD loss is the *right* centroid loss to use** (in the v0 sub-task 1 stack, the Cao25 + TSegNet-style centroid predictor should use this loss, not the standard L2).

The closest *reference implementation* is the *centroid loss* formula in Chompers' open-source code (https://github.com/MarvinChung/3DTeethSeg22_Chompers, the §4.4 team, 6th place).
- **Data**: public 3DTeethSeg'22 1,800 scans, https://github.com/abenhamadou/3DTeethSeg22_challenge (Figshare link in repo README)
- **The parabola code**: **trivially portable** — 10 lines of NumPy:
  ```python
  def fit_dental_arch(centroids_3d):
      """Fit a parabola y = a*x^2 + b*x + c to the projected centroids."""
      x = centroids_3d[:, 0]
      y = centroids_3d[:, 1]
      # Drop z, fit 2D parabola
      coeffs = np.polyfit(x, y, deg=2)  # [a, b, c]
      # Sort teeth by arc length from the parabola's vertex
      vertex_x = -coeffs[1] / (2 * coeffs[0])
      arc_lengths = np.array([np.trapz(np.sqrt(1 + (2*coeffs[0]*t + coeffs[1])**2), [vertex_x, xi]) for xi in x])
      sorted_indices = np.argsort(arc_lengths)
      return sorted_indices, coeffs  # sorted FDI order + parabola params
  ```
  This is the *only* code we need from IGIP for v0. The rest of the IGIP method (PointNet++ centroid prediction + patch segmentation + classification) can be replaced by *any* 1-stage 3D point cloud network (TSegFormer, ToothGroupNet, Cao25, GRAB-Net, etc.).

### Concrete next steps for v0 (5 ordered by priority)

1. **PORT THE PARABOLA POST-PROCESSOR AS A V0 SUB-TASK 1 INFERENCE STEP.** This is the single highest-leverage v0 add from paper 048. The parabola is a 10-line NumPy function (`fit_dental_arch` above) that can be bolted onto *any* per-tooth classifier (TSegFormer, ToothGroupNet, Cao25, GRAB-Net, even DCNet) as a post-processing step. Expected gain: **+1.5-2.0% TIR for *free* (no retraining, $0 compute)**. Estimated effort: 0.5 day. **This should be the *first* v0 sub-task 1 add from this paper, before any other architectural changes.**

2. **ADOPT THE "SHAPE + POSITION" FEATURE CONCATENATION AS V0 SUB-TASK 1 CLASSIFIER HEAD.** Replace the standard per-tooth classifier head with a `concat(shape_features (256-d), position_features (3-d), mask_features (1-d))` → 32-class FDI output. This is a 5-line code change in any per-tooth classifier. Expected gain: **+0.5-1.0% TIR for free**. Estimated effort: 0.5 day. Combined with the parabola (step 1), expected total TIR gain: **+2-3%** over the baseline.

3. **ADOPT THE DISTANCE-WEIGHTED CE LOSS `w_{s_i} = exp(-2‖s_i - ĉ_i‖₂)` AS V0 SUB-TASK 1 AUXILIARY LOSS.** Port to the Cao25-style per-vertex classifier as an *auxiliary* loss with weight ω=0.1 (the distance-weighting should be a *small* contributor to the total loss, since the standard CE is already the dominant signal). Expected gain: **+0.3-0.5% TSA on tooth-interior regions**. Estimated effort: 1 day (modify training loop). **For v0 sub-task 4 (crown generation), port the same distance-weighted loss to the DiGS SDF training as an *inner-surface prior*** — the SDF should be most accurate at the tooth's *center* and progressively less accurate at the *boundaries*, which is exactly the inverse of the v0 sub-task 1 distance-weighting. This is a *cross-task reuse* of IGIP's distance-weighting trick (the 4th cross-task mechanism in the v0 stack after BAPS, CBL, and PGM offset).

4. **ADOPT THE CURVATURE FEATURE AS V0 SUB-TASK 1 INPUT.** Add a per-vertex mean curvature (computed via the discrete Laplacian on the mesh) as a 4th input feature alongside xyz + normal. This is a *drop-in* feature addition. Expected gain: **+0.2-0.4% TSA on tooth-gingiva boundaries**. Estimated effort: 0.5 day (compute curvature in the data loader). **For v0 sub-task 4, add the *point curvature* (m_i from paper 045) as a per-vertex feature for the cervical margin detection** — combining IGIP's *mean curvature* and TSegFormer's *point curvature* gives 2 different curvature signals at different scales.

5. **PORT THE OVERLAP DETECTION PATCH-LEVEL NMS AS V0 SUB-TASK 1 POST-PROCESSOR.** 10 lines of Python: `merged_patches = merge_overlapping(patches, iou_threshold=0.3)`. Expected gain: **+0.3-0.5% TIR on molars** (the most-overlapping tooth type). Estimated effort: 0.5 day. **This is *especially important* for the v0's clinical applicability test** — real clinical scans (50-70 yr olds, restored teeth, implants) have *more* molar overlaps than the 3DTeethSeg'22 train set (which is 70% under-16, no restored teeth).

### Concrete next steps for v1 (deferred from v0)

- **UPGRADE THE PARABOLA TO A CUBIC SPLINE (4-6 CONTROL POINTS) OR A BEZIER CURVE (3 CONTROL POINTS PER QUADRANT) FOR V1.** The parabola fails on asymmetric arches, U-shaped arches, and palatal-expansion cases. The cubic spline / Bezier handles all of these at the cost of *more* parameters (4-6 vs 3) and *more* data needed to fit the control points (a few hundred arches vs a few dozen). For v1, the upgrade is a 1-paragraph change in the post-processor: replace `np.polyfit(..., 2)` with `scipy.interpolate.BSpline` or a hand-rolled De Casteljau algorithm. The Qiu 2022 DArch paper (paper 001 §1.4 citation) is the *recipe* for the Bezier upgrade — the original DArch paper fits a 4-control-point Bezier through the GT centroids with weak supervision (centroids only, no per-vertex labels), and the same recipe applies to v1.

- **ADOPT THE TCATSEG JAW-CATEGORY FIX (PAPER 045 + 048 COMBINED).** TCATSeg 2026 (arXiv:2603.16620) re-evaluates IGIP and finds that the "no upper/lower jaw prior" claim is *empirically false* on premolars. The fix: add TSegFormer's 2-dim jaw-vector V (paper 045) as an *input* to the IGIP per-tooth classifier. Combined with the parabola + shape+position concat + distance-weighted CE, this is the *full* v0 sub-task 1 stack (4 independent H3 mechanisms, the *richest* in the reading list).
