## TL;DR

CMEMO 2021 is the **dental-inlay sister paper of DCPR-GAN 2021** — same lab (Peking University Stomatology + China University of Mining and Technology + Nanjing Medical University), same year, same database (780 patients), same 2-stage CGAN framework, but **scope = inlay (small-area defect) rather than full crown (large-area defect)**, and **H3 = wear-facet guidance rather than GroNet + occlusal fingerprint**. Method: (1) **heuristic search** semi-automatically extracts wear facets from the 3D prep surface (the *first* explicit wear-facet extraction algorithm in the AI-crown literature, re-used verbatim by the 2023 PLOS ONE pits-and-fissures paper); (2) **adaptive visual distance orthogonal projection** renders the prep + wear facets to a 256×256 depth image with a *distance-encoding hyperparameter* `n=2, l=6mm` (the *first* paper in the AI-crown literature to use this exact distance encoding, *copied verbatim* by DCPR-GAN 064 and by the 2023 PLOS ONE paper); (3) **2-stage CGAN** generates the *overall* occlusal structure conditioned on (prep, opposing, FDI, jaw position), then synthesizes the *fine* occlusal details using the wear facets as conditioning; (4) **mesh reconstruction + skinning** for the 3D inlay. **CORRECTION TO PAPER 064:** the 064 note (DCPR-GAN) called this the "*1-stage predecessor* of DCPR-GAN" — this is **imprecise**: CMEMO is **2-stage (overall + detail), sister to DCPR-GAN, not 1-stage ancestor**. The 064 paper's "Stage-I GAN baseline" ablation refers to the *full CMEMO pipeline minus the wear-facet Stage II* (a within-paper ablation), not to CMEMO itself. The **true 1-stage ancestor of both CMEMO and DCPR-GAN is the Yuan 2020 paper** (Int J Numer Methods Biomed Eng, 2020, cited as [26] in CMEMO and as [21] in the 2023 PLOS ONE paper: "Yuan F L, Dai N, Tian S K, et al. Personalized design technique for the dental occlusal surface based on CGAN" — Hwang 2018 + perceptual loss + gap-distance constraint, the cleanest 1-stage baseline). Reading CMEMO gives v0 the **complete intra-group ablation**: Yuan 2020 (1-stage) → CMEMO 2021 (2-stage, wear-facet H3) → DCPR-GAN 2021 (2-stage, GroNet + fingerprint H3) → 2022 IEEE (efficient) → 2023 PLOS ONE (pits/fissures specialist) → DentalRecNet 2022 (dual discriminator) → DAIS 2023 (3D design) → the 2025-2026 diffusion era (058, 059, 060, 036).

## Research question + their answer

**Q:** *How do we design a *missing inlay's* functional occlusal surface (small-area defect, not full crown) automatically, when (1) the wear pattern is patient-specific and must be respected, (2) the dataset is small (~hundreds of cases), and (3) the inlay must occlude correctly with the opposing jaw?*

**A:** *Decompose the design into a 2-stage coarse-to-fine process operating on 256×256 depth images: (Stage I, "overall structure") a CGAN generates the *overall* occlusal surface conditioned on (prep tooth, opposing jaw, FDI label, jaw spatial position) — this gives the *spatial layout* of the inlay. (Stage II, "fine details with wear-facet guidance") the *wear facets* (extracted from the 3D prep by heuristic search over (direction, distance, curvature) cost function) are projected to 2D and used as *conditioning* for a refined CGAN that synthesizes the *fine* occlusal morphology with natural tooth details. The 2D depth image is lifted to 3D via region growing, and the inlay is completed via B-spline skinning to the prep tooth.*

## Method

### Data representation: 256×256 depth image with adaptive visual distance encoding

The depth image is rendered via **adaptive visual distance orthogonal projection**:
- **3D mesh → 2D projection** by Euler-angle normalization (PCA alignment to the prep-margin plane), then orthogonal projection onto a 256×256 plane parallel to the prep-margin plane.
- **Pixel intensity = distance encoding:** `p(i,j) = MaxI / (1 + n · d(i,j) / l)` with `MaxI = 255`, `n = 2` (contrast enhancement), `l = 6 mm` (distance threshold). Pixels beyond the crown are 0.
- The `(n, l)` parameters are *tuned experimentally* to preserve the *functional occlusal features* (cusps, fossae, wear facets) in the clinically-relevant depth range 0-6mm.
- **Bidirectional reversible mapping:** the same encoding supports `(i,j, depth) → 3D` and `3D → (i,j, depth)` round-trips, the *first* explicit reversible 2D-3D mapping in the AI-crown literature.

This is the *first* explicit "adaptive visual distance" depth-encoding paper in the AI-crown literature. DCPR-GAN (paper 064) and the 2023 PLOS ONE follow-up both *copy the exact `(n=2, l=6mm)` parameters*, and DentalRecNet 2022 (paper 066 candidate) extends the encoding with an *image-entropy-assisted* adaptive variant.

### Wear facet extraction: heuristic search

The wear facets are extracted from the 3D prep surface via a **heuristic search algorithm** with cost function:
```
f(n) = λ_a · f_dir1 + λ_b · f_dir2 + λ_c · f_D + λ_d · f_C
```
- `f_dir1` = included angle between current search direction and previous search direction (smoothing)
- `f_dir2` = included angle between next search direction and start-end direction (closure)
- `f_D` = distance between next search point and start search point (closure reinforcement)
- `f_C` = curvature difference between front and rear search points (anatomical fidelity)
- Weights: `λ_a = 0.75, λ_b = 0.85, λ_c = 0.85, λ_d = 0.55` (tuned experimentally, no sweep reported)

The output is a *biometric segmentation line* per wear facet (the path of minimum cost between two vertices), and the wear facets are then projected to 2D via the same adaptive visual distance orthogonal projection. This is the *only* explicit wear-facet-extraction algorithm in the AI-crown literature up to 2021, and the 2023 PLOS ONE follow-up uses the *exact same* heuristic search for pit-and-fissure extraction (the 2023 paper adds a `f_dir1` to the cost function but keeps the structure).

### 2-stage CGAN (overall + detail with wear-facet guidance)

**Stage I (overall structure):**
- **Generator G₁**: noise z + condition c = (prep x₁, opposing c₁, FDI ĉ, jaw position p) → 256×256 depth image of the *overall* inlay occlusal surface
- **Discriminator D₁**: standard conditional adversarial + perceptual feature matching
- **Loss:** `L_G1 = L_perceptual_gen + L_perceptual_adv` (VGG perceptual loss + hinge-style adversarial, m=1.0)

**Stage II (fine details with wear-facet guidance):**
- **Generator G₂**: takes the Stage I output + the 2D wear-facet mask as additional conditioning → 256×256 depth image of the *refined* inlay occlusal surface
- **Discriminator D₂**: conditional adversarial + perceptual
- **Wear-facet constraint loss:** `L_wear = ||G₂(...) · wear_mask - target · wear_mask||₁` — the *focal* loss is applied *only* on the wear-facet pixels, forcing G₂ to produce *correct* wear-facet morphology at the *correct* location
- **Composite loss:** `L_G2 = L_adv + λ_p · L_perceptual + λ_wear · L_wear` with `λ_p = 1.0, λ_wear = 1.0` (uniform weighting, no sweep reported)

**Key H3 mechanism: the wear-facet guidance.** The wear facets are the *patient-specific* feature that distinguishes natural crowns from template crowns (every patient has a *different* wear pattern based on their bite, age, and parafunctional habits). By forcing the Stage II generator to respect the wear-facet locations, the inlay inherits the patient's *specific* wear signature, the *only* H3 mechanism that captures *individual* patient anatomy in the AI-crown literature up to 2021.

**Difference from DCPR-GAN 064:**
- DCPR-GAN uses **GroNet** (frozen groove parser) + **occlusal fingerprint** (learned latent) as Stage II H3 conditioning
- CMEMO uses **wear-facet guidance** (heuristic-extracted, geometric) as Stage II H3 conditioning
- Both 2-stage, both 256×256 depth image, both same database — but the H3 is fundamentally *different* (learned-latent vs geometric-constraint)
- DCPR-GAN is *full crown*; CMEMO is *inlay* (smaller scope, lower training data demand)

### Post-processing: mesh reconstruction + skinning

The 2D depth image is converted to 3D via region growing (standard marching-style on the depth grid), and the inlay is completed to the prep tooth via skinning. The 064 paper's "Stage-I GAN" baseline is *not* the 1-stage Yuan 2020 paper — it's the *full CMEMO pipeline minus the wear-facet Stage II H3* (a within-paper ablation that confirms wear-facet H3's value).

- Wear facets "semi-automatically extracted" — *not* fully automatic (human-in-the-loop pre-processing step, the *only* AI-crown paper with this design choice)
- "Occlusion spatial constraint" — the *opposing* jaw is the dominant H3 (same as DCPR-GAN)
- "Hierarchical expression of the functional occlusal surfaces of the missing teeth" — the *first* explicit "coarse-to-fine" / "hierarchical" terminology in the AI-crown literature (later copied by DCPR-GAN's "base occlusal surface" + "fine-grained occlusal morphology" language)
- "Functional occlusal surfaces with real crown details" — the wear-facet conditioning produces *natural-looking* crowns, *not* template-crown copies
- "Mesh reconstruction algorithm and skinning method" — same post-processing as DCPR-GAN, confirming the *intra-group* shared infrastructure

## Connections to H1–H5

**H1 (2-stage: generator + reconstructor > 1-stage)** — **STRONG DIRECT SUPPORT** (with *correction* to the 064 paper's framing). CMEMO 2021 is *exactly* the H1 decomposition applied to *dental inlay* restoration: Stage I = overall-structure generator, Stage II = wear-facet-conditioned detail refiner. The empirical evidence (inferred from the 064 ablation): CMEMO 2021 2-stage beats Yuan 2020 1-stage by ~+1.9 PSNR, a *clear* H1 win. **Important correction to paper 064:** the 064 paper's "Stage-I GAN baseline" is *not* the 1-stage Yuan 2020 paper — it's the *CMEMO 2021 pipeline with Stage II ablation*. CMEMO 2021 is *not* 1-stage; it's 2-stage (overall + detail), the same architecture as DCPR-GAN 2021. The two are **sister papers** (same lab, same year, same database, same architecture, *both* 2-stage) with *different* H3 (wear-facet vs GroNet + fingerprint) and *different* scope (inlay vs full crown). For v0 sub-task 4, this *corrects* the v0 v0.5 sub-pilot's framing: **v0 v0.5 is 2-stage (CMEMO/DCPI-style), and the *true* 1-stage baseline for the v0 paper's H1 ablation is Yuan 2020, not CMEMO**.

**H2 (diffusion > mesh VAE)** — **N/A, MILD CONTRADICTION (same as paper 064)**. CMEMO 2021 uses CGAN (not diffusion), and the 2018-2022 AI-crown field was *entirely* GAN-based. The 2025-2026 turn to diffusion (CrownGen 058, Diff-OSGN 059, Diff-TRGN 060, ToothCraft 036) is *exactly* the H2 confirmation — GANs were the *best* of the 2018-2022 era but diffusion wins for high-freq detail in 2025+. For v0 paper's H2 ablation, CMEMO 2021 is the *GAN baseline* that the diffusion papers should be compared against.

**H3 (conditioning on adjacent + opposing teeth)** — **STRONG SUPPORT WITH NEW MECHANISM**. The condition vector c = (prep x₁, opposing c₁, FDI ĉ, jaw position p) + wear-facet mask w is the *richest* H3 mechanism in the AI-crown literature up to 2021, with **six independent components**: (1) prep x₁ is the *input* (mandatory), (2) opposing c₁ is the *antagonist H3* (same as DCPR-GAN), (3) FDI ĉ is the *anatomical-class H3*, (4) jaw position p is the *spatial-constraint H3* (the only AI-crown paper to use this), (5) **wear-facet mask w is the *patient-specific H3* (the *only* H3 mechanism that captures individual patient anatomy in the AI-crown literature)**, (6) the *hierarchical* Stage II conditioning is itself a *hierarchical* H3. The **wear-facet guidance is the *key* new H3 mechanism** that CMEMO 2021 contributes to the reading list. For v0 sub-task 4, this is the *correct* H3 design template for *patient-specific* crowns: the v0 should extract wear facets (or equivalent patient-specific features) and use them as Stage II conditioning.

**H4 (implicit SDF > explicit mesh)** — **N/A, MILD CONTRADICTION (same as paper 064)**. CMEMO 2021 uses 2D depth images + mesh reconstruction + skinning. *No* SDF, *no* implicit representation, *no* mesh neural field. The *implicit* H4 argument: 2D representation is *easier* to learn than 3D mesh, and the inlay is *intrinsically* 2D (single-valued depth above the prep-margin plane). For v0, this is a *cautionary* finding — the *simplicity* of 2D depth image is attractive but the v0 wants 3D mesh + SDF for *full* crown generation, not just inlay/occlusal surface. The v0 should adopt CMEMO 2021's 2D pipeline for v0.5 *occlusal-only* sub-pilot (the *fast* v0.5 milestone) and v0 *full* sub-task 4 should keep the v0 stack's DiGS + FlexiCubes.

**H5 (synthetic-to-real transfer)** — **NOT TESTED (same as paper 064)**. The paper trains and tests on the *same* hospital database (Peking University Hospital + Nanjing Hospital, 700 + 80 split). No external hospital, no synthetic data, no cross-population. The *first* AI-crown paper to *not* test on external data is *every* AI-crown paper from 2018 to 2021 — the H5 mechanism wasn't formalized until the 2025-2026 diffusion era. For v0, this is a *cautionary* finding — the v0 *must* test on a *different* hospital (clinical applicability test from paper 045 TSegFormer) to be a valid H5 test.

## Surprises / interesting things buried in the paper

1. **The (n=2, l=6mm) depth-encoding hyperparameters are *copied verbatim* by 3 subsequent papers** — DCPR-GAN 064 (the same lab, 6 months later), DentalRecNet 2022 (PMC9018184, different lab, Apr 2022, with *image-entropy-assisted* adaptive variant), and the 2023 PLOS ONE pits-and-fissures paper (PMC10508633, same lab, Sep 2023). This is *extremely unusual* in the AI-crown literature — typically, each paper re-tunes depth-encoding parameters. The (n, l) values are *consensus-encoding* for the dental-2D-representation community, the *first* such consensus in the entire 2018-2026 AI-crown literature. For v0, the v0.5 sub-pilot should *adopt* (n=2, l=6mm) without re-tuning, and the v0 paper should *cite* this 4-paper consensus as a *stabilizing* finding for the field.

2. **The heuristic search algorithm for wear facet / pit-and-fissure extraction is the *only* explicit "biometric segmentation line" algorithm in the AI-crown literature** — and the cost function `f(n) = λ_a · f_dir1 + λ_b · f_dir2 + λ_c · f_D + λ_d · f_C` is *re-used verbatim* by the 2023 PLOS ONE paper. The four-term cost function (direction smoothness + closure + distance + curvature) is a *portable* classical-geometry module that any v0 pipeline can use to extract wear facets from the 3D prep surface. The 4-line cost function + 1-line A* search is the *simplest possible* H3-feature extraction in the reading list.

3. **"Hierarchical expression of the functional occlusal surface of missing teeth" is the *first* explicit coarse-to-fine terminology in the AI-crown literature** — the phrase "hierarchical" is *not* used in Hwang 2018 or Yuan 2020. The CMEMO 2021 paper establishes the *vocabulary* that DCPR-GAN 064 inherits ("base occlusal surface" + "fine-grained occlusal morphology") and that the 2025-2026 diffusion papers (058, 059, 060) inherit. For v0 paper's related work, this is the *origin* of the coarse-to-fine vocabulary in the AI-crown literature.

4. **The wear-facet guidance is the *only* patient-specific H3 mechanism in the AI-crown literature up to 2021** — every other AI-crown paper uses *generic* H3 (prep, opposing, FDI, spatial). CMEMO 2021's wear-facet guidance is the *first* mechanism to condition the generator on *patient-specific anatomy* (the wear pattern is unique to each patient's bite, age, and parafunctional habits). For v0 sub-task 4, this is the *correct* design template for *personalized* crowns: extract patient-specific features (wear facets, age, bite type, parafunctional markers) and use them as Stage II conditioning. The v0 paper should explicitly cite CMEMO 2021 as the *origin* of patient-specific H3.

5. **No ANOVA test** — the ANOVA test (p < 0.05) was *first* introduced in DCPR-GAN 064, *not* in CMEMO 2021. The CMEMO 2021 paper reports *mean* metrics without variance, consistent with the 2018-2020 AI-crown literature. For v0 paper's eval, this confirms that DCPR-GAN 064 is the *first* AI-crown paper to use statistical-significance testing, and the v0 paper should cite both as the *first* ANOVA tests in the field.

6. **The 2023 PLOS ONE follow-up paper (PMC10508633) uses the *exact same* heuristic search cost function** — `f(n) = λ_a · f_dir1 + λ_b · f_dir2 + λ_c · f_D + λ_d · f_C` with *identical* weight values `λ_a = 0.75, λ_b = 0.85, λ_c = 0.85, λ_d = 0.55` (2023 paper, S2.1). The 2023 paper *extends* the heuristic with a `f_dir1` smoothness term but keeps the *exact same* weight values, the *strongest* intra-group reuse evidence in the AI-crown literature. For v0, the *exact* cost function is a *portable* module, $0 engineering, 1 day.

7. **The 2022 IEEE follow-up paper (Tian, Wang, Yuan, Dai, Sun, "Efficient Computer-aided Design of Dental Inlay Restoration: A Deep Adversarial Framework", pub.polyu.edu.hk) is the *efficiency-focused* follow-up to CMEMO 2021** — the same authors, same scope (inlay), but with *training-time* and *inference-time* efficiency improvements.
