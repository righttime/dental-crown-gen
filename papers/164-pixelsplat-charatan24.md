## Research Question + Their Answer

**RQ:** 3D scene reconstruction and novel-view synthesis from *very sparse views* (as few as 2) is a *long-standing* *long-tailed* challenge in *computer vision* with *broad* applications (VR/AR, robotics, autonomous driving, 3D-content creation). *Recent* 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS, Kerbl 2023 SIGGRAPH) has emerged as an *efficient* + *expressive* scene representation thanks to *fast rendering* (rasterization-based, *no* volume sampling) + *high quality*, but *original 3DGS* requires *per-scene optimization* (the *expensive* *drawback*: 10-30 minutes per scene on a single GPU, *incompatible* with *real-time* use cases like *autonomous driving* or *clinical chairside*). **Can we design a *feed-forward* 3DGS model that (a) reconstructs 3D Gaussians in a *single* forward pass, (b) generalizes across scenes *without* per-scene optimization, (c) renders at *real-time* (sub-second) inference, (d) is *scalable* (train on diverse scenes, deploy on novel scenes), and (e) handles the *fundamental* challenges of *real-world* multi-view 3DGS — scale ambiguity (SfM does NOT preserve metric scale, only relative scales), local minima (Gaussian primitives have local support, gradients vanish if the primitive is far from its final location), and high memory cost (millions of Gaussians per scene)?**

**A:** Yes — **pixelSplat** demonstrates that a *single* feed-forward network can reconstruct 3D Gaussians from *just 2 input images* in a *single* forward pass, addressing the *three* fundamental challenges of real-world feed-forward 3DGS via:
1. **Epipolar transformer (Sec 4.1) for scale ambiguity:** A multi-view epipolar transformer finds cross-view pixel correspondences by attending to features *sampled along the epipolar line* on the source view, *augmented with positionally-encoded depth values* (Hartley 2003-style parameterization). The transformer learns to *associate* 2D pixel correspondences with *depth* values along the ray, *inferring* the per-scene scale factor *from the data itself* (no need for metric-scale poses or known camera intrinsics). This is the *first* mechanism in the 3DGS arc to *directly* address scale ambiguity, and it remains the *founding* mechanism for the *pose-free 3DGS* sub-arc (PF3plat 162, FLARE 163, NoPoSplat 160 *partially*, AnySplat 161 *partially*).
2. **Probabilistic depth distribution + reparameterization trick for Gaussian means (Sec 4.2) for local minima:** The encoder predicts a *dense per-pixel probability distribution* over depth values (parameterized as a softmax over D depth candidates). The *Gaussian primitive means* are *sampled* from this distribution. The sampling operation is made *differentiable* via the reparameterization trick (Kingma 2014, *the same* trick as VAE): the *density* of a sampled Gaussian is *coupled* to the *probability* of that 3D location, so when a gradient would *increase* the opacity of a Gaussian at a 3D location, the model *increases* the probability that the Gaussian will be sampled there *in the future*. This *avoids* the *local-minima* problem that 3DGS suffers from when primitives are initialized at random locations and have to move through space (3D-GS uses *non-differentiable* "Adaptive Density Control" pruning/splitting to address this, but those operations are *incompatible* with the generalizable setting where primitives are predicted by a *neural network* that *must* receive gradients). The *killer* practical insight: *feed-forward* 3DGS can *inherit* 3DGS's *high-quality* representation *without* the *expensive* per-scene optimization.
3. **3 Gaussians per pixel + diff-gaussian-rasterization for high memory cost:** Each pixel of the reference view predicts 3 Gaussian primitives (opacity, mean, covariance, color), giving *3 × H × W* primitives per scene (for 256×256 reference view = 196,608 primitives, vs 3D-GS's typical 1-2M primitives per scene). The rendering uses the *standard* diff-gaussian-rasterization kernel (the *open-source* CUDA kernel from 3D-GS), the *killer* practical detail that makes pixelSplat *deployable* on *any* GPU with the *same* rendering stack as 3D-GS.

The *key insight* is that **scale ambiguity, local minima, and high memory cost are the *fundamental* challenges of feed-forward 3DGS, and pixelSplat's *three* innovations (epipolar transformer, reparameterization trick, 3 Gaussians per pixel) are the *first* solutions to all three in a *single* feed-forward network**. The *comparison* to the *alternative* *feed-forward* 3D-reconstruction paradigms is *direct*:
- *Per-scene optimization* (NeRF Mildenhall 2020, 3D-GS Kerbl 2023): *high quality* but *slow* (10-30min/scene), *not scalable*
- *Light-field transformers* (LFN Sitzmann 2021, SRF Sitzmann 2021, AttnRend Du 2023): *fast* inference but *no 3D representation*, *not editable*, *not exportable* to downstream tasks
- *pixelSplat*: *fast* inference (0.104s on A100) + *interpretable 3D representation* (3D Gaussians) + *editable* (can be modified, animated, exported to mesh via SuGaR) + *generalizable* (no per-scene optimization) + *scalable* (trains on diverse scenes, deploys on novel scenes), the *only* method that achieves *all four* properties

The *killer empirical result* is that **pixelSplat outperforms previous *light-field transformers* (LFN, AttnRend) on the *real-world* RealEstate10K and ACID datasets while being *orders of magnitude* faster (2.5 orders of magnitude faster rendering)** — the *founding* evidence that *feed-forward 3DGS* is the *right* paradigm for *real-world* novel-view synthesis, the *foundation* for the *entire* 156-163 3DGS arc.

## Method (architecture, training, data)

### Architecture overview (Sec 4, Fig. 1)

pixelSplat is a *epipolar-transformer-based* *feed-forward 3DGS* model with *four* *simple* components:

1. **Per-view feature extraction (Sec 4.1):** A *ResNet* (the *standard* ResNet18, the *first* time a *standard* backbone was used for feed-forward 3DGS, *not* a custom ViT) extracts per-view features F_i ∈ ℝ^{H/4 × W/4 × C} (C=128, the *standard* ResNet output dimension), the *lightweight* feature extraction that *enables* 0.104s inference on A100
2. **Multi-view epipolar transformer (Sec 4.1, the *founding* architecture choice):** For *each* reference view i and *each* source view j, an *epipolar transformer* finds cross-view pixel correspondences by attending to features *sampled along the epipolar line* on the source view. The *epipolar line* is computed from the camera parameters (relative pose + intrinsics), and the *features along the line* are *augmented with positionally-encoded depth values* (Hartley 2003-style, the *standard* epipolar parameterization in classical multi-view stereo). The transformer *learns* to *associate* 2D pixel correspondences with *depth* values along the ray, *inferring* the per-scene scale factor *from the data itself* (the *killer* mechanism that makes pixelSplat *work* on *real-world* data with *unknown* scale). Output: cross-view-aware features F̃_i ∈ ℝ^{H/4 × W/4 × C} for each view, with the *scale* information *baked* into the features
3. **Per-pixel depth distribution prediction (Sec 4.2):** For *each* pixel of the reference view, predict a *per-pixel probability distribution* over D=128 depth candidates (uniformly sampled in the *inverse-depth* domain, the *killer* depth-discretization choice that handles *near* + *far* in the *same* resolution). The depth distribution is *softmaxed* to produce probabilities p_d ∈ ℝ^D, the *probabilistic depth prediction* that is the *killer* mechanism for *back-propagation* through the depth sampling
4. **Differentiable Gaussian primitive prediction (Sec 4.2):** For *each* pixel of the reference view, predict 3 Gaussian primitives (opacity α_g, mean μ_g, covariance Σ_g, color c_g, 11 parameters per Gaussian). The *mean* μ_g is *sampled* from the predicted depth distribution via the *reparameterization trick* (Eq. 6-7, the *killer* mechanism for *back-propagation* through depth sampling): μ_g = proj(C_i, d_g) where d_g is *sampled* from the depth distribution p_d, and the *sampling* is made *differentiable* by *coupling* the *density* of the sampled Gaussian to the *probability* of that depth (Eq. 7). The *opacity, covariance, color* are *directly* predicted from the epipolar-transformer features + the depth distribution + the image features via a *small* CNN head
5. **Rendering via diff-gaussian-rasterization:** Use the *standard* diff-gaussian-rasterization kernel from 3D-GS to render the *predicted 3D Gaussians* to *novel viewpoints* (the *open-source* CUDA kernel, the *killer* practical detail that makes pixelSplat *deployable* on *any* GPU with the *same* rendering stack as 3D-GS)

### Scale ambiguity resolution (Sec 4.1, the *killer* mechanism)

The *fundamental* challenge of *real-world* multi-view 3DGS is that **camera poses from SfM (Structure-from-Motion) do NOT preserve metric scale** — different scenes are scaled by *individual, arbitrary* scale factors s_i. A given scene C_i provides poses {(I_j, s_i · T_j^m)}_j where s_i is the *unknown* per-scene scale and T_j^m is the *metric-scale* pose (the *true* metric pose that we don't have access to). pixelSplat's *epipolar transformer* addresses this by:
- For *each* reference view i and *each* source view j, compute the *epipolar line* on view j corresponding to *each* pixel of view i
- *Sample* D=128 features *along* the epipolar line on view j, *augmenting* each sampled feature with its *positionally-encoded depth* (the *standard* Hartley 2003 epipolar parameterization)
- The epipolar transformer *attends* to these D sampled features, *learning* to *associate* 2D pixel correspondences with *depth* values
- The *output* features F̃_i *encode* the *scale* information, so downstream prediction heads can *use* the *correct* depth values (in the *unknown* scale)

This is the *founding* mechanism for the *pose-free 3DGS* sub-arc, and it remains the *direct* ancestor of:
- *NoPoSplat 160's intrinsic-token embedding* (a *different* mechanism for scale ambiguity, but *inspired* by pixelSplat's *idea* of *inferring* scale from the data)
- *AnySplat 161's* pose-free design (uses *alternating-attention* + *VGGT* distillation, but the *motivation* of *inferring* scale comes from pixelSplat)
- *PF3plat 162's* coarse alignment with *frozen foundation models* (DepthPro + LightGlue) (a *different* mechanism for scale ambiguity, but *also* *inspired* by pixelSplat's *idea* of *inferring* scale from the data)

### Local minima resolution (Sec 4.2, the *killer* mechanism)

The *fundamental* challenge of *3DGS* is that **Gaussian primitives have *local support*, meaning gradients *vanish* if the primitive is far from its final location**, and the *fitting* problem is famously *non-convex* (similar to Gaussian Mixture Model fitting, generally solved with EM but with local minima). 3D-GS addresses this with *non-differentiable* "Adaptive Density Control" (pruning/splitting) operations, but those operations are *incompatible* with the *generalizable* setting where primitives are *predicted* by a *neural network* that *must* receive gradients. pixelSplat's *reparameterization trick* (Eq. 6-7) addresses this by:
- Predicting a *dense per-pixel probability distribution* over D=128 depth candidates (a *softmax* output, the *probabilistic depth prediction*)
- *Sampling* D=128 depth values from this distribution for the D=128 Gaussians per pixel
- *Coupling* the *density* of the sampled Gaussian to the *probability* of that depth (Eq. 7): if a Gaussian is sampled at depth d with probability p_d, then its *density* is *set* to p_d (the *reparameterization trick*)
- The *sampling* is now *differentiable*: when a gradient would *increase* the opacity of a Gaussian at depth d, the model *increases* the probability p_d (so the Gaussian will be *sampled at d* in the *future*)

This is the *founding* mechanism for *end-to-end* 3DGS, and it remains the *direct* ancestor of:
- *NoPoSplat 160's* canonical-space design (predicts Gaussians in a *shared* canonical space, *avoiding* the *per-view initialization* problem that *causes* local minima in 3D-GS)
- *AnySplat 161's* differentiable voxelization (aggregates per-voxel attributes via *softmax-weighted* confidences, a *different* mechanism for *primitive count control* that *also* addresses local minima)
- *MVSplat 156's* cost volume (replaces pixelSplat's *probabilistic depth* with *deterministic cost-volume depth*, *avoiding* the *local-minima* problem *entirely* by predicting *single-mode* depth)

### 3 Gaussians per pixel (Sec 4.2, the *marginal* design choice)

Each pixel of the reference view predicts *3* Gaussian primitives (11 parameters each: opacity, mean 3D, covariance 6, color 3, for a total of 33 parameters per pixel). For 256×256 reference view, this gives *196,608* primitives per scene, *fewer* than 3D-GS's typical 1-2M primitives per scene but *more* than MVSplat 156's 1 Gaussian per pixel. MVSplat 156's Tab. C shows that 3 Gaussians per pixel gives +0.15 dB over 1 Gaussian for 2× slower, the *marginal* design choice, *inherited* by all subsequent 3DGS methods (with MVSplat 156 defaulting to 1 Gaussian for *speed* and AnySplat 161 + NoPoSplat 160 defaulting to 1 Gaussian per *view* with *voxelization* for primitive control).

### Training (Sec 5)

pixelSplat is *trained* *end-to-end* *purely* with *photometric* supervision (the *killer* scalability insight, *no* GT depth *no* GT pose, *no* SfM preprocessing):
- **Loss = L1 + LPIPS** (the *standard* 3DGS *photometric* loss, *no* depth regularization, *no* silhouette loss, *no* sparse prior)
- **Optimizer:** Adam, default PyTorch settings
- **Iterations:** 200K (RE10K) or 200K (ACID) with *batch* size 1 on a *single* A100 80GB
- **Inference:** 0.104s on A100 80GB for 2 views at 256×256 (the *founding* real-time benchmark, *chairside-feasible* in the v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 sense)
- **Backbone:** ResNet18 (the *standard* ResNet, *pre-trained* on ImageNet, the *lightweight* feature extraction that *enables* 0.104s inference)
- **Epipolar transformer:** 6 layers, *windowed* attention, *not* full self-attention (the *practical* design for *real-time* inference)
- **Hyperparameters:** *D=128* depth candidates, *H=W=256* resolution, *3* Gaussians per pixel, *256* samples per ray during training, *cosine annealing* learning rate schedule

### Data (Sec 5.1)

- **RealEstate10K (RE10K, Zhou et al. TOG 2018):** 67,477 train + 7,289 test *real-estate* YouTube videos with *estimated* camera poses (the *de facto* 2018-2024 *sparse-view NVS* benchmark, *the* benchmark that pixelSplat *established* for feed-forward 3DGS)
- **ACID (Liu et al. ICCV 2021):** 11,075 train + 1,972 test *nature* drone scenes (the *outdoor* complement to RE10K's *indoor* real-estate, the *second* benchmark for feed-forward 3DGS)
- **No cross-dataset evaluation** (the *early* feed-forward 3DGS, later MVSplat 156 + DepthSplat 157 would add DTU cross-dataset)

## Connections to H1-H5

### H1 (Modular reuse of strong backbones / multi-stage refinement): **STRONGEST DIRECT SUPPORT in 164-paper reading list**

pixelSplat is *literally* a *modular* *multi-stage* pipeline: (a) ResNet18 backbone (a *strong* pre-trained backbone, *inherited* from ImageNet) → (b) epipolar transformer (a *modular* cross-view attention layer) → (c) depth distribution prediction head (a *modular* softmax head) → (d) Gaussian parameter prediction head (a *modular* CNN head) → (e) diff-gaussian-rasterization (a *modular* CUDA kernel from 3D-GS). Each *stage* is *independently* trainable and *independently* replaceable, the *founding* evidence that *modular* 3DGS design is *strictly better* than *end-to-end monolithic* design. The *direct* v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 *lesson* is that v0's sub-task 1 (full-arch synthesis) should use a *modular* pipeline: (a) 3DTeethSeg22 7K + ToSynFCD 30K *pre-trained* arch segmentation backbone (FDI segmentation, *inherited* from Cao 25) → (b) cross-view epipolar attention (or *better*, cross-view cost volume from MVSplat 156) → (c) depth distribution head (or *better*, deterministic cost-volume depth from MVSplat 156) → (d) Gaussian parameter head (with Hwang 061 histogram loss for *clinical-fit*) → (e) diff-gaussian-rasterization for v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 chairside-real-time rendering. The *2-stage* design of pixelSplat (encoder → Gaussian head) is the *founding* design for the *2-stage* design of MVSplat 156 (encoder → cost-volume → depth → Gaussian), the *2-stage* design of DepthSplat 157 (encoder → cost-volume + monocular depth → Gaussian), the *2-stage* design of NoPoSplat 160 (encoder → DPT depth/pose → Gaussian), the *2-stage* design of AnySplat 161 (encoder → DPT depth/pose/camera → Gaussian), the *2-stage* design of PF3plat 162 (coarse-to-fine), the *3-stage* design of FLARE 163 (pose → local point map → global point map → Gaussian). **The 156-164 arc is *literally* the *modular* *multi-stage* *3DGS* design space**, with pixelSplat as the *founding* paper.

### H2 (Latent diffusion > direct 3D generation): **NOT TESTED, STRONGEST CONTRADICTION in 164-paper reading list**

pixelSplat does *not* use diffusion at all — it's a *purely deterministic* feed-forward network. The *3DGS* representation itself is *deterministic* (a set of Gaussian primitives with *explicit* means, covariances, opacities, colors), and pixelSplat predicts these *deterministically* from the input images in a *single* forward pass. The *killer* empirical result is that **pixelSplat *outperforms* previous *light-field transformers* (LFN, AttnRend) on *real-world* RE10K and ACID while being *2.5 orders of magnitude faster* (0.104s vs ~1-10s for LFN/AttnRend)**, the *direct* evidence that *deterministic* feed-forward 3DGS *dominates* diffusion-based or autoregressive-based methods for *real-world* sparse-view NVS. This is *consistent* with the *strongest* H2 contradiction in the 154-163 arc (NoPoSplat 160's "deterministic feed-forward > diffusion *for reconstruction*", PF3plat 162's 135× speedup vs InstantSplat at matched PSNR, FLARE 163's deterministic cascade *outperforms* PoseDiffusion by +28.3 pts on RealEstate10K AUC@30°). **The 156-164 arc is the *strongest* H2 contradiction in the entire reading list, with pixelSplat as the *founding* paper.** The *direct* v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 *lesson* is that v0's sub-task 2 (crown generation) should *not* use diffusion (per DMC 033's H2 contradiction "deterministic+good losses wins for constrained patient-specific tasks, diffusion is a prior not a backbone"). The *practical* v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 design is DMC 033 (deterministic point cloud + SAP/DPSR) + MCAM + CPL + MRL (the *killer* deterministic losses), *not* diffusion.

### H3 (Opposing-jaw / multi-view conditioning): **STRONGEST DIRECT SUPPORT in 164-paper reading list**

pixelSplat is *literally* a *multi-view* model: 2 input views with *known* relative pose + intrinsics, the *direct* H3 mechanism for *scene-level* multi-view 3DGS. The *epipolar transformer* is the *killer* H3 mechanism: it *attends* to features *sampled along the epipolar line* on the source view, the *direct* use of *multi-view geometry* (epipolar constraint Hartley 2003) for *cross-view feature aggregation*. The *killer* insight is that *epipolar attention* is a *general* H3 mechanism that can be *extended* to *arch-level* 3DGS (e.g., 6-tooth context per DMC 033: 1 prep + 2 adjacent + 3 opposing + gum): just *replace* the *epipolar line* (which is for *2 views*) with the *projection ray* of the prep tooth from *multiple* adjacent + opposing views, and *attend* to features *along the projection ray* on the adjacent + opposing views. The *direct* v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 *lesson* is that v0's sub-task 1 (full-arch synthesis) should use a *multi-view* epipolar-attention or *better* cost-volume (per MVSplat 156) for *arch-level* conditioning, with the *6-tooth context* (1 prep + 2 adjacent + 3 opposing + gum) as the *conditioning signal*. The *3DGS* H3 toolkit now has 9+ mechanisms (parabola 048, DITA 058, point-curvature 045, OCM 044, PGM offset 046, O_cp/O_ce/O_cr operator 059, 2D-projection-consistency 060, gap-distance-map+opposing-jaw conditioning 061, epipolar-attention 164), the *richest* in world.

### H4 (Implicit SDF > mesh as substrate): **STRONGEST REFINEMENT, NEW NUANCE in 164-paper reading list**

pixelSplat REJECTS H4 in its *strict* form (SDF is *not* the right substrate for *real-time* NVS) but SUPPORTS the *underlying* H4 motivation (3D Gaussians are an *implicit* representation, *parameterized* by a *small* set of *local* primitives, *not* an *explicit* mesh). The *key insight* is that **3D Gaussians are the *right* substrate for *real-time* NVS because they can be *efficiently rendered* via rasterization (no volume sampling, no SDF query, *no* mesh extraction at *inference* time), but they *can be converted* to mesh *post-hoc* via SuGaR (Guédon 2024) or BFS-Colmap (Yu 2024) for downstream CAD/CAM tasks**. This is the *new* 2024 nuance: *implicit* representations like *3D Gaussians* can be *rendered* at *real-time* (sub-second) AND *converted* to *explicit* mesh for downstream tasks, the *best of both worlds*. The *direct* v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 *lesson* is that v0's sub-task 2 (crown generation) should use DMC 033's *SAP/DPSR* (which is *exactly* the *mesh extraction* from 3D Gaussians / point clouds), the *killer* H4 mechanism that *combines* the *3DGS substrate* (per pixelSplat 164) with the *mesh output* (per DMC 033). The *2-step* pipeline is: (a) pixelSplat-style 3DGS prediction from 2 input views (per pixelSplat 164) → (b) SAP/DPSR mesh extraction (per DMC 033) → (c) FlexiCubes post-processing (per paper 007) → (d) *clinical-fit* margin refinement (per Hwang 061 + DMC 033's MRL trick) → (e) dental CAD output (per exocad / 3Shape)

### H5 (Synthetic+finetune, pretrained backbone > from-scratch): **PARTIAL SUPPORT in 164-paper reading list**

pixelSplat trains from *scratch* on RealEstate10K + ACID (2 real-world datasets, *no* synthetic pre-training, *no* ImageNet pre-training for the epipolar transformer, *no* MASt3R pre-training, *no* VGGT distillation), the *early* feed-forward 3DGS that *does not* use the *rich* pre-training recipes that later papers would adopt. The *partial* H5 support is the *ResNet18* backbone: it is *pre-trained* on ImageNet, which gives a *significant* quality boost (per the standard *transfer learning* lesson) and is the *only* form of pre-training in pixelSplat. The *killer* practical insight is that **later feed-forward 3DGS methods adopted *much richer* pre-training recipes**: MVSplat 156 uses *UniMatch* (Xu 2023) pre-trained backbone, DepthSplat 157 uses *monocular depth* (ZoeDepth) pre-training, Splatt3R 159 *freezes* MASt3R pre-trained backbone, NoPoSplat 160 uses *CroCo v2* pre-trained backbone, AnySplat 161 uses *DINOv2* pre-trained backbone + *VGGT* distillation, PF3plat 162 uses *DepthPro* + *LightGlue* pre-trained foundation models, FLARE 163 uses *MASt3R* pre-trained backbone. **pixelSplat's *minimal* pre-training is the *baseline*; the *rich* pre-training is the *evolution*.** The *direct* v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 *lesson* is that v0's sub-task 1 (full-arch synthesis) should use a *rich* pre-training recipe: (a) *pre-trained* arch segmentation backbone (FDI segmentation, *inherited* from Cao 25) + (b) *pre-trained* monocular depth (ZoeDepth or DepthPro) + (c) *pre-trained* 2D features (DINOv2) + (d) *clinical fine-tuning* on 3DTeethSeg22 7K + ToSynFCD 30K + clinical 5K, the *killer* H5 mechanism that *combines* all 3 pre-training recipes + the *clinical* data for v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 chairside-real-time sub-task 1.

## Surprises / Interesting Things Buried in Section 4

1. **★ The "EM algorithm for 3DGS fitting" analogy is *the* theoretical foundation for the reparameterization trick.** Sec 3 explicitly compares 3DGS fitting to Gaussian Mixture Model fitting (a *famously* non-convex problem, generally solved with EM but with *local minima*). The reparameterization trick is *the* mechanism to *avoid* local minima in the *feed-forward* setting (where 3D-GS's *non-differentiable* Adaptive Density Control is *not applicable*). This is *the* theoretical insight that makes *feed-forward 3DGS* possible, and it is *the* *killer* contribution that *enables* the *entire* 156-163 arc.

2. **★ Epipolar attention + positionally-encoded depth is a *classic* classical CV technique (Hartley 2003, *the* multi-view stereo bible) brought into the *deep learning* era.** The fact that the *founding paper* of feed-forward 3DGS uses a *25-year-old* CV technique (epipolar parameterization) is *the* *killer* design lesson: the *best* 3DGS designs are *not* pure deep learning, they are *hybrid* designs that *combine* deep learning with *classical* geometric priors. This is *exactly* the *same* design philosophy as MVSplat 156's *cost volume* (classical MVSNet 2018 technique), NoPoSplat 160's *epipolar attention avoidance* (which *rejects* Hartley 2003 in favor of *pure ViT*), and AnySplat 161's *alternating-attention* (a *novel* deep learning technique *without* classical CV priors). The *design space* is: *classical CV priors* (pixelSplat 164, MVSplat 156, DepthSplat 157) vs *pure deep learning* (NoPoSplat 160) vs *hybrid* (AnySplat 161, PF3plat 162, FLARE 163).

3. **★ The 3DGS "Adaptive Density Control" is *explicitly* called out as *incompatible* with the generalizable setting.** This is *the* *killer* practical insight: 3D-GS's *non-differentiable* pruning/splitting operations *cannot* be used in feed-forward 3DGS (because the *primitives* are *predicted* by a *neural network* that *must* receive gradients). The reparameterization trick is *the* mechanism to *replace* Adaptive Density Control in the feed-forward setting. This is *the* *founding* insight that *enables* the *entire* 156-163 arc, and it is *the* reason why *all* feed-forward 3DGS methods use *probabilistic* or *deterministic* depth prediction (NOT *optimization-based* primitive placement).

4. **★ 3 Gaussians per pixel is *the* *marginal* design choice that *survives* across the 156-164 arc.** MVSplat 156's Tab. C shows that 3 Gaussians per pixel gives +0.15 dB over 1 Gaussian for 2× slower, the *marginal* design choice, *inherited* by all subsequent 3DGS methods (with MVSplat 156 defaulting to 1 Gaussian for *speed* and AnySplat 161 + NoPoSplat 160 defaulting to 1 Gaussian per *view* with *voxelization* for primitive control). This is *the* *killer* practical detail that 3DGS *scaling* is *not* a *parameter* problem, it's a *primitive count* problem.

5. **★ The ResNet18 backbone (a *standard* ImageNet pre-trained backbone) is *the* *killer* practical detail that *enables* 0.104s inference.** Later feed-forward 3DGS methods that adopt *ViT* backbones (NoPoSplat 160, AnySplat 161) are *slower* (0.1-0.767s for 2-16 views) but *more accurate* (the *right* trade-off for v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 *clinical-quality* mode). The *practical* insight is that v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 v0 should *use a ResNet* for *chairside-real-time* mode (the *faster* design) and a *ViT* for *clinical-quality* mode (the *better* design), the *3-tier latency/quality workflow* per AnySplat 161.

6. **★ The 0.104s inference is *the* *founding* real-time benchmark, but *not* the *fastest* in the 156-164 arc.** MVSplat 156 at 0.044s is *2.4× faster* (the *new* real-time SOTA), LGM 154 at 0.07s is *1.5× faster*, GRM 155 at 0.11s is *roughly* the same, MuRF Xu 2024 at 0.186s is *0.6×* the speed. The *design space* is: *speed* (MVSplat 156, LGM 154, pixelSplat 164) vs *quality* (GRM 155, AnySplat 161) vs *pose-free* (NoPoSplat 160, AnySplat 161, PF3plat 162, FLARE 163) vs *4K* (PanSplat 158) vs *sparse-to-dense* (AnySplat 161).

## Quote-Worthy Sentences

- **"3DGS parameterizes a 3D scene as a set of 3D Gaussian primitives {g_k = (μ_k, Σ_k, α_k, S_k)} which each have a mean, a covariance, an opacity, and spherical harmonics coefficients."** (Sec 3) — the *canonical* 3DGS definition, *inherited* from Kerbl 2023.
- **"The fitting of a 3D-GS model is closely related to the fitting of a Gaussian mixture model, where we seek the parameters of a set of Gaussians such that we maximize the likelihood of a set of samples. This problem is famously non-convex and generally solved with the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm."** (Sec 3) — the *theoretical* connection between 3DGS and GMM, the *foundation* for the reparameterization trick.
- **"3D-GS relies on non-differentiable pruning and splitting operations dubbed 'Adaptive Density Control' to address this problem. However, these techniques are incompatible with the generalizable setting, where primitive parameters are predicted by a neural network that must receive gradients."** (Sec 3) — the *killer* practical insight that *motivates* the reparameterization trick.
- **"We thus propose a method by which Gaussian primitives can implicitly be spawned or deleted during training, avoiding local minima, but which nevertheless maintains gradient flow."** (Sec 1) — the *killer* summary of the reparameterization trick's motivation.
- **"Specifically, we parameterize the positions (i.e., means) of Gaussians implicitly via dense probability distributions predicted by our encoder. In each forward pass, we sample Gaussian primitive locations from this distribution."** (Sec 1) — the *killer* summary of the probabilistic depth sampling.
- **"We make the sampling operation differentiable via a reparameterization trick that couples the density of a sampled Gaussian primitive to the probability of that location. When receiving a gradient that would increase the opacity of a Gaussian at a 3D location, our model increases the probability that the Gaussian will be sampled at that location again in the future."** (Sec 1) — the *killer* summary of the reparameterization trick's *mechanism*, the *founding* contribution of the paper.
- **"In real-world datasets, camera poses are only reconstructed up to an arbitrary scale factor. We address this by designing a multi-view epipolar transformer that reliably infers this per-scene scale factor."** (Sec 1) — the *killer* summary of the epipolar transformer's motivation.
- **"We accomplish this using an epipolar transformer that finds cross-view pixel correspondences and associates them with positionally encoded depth values."** (Sec 1) — the *killer* summary of the epipolar transformer's *mechanism*, the *founding* contribution for *pose-free 3DGS*.
- **"We significantly outperform previous black-box based light field transformers on the real-world ACID and RealEstate10k datasets while drastically reducing both training and rendering cost and generating explicit 3D scenes."** (Sec 1) — the *killer* summary of the empirical results.
