## TL;DR

The *first* 3D Part Amodal Segmentation model — a 2-stage pipeline (SAMPart3D segmentation + HoloPart diffusion completion) that takes an *incomplete* surface patch from a 3D part segmenter and *hallucinate-generates* the *full* 3D part geometry (including the *occluded* regions), with a dual attention design (local for fine detail + context-aware for global coherence) and rectified-flow diffusion in a (512, 2048) latent space, achieving **2.5-5× Chamfer Distance reduction vs the SOTA (PatchComplete, DiffComplete, SDFusion, Finetune-VAE) on both new ABO and PartObjaverse-Tiny benchmarks**, with the *killer* finding that *diffusion in a 3D-shape latent space + cross-attention to the whole shape* is *the* mechanism for "complete occluded 3D parts" — the *exact* problem for v0 sub-task 4 *occluded* tooth-surface completion (distal margin of 2nd molars, interproximal surfaces, subgingival prep margins).

## Research question + answer

**RQ:** Given an *incomplete* surface patch of a 3D part (the visible portion of a chair's leg, the visible portion of a car's wheel, the visible portion of a tooth's distal surface), how do we *generatively complete* the *missing* portions such that the completed part is (1) *complete* (no holes, no missing occluded regions), (2) *geometrically consistent* with the visible portions of the part, (3) *semantically meaningful* (a chair leg is a chair leg, not a malformed blob), and (4) *globally consistent* with the whole 3D shape (the completed chair leg fits the chair seat in a *plausible* way)?

**Answer (HoloPart's):** A *2-stage* approach that *separates* segmentation (use an existing 3D part segmenter like SAMPart3D) from completion (a novel rectified-flow-diffusion model with a *dual attention* design), with the *key insight* that the *shape generative prior* learned from a large-scale 3D dataset (Objaverse 800k) can be *fine-tuned* to a *part completion* task by adding *two* conditioning mechanisms — (1) *local attention* from the part queries to the part's surface points (for fine detail) and (2) *context-aware attention* from the part queries to the whole shape's points + binary segmentation mask (for global coherence) — and the *resulting* model not only achieves 2.5-5× SOTA on the part-completion benchmark but also *generalizes zero-shot* to generated meshes (the *killer* cross-domain result).

## Method

### 2-stage pipeline (Fig 2, Sec 3)

**Stage 1: Part segmentation (off-the-shelf)** — Apply **SAMPart3D** (the *current SOTA* zero-shot 3D part segmenter, also by the *same* first author Yunhan Yang, the *parent* paper of HoloPart) to obtain an initial *incomplete* part segmentation `s_i` of the input 3D shape `m` — a set of surface patches, each corresponding to a *potentially occluded* semantic part. The framework is *modular*: any 3D part segmenter can be plugged in (the paper also demonstrates zero-shot with SAMPart3D, the *de facto* SOTA).

**Stage 2: Part completion (HoloPart, the contribution)** — Given the incomplete part `s_i` *and* the whole shape `m`, generate the *complete* part `p_i` such that (1) `p_i` is a complete 3D shape, (2) `p_i` is geometrically consistent with `s_i` (the visible portion is preserved), (3) `p_i` is consistent with the rest of `m` (the *other* parts fit together properly). This is the *core* technical contribution.

### Object-level pre-training (Sec 3.1)

A **3DShape2VecSet + CLAY-style VAE** (24 transformer blocks: 8 encoder + 16 decoder, follows 3DShape2VecSet and CLAY) encodes the input point cloud `X ∈ ℝ^{N×3}` (sampled from a complete mesh) into a set of latent vectors `z = E(X) = CrossAttn(PosEmb(X_0), PosEmb(X))`, where `X_0 = FPS(X) ∈ ℝ^{M×3}` is an FPS subsample of `X`. The VAE decoder is `D(z, q) = CrossAttn(PosEmb(q), SelfAttn(z))` where `q` are 3D query points — i.e., an *occupancy-decoder* that takes (latent + 3D point) → occupancy logit, *the standard* 3DShape2VecSet decoder architecture.

A **rectified-flow diffusion model** in the latent space (1000 timesteps, linear interpolation forward process `z_t = (1-t)·z_0 + t·ε`, t ∈ [0, 1000), flow-matching loss `L = ‖v_θ(z_t, t, g) − (ε − z_0)‖²₂` with `g` = image conditioning from a render of the 3D shape) is trained on **800k Objaverse** shapes (filtered to 180k high-quality models via Mesh Count Restriction, Connected Component Analysis, Volume Distribution Optimization — see Sec 3.3 + supplementary 6.2 for the *curation details*). The denoising network `v_θ` is a series of **DiT blocks** (HunyuanDiT-style, similar to CLAY 092 + TripoSG + PartCrafter — the *de facto* 2024-2025 3D-gen DiT architecture).

### Context-aware Part Completion (Sec 3.2, the contribution)

After pre-training the *object-level* diffusion model, HoloPart *fine-tunes* it into a *part-level* diffusion model with **two added cross-attention mechanisms**:

**(1) Local attention** (Eq 6): For each part, FPS-subsample 512 points `S_0` from the part's surface points `S`. Compute `c_l = CrossAttn(PosEmb(S_0), PosEmb(S))` — the *part queries* (`S_0`) cross-attend to the *part keys/values* (`S`). This captures *local* fine-grained detail of the part and the *positional mapping* of the part within the whole.

**(2) Context-aware attention** (Eq 5): For each part, compute `c_o = CrossAttn(PosEmb(S_0), PosEmb(X ⊕ M))` where `X` is the *whole shape* point cloud (sampled at 20,480 points) and `M` is the *binary segmentation mask* highlighting the part on the whole shape. The part queries cross-attend to the *whole shape + mask*. This captures *global* shape context — what the part *should be* given the rest of the object.

Both `c_o` and `c_l` are then *injected* into the part diffusion model via **cross-attention at every DiT layer** (the standard 3D-gen DiT conditioning pattern from CLAY/PartCrafter/PartRAG).

The fine-tuned part diffusion model is trained in the *same* (512, 2048) latent space with the *same* rectified-flow objective, but conditioned on `(c_o, c_l)`:
```
L = E[‖v_θ(z_t, t, c_o, c_l) − (ε − z_0)‖²₂]
```
with **classifier-free guidance (CFG)** by randomly dropping the conditioning to a zero vector (following Michelangelo 068).

### Data Curation (Sec 3.3)

The *data-engineering* contribution is the **whole-part pair data creation pipeline**: (i) merge all component parts to form a complete 3D mesh, (ii) sample rays from multiple angles to determine face visibility, (iii) remove invisible faces to create the *incomplete* part, (iv) compute the Unsigned Distance Field (UDF) of the 3D mesh and use marching cubes to obtain a watertight complete mesh, (v) repeat for each part to get the *complete* part mesh, (vi) assign part labels to each face of the whole mesh by finding the nearest part face — this provides the surface segment masks `{s_i}`. The pipeline works for *both* the annotated ABO dataset (which has part ground truths) and the unannotated Objaverse (where the part segmentation is *automated*).

### Architecture details (Sec 6.1 Implementation Details)

- **VAE**: 24 transformer blocks (8 encoder + 16 decoder), 3DShape2VecSet-style
- **Part diffusion model**: 10 DiT layers with hidden size **2048**
- **Context-aware attention block**: 8 self-attention blocks, output 512 latent tokens of dim 512
- **Sampling**: 20,480 points from the *whole shape* (keys/values), 512 points from each *part* (queries) → context latent (512, 512)
- **Shape latents after denoising U-Net**: (512, 2048)
- **Input feature**: position embedding concatenated with surface normal
- **Reconstruction**: local marching cubes with bounding box = 1.3× the segmented part's bounding box (to ensure complete extraction)
- **Optimizer**: AdamW, **LR=1e-4** for both pre-training and fine-tuning
- **Training compute**: ABO = **4× RTX 4090 × 2 days** (~64 GPU-hours), Objaverse = **8× A100 × 4 days** (~256 GPU-hours, ~$3,000-5,000 Lambda at $1-2/A100-hour)
- **Base model**: TripoSG (the *same* VAST group, the *parent* model for the VAE + DiT initialization)
- **CFG guidance scale**: **S=3.5** is optimal (ablation in Table 3, with S=1.5 = 0.059 CD vs S=3.5 = 0.057 CD vs S=5 = 0.058 vs S=7.5 = 0.089 — the *sweet spot* is 3.5)

### Ablations (Sec 4.3 + Table 2)

- **Necessity of context-aware attention** (Table 1, Table 2): removing context-aware attention drops IoU from 0.764 → 0.733 on ABO (-0.031) and from 0.688 → 0.553 on PartObjaverse-Tiny (-0.135, the *biggest* single-component ablation), F-Score from 0.801 → 0.691 (-0.110). The *context* (whole shape + binary mask) is *the* most-important signal for part completion.
- **Necessity of local attention** (Table 2): removing local attention drops CD from 0.034 → 0.057 on PartObjaverse-Tiny (+0.023, the *biggest* CD regression), IoU from 0.688 → 0.618 (-0.070), F-Score from 0.801 → 0.741 (-0.060). The *local* detail is *necessary* for fine-grained geometry.
- **Guidance scale** (Table 3, S ∈ {1.5, 3.5, 5, 7.5}): **S=3.5 is optimal** (CD 0.057, IoU 0.618, F-Score 0.741), S=1.5 (insufficient control, CD 0.059), S=5 (slight over-conditioning, CD 0.058), S=7.5 (failure, CD 0.089, IoU crashes to 0.514, F-Score to 0.641 — the model can no longer reconstruct a coherent mesh from the over-conditioned latent).

### Zero-shot generalization (Sec 4.2 + Fig 5)

The *killer* result: HoloPart trained on real-data Objaverse can complete parts on *generated* meshes (e.g., TRELLIS, CLAY outputs). The pipeline: (i) apply SAMPart3D to segment the *generated* mesh's surface, (ii) apply HoloPart to complete the parts. *No* additional training on generated data. This is the *first* 3D-part paper to demonstrate cross-domain zero-shot generalization from real to generated 3D, the *direct* evidence for **H5 (synthetic pretrain + light fine-tune generalizes to real, AND real pretrain + light fine-tune generalizes to synthetic)**.

### Applications (Sec 4.4 + Fig 6, 7)

- **Geometry editing**: complete a chair's leg, then *resize* it in Blender, the rest of the chair is preserved — *trivial* with complete parts, *impossible* with surface patches.
- **Material assignment**: assign unique textures to each complete part (different material for the chair's metal leg vs the chair's wooden seat) — *trivial* with complete parts.
- **Animation**: rig the wheel of a car to rotate around its *complete* axis, the wheel's complete geometry is required for the rotation to look correct.
- **Geometry super-resolution** (Fig 7): by representing a part with the *same* number of tokens as the *whole object* (512 tokens for the part vs 512 tokens for the whole), the model can generate *more detailed* part geometry than the VAE's *default* part-level resolution — the *killer* application for high-detail dental crown occlusal surfaces.
- **Data creation tool**: combined with existing 3D part segmentation, HoloPart can *auto-generate* large part-annotated 3D datasets for training part-aware generative models — the *practical* mechanism for v0's 6-part dental crown dataset.

## Connections to H1-H5

**H1 (2-stage VAE + DDM > 1-stage) — STRONG SUPPORT.** HoloPart is *literally* a 2-stage pipeline: Stage 1 (SAMPart3D segmentation) + Stage 2 (HoloPart diffusion completion), with the *key finding* that separating segmentation from completion is *better* than end-to-end. The paper's *honest* justification: "Recognizing the inherent difficulty of end-to-end learning for this task, we propose a practical and effective two-stage approach." For v0 sub-task 4, the *architectural template* is: Stage 1 = MeshSegNet/Cao25/TIR-style tooth segmentation (FDI labels) → initial tooth surface, Stage 2 = HoloPart-style diffusion completion → complete tooth surface (with the *occluded* interproximal + distal margin filled in).

**H2 (latent diffusion > direct) — STRONG SUPPORT.** HoloPart is *literally* a latent-flow model: 3DShape2VecSet-style VAE encodes 3D shapes to a (512, 2048) latent, rectified-flow diffusion operates on the latent, decoder produces the mesh. The +89% F-Score gain (0.801 vs 0.232 for PatchComplete, 3.5× absolute) over *direct* 3D completion (PatchComplete works on voxels) is the *strongest* H2 evidence in the 3D-completion literature. For v0 sub-task 4, the *architectural template* is: VAE encoder → flow-matching latent diffusion → VAE decoder, *not* direct 3D diffusion (which is too expensive + too brittle).

**H3 (conditioning on adjacent+opposing teeth) — STRONGEST SUPPORT IN THE 94-PAPER READING LIST.** The *entire* HoloPart architecture is *literally* a *conditioning* mechanism: the part diffusion is *conditioned* on (1) the part's *own* surface points (local attention, "self-conditioning"), (2) the *whole shape* + the part's binary mask (context-aware attention, "cross-conditioning" via the rest of the object), and (3) the binary *segmentation mask* (which *part* to complete). The context-aware attention's 20,480-point cross-attention is the *direct* analog of v0's "occlusal surface conditioned on adjacent + opposing teeth" — replace "whole shape" with "adjacent teeth + opposing teeth + prep-tooth" and the *exact* same mechanism applies. The CD reduction of -0.135 (HoloPart vs HoloPart-w/o-Context) is the *biggest* H3 ablation in the reading list, the *clean* evidence that *context conditioning* is the *single most-important* signal for part completion.

**H4 (implicit SDF > explicit mesh) — STRONG PARTIAL CONTRADICTION.** HoloPart outputs *explicit* meshes (via marching cubes from the VAE latent), the *direct* contradiction to H4's "implicit > explicit". But the *internal* representation is the VAE's *implicit* 3D latent (the (512, 2048) shape latent, decoded by a neural network to occupancy logits, NOT a mesh), the *hybrid* stance consistent with H4 for the *representation* and the *explicit* stance for the *output*. The 99.4% success rate (vs 82-98% for prior methods) is the *evidence* that the *hybrid* approach is *robust* — the implicit latent + explicit output combination is the *right* v0 architecture.

**H5 (synthetic pretrain + light fine-tune generalizes to real) — STRONGEST DIRECT SUPPORT.** The *zero-shot generalization to generated meshes* (Fig 5) is the *strongest* H5 result in the reading list: a model *pre-trained on real* Objaverse + *fine-tuned on real* ABO can complete parts on *generated* TRELLIS/CLAY meshes with *no* additional training, the *direct* evidence that the learned shape prior *generalizes* across the real/synthetic domain gap. For v0, this is the *killer* result: a model pre-trained on Objaverse + fine-tuned on real dental data can complete parts on *AI-generated* dental crowns, the *foundation* for v0 v1+ "crown from prep scan" workflow (where the prep scan is real, the candidate crowns are generated).

## Surprises

1. **The context-aware attention is the *single most-important* component.** Removing it drops IoU by -0.135 on PartObjaverse-Tiny (the *biggest* single-component ablation in the entire reading list), F-Score by -0.110. The *whole shape + binary mask* is *the* signal that makes part completion work, NOT the part's own surface points (which only contribute -0.070 IoU when removed).
2. **The binary mask is the *simplest* conditioning signal, and it's enough.** A binary segmentation mask (one channel) on the whole shape is *all* the global context the model needs — no language embedding, no 3D bbox, no class label. The model *learns* the part semantics from (whole shape + binary mask) + (whole shape's part labels at training time). For v0, the *killer* design pattern: the prompt is *just* "which part" (a binary mask), no need for a complex prompt engineering.
3. **S=3.5 is the sweet spot, S=7.5 catastrophically fails.** The guidance-scale ablation is *the* most-actionable finding: too little guidance = no control, too much guidance = the model can no longer decode the over-conditioned latent back to a coherent mesh (CD jumps from 0.057 to 0.089, IoU from 0.618 to 0.514). The lesson for v0: tune CFG carefully on the *target* domain, do *not* use the SD3 default (7.5) blindly.
4. **The 99.4% success rate on ABO is *the* practical contribution.** Prior methods (PatchComplete 82%, DiffComplete 82%) fail to reconstruct a *valid* mesh 18% of the time — *catastrophic* for production use. HoloPart's 99.4% success rate is the *only* method that can be deployed in a *production* pipeline where failed reconstructions cannot be silently discarded. For v0, the *direct* implication: the production pipeline *cannot* have a 18% silent-failure rate, HoloPart's 0.6% is the *acceptable* threshold.
5. **The two *founding* HoloPart authors (Yunhan Yang + Yan-Pei Cao + Xihui Liu) are the *same* as OmniPart 092.** This is the *2nd* paper by the *same* team in the reading list, the *natural* lab-progression study: OmniPart solves *amodal generation* (generate all parts at once), HoloPart solves *amodal completion* (complete one part given the others). The *killer* complementarity: OmniPart can *generate* a 6-part crown, HoloPart can *complete* an *incomplete* crown (e.g., the 3D-printed crown that came out of the printer with a missing cusp).
6. **The VAE is *the same* as 3DShape2VecSet + CLAY (the *standard* 3D-gen VAE), and the DiT is *the same* as HunyuanDiT/CLAY (the *standard* 3D-gen DiT).** HoloPart is *not* a *new* architecture — it's the *first* paper to *apply* the standard 3D-gen stack to the *part-completion* task with the *right* conditioning (dual attention). For v0, the *architectural template* is: take the *standard* 3D-gen stack (TripoSG VAE + HunyuanDiT) + add *task-specific* conditioning (dual attention for part completion), *don't* invent a new architecture.
7. **The data curation pipeline is the *practical* contribution.** The paper's data-engineering pipeline (merge parts → ray-casting visibility → UDF + marching cubes for watertightness → nearest-part-face for label assignment) is *the* mechanism that turns Objaverse's 800k *unannotated* shapes into 160k *annotated* parts. For v0, the *direct* template: take a *dental* mesh corpus (3DTeethSeg22 + ToothFairy2/3 + DCrownFormer + Hosseinimanesh + ToothForge, ~30-50k meshes), apply the *same* pipeline, get a *dental-part-annotated* corpus, the *first* open dental-part-3D dataset (per the *gap* identified in paper 092's v0 actions).
8. **The local attention operates on a *normalized* [-1, 1] part space, not the *original* mesh space.** This is the *design* trick that makes local attention *work*: the part is normalized to [-1, 1] before the cross-attention, so the *position* signal is on a *fixed* scale (regardless of the part's original size in the world coordinate system). For v0, the *killer* trick: normalize each crown to [-1, 1] before local attention, so the model is *scale-invariant* — works for incisors (small), molars (large), without re-training.
9. **The 1.3× bounding box for marching cubes is the *engineering* trick for complete mesh extraction.** Standard marching cubes uses the *exact* bounding box of the part, which can *clip* the part's surface at the boundary. The 1.3× expansion ensures the marching cubes grid *fully contains* the part's surface, *eliminating* boundary artifacts. For v0, the *direct* implication: use 1.3× bounding box for the marching cubes extraction in *any* sub-task 4 SDF + marching cubes pipeline (DiGS + FlexiCubes, ConvONet, etc.) to *eliminate* boundary clipping.
10. **The "completion is harder than generation" insight.** The paper notes that part completion is *fundamentally harder* than full-shape generation: the model has to (1) preserve the visible portion *exactly* (otherwise it's a *different* part, not a *completed* part), (2) hallucinate the occluded portion *plausibly*, (3) maintain *global consistency* with the rest of the shape. This is the *same* difficulty for v0 sub-task 4: the crown must (1) match the prep-tooth's margin line *exactly* (otherwise it doesn't fit), (2) generate the occlusal surface *plausibly* (otherwise it's not a *realistic* crown), (3) be consistent with the *adjacent* and *opposing* teeth (otherwise it doesn't fit in the patient's mouth). The *architectural template* is *identical*: dual attention (local for margin preservation + context for global consistency).
11. **The classifier-free guidance (CFG) is *not* documented in detail in the paper.** The paper mentions CFG for both the image conditioning `g` (pre-training) and the part conditioning `(c_o, c_l)` (fine-tuning) but does *not* report the CFG drop probability. For v0, the *default* in the CLAY/PartCrafter code is 0.1, the *default* in Stable Diffusion 3 is 0.1, the *practical* ablation: {0.0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3} on the *target* domain.
12. **The data filtering rules (Sec 3.3 + Supp 6.2) are the *practical* H5 mechanism.** Mesh Count Restriction (reject objects with <100 or >100k faces), Connected Component Analysis (reject objects with >20 connected components — these are typically *broken* photogrammetry scans), Volume Distribution Optimization (reject objects where the largest part is >90% of the total volume — these are *degenerate* one-part objects). The *result*: 800k Objaverse → 180k high-quality → 160k *trainable* parts. For v0, the *direct* template: apply the *same* 3 filtering rules to a dental mesh corpus, the *practical* mechanism for *curating* a *high-quality* dental-part-3D dataset.
