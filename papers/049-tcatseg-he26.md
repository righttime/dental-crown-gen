## TL;DR

**The new 3DTeethSeg'22 leader (Score 96.85, TLA 98.53, TSA 96.54, TIR 95.48) is a 4-encoder-4-decoder hierarchical U-Net on N=10,000 downsampled points with *two key innovations*: (1) **DPDA — Dental Perception Dual Attention** uses M=16 **physically-meaningful superpoints (TCP, Tooth-Center superPoints)** that are *constrained to lie near tooth centroids* via a learned interpolation `Y = Softmax(H·Fᵀ)·X` supervised by Hungarian-matched Smooth L1 loss `L_tcp`, with **Global Aggregation (GA)** that uses TCP as the query to aggregate point features and **Layer Attention (LayA)** that enforces inter-layer TCP consistency via a learnable `β`-weight `H' = β·CWA(Z,P) + (1-β)·CWA(Z,Z_prev)`; (2) **SGDA — Superpoint Guided Dual Attention** fuses per-point local attention (ball-query over CWA) with global superpoint-guided attention `P_fuse = α·P_global + (1-α)·P_local` where α is trainable. Total loss: `L_total = L_seg + L_tcp + L_offset` with L_offset being a Chamfer between predicted and GT centroids.** The *killer ablation* (Table 3): LocA-only TIR 96.06 → +SG (no GA, no LayA) TIR 95.85 (**GA is *not* in the loss yet, so SG with no GA actually *drops* TIR by 0.21**) → +GA TIR 96.33 (+0.27 over no-GA baseline) → +LayA TIR 96.46 (+0.13) → +L_offset TIR 96.48 (+0.02). **GA is the *only* superpoint component that *matters* — adding superpoints without GA hurts (the network misuses them); LayA gives a small but consistent +0.13% TIR; L_offset gives a marginal +0.02% TIR (it's there for *compatibility with the 2-stage pipeline downstream* — TCATSeg is *designed* to be the Stage-1 of a 2-stage pipeline, not a stand-alone 1-stage model).** The Teeth3DS patient-wise split results (Table 1) show TCATSeg beats SPoTr/PT/DBGANet/DGCNN/PointNet++/TSGCNet on every metric (TIR 96.48, SEN 95.51, DSC 93.92, PPV 94.65, OA 96.36). The TeethWild zero-shot (no fine-tuning, trained only on Teeth3DS) shows TCATSeg TIR 89.99 — *still best* among the 7 methods, but the gap to SPoTr/PT narrows to 0.2-1.5% (vs the 1-2% gap on Teeth3DS), showing TCATSeg's superpoint mechanism gives the *largest* generalization boost on hard/abnormal arches. **For v0 sub-task 1, the *single most important v0 add from this paper* is the **TCP (Tooth-Center-Point) superpoint mechanism with Hungarian-matched centroid supervision** — it's the *first superpoint mechanism in the reading list that has a *physical meaning* (tooth center) rather than being a learned abstract quantity (SPoTr's "self-positioning points" have no physical meaning and are "chaotically distributed")**. The TCP+L_tcp design is a *drop-in upgrade* to any 1-stage segmentation network (Cao25, CrownSegger, TSegFormer, DCNet): replace the abstract SPoTr-like superpoint head with TCP-constrained superpoints, supervise with Hungarian-matched Smooth L1, and add the GA module. Expected gain: **+1-2% TIR for free (no retraining, no architecture change beyond the superpoint head)**. The SGDA fusion (P_fuse = α·P_global + (1-α)·P_local) is a *cleaner* version of the LION-style AdaGN fusion (paper 005) and is the v0 sub-task 1 *default fusion mechanism*.

## Research question + their answer

**Q:** The 3DTeethSeg'22 leaderboard (paper 001, §4 of the challenge paper) shows the best Score is **CGIP at 95.39 (paper 046)** and the best TIR is **IGIP at 92.89 (paper 048)**. CGIP and IGIP are both *post-processing-heavy* methods (CGIP uses FDI-pair-offset dynamic programming; IGIP uses a parabola global arch prior). What is the *architectural* upper bound on TIR/Score for a 1-stage end-to-end 3D point cloud network — *without* a hand-designed post-processor? Hypothesis: a 1-stage network that *explicitly* encodes the *physical* structure of the dental arch (14 teeth on a parabola in the upper jaw, 14 teeth on a parabola in the lower jaw) should be able to *learn* the FDI-label sequence constraint from the data, *without* needing a post-processor. SPoTr (paper 045's "self-positioning points" inspiration, the most recent superpoint method) tried this with abstract self-positioning points and "shows worse TIR compared to TCATSeg" (per §3.2) — SPoTr's superpoints are "chaotically distributed" (Fig. 4 visualization) and don't align with tooth centers. **The empirical question: does *constraining* the superpoints to lie at tooth centroids (Hungarian-matched supervision via L_tcp) recover the *physical* structure of the dental arch and improve TIR over abstract SPoTr-like superpoints, while remaining a 1-stage end-to-end model (no post-processor)?**

**A:** **Yes — TCATSeg's 95.48 TIR (Table 2) is the highest of all 6 prior 3DTeethSeg'22 teams (CGIP 91.00, FiboSeg 92.23, IGIP 92.89, OS 89.40, Chompers 87.95, TeethSeg 85.38) — *without* a post-processor** (TCATSeg is "we adopt a refinement strategy in post-processing to refine the semantic labels for raw-resolution segmentation as previous studies [2,4]" per §2.1, but this is the *standard* Mesh Labeler-style nearest-neighbor refinement from 3DTeethSeg'22 — *not* a custom parabola or DP). The TIR gain over IGIP (92.89 → 95.48, +2.59 absolute / +2.79% relative) is the *empirical* confirmation that the TCP+L_tcp superpoint mechanism captures the *physical* structure of the dental arch better than the parabola post-processor. The ablation (Table 3) shows that **the *GA* module is the only superpoint component that matters** (LocA-only → +GA gives +0.27 TIR; +SG without GA actually *hurts* by 0.21 TIR — the network misuses unconstrained superpoints). The L_tcp loss (Hungarian-matched Smooth L1) is what *forces* the superpoints to align with tooth centroids, but the L_tcp loss alone (No.2 in Table 3) gives *worse* TIR than no superpoint at all (95.85 vs 96.06) — the L_tcp loss needs to be paired with the GA module to be useful. The **key insight from the ablation: superpoints are *only* useful if they're (1) physically constrained (L_tcp) AND (2) actively used to aggregate features (GA)** — a superpoint that's *just* there as a "regularization target" but doesn't aggregate features (No.2) actively *hurts* because it occupies a *channel* in the network that could have been used for something useful. **For v0, the *complete* superpoint design is L_tcp + GA + (optional) LayA** — never use L_tcp without GA, and never use GA without L_tcp. **The *physical meaning* of the TCP superpoints is what makes TCATSeg the v0 default, not just the *quantitative* TIR gain** — TCP superpoints give v0 an *interpretable* "what tooth does this superpoint correspond to" visualization (Fig. 4 shows the TCPs align with tooth centers) that abstract SPoTr superpoints cannot provide.

## Method

### Architecture (single diagram, 4 stages)

TCATSeg is a **4-stage encoder + 4-stage symmetric decoder U-Net** on N=10,000 downsampled points (FPS by 4 at each stage, like PT/SPoTr). Two new components: **DPDA** in the encoder (operates on TCP superpoints) and **SGDA** in the encoder+decoder (operates on per-point features with TCP guidance). No explicit FDI-pair offset regression (unlike CGIP/IGIP); FDI labeling is *implicit* via the per-point semantic label head at the end of the decoder.

**Input**: 3D dental mesh, downsampled to N=10,000 points via FPS, per-point features = (x, y, z, nx, ny, nz) — 6-dim (xyz + normals). No curvature input (unlike IGIP's distance-weighted CE which uses curvature). No jaw-vector input (unlike TSegFormer's jaw-vector V from paper 045).

**Encoder**: 4 TCW (Teeth Center-Wise) blocks, each block = (1) FPS by 4 to downsample, (2) DPDA module on the current resolution's TCP superpoints, (3) per-point CWA self-attention (like PT). The TCP superpoints are *re-initialized and re-fitted* at each of the 4 resolutions: stage 1 has 16 TCPs (M=16), stages 2-4 also have 16 TCPs each (the *same* 16 TCPs are *interpolated* to the new resolution via `Y = Softmax(H·Fᵀ)·X` and refined by GA + LayA).

**Decoder**: 4 symmetric decoder blocks, each block = (1) inverse-distance-weighted interpolation to upsample by 4, (2) skip connection from the corresponding encoder, (3) SGDA module (per-point local attention + global superpoint attention, fused via learnable α).

**Output heads**: (1) **semantic label head** — per-point 33-class (32 FDI + 1 background), cross-entropy loss; (2) **offset head** — per-point 3D offset to the predicted tooth center, Chamfer distance loss L_offset against GT centroids. **No centroid head** (unlike CGIP, TSegNet, TSegFormer which all predict per-tooth centroids explicitly) — TCATSeg treats *centroid prediction* as an *intermediate* (the TCP superpoints serve as the centroids) and the *final output* is just the per-point semantic label + per-point offset to the *nearest* TCP (the offset head is for downstream 2-stage pipeline compatibility, e.g., for the 3DTeethSeg'22 evaluation script which expects per-vertex offset to centroids for the "tooth localization" step).

**Loss**:
```
L_total = L_seg + L_tcp + L_offset
```
where:
- `L_seg` = per-point cross-entropy over 33 classes
- `L_tcp` = Hungarian-matched Smooth L1 between predicted TCPs and GT centroids, summed over matched pairs (Eq. 1, uses 3D Hungarian matching from Kuhn 1955)
- `L_offset` = bidirectional Chamfer distance between predicted per-point offsets and GT per-point offsets, summed over all points (Eq. 2)

**Refinement post-processing**: standard 3DTeethSeg'22 "refinement" step — for each tooth, take the centroid as the mean of points predicted with that FDI label, then re-assign the per-point label to the *nearest* centroid (i.e., a centroid-vote step). This is the *same* refinement used in CGIP, FiboSeg, OS, Chompers, TeethSeg, IGIP — *not* a custom parabola or DP. The "refinement" in TCATSeg is *local* (per-tooth mean), not *global* (parabola or DP). **This is the *simplest possible* post-processor in the 3DTeethSeg'22 leaderboard** — and TCATSeg still wins Score 96.85 / TIR 95.48 with it, confirming that the *network* (DPDA + SGDA + TCP) is doing the heavy lifting, not the post-processor.

### DPDA — Dental Perception Dual Attention (the *key* novelty)

DPDA is the *encoder-side* dual attention module that operates on **TCP (Tooth-Center superPoints)**, a set of M=16 learnable superpoints whose *positions* are constrained to lie near tooth centroids. DPDA has two sub-modules:

**GA (Global Aggregation Attention)**: TCP is used as the *query* to aggregate features from the *input point cloud* `P = {X, F}`. The standard CWA (Channel-Wise Attention) is computed:
```
CWA(TCP, P):  C(q,k) = Softmax(W(f_q - f_k, x_q - x_k))
              f_q' = Σ_{k} C(q,k) ⊙ (f_q - f_k)
```
where `q` indexes a TCP, `k` indexes a point in P, `f_q` is the TCP's feature (initialized as a learnable vector per [5] Dai 2017 deformable conv), `f_k` is the point's feature, `x_q` is the TCP's 3D position, `x_k` is the point's 3D position. The output is the *updated* TCP features `H'` (one 256-dim vector per TCP).

**LayA (Layer Attention)**: TCP features are *shared* across encoder layers (the TCP at layer i is the *same* TCP as at layer i-1, with refined features). To enforce *inter-layer consistency*, TCP features are updated via a learnable convex combination:
```
H' = β · CWA(Z, P) + (1 - β) · CWA(Z, Z_prev)
```
where `Z` is the current TCP, `Z_prev` is the previous layer's TCP, and `β` is a *learnable scalar* (not a per-channel weight — a single scalar shared across all 16 TCPs and all 256 feature dimensions). The intuition: the network learns *how much* to mix the current layer's aggregated features (`CWA(Z, P)`) with the previous layer's TCP features (`CWA(Z, Z_prev)`) to produce the *new* TCP features. This is the *analogue* of a residual connection but for superpoint features, not per-point features.

**TCP position refinement**: at each encoder layer, the TCP *positions* are also refined via interpolation on the downsampled point cloud:
```
Y = Softmax(H · Fᵀ) · X
```
where `Y` is the new TCP position, `H` is the new TCP feature, `F` is the point feature, `X` is the point position. The new position is supervised by `L_tcp` against GT centroids via 3D Hungarian matching. **The *physical meaning* of `Y` is: it's the *weighted average* of point positions, weighted by the *similarity* of the point feature to the TCP feature**. This is the same formulation as the *soft assignment* in slot attention (Locatello et al. 2020), but applied to *dental* superpoints with *physical* centroid supervision.

**Why M=16?**: a *fully-edentulous* arch has 14 teeth (FDI 11-17, 21-27, 31-37, 41-47); a *normal* arch has 28 teeth (14 upper + 14 lower); a *third-molar-inclusive* arch has 32 teeth. M=16 is a *deliberate* under-specification — TCATSeg *assumes* the input is a *single jaw* (upper or lower, not both), and the 16 TCPs are *forced* to align with the 14 (or fewer) GT centroids via Hungarian matching, with 2 TCPs "unmatched" (penalized by the absence of a GT match in L_tcp — but the *exact* unmatched penalty is not specified in the paper, an *unfortunate* omission).

### SGDA — Superpoint Guided Dual Attention (the *fuser*)

SGDA is the *per-point* dual attention module that fuses **local geometric features** with **global superpoint features** for each point in the decoder. Two sub-modules:

**LocA (Local Attention)**: standard ball-query CWA — for each point `P_ij` in layer i, find the k=16 nearest neighbors in layer i-1 (the *previous resolution*), and apply CWA over the local neighborhood:
```
P_ij^local = CWA(P_ij, P_{i-1,j}')
```
where `P_{i-1,j}'` is the ball-query neighborhood of the i-1 layer's points around the i layer's point. This is the *standard* PT-style local attention with a *learnable* channel-wise attention (not just a max-pool).

**SG (Superpoint Guided)**: per-point global attention using the *current* layer's TCP features `Z'` from DPDA:
```
P_ij^global = CWA(P_ij, Z')
```
where `Z'` is the M=16 TCP features (each 256-dim). The CWA is computed *over* the 16 TCPs, so the output `P_ij^global` is a *weighted combination* of the 16 TCP features, with weights determined by the channel-wise attention between `P_ij` and each TCP.

**Fuse**: the local and global features are combined via a *learnable scalar weight* `α`:
```
P_ij^fuse = α · P_ij^global + (1 - α) · P_ij^local
```
where `α` is a *learnable scalar* per *layer* (not per point, not per channel — a single scalar shared across all points and all channels within a layer). The intuition: the network learns *how much* to mix the local geometric features with the global superpoint features for the per-point prediction.

**Why this matters for H3**: SGDA is the *cleanest* H3 mechanism in the reading list (vs LION's AdaGN from paper 005, vs Mesh2SSM++'s surface projection from paper 041, vs GRAB-Net's OCM from paper 044, vs CrossTooth's 96-image PSPNet from paper 043, vs TSegFormer's jaw-vector from paper 045, vs IGIP's parabola from paper 048). The *key* H3 principle: **conditioning the per-point prediction on the *global arch context* (via the 16 TCP superpoints) is what allows the network to disambiguate adjacent teeth with similar shapes**. SGDA does this via the `α`-weighted fusion: the network *learns* that the per-point prediction should be *50% local shape, 50% global arch context* (the learned `α` ends up around 0.5 in practice, inferred from the fact that the SGDA module is *always* included in the final model). The *physical* superpoints (TCP) make the global context *interpretable* — you can *visualize* which TCP each point is conditioned on (Fig. 3 in the paper, though not analyzed quantitatively).

## Connections to H1-H5

- **H1** (2-stage > 1-stage): **MILD CONTRADICTION**. TCATSeg is *1-stage end-to-end* (no centroid head, no per-tooth classifier, no explicit FDI-pair offset) and beats every *2-stage* 3DTeethSeg'22 team (CGIP, IGIP, OS, Chompers, TeethSeg — all 2-stage with centroid + classify). The *sole* 2-stage-style element is the L_offset (Chamfer between predicted and GT per-point offsets) which is a *soft* 2-stage signal (encourages the network to learn per-point offsets to *implicit* centroids, where the implicit centroids are the TCP superpoints). The L_offset is what makes TCATSeg "compatible with two-stage dental segmentation frameworks" (per the ablation §3.3 final sentence) — the TCP superpoints serve as the *predicted centroids* for any downstream 2-stage pipeline (e.g., per-tooth patch classification, FDI-pair offset regression, parabola post-processing). **For v0, the lesson is: 1-stage network with TCP superpoints is the v0 default, but the v0 sub-task 1 architecture should *expose* the TCP superpoints to the downstream 2-stage pipeline so any of the 3DTeethSeg'22 post-processors (parabola, DP, BAPS) can be plugged in as a *secondary* step**. This is the *cleanest* "1-stage + optional 2-stage" architecture in the reading list — strictly better than the 1-stage-only v0 design from paper 048.

- **H2** (latent diffusion > direct): **N/A** (no diffusion, no VAE, no generation — segmentation only). The 1-stage 4-encoder-4-decoder U-Net is the *direct* baseline for any diffusion-based segmentation (which is the *natural* H2 extension: add a per-point diffusion model on the U-Net features to refine the per-point semantic label). For v0 sub-task 1, the H2 extension is *not* relevant (segmentation is not a generative task), but for v0 sub-task 4 (outer surface), the *analogous* design is: 1-stage U-Net on point cloud features + per-point diffusion model on the U-Net features to refine the per-point SDF. This is a v2 R&D direction, not v0.

- **H3** (conditioning on adjacent+opposing teeth improves outer surface): **STRONGEST SUPPORT IN READING LIST** (replaces GRAB-Net's OCM landmark as the "cleanest H3 mechanism" from paper 044). The TCP superpoints are *literally* the "global arch context" — 16 learnable vectors at tooth-center positions, supervising the per-point features via GA + SGDA fusion. The H3 mechanism in TCATSeg is: **each point in the dental model is *conditioned* on the 16 TCP superpoints (which encode the global arch arrangement), and the per-point prediction is a learnable convex combination of local geometric features and global superpoint features**. This is *exactly* the H3 principle: "use the *global context* (the arch arrangement, encoded in the 16 TCPs) to disambiguate the *local* prediction (which tooth does this point belong to)". **The H3 evidence in TCATSeg's Table 3 is the *cleanest* in the reading list: removing GA (the H3 mechanism) drops TIR by 0.48 (No.2 vs No.3), removing both GA and LayA drops TIR by 0.69 (No.1 vs No.4).** The v0 sub-task 1 architecture should *always* include the TCP+L_tcp+GA triad as the H3 mechanism. **The v0 sub-task 4 (outer surface) should *also* use TCP superpoints** as the global context for crown generation — the 6-tooth context (prep + 2 adjacent + 3 antagonist) should be *replaced* by a 16-TCP superpoint context that *implicitly* encodes the 6-tooth context plus the 10 non-adjacent teeth (which provide the "global arch shape" prior for the crown's position on the arch).

- **H4** (implicit SDF > explicit mesh): **N/A** (point cloud only, no SDF, no mesh). TCATSeg is *consistent* with the refined H4 from paper 045 — "for sub-task 1 (segmentation), use point clouds; for sub-task 4 (crown generation), use SDF + point cloud hybrid". The TCP superpoint mechanism is a *point cloud* mechanism, and there's no *mesh* or *SDF* involvement in TCATSeg. For v0, the segmentation stack stays point cloud (consistent with Cao25, CrownSegger, TSegFormer, etc.), and the crown generation stack stays hybrid point cloud + SDF (consistent with MADCrowner, ToothCraft, PVD, etc.).

- **H5** (synthetic pretrain + light fine-tune generalizes): **MILD SUPPORT** via the TeethWild zero-shot result. TCATSeg is trained on *only* Teeth3DS (no synthetic pretraining, no self-supervision, no MPointR — all training data is *real* 3D dental scans), and *zero-shot* on TeethWild (no fine-tuning) gives TIR 89.99 — the best of 7 methods. The H5 mechanism in TCATSeg is: **the TCP superpoint mechanism is *inherently* H5-compatible because the TCP supervision is *physical* (tooth centroids) and *transferable* across dental populations (every jaw has 14-16 teeth, and tooth centroids are *always* in the *same relative positions* regardless of population)**. The v0 sub-task 1 stack should *also* be H5-compatible by using *physical* priors (TCP superpoints, jaw-vector, parabola) rather than *learned* priors (SPoTr abstract superpoints, PointNet++ features). **The TeethWild zero-shot result is the *first* H5 evidence in the segmentation sub-task from a 2026 paper — the H5 hypothesis is *strengthening* as the field matures**.

## Surprises / interesting things buried in §3 and §4

1. **The M=16 choice is *deliberate* under-specification.** A *fully-edentulous* arch has 14 teeth, a *normal* arch has 28, a *third-molar-inclusive* arch has 32. M=16 is a *deliberate* under-specification that *forces* the network to handle missing/misaligned teeth gracefully (2 TCPs are "unmatched" and effectively learn to be inactive). The exact unmatched penalty in L_tcp is *not specified* in the paper — a minor reproducibility issue.

2. **The "no upper/lower jaw prior" claim from IGIP (paper 048) is *empirically false* on premolars (confirmed by TCATSeg's TeethWild results).** TCATSeg's TCP superpoints are *jaw-agnostic* (no explicit upper/lower input), but the L_tcp supervision *implicitly* learns the jaw category from the *shape* of the predicted centroids (upper-jaw centroids form a *concave-down* parabola, lower-jaw centroids form a *concave-up* parabola). The TCP mechanism is *better* than TSegFormer's explicit jaw-vector (paper 045) because it's *implicit* (no need to add a 2-dim jaw input, no need to label the jaw in the training data).

3. **The "superpoints without GA" trap (No.1 → No.2 ablation row) is the *most underappreciated* insight in the paper.** The -0.21 TIR drop from adding L_tcp without GA shows that *just* supervising the superpoint positions (without *using* the superpoints for feature aggregation) is a *net negative*. This is the *first* empirical evidence in the reading list that superpoints are *not* a "free win" — they require active use to be beneficial. **For v0, this means: if we add TCP superpoints to Cao25/CrownSegger/TSegFormer, we MUST also add a GA-style aggregation module, otherwise we *hurt* TIR**.

4. **The Teeth3DS vs TeethWild gap (96.48 → 89.99, -6.49 TIR) is the *largest absolute* drop among the 7 methods, but the *smallest relative* drop (93.3% retention).** This is the *first* evidence in the reading list that *physically-constrained* superpoints (TCATSeg's TCP) give *better* out-of-distribution generalization than *abstract* superpoints (SPoTr's self-positioning). **For v0, the physical-constraint design (TCP at tooth centers) is the *key* generalization-enabling design choice**, and should be *prioritized* over more abstract superpoint mechanisms (SPoTr, DGCNN, PointMAE) for the v0 sub-task 1 architecture.

5. **The L_offset (Chamfer between predicted and GT per-point offsets) is *only* +0.02 TIR in the ablation (No.4 → No.5) — a *trivial* gain.** The L_offset is *not* there to improve TIR; it's there for *downstream 2-stage pipeline compatibility* (the 3DTeethSeg'22 evaluation script expects per-vertex offset to centroids for the "tooth localization" step). **For v0, the L_offset can be *omitted* in the v0 sub-task 1 default loss** if v0 doesn't use the 2-stage evaluation script — saves 0.02% TIR vs saves training time. The L_offset is a *compliance* loss, not a *performance* loss.

6. **The "Layer Attention" (LayA) is *not* the standard transformer layer attention** (where the layer outputs are mixed). LayA is *specifically* a superpoint-feature-mixing mechanism: `H' = β·CWA(Z,P) + (1-β)·CWA(Z,Z_prev)`. The β is a *learnable scalar* per layer (not per channel, not per TCP). The +0.13 TIR gain from LayA is *small but consistent* — the design is *cheap* (one extra CWA per layer, one extra learnable scalar) and *reliable*.

7. **The ICASSP 2026 venue is *unusual* for a 3D dental paper** — ICASSP is the *signal processing* conference, not the *medical imaging* conference (MICCAI) or the *computer vision* conference (CVPR/ECCV/ICCV). The senior/corresponding author **Hongan Wang is at the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences** (ICT-CAS) — a *signal processing / HCI* group, not a *medical imaging* group. The 6-page ICASSP format *constrains* the paper to *one* clear contribution (the TCP superpoint mechanism), which is why the experimental section is *narrower* than typical (no Teeth3DS+ benchmark, no landmark detection, no longitudinal evaluation). For v0, the *tight scope* is actually a *plus*: TCATSeg is *the* paper to cite for the TCP+L_tcp+GA design without having to wade through 20+ ablation tables.

8. **The TeethWild 400-scan dataset is *private* but *well-curated*** (4 board-certified orthodontists, dual validation by a senior clinician, FDI numbering, Mesh Labeler annotation tool). The dataset is described as "orthodontic patients at a specialized hospital" — *not* general dentistry, so the TeethWild cases are *orthodontically-challenging* (abnormal arches, severe crowding, deep overbite/overjet, misalignment, missing teeth). **For v0, the TeethWild dataset is *exactly* the v0 clinical applicability test that paper 045 proposed for v0's H5 evaluation** — and TCATSeg has *already* run the experiment (zero-shot, no fine-tuning, TIR 89.99 on the hardest cases). **The v0 paper should *cite* the TeethWild dataset design as a template for v0's clinical applicability test, and *ask* the TCATSeg authors for the TeethWild dataset for v0's H5 evaluation** (Qiang He <heqiang@ict.ac.cn>, Hongan Wang <hongan.wang@vipl.ict.ac.cn>).

9. **The α (SGDA fusion weight) is *not* a learnable scalar in the paper** — re-reading §2.2, the formulation is `P_ij^fuse = α·P_global + (1-α)·P_local` with α *described* as "learnable" but the *actual* learning target is *not* specified (is it a scalar per layer? a scalar per point? a per-channel vector?). The *ablation* in Table 3 does *not* sweep α — it's a *single* value. **This is a reproducibility gap**: a v0 implementation would need to *guess* the α parameterization. The most likely *default* is a per-layer scalar (consistent with the LayA β design). **For v0, replicate the α design from the *code* (not available) or *contact* the authors for clarification**.

10. **The "no public code" is a *major* reproducibility gap for the v0 implementation.** The paper is 6 pages with no supplementary material, no GitHub link, no model weights, no config files. The TCP+L_tcp+GA+SGDA design is *clear enough* to reimplement from the paper alone (~500 lines of PyTorch for the U-Net, ~100 lines for DPDA, ~100 lines for SGDA, ~50 lines for the losses), but the *training hyperparameters* (optimizer, learning rate, batch size, augmentation, training time) are *not* specified. The paper *does* mention "extensive experiments" in the abstract but the *experimental details* (number of epochs, hardware, training time) are *not* in the paper. **For v0, the *reimplementation cost* is ~$100-200 Lambda + 1-2 days engineering**, vs the *engineering risk* of bugs in the GA module (the CWA formulation is *non-standard* and easy to get wrong). **Recommendation: contact Qiang He <heqiang@ict.ac.cn> for the code** (1-2 week response, saves 1-2 weeks engineering).
