# Paper 194 — Reliev3R: Relieving Feed-forward 3D Reconstruction from Multi-View Geometric Annotations

## TL;DR

**FOUNDING PAPER** of the **weakly-supervised / SfM-free** paradigm for training feed-forward 3D-reconstruction models (FFRMs) from scratch — the *first* end-to-end 3R training recipe that **does not require multi-view geometric annotations** (3D point maps, registered camera poses) and instead **distills 3D knowledge directly from two *zero-shot* pretrained monocular priors** (Depth Pro for monocular relative depth, CoTracker for sparse 2D correspondences) using **two novel weakly-supervised losses**: (1) an **ambiguity-aware scale-invariant depth loss** `L_d` (Eq. 4) that learns a *per-pixel* confidence weight `W_i ∈ (0, 2)` via WMAD (weighted median absolute deviation) to *self-adaptively mask out* unreliable regions (sky, reflective surfaces, far backgrounds), and (2) a **trigonometry-based reprojection loss** `L_rgst` (Eq. 6) that **optimizes the angle between `(p_{i,k} - t_i)` and `(p_{j,k} - t_i)`** instead of point-cloud-distance or pixel-reprojection — the *killer* design choice that **prevents the camera-center-collapse pathology** where naive L2 minimization on `||p_{i,k} − p_{j,k}||²` collapses all cameras to the point-cloud centroid (a known failure mode of weakly-supervised 3D, which Reliev3R's angle-based objective structurally avoids). The deceptively simple but architecturally radical insight: **the expensive SfM/MVS pipeline used to produce the 3D labels that all prior FFRMs (DUSt3R → MASt3R → MUSt3R → CUT3R → FLARE → π³ → VGGT → MapAnything 193) consume during training is itself an *implementation* of multi-view geometric consistency** — so instead of *learning to imitate* the SfM/MVS output (which costs hours-per-scene + brittle on low-texture scenes), **learn the *multi-view geometric consistency constraints directly* from the raw multi-view images**, and the SfM/MVS pipeline becomes obsolete. Architecture: **π³ backbone with the pointmap prediction head replaced by a depth prediction head + an FoV (intrinsics) prediction head**, 450M parameters total. Trained on **DL3DV-10K** (10K scenes / 3M+ image frames, indoor + outdoor + different times of day — the most diverse FFRM training set in the field, *all* without multi-view geometric annotations), for **60k steps on 64 Ascend 910B3 NPUs in 3 days** (the *cheapest* FFRM training in the reading list, ~$3-5K Lambda equivalent at 2026 Lambda pricing). Pseudo-labels: **Depth Pro** (Bochkovskii 2024, Apple/MIT, ICML 2024, the *only* monocular depth model that produces *zero-shot metric* depth for open-domain images in <1s on a GPU) for relative depth, and **CoTracker** (Karaev 2024, Meta AI, ECCV 2024) for sparse 2D correspondences. **★ Results (Tab. 1, DL3DV-benchmark 8-view reconstruction, *not* trained on DL3DV-benchmark but on the much larger DL3DV-10K):** Reliev3R Point-Map rel 0.122 / τ 0.663 (the *third-best* in the 8-view DL3DV-benchmark table, *trailing only the SOTA SOTAs MapAnything 0.051/0.911, π³ 0.056/0.927, VGGT 0.061/0.905* and *beating* MVDUSt3R 0.593/0.233 by 4.9×, FLARE 0.134/0.771, and AnyCam 0.262/0.490 by 2.1×), Pose ATE 0.018 / AUC 49.426 (*third-best* behind π³† 0.013/63.314 trained on the same DL3DV-10K set with full supervision, *beating* AnyCam 0.023/29.527), Depth rel 0.115 / τ 0.657 (the *third-best* depth, *trailing only* MapAnything 0.045/0.896, π³ 0.054/0.916). **★ The killer zero-shot result (Tab. 2, ScanNet++, *unseen* focal length + aspect ratio):** Reliev3R Point-Map rel 0.172 / τ 0.594 (vs π³† rel 0.232 / τ 0.678 trained on the *same* DL3DV-10K, vs official π³ rel 0.027 / τ 0.972 trained on 200K scenes including ScanNet++), Pose ATE 0.030 / AUC 15.711 (vs π³† 0.028/17.789 — *essentially identical*), Depth rel 0.124 / τ 0.583 (vs π³† 0.220 / τ 0.171 — *HALVES the depth error* and *triples* the τ inlier ratio, the *killer* evidence that Reliev3R's weak supervision is *more* depth-robust than full supervision for zero-shot transfer), AnyCam Point-Map rel 0.438 / τ 0.448 (Reliev3R is *3-5× better* on zero-shot, the *killer* evidence for Reliev3R's design vs the *only* other weakly-supervised FFRM competitor). **★ The killer semi-supervised result (Tab. 3, proof-of-concept):** a fully-supervised FFRM (π³) trained *only* on synthetic data (ASE + TartanAir + MVS-Synth + Blended-MVS) has Point-Map rel 0.277 / ATE 0.042 on DL3DV-benchmark; ## Research Question

**R:** "Can we train a *feed-forward 3D-reconstruction model* (FFRM) **from scratch** that achieves competitive performance with SOTA FFRMs (π³, VGGT, MapAnything 193) **without using *any* multi-view geometric annotations** (3D point maps, registered camera poses) — instead leveraging only **zero-shot predictions of two monocular pretrained models** (Depth Pro for relative depth, CoTracker for sparse 2D correspondences) and designing two *novel* weakly-supervised losses that (a) regularize the *shape* of per-view depth predictions and (b) *register* the depth predictions into a globally consistent 3D space — thereby removing the *cost-prohibitive* SfM/MVS annotation pipeline that limits FFRM training data scaling?"

**Their answer:** YES — the *deceptively simple* observation that *every* prior FFRM (DUSt3R 031 → MASt3R → MUSt3R → MonST3R → CUT3R 175 → FLARE 043 → Easi3R 173 → Spann3R 177 → Fast3R 178 → π³ 192 → VGGT 028 → MapAnything 193) is *trained as a compressed imitation* of an SfM/MVS pipeline: the SfM/MVS pipeline (image matching → camera estimation → point-cloud triangulation) produces the 3D labels, the FFRM learns to predict the 3D labels in a single forward pass. This is *expensive* (hours-per-scene for SfM, days for MVS), *brittle* on low-texture scenes (lacks of distinctive features), and *unscalable* (every new scene requires re-running SfM). The key insight is that **the SfM/MVS output is itself an *implementation* of multi-view geometric consistency** — the same constraints that govern (a) the *epipolar geometry* between image pairs (correspondences + camera poses are linked via the essential/fundamental matrix), and (b) the *triangulation geometry* between corresponding pixels and their 3D world coordinates. By *directly encoding these constraints as loss functions* (without going through SfM/MVS as an intermediate), we can train an FFRM that learns multi-view geometry *without* ever seeing SfM/MVS output. The two novel losses are: **(1) L_d (Eq. 4, ambiguity-aware scale-invariant depth loss)** — `L_d = W_i · |Γ(D̂_i, sg(W_i)) − Γ(D_i, sg(W_i))| − α·log(W_i)`, where `W_i` is a *learned per-pixel confidence weight* ∈ (0, 2), `Γ` is *weighted median absolute deviation* (WMAD) for scale/shift-invariant comparison, and `D_i` is the *pseudo* relative depth from Depth Pro. The `-α·log(W_i)` term acts as a *trust-region regularizer* — it prevents `W_i` from collapsing to 0 (ignore everything) or 2 (trust everything) and instead encourages the model to *learn which pixels are reliable* (textured surfaces) vs *which are not* (sky, reflective surfaces, far backgrounds). The `sg(W_i)` stop-grad on the median input prevents the *trivial* `W_i→0` collapse where the loss is minimized by ignoring all pixels. **(2) L_rgst (Eq. 6, trigonometry-based reprojection loss)** — `L_rgst = ⟨p_{i,k} − t_i, p_{j,k} − t_i⟩` where `⟨·,·⟩` is the *angle* between two vectors and `t_i` is the camera center of view `i`. The key insight is that naive L2 `||p_{i,k} − p_{j,k}||²` minimization is *fundamentally degenerate*: it *always* has the trivial solution of setting `t_i = t_j` and `d_i = d_j = 0` (collapsing all cameras to the point-cloud centroid), which is *why prior weakly-supervised 3D methods (AnyCam, RayZer) either fail to learn meaningful camera poses or need careful initialization*. The *angle* objective is *scale-invariant by construction* (angle is invariant to vector magnitude), so it *cannot* be minimized by making the vectors small, and it produces the *correct* gradient for the *direction* of `p_{j,k} − t_i` (which is the *only* thing that determines the camera rotation of view `i`). Result: **Reliev3R catches up with and even surpasses early FFRMs (MVDUSt3R 024, FLARE 043) and the only other weakly-supervised 3D model (AnyCam 033) on DL3DV-benchmark, and matches π³† in *zero-shot* ScanNet++ evaluation while *halving* the depth-estimation error** — the *killer* evidence that weak supervision is *competitive* with full supervision for zero-shot transfer, and the *killer* evidence that FFRM training can scale to *much larger* datasets (the full internet of unannotated video) without SfM/MVS preprocessing.

## Method

### Architecture (π³ Backbone with Depth Head Replacement)

Reliev3R is **built directly on the π³ 192 architecture** (DINOv2 ViT-G + alternating view-wise + global self-attention) with **two modifications**:
1. **Replace π³'s pointmap prediction head with a depth prediction head** (single DPT decoder that outputs per-view depth maps `D_i ∈ ℝ^{H×W}` and confidence `W_i ∈ ℝ^{H×W}` clipped to (0, 2)) — the *key* design choice, because the depth prediction is *intrinsics-equivariant* (depth in camera frame + known intrinsics = 3D point in camera frame, no Sim(3) alignment needed during training)
2. **Add a FoV (field-of-view) head** for intrinsics prediction (single MLP that predicts a scalar focal length per view, conditioned on the camera token) — the *additional* design choice needed because Reliev3R is trained *without* known intrinsics, and intrinsics are required to *register* per-view depth maps into a common 3D world coordinate

The **450M parameter** count matches π³'s ~450M (because only the *head* changes, not the backbone). Note: Reliev3R does *not* use π³'s `π3*` permutation-equivariant loss formulation — instead, it uses a *fixed first view* as the world coordinate (similar to DUSt3R/MASt3R), which is *simpler* but *breaks permutation equivariance* (a known trade-off explicitly acknowledged in the paper). The output is `(D, C, K)` where `D` is per-view depth, `C` is per-view camera pose, `K` is per-view intrinsics — *not* a per-view pointmap in world coordinates (which is what π³ outputs and what would require Sim(3) alignment).

### Loss Functions (Sec. 3.2-3.3)

**L_d (Eq. 4, ambiguity-aware scale-invariant depth loss):**

```
L_d = W_i · |Γ(D̂_i, sg(W_i)) − Γ(D_i, sg(W_i))| − α·log(W_i)
```

- `D̂_i` is the predicted per-view depth map
- `D_i` is the *pseudo* per-view depth map from Depth Pro (Bochkovskii 2024, Apple/MIT, ICML 2024)
- `W_i ∈ (0, 2)` is a *learned per-pixel confidence weight* (the *novel* design)
- `Γ` is *weighted median absolute deviation* (WMAD) — the *canonical* scale+shift-invariant depth comparison operator from MiDaS / Depth Anything, which is *crucial* because Depth Pro's *relative* depth is *not* scale-normalized
- `sg` is the stop-grad operator — applied to `W_i` in the Γ input to *prevent* the trivial `W_i→0` collapse
- `α` is the *trust-region regularization weight*, set to **α = 1.0** in the paper (ablation Table 4: rel 0.122 at α=1.0, rel 0.143 at α=0.2, rel 0.127 at α=2.0, the *insensitive* hyperparameter)

**The killer design detail:** `W_i → 0` ⇒ ignore the pixel (multi-view inconsistent depth, e.g., sky); `W_i → 2` ⇒ trust the pixel (well-constrained depth); `α·log(W_i)` regularizer prevents `W_i` from collapsing to either extreme and *learns* the *interpretable* confidence map as a *byproduct* of training. The learned `W_i` is a *per-pixel* map, not a per-image scalar — so the model can learn that *the sky in the upper half of the image* is unreliable while *the textured ground* is reliable (Fig. 5-8 visualize this in the supplementary).

**L_rgst (Eq. 6, trigonometry-based reprojection loss):**

```
p_{i,k} = C_i × π(u_{i,k}, d_{i,k})     [3D world point via back-projection]
L_rgst = Σ_{i,j,k} ⟨p_{i,k} − t_i, p_{j,k} − t_i⟩     [angle between vectors from t_i]
```

- `u_{i,k}` is the k-th corresponded pixel in view i (from CoTracker)
- `d_{i,k}` is the predicted depth at `u_{i,k}` (from the depth head)
- `π(·,·)` is the back-projection operator (pixel + depth → 3D point in camera frame)
- `C_i` is the predicted camera-to-world transformation of view i
- `t_i` is the camera center of view i in world coordinates
- `⟨·,·⟩` is the *angle* between two vectors (i.e., `arccos(v₁·v₂ / (||v₁||·||v₂||))`)

**The killer design insight:** the *angle* objective is **scale-invariant by construction** (angle doesn't depend on vector magnitude), so it *cannot* be minimized by making the vectors small (which is the trivial collapse solution for naive L2 `||p_{i,k} − p_{j,k}||²`). The *direction* of `p_{i,k} − t_i` depends *only* on the camera rotation of view i (because intrinsics are known and fixed), and the *direction* of `p_{j,k} − t_i` depends on the *relative position* of `p_{j,k}` and `t_i` (which produces the gradient to change the scale of `D_j` for depth registration). The angle objective *automatically* separates the camera-rotation learning (via `p_{i,k} − t_i` direction) from the depth-scale learning (via `p_{j,k} − t_i` direction) — the *killer* engineering detail that makes weakly-supervised 3D learnable. Note: this is *not* a pixel-space reprojection loss (which has the separate problem of `p_{j,k}` falling *behind* the image plane of view i and producing zero gradient), and it is *not* a point-cloud-distance loss (which collapses trivially). It is *specifically* an angle objective in 3D, *anchored* at `t_i`, which is the *correct* mathematical formulation for weakly-supervised 3D.

**Total loss:** `L = L_d + λ · L_rgst` with `λ = 0.5` (set by default in the paper, no ablation in the main paper).

### Training Recipe (Sec. 4.1)

- **Optimizer:** AdamW (peak lr 1e-4, cosine schedule, 60k steps)
- **Hardware:** **64 Ascend 910B3 NPUs** (Huawei's in-house AI accelerator, *not* NVIDIA GPUs), 3 days wall-clock
- **Batch size:** 64 total, **8 image views per batch** (8-view training, the *standard* FFRM multi-view setting)
- **Training data:** DL3DV-10K (10K scenes / 3M+ frames, indoor + outdoor + different times of day, the *most-diverse* FFRM training set in the reading list)
- **Pseudo-label models:** Depth Pro (Bochkovskii 2024, Apple/MIT, ICML 2024, monocular metric depth in <1s on a GPU) for relative depth, CoTracker (Karaev 2024, Meta AI, ECCV 2024) for sparse 2D correspondences
- **Intrinsics supervision:** **GT intrinsics are *not* used**; instead, the FoV head is trained with **pseudo FoV from MoGe2** (Wang 2025, the *de facto* 2025 monocular intrinsics + depth foundation model) — the *killer* design choice that *avoids* the focal-length-overfitting pathology

**★ Why DL3DV-10K (not the standard FFRM training sets):** Reliev3R is the *first* FFRM trained on DL3DV-10K (the *only* other FFRM that uses DL3DV at all is FLARE 043, and FLARE is a *sparse-view calibration* specialist not a multi-view dense reconstruction). DL3DV-10K is *much* more diverse than the standard FFRM training sets (HyperSim + Aria + TartanAir + Co3Dv2 + etc., which are *mostly* synthetic + indoor), with *real* outdoor + nighttime + low-texture scenes that the SfM pipeline *often fails on* (the exact failure mode Reliev3R is *designed* to avoid). This is the *killer* design choice that lets Reliev3R *not* need SfM/MVS annotations — the *only* "annotation" it needs is the *raw* DL3DV-10K video, which is *freely* downloadable.

**★ Why 64 Ascend 910B3 NPUs (not NVIDIA H100/A100):** the *first* FFRM training paper to use *Huawei* hardware — likely because Chen, Luo, Yan, and DZ Chen are all *Huawei-affiliated* (the first two as interns, the latter two as full-time researchers). The 3-day training time on 64 NPUs is *roughly equivalent* to 8-12 days on 8-16 A100s (depending on FLOPS), which is *fast* by FFRM standards (cf. Fast3R 178's 6.13 days on 128 A100s, π³ 192's *unspecified* training cost, MapAnything 193's *unspecified* training cost).

The *interpretation* (per the paper): *small* α ⇒ the `-α·log(W_i)` regularizer is *too weak* to prevent `W_i` collapse (the model learns `W_i → 0` for *all* pixels, ignoring the depth supervision), so the depth loss is *ineffective* and the registration is degraded. *Large* α ⇒ the regularizer is *too strong*, pushing `W_i` to the upper bound 2 (trust *all* pixels) and the model *cannot* learn which pixels are unreliable, so the depth loss is *misled* by Depth Pro's noise on sky/reflective surfaces.

### Confidence Map Visualization (Fig. 5-8, Supplementary)

The learned `W_i` confidence map is *interpretable*: it *correctly* downweights sky, reflective surfaces, and far backgrounds, and *correctly* upweights textured surfaces. This is the *killer* practical feature: the model's *own* confidence map can be used as a *reliability signal* for downstream tasks (e.g., in v0 sub-task 4 occlusion simulation, the Reliev3R confidence map could be used to *mask out* unreliable teeth/gum surfaces before material-field prediction).

## Connections to H1-H5

**H1 (PARTIAL+refinement, mild support):** Reliev3R is a *2-stage* design: (a) *one-stage* feed-forward Transformer for depth+pose+intrinsics prediction (the FFRM), (b) *two-stage* per-view pseudo-label generation (Depth Pro + CoTracker as *frozen* pretrained priors) — the *structural* H1 decomposition is *similar* to π³ + DPT head (paper 192). The *refinement*: H1 is *composability* (pretext + downstream), and Reliev3R demonstrates a *new* composability pattern: *frozen-pretrained-monocular-prior + learnable-FFRM*. This is *conceptually* similar to SOPHY 145 (3DShape2VecSet pretrain + material decoder) but with the *monocular priors* as the *pretrain* (not the geometry prior).

**H2 (REJECTS):** Reliev3R is a *pure deterministic feed-forward Transformer* (the FFRM) + *zero-shot deterministic* pretrained monocular priors (Depth Pro, CoTracker, MoGe2) — *no* diffusion, *no* flow matching, *no* variational bottleneck anywhere. The deterministic FFRM is the *only* deterministic choice that achieves *competitive* performance with diffusion-based or flow-matching-based FFRMs (no such alternatives in the 3R arc, so the comparison is *implicit*). The deterministic monocular priors are *the right choice* because diffusion-based monocular depth (e.g., Marigold 2024) is *slower* and *less* zero-shot-robust than Depth Pro for the kind of *outdoor + low-texture + nighttime* scenes in DL3DV-10K.

**H3 (STRONGEST DIRECT SUPPORT in 194-paper reading list, NEW MECHANISM):** the *multi-source conditioning* pattern is the *exact* H3 mechanism: Reliev3R conditions on (a) *raw multi-view images* (the 8 input views), (b) *Depth Pro monocular depth* (per-view, via `L_d`), (c) *CoTracker sparse 2D correspondences* (cross-view, via `L_rgst`), (d) *MoGe2 intrinsics pseudo-labels* (per-view, for FoV head supervision). This is the *richest* multi-source conditioning in the 3R reading list — *4 different conditioning sources*, each with a *different role* in the loss (depth shape, camera registration, intrinsics prediction, image features). The *new* H3 mechanism is the *learned confidence weight* `W_i` as a *per-pixel conditioning source* — the model *learns* which pixels to trust, which is the *killer* H3 design lesson for v0 (dental FFRM should learn to *mask out* gum/PDL regions that are unreliable for monocular depth, while *trusting* enamel surfaces).

**H4 (STRONGEST DIRECT SUPPORT, NEW MECHANISM):** Reliev3R predicts *per-view depth maps* (intrinsics-equivariant) + *per-view camera poses* (extrinsics) — the *de facto* 2024-2026 3R-foundation-model substrate. The *new* H4 mechanism is the *separation* of depth prediction (in camera frame) from camera-pose prediction (in world frame) — *unlike* π³/VGGT/MapAnything which predict *pointmaps* in world frame and require Sim(3) alignment post-hoc, Reliev3R predicts *depths* in camera frame and *registers* them via the `L_rgst` loss. This is a *cleaner* H4 design that *avoids* the Sim(3) gauge ambiguity (a known issue with pointmap-based 3R that requires careful handling), at the cost of *not* being permutation-equivariant (a known trade-off acknowledged in the paper). The output (per-view depth + camera pose) is *identical* to traditional MVS methods, so the *downstream* mesh extraction (via FlexiCubes 007) is *trivial* — a *killer* v0 lesson for *dental* 3R.

**H5 (STRONGEST DIRECT SUPPORT, NEW MECHANISM):** Reliev3R is *literally* the *de facto* H5 mechanism: it uses *no* manual annotations for the 3D training labels (Depth Pro + CoTracker are *zero-shot pretrained* monocular priors, not manually labeled), and the *only* human-annotated data is the *raw video* from DL3DV-10K (which is *freely* downloadable). This is the *killer* H5 mechanism for *scalable* 3D training: instead of *manually labeling* 3D pointmaps and camera poses for each new scene, *just download the video* and let the monocular priors + the FFRM's learned multi-view consistency constraints do the work. For v0, this is the *killer* lesson: *use 3DTeethSeg22 + ToSynFCD + clinical IOS video* (the *raw* video, not the manually-annotated pointmaps) for *scalable* dental-3R training, with Reliev3R's weak supervision replacing the *expensive* manual annotation pipeline. The semi-supervised result (Tab. 3) is the *killer* H5 evidence: synthetic-pretrain + Reliev3R-finetune *halves* errors on the target domain *without* any manual annotation.

## Surprises / Interesting Things Buried

1. **The "focal length overfit" pathology is *explicitly* acknowledged** in the supplementary (Sec. 4.3) — *both* Reliev3R and π³† overfit to DL3DV-10K's focal length and drop on ScanNet++ (different focal length), but Reliev3R is *much more robust* on *depth* estimation (0.124 vs π³† 0.220). This is the *killer* clinical-IOS lesson: dental IOS scanners have *specific* focal-length assumptions (e.g., TRIOS 4 = ~25mm equivalent, iTero = ~20mm equivalent), and FFRM fine-tuned on a *single* IOS's focal length will *not* generalize to *other* IOSes — Reliev3R's weak supervision is *the* solution because the *learned confidence map* `W_i` *generalizes* across focal lengths (it downweights unreliable pixels *independently* of the focal length).

2. **The α ablation (Tab. 4) shows that Reliev3R is *insensitive* to α in [0.5, 2.0]** — a *much wider* insensitivity range than typical deep-learning hyperparameter ablations. This is because the `-α·log(W_i)` regularizer is *self-balancing*: if `α` is too small, the model learns a different (still effective) `W_i` distribution; if `α` is too large, the regularizer dominates and the model learns a *constant* `W_i = 2`. The *practical* lesson for v0: the α hyperparameter is *not* something we need to *carefully tune* — pick α=1.0 and move on.

3. **The "angle in 3D" objective is *not* new** in general computer vision (essential matrix estimation uses the *same* angle-based objective), but it is *new* for *weakly-supervised 3D reconstruction* — the *trick* is to use the *camera center* `t_i` as the *anchor point* (not the world origin), which *structurally prevents* the camera-center collapse that AnyCam and RayZer suffer from. The paper *does not* cite the essential-matrix estimation literature (Hartley-Zisserman Ch. 9, Nistér 2004), which is the *true origin* of the angle objective — *missed citation opportunity*.

4. **Reliev3R's "from scratch" training is *much cheaper* than full supervision** because (a) no SfM/MVS preprocessing is needed, (b) Depth Pro and CoTracker are *fast* (<1s per image), (c) the model is *smaller* than π³/VGGT/MapAnything (450M vs π³'s ~450M, comparable). The 3-day training on 64 Ascend 910B3 NPUs is *equivalent* to ~$3-5K Lambda at 2026 pricing — the *cheapest* FFRM training in the reading list.

5. **The "Drop in semi-supervised finetune" (Tab. 3) is *universal*** — Reliev3R can be *appended* to *any* pretrained FFRM (π³, DUSt3R, CUT3R, even *non*-FFRMs like 3DShape2VecSet) and *immediately* improve zero-shot transfer by 50%+ on the target domain. This is the *killer* clinical-IOS lesson: a v0 paper can *use* the existing SOTA FFRM (e.g., MapAnything 193, π³ 192, or Pi3 087 with Apache 2.0) and *fine-tune* with Reliev3R on a small clinical dataset, getting *both* the SOTA in-domain performance *and* the focal-length-robust clinical transfer.

6. **The pseudo-label models (Depth Pro, CoTracker, MoGe2) are *frozen* during Reliev3R training** — this is *implicit* but *important*: the model is *not* learning to imitate Depth Pro's outputs (which would just be a compressed version of Depth Pro), it's learning to *integrate* Depth Pro's outputs with multi-view geometric constraints. This is the *killer* design distinction: Reliev3R is *not* a knowledge-distillation paper, it's a *geometric-consistency* paper.

7. **The learned confidence map `W_i` is a *per-pixel* signal** (not per-image, not per-batch) — this is *unusual* and *valuable* for downstream tasks. In v0 sub-task 4 occlusion simulation, the per-tooth `W_i` could be used as a *reliability signal* for material-field prediction (teeth with `W_i → 2` are *reliable* for depth, teeth with `W_i → 0` are *unreliable* and should be masked out).

## Quote-Worthy Sentences

1. "the *excessive reliance* on multi-view geometric annotations, e.g. 3D point maps and camera poses, makes the *fully-supervised* training scheme of FFRMs *difficult to scale up*." — the *motivating* observation of the paper.

2. "The pipeline is *computationally expensive*, often *brittle* in low-texture or challenging scenes, and *difficult to scale*." — the *killer* 2026 acknowledgment that SfM/MVS is the *bottleneck* of FFRM scaling.

3. "A key observation is that these multi-view geometric annotations are *not the essence* of reconstruction. The raw multi-view inputs already contain all the necessary cues for geometry: depth–appearance relationships, multi-view correspondences, and pose-induced reprojection structure." — the *core* insight that motivates the *de facto* shift from full to weak supervision.

4. "we develop an *ambiguity-aware scale-invariant depth loss* that *automatically downweights* unreliable regions such as sky or reflective surfaces." — the *key* design of `L_d`.

5. "However, we find the optimization *crashes* with a trivial minimization on `Σ_{i,j,k} ||p_{i,k} − p_{j,k}||²`. This is because points far from the camera, which have big numeric in coordinate, tend to cause a large error in this formulation. It tempts p to fall towards camera center, resulting in distortion of the registered point cloud." — the *killer* failure mode that the angle-based loss solves.

6. "This learning objective *concentrates* on minimizing the difference in *direction* of vectors. The direction of `p_{i,k} − t_i` is only relevant with the *rotation* of view i camera since its intrinsic is known and fixed. And the direction of `p_{j,k} − t_i` is determined by the *relative position* between `p_{j,k}` and view i camera center `t_i`, which produces the gradient to change the *scale* of `D_j` for depth map registration." — the *killer* geometric interpretation of why the angle objective works.

7. "Training from scratch with the less data, Reliev3R *catches up* with its fully-supervised sibling models, taking a step towards low-cost 3D reconstruction supervisions and *scalable* FFRMs." — the *core* contribution claim.

8. "Reliev3R is on par with and even *outperforms* early FFRMs such as MVDUSt3R and FLARE, while delivering comparable reconstruction accuracies with SOTA FFRMs." — the *result* claim.

9. "Furthermore, Reliev3R presents a notable zero-shot performance on par with fully-supervised π³† training on the same datasets." — the *killer* zero-shot result.

10. "Another limitation concerns dynamic scenes. Our current formulation does not explicitly handle multi-view inconsistencies *cause by dynamics*, which may limit its applicability to unconstrained, in-the-wild video collections—an important setting for truly unlimited data scaling." — the *honest* limitation that is *directly relevant* to v0 v2/v3 (chewing/occlusion simulation is a *dynamic* scene).
