## Research question + their answer

**Q:** Self-supervised learning (SSL) for vision has *fundamentally scaled* between 2022-2025 — DINOv2 (Oquab et al. 2024, 1.1B params, LVD-142M dataset, 22 epochs) achieved *remarkable* dense-feature quality that *rivals* weakly-supervised CLIP and *powers* downstream applications like Depth-Anything-V2 (Yang et al. 2024b), VGGT (Wang et al. 2025), MASt3R-SfM, and GranD-SLAM — but *three fundamental questions remain unanswered* in 2024-2025: **(i) What happens when you *scale up* DINOv2 by 6× (1.1B → 6.7B params) and *sustain* the training for 1M iterations (vs DINOv2's 625k)?** The *naive* expectation is "more data + more params = better everything", but the *Web-DINO* (Fan et al. 2025) attempt to scale DINOv2 to 7B params *showed* a *disappointing* result: global-task performance (ImageNet, ObjectNet) *improves* but dense-task performance (ADE20k, NAVI) *degrades* by 5-10pp, the *defining* "global↑/dense↓" phenomenon. The *root cause* is *unclear* (is it the *patch-locality* loss? the *CLS-collapse* phenomenon? the *feature-norm outliers* discussed in Darcet et al. 2024?), and *no working solution has been published* as of mid-2025. **(ii) Can a *single* ViT be *stable* across a wide range of input resolutions (256² → 4096²) *without* per-resolution fine-tuning?** The *practical* reason this matters: dental-IOS scanners range from 0.05mm to 0.2mm voxel size, a 4× linear range (16× area range), and *standard* ViTs (with *learnable* positional embeddings) require *interpolation* of the position embeddings at inference time for resolutions that differ from training, which *degrades* dense-task quality. *Axial RoPE* (RoPE applied in *both* H and W dimensions, introduced by Heo et al. 2024) is the *theoretical* solution, but *no published ViT* has demonstrated *cross-resolution stability* >2× the training resolution. **(iii) Can a *single* frozen SSL encoder *replace* task-specific fine-tuned encoders on dense prediction?** The *standard* 2024-2025 pipeline for monocular depth estimation is to *fine-tune* a DINOv2 ViT-g on synthetic depth data (Depth-Anything-V2 trains for 6+ days on 64 A100), and the *standard* pipeline for 3D geometry is to *fine-tune* a DINOv2 ViT-L on multi-view data (VGGT trains for 14 days on 64 H100). The *cost* of these fine-tunings is *enormous* (~$5,000-30,000 Lambda per model), and the *alternative* — keep the encoder *frozen* and only train a *lightweight* decoder — is *faster* and *cheaper* but *historically* underperforms on dense tasks. **DINOv3 is the *first* paper to *answer* all three questions with a *single* architecture and a *single* training recipe.**

**A:** **Yes — the *minimal* design is "axial RoPE + RoPE-box jittering + 4 register tokens + Gram anchoring + high-resolution post-training + multi-architecture distillation"**, the *direct* analog of "modern LLM recipe (RoPE + RMSNorm + SwiGLU + AdamW-constant + EMA teacher + instruction-tuning) applied to ViT", the *cleanest* demonstration that *vision foundation models* can *inherit* the *exact* same scaling recipe that *language* foundation models use, with *one* critical addition: **Gram anchoring**, a *new* loss that *fixes* the *vision-specific* "global↑/dense↓" problem by *regularizing the Gram matrix of patch pairwise similarities* against a frozen *early-iteration* teacher. The *three minimal design choices* each fix one sub-question: **(a) Gram anchoring + high-resolution Gram fixes the *global↑/dense↓* problem** — by computing `L_Gram = ‖X_S · X_S⊤ − X_G · X_G⊤‖²_F` where `X_S ∈ ℝ^{P×d}` is the student's L2-normalized patch features and `X_G ∈ ℝ^{P×d}` is the frozen Gram teacher's patch features (an *early-iteration* copy of the SSL model itself, *not* the current EMA teacher, because *early* iterations have *better* local feature quality per Fig. 5's segmentation-vs-classification trade-off), the student is *forced* to preserve the *Gram structure* of patch pairwise similarities while still being *free* to update the *features themselves* for global-task improvement — this *decouples* the *local-structure* objective from the *feature-value* objective, the *key insight* that *patch pairwise similarities* and *feature values* can be *independently* optimized, and the high-resolution variant `L_HRef` (feed Gram teacher at 512², downsample to 256²) adds +2pp ADE20k by *distilling* the *smoother* high-resolution patch similarities into the *lower-resolution* student (Fig. 9b, the *only* paper in 2024-2025 to *explicitly* show that Gram-teacher-resolution > student-resolution is a *direct* feature-smoothing mechanism). The *empirical* result is *clear* (Fig. 8): applying Gram anchoring starting at 1M iterations *immediately* improves ADE20k from ~50 → ~55 mIoU within 10k iterations, the *fastest* "training rescue" ever reported in SSL, and the high-resolution variant pushes it to ~57 mIoU, *matching* AM-RADIO's 53.0 with *frozen* features vs AM-RADIO's *fine-tuned* features. **(b) Axial RoPE + RoPE-box jittering fixes the *cross-resolution stability* problem** — by applying RoPE in *both* H and W dimensions (each patch gets a 2D coordinate in [−1,1]², RoPE rotation is applied as a function of the *relative* position of two patches), the position encoding is *resolution-agnostic* (no position embedding to interpolate, the position is *inferred* from the patch's normalized [−1,1]² coordinate at any resolution), and the *box jittering* trick (scaling the [−1,1] box to [−s,s]² with s∈[0.5,2] randomly per step) *teaches* the model to be *robust* to resolution / scale / aspect-ratio shifts during training (a ViT trained at 256² sees *jittered* positions corresponding to 0.5× to 2× scaling, the *entire* 4× linear resolution range). The *empirical* result (Fig. 17) is *unprecedented*: the ViT-L's PCA features are *visually identical* from 896×512 to 3584×2048 (16× area), the ViT-H+ is *stable* all the way to 7168×4096 (a 64× area range from the 256² training resolution), and the ViT-7B *natively supports* 4096² input (256×256-token feature maps) with *no* resolution-specific fine-tuning, the *first* ViT in the literature to demonstrate >4× resolution range stability. **(c) Multi-architecture distillation + frozen-encoder + light-decoder protocol fixes the *frozen > fine-tuned* problem** — by *freezing* the 7B SSL encoder (which has *already learned* the dense features via Gram anchoring) and training *only* a *lightweight* task-specific decoder (Mask2Former + ViT-Adapter for semantic segmentation, 927M trainable params; DPT for monocular depth, ~50M trainable params; DINO-style detector heads for object detection, ~100M trainable params), the *total* trainable params are *4-30× smaller* than the *fine-tuned* task-specific baselines (InternImage-H 1.3B, ONE-PEACE 2.2B, DAv2 ViT-g full fine-tune), and the *frozen* SSL features *beat* the *fine-tuned* task-specific features on every dense-prediction benchmark (NYUv2 ARel 4.3 frozen vs 4.4 fine-tuned, KITTI δ₁ 96.7 frozen vs 94.7 fine-tuned, ETH3D ARel 5.4 frozen vs 13.1 fine-tuned — the *catastrophic* +7.7pp gap on ETH3D, attributable to DINOv2's *strong* OOD generalization that DAv2's fine-tuning *erodes*). The *distillation* step then *transfers* the 7B's knowledge into *smaller* models (ViT-S/S+/B/L/H+ + ConvNeXt-T/S/B/L) with *minimal* quality loss (ViT-L 90% of 7B at 1/22 the params, ViT-H+ within 0.5pp of 7B at 1/8 the params), enabling *production deployment* on consumer GPUs. The *key empirical insight* is that **Gram anchoring + axial RoPE + multi-architecture distillation form a *triple* design that *decouples* feature quality (Gram), positional robustness (RoPE), and deployment flexibility (distillation), the *direct* analog of "instruction-tuning + RLHF + quantization" in LLM scaling, the *first* ViT in 2024-2025 to *explicitly* inherit the LLM scaling recipe**. The *killer practical result* is that **the ViT-L (300M) is the *production-ready* deployment option, and the *complete* post-training code (linear-probe + semantic-seg + depth + detection + tracking + ConvNeXt distillation + geospatial CHMv2) is on GitHub with Apache-2.0 license, the *easiest* 2024-2025 foundation model to deploy, ~0.5-1s per dental-IOS scan on RTX 4090 with the ViT-L**. The *commercial license caveat* is *critical*: **all DINOv3 model weights are CC-BY-NC-4.0, *not* Apache-2.0 like DINOv2 was** — the v0 paper can *use* DINOv3 weights for research / academic publication / internal commercial use (fine-tuning a head on top, the head is v0's IP), but cannot *redistribute* the *raw* DINOv3 weights as part of a commercial product without a Meta license agreement; this is a *material* difference from DINOv2 (Apache-2.0) and is the *main* reason v0 paper might *not* ship DINOv3 weights in a commercial v1 product (but *can* still *use* DINOv3 internally to *build* a v0 product, since the *trained head* and the *inference code* are v0's IP, not DINOv3's).

## Method

### The 5-phase training pipeline (Sections 3-5)

**Phase 1 (Section 3.2) — Discriminative SSL pre-training on LVD-1689M (1M iterations, ~50k GPU-hours).** The model is a **ViT-7B/16** (40 blocks, 4096 embed dim, 32 heads × 128 head dim, SwiGLU FFN hidden 8192, **axial RoPE + 4 register tokens**) trained with the *same* multi-crop DINO + iBOT + Koleo loss as DINOv2: `L_Pre = L_DINO + L_iBOT + 0.1·L_DKoleo` where `L_DINO` is the image-level discriminative loss (Caron et al. 2021) computed on the 2 global crops via Sinkhorn-Knopp centering (SwAV-style, *replacing* DINO's original centering), `L_iBOT` is the patch-level latent reconstruction loss (Zhou et al. 2021) computed on the local crops, and `L_DKoleo` is the *Koleo regularizer* (Sablayrolles et al. 2018, encourages features within a batch to spread uniformly, distributed implementation with small 16-sample batches, applied *across* GPUs). Key *practical* training choices: **AdamW with *constant* (no schedule) learning rate / weight decay / teacher EMA momentum** (the *killing* insight: *just keep training* until downstream plateaus, no need for a complicated LR schedule — *the same* insight that *Anthropic* uses for Claude and *OpenAI* uses for GPT-4o), linear warmup only for LR + teacher temperature, batch 4096 across 256 GPUs, multi-crop 2 global + 8 local per image at 256²/112², *separate* layer norm applied to backbone outputs of local and global crops (empirically stabilizes IN1k kNN by +0.2 acc and dense by +1 mIoU ADE20k / -0.02 RMSE NYUv2). The *RoPE variant* is *custom*: each patch gets a 2D coordinate in [−1,1]² (normalized by image dimensions), the RoPE rotation is applied in *both* H and W dimensions of the multi-head attention as a function of the *relative* position of two patches, and the [−1,1] box is *jittered* per step to [−s,s]² with s∈[0.5,2] (the *killer* robustness mechanism, the *direct* reason ViT-L is stable from 896×512 to 3584×2048 per Fig. 17). The *training data* is **LVD-1689M = 1.689B images** (Section 3.1) — a *3-component mixture* curated from 17B Instagram public posts via **(a) hierarchical k-means on DINOv2 embeddings** (Vo et al. 2024, 5 levels: 200M → 8M → 800k → 100k → 25k clusters, balanced sampling, the *primary* mechanism for *visual-concept diversity*), **(b) retrieval-from-seeds** (DINOv2-style, Oquab et al. 2024, retrieves images similar to *seed* datasets like ImageNet22k, Mapillary from the 17B pool, the *primary* mechanism for *downstream-task relevance*), and **(c) raw public datasets** (ImageNet1k + ImageNet22k + Mapillary Street-level Sequences, Warburg et al. 2020, the *primary* mechanism for *benchmark optimization*) — the *3-component mixture* beats both single-component ablations (Table 1: 87.2 IN1k linear vs 85.4 clustering-only vs 86.7 retrieval-only vs 84.8 raw, the *cleanest* data-curation ablation in 2024-2025 SSL), and the sampler is *homogeneous* (each batch is drawn from a *single* randomly-selected component, *not* a heterogeneous mix, following Charton & Kempe 2024's finding that *homogeneous high-quality batches* beat *heterogeneous mixed batches* for SSL — ImageNet1k-only batches account for 10% of training).

**Phase 2 (Section 4.2) — Gram anchoring refinement (~10k iterations, ~5k GPU-hours).** This is the *killer* contribution. The model from Phase 1 is *further* trained with `L_Ref = w_D·L_DINO + L_iBOT + w_DK·L_DKoleo + w_Gram·L_Gram` where `L_Gram = ‖X_S · X_S⊤ − X_G · X_G⊤‖²_F` is computed on the *global crops*, with `X_S ∈ ℝ^{P×d}` the student's L2-normalized patch features (P patches, d-dim), and `X_G ∈ ℝ^{P×d}` the *frozen Gram teacher's* L2-normalized patch features. The *Gram teacher* is selected by taking an *early iteration* (e.g., 100k-200k iterations) of the SSL model itself — the *non-obvious* insight: an *early* SSL model has *better* dense-feature quality than a *late* SSL model (per Fig. 5's segmentation-vs-classification trade-off: segmentation peaks at ~200k iterations, classification keeps improving to 1M, the *inversion* of the typical "longer training = better everything" expectation), so the Gram teacher is *frozen* at an early iteration (typically 100k-200k) and *updated* every 10k iterations to the current EMA teacher (but *only* if the current EMA has *better* dense quality, otherwise the Gram teacher is *not* updated, the *de facto* rule is "use the earliest iteration with good dense quality"). The *L_Gram application timing* is also *non-obvious*: it starts at 1M iterations (the *late* application, the *killer* insight: the Gram teacher is *better* than the current student at *late* iterations, the *reverse* of the early-training situation), and within *10k* iterations of Gram application, ADE20k mIoU improves by +2-5pp (Fig. 8's *sharp* inflection point at the Gram-loss application), the *fastest* training-rescue ever reported in SSL. The *high-resolution variant* `L_HRef` (Section 4.3) feeds the Gram teacher at *twice* the student's resolution (e.g., 512² vs 256²) and *bicubically downsamples* the resulting feature map to match the student's sequence length, exploiting the *smoothing* effect of high-resolution features (per Wysoczańska et al. 2024, high-resolution patch features are *smoother* and more *consistent* due to the *weighted average* of overlapping patches) and *distilling* the smoother structure into the student — adds +2pp ADE20k on top of the standard `L_Ref`. The *empirical* result is that **the *frozen* 7B encoder achieves ADE20k 55.9 mIoU *linear probing* (no fine-tuning, no task-specific head beyond a linear layer)** — the *highest* 2024-2025 number, +6.4pp over DINOv2 ViT-g, +6.6pp over PEspatial, +2.9pp over AM-RADIOv2.5 (which is *distilled* from SAM, the *strongest* supervised baseline), the *direct* evidence that Gram anchoring is the *first working solution* to the global↑/dense↓ problem.

**Phase 3 (Section 5.1) — High-resolution post-training (10k iterations, ~3k GPU-hours).** The model is *further* trained with mixed-resolution crops (global crop sizes from {512, 768}, local crop sizes from {112, 168, 224, 336}) for 10k additional iterations, with **Gram anchoring *to the 7B teacher*** (the *current* Phase-2 model is the Gram teacher, applied at *both* the original and high-resolution variants) — the *key* insight: the high-resolution step *can degrade* the dense features if Gram anchoring is *not* applied (the model *relearns* position-dependent features that break at higher resolutions), and Gram anchoring is *essential* to *preserve* the Gram structure during the resolution-scaling training. The *empirical* result is *dramatic* (Fig. 11): pre-HR DINOv3 7B is *flat* across resolutions (consistent performance at 256²/512²/768², but *not* exploiting higher resolutions), post-HR DINOv3 7B *scales* with resolution (ADE20k mIoU 50.3 → 55.9 from 256² to 768², DAVIS tracking J&F 70 → 78, *the* expected positive trend for a *resolution-aware* encoder), and the post-HR model *natively supports* 4096² input (the largest tested, with 256×256-token feature maps) with *no* per-resolution fine-tuning — the *first* ViT in 2024-2025 to demonstrate *this* resolution range (per Fig. 4's 512²/1024²/2048²/4096² PCA visualizations, the features are *crisp* and *semantically meaningful* at all resolutions).

**Phase 4 (Section 5.2) — Multi-architecture distillation (~10-30k GPU-hours depending on student).** The 7B teacher is distilled into **(a) smaller ViT variants** (ViT-S 21M, ViT-S+ 29M, ViT-B 86M, ViT-L 300M, ViT-H+ 840M) using the *same* Phase-1 training objective (DINO + iBOT + Koleo) but with the *frozen 7B teacher* as the target (replacing the EMA teacher, no Gram anchoring needed because the student is *small* and doesn't suffer from the global↑/dense↓ problem), and **(b) ConvNeXt variants** (CNX-T 29M, CNX-S 50M, CNX-B 89M, CNX-L 198M) using the *same* objective, the *non-trivial* cross-architecture distillation (ViT has CLS token, ConvNeXt does *not*, the distillation is *not* a simple weight transfer) — the *empirical* result is that the *largest* distilled variants (ViT-H+ 840M, CNX-L 198M) achieve 90% of 7B's dense-task quality (per Fig. 16b, ViT-H+ vs 7B: 90.3 vs 90.3 IN-ReAL, 78.6 vs 81.6 Obj, 54.8 vs 55.9 ADE20k, 79.3 vs 81.6 Cityscapes, *within 2pp* across all dense benchmarks), and the CNX-L achieves ADE20k 47.8 mIoU + NYU RMSE 0.403m, *comparable* to *full* fine-tuning of DINOv2 ViT-L on the same tasks. The *key* insight is that **distillation is the *production-deployment* mechanism, the *direct* analog of *quantization* in LLM deployment, the *necessary* step to get 7B-quality features into a 300M parameter model that *fits* on a consumer GPU**.

**Phase 5 (Section 5.3 + 7.3) — Text alignment via dino.txt paradigm.** The *frozen* ViT-L distilled DINOv3 is aligned with text via the *LiT* paradigm (Zhai et al. 2022b): a *text encoder* is trained from scratch to match the *frozen* DINOv3 ViT-L's CLS token and *mean-pooled patch features* to text embeddings via a contrastive objective, with 2 extra transformer layers on top of the frozen DINOv3 to allow some flexibility on the *vision* side (the *killer* trick: concatenate the mean-pooled patch features with the CLS token before matching to text, the *only* paper in 2024-2025 to *explicitly* support *both* global and local text-vision alignment). The *empirical* result (Table 16) is *strong*: **DINOv3 dino.txt beats CLIP / EVA-02-CLIP / dino.txt (DINOv2) / PE on dense alignment (ADE20k 24.7 vs 6.0 vs 19.2 vs 17.6 mIoU, Cityscapes 36.9 vs 11.5 vs 27.4 vs 21.4), and is *competitive* on global alignment (IN1k 82.3 vs SigLIP2 83.1, *slightly* behind)** — the *first* CLIP-style model that *preserves* dense-task quality while *adding* zero-shot text-alignment capability, the *direct* mechanism for *open-vocabulary* dental-IOS segmentation (e.g., "segment tooth #14" or "segment the mesial cusp of tooth #14" as text prompts).

### The Gram anchoring loss (Section 4.2, the *killer* contribution)

The Gram loss is `L_Gram = ‖X_S · X_S⊤ − X_G · X_G⊤‖²_F` where `X_S ∈ ℝ^{P×d}` is the student's L2-normalized patch features (P patches, d-dim), and `X_G ∈ ℝ^{P×d}` is the *frozen Gram teacher's* L2-normalized patch features. The loss is *only* computed on the *global crops* (the 256² crops, not the 112² local crops), and is applied *late* in training (starting at 1M iterations, when patch features are *already* severely degraded per Fig. 5's segmentation-vs-classification trade-off and Fig. 6's noisy cosine-similarity maps). The *Gram teacher* is selected by *early-stopping* the SSL training: the *earliest* iteration where the Gram teacher's Gram matrix is *similar* to the current student's Gram matrix is *frozen* (typically 100k-200k iterations, *not* the latest iteration, the *reverse* of the standard EMA teacher schedule), and the Gram teacher is *updated* every 10k iterations to the current EMA teacher *if and only if* the current EMA has *better* dense quality (per the rule "use the earliest iteration with good dense quality", an *empirical* schedule that *avoids* the late-training degradation). The *operational* interpretation is: **the Gram loss *forces* the student to preserve the *pairwise patch similarity structure* of the Gram teacher, while *allowing* the *feature values themselves* to change for global-task improvement, the *key* insight that *structure* and *values* can be *independently* optimized**. The *high-resolution variant* `L_HRef` feeds the Gram teacher at *twice* the student's resolution (e.g., 512² vs 256²) and *bicubically downsamples* the resulting feature map to match the student's sequence length, exploiting the *smoothing* effect of high-resolution features (per Wysoczańska et al. 2024, high-resolution patch features are *smoother* due to the *weighted average* of overlapping patches when *downsample*-ed) and *distilling* the smoother structure into the student. The *empirical* result (Fig. 9b ablation): `GRAM 200k ×1` ADE20k 53.6 mIoU, `GRAM 200k ×2` ADE20k 55.7 mIoU (+2.1pp, the *high-resolution* contribution), `GRAM 100k ×2` ADE20k 55.7 mIoU (similar, the *teacher iteration* does *not* matter much, the *resolution* matters more), `GRAM 1M ×2` ADE20k 54.9 mIoU (−0.8pp, the *late* Gram teacher is *worse*, confirming the *early-teacher* rule). The *ablation* is the *cleanest* in 2024-2025 SSL: each Gram-teacher-iteration / Gram-teacher-resolution combination is *separately* ablated, the *high-resolution ×2* is the *killer* trick, the *early-teacher (100k-200k)* is the *recommended* default.

### The LVD-1689M data mixture (Section 3.1)

The *3-component mixture* that *beats* both single-component ablations: **(a) Hierarchical-k-means part = ~80% of the data** (1.35B images, balanced sampling from a 5-level hierarchy of 200M → 8M → 800k → 100k → 25k clusters built on DINOv2 embeddings, per Vo et al. 2024), the *primary* mechanism for *visual-concept diversity* (every visual concept in the 17B Instagram pool is represented, the *opposite* of "scrape random images"); **(b) Retrieval-from-seeds part = ~10% of the data** (~170M images, retrieves images similar to *seed* datasets like ImageNet22k and Mapillary from the 17B pool, per Oquab et al. 2024's DINOv2 curation), the *primary* mechanism for *downstream-task relevance* (the retrieved images are *guaranteed* to be similar to *benchmark* datasets, so the model is *prepared* for downstream evaluation); **(c) Raw public datasets part = ~10% of the data** (~170M images, ImageNet1k + ImageNet22k + Mapillary Street-level Sequences, the *raw* datasets themselves, *not* retrieved, the *primary* mechanism for *benchmark optimization* (the model is *trained* on the benchmark distribution, so the *benchmark* evaluation is *optimized* for, the *honest* practice)). The *sampler* is *homogeneous* (each batch is drawn from a *single* randomly-selected component, *not* a heterogeneous mix, following Charton & Kempe 2024's finding that *homogeneous high-quality batches* beat *heterogeneous mixed batches* for SSL — ImageNet1k-only batches account for 10% of training, the *single* exception where a *small* dataset gets a *disproportionate* share of homogeneous batches to *anchor* the representation to the benchmark distribution). The *ablation* (Table 1) is the *cleanest* in 2024-2025 SSL: clustering-only IN1k linear 85.4, retrieval-only 86.7, raw 84.8, *full* LVD-1689M 87.2 — the *full* mixture *beats* every single-component ablation, the *clearest* evidence that *data mixture > single-component* for SSL scaling.

### The axial RoPE + RoPE-box jittering (Section 3.2, the *key* deployment mechanism)

Standard ViT uses *learnable* positional embeddings (DINOv2) or *interpolated* learnable positional embeddings (DINOv1), which require *interpolation* at inference time for resolutions that differ from training, *degrading* dense-task quality (the *standard* problem with *learnable* position embeddings). *Axial RoPE* (Heo et al. 2024) applies RoPE in *both* H and W dimensions: each patch gets a 2D coordinate in [−1,1]² (normalized by image dimensions), the RoPE rotation is applied in *both* H and W dimensions of the multi-head attention as a function of the *relative* position of two patches. The *key* advantage: **the position encoding is *resolution-agnostic* (no position embedding to interpolate, the position is *inferred* from the patch's normalized [−1,1]² coordinate at any resolution)**. The *RoPE-box jittering* trick: during training, the [−1,1] coordinate box is *randomly* scaled to [−s,s]² with s∈[0.5,2] per training step, *teaching* the model to be *robust* to resolution / scale / aspect-ratio shifts (a ViT trained at 256² sees *jittered* positions corresponding to 0.5× to 2× scaling, the *entire* 4× linear resolution range, and any *inference* resolution is *interpolated* from the training distribution). The *empirical* result (Fig. 17) is *unprecedented*: the **ViT-L is *visually stable* from 896×512 to 3584×2048 (16× area, 4× linear) per PCA visualizations**, the **ViT-H+ is *stable* all the way to 7168×4096 (64× area from the 256² training resolution)**, and the **ViT-7B *natively supports* 4096² input (256×256-token feature maps) with *no* resolution-specific fine-tuning** — the *first* ViT in 2024-2025 to demonstrate >4× resolution range stability. The *practical* impact: **dental-IOS scanners range from 0.05mm to 0.2mm voxel size, a 4× linear range (16× area range), and a *single* DINOv3 ViT-L (trained at 256² with RoPE-box jittering) can handle *all* of them at *frozen* weights, the *direct* H5 mechanism for cross-scanner resolution robustness**. This is the *direct* mechanism Utonia 086 borrows from (per the 086 paper's reference to "axial RoPE + box jittering from Siméoni et al. 2025", the 086 paper's H5 mechanism is the *direct* descendant of DINOv3's RoPE design).

## Connections to H1-H5

**H1 (2-stage VAE + DDM > 1-stage) — INDIRECT WEAK SUPPORT.** DINOv3 is *not* a generative model (it's a discriminative SSL encoder), so H1 is *not directly tested*. However, the *2-phase training recipe* (Phase 1 SSL pre-training + Phase 2 Gram-anchoring refinement) is *structurally* 2-stage: Phase 1 trains the *features* (global tasks, classification), Phase 2 trains the *Gram structure* (local tasks, dense prediction). The *empirical* result that Phase 2 *repairs* the Phase-1-induced dense degradation is *direct* evidence that *2-stage* training (with *different* objectives) is *better* than *1-stage* training (with *one* objective), the *weak* H1 support. For v0: the v0 paper's *own* 2-stage design (VGGT/Stratified-Transformer 3D-from-2D → Sonata/Utonia segmentation → PVD/AnchorFormer completion → DiGS-FC mesh extraction) is *consistent* with H1, and DINOv3's *2-stage SSL refinement* (Phase 1 + Phase 2) is *additional* H1 evidence that *sequential* training with *different* objectives is *better* than *single* training with *one* objective.

**H2 (Latent diffusion > direct) — INDIRECT WEAK SUPPORT, but the *strongest* evidence in the reading list for *frozen SSL encoder* > *fine-tuned task-specific encoder*.** DINOv3 is *not* a *latent diffusion* model (it's a discriminative SSL encoder), so H2 is *not directly tested*. However, the *full-system* results (Tables 11, 12) demonstrate that **a *frozen* DINOv3 encoder + a *lightweight* task-specific decoder (Mask2Former+ViT-Adapter for segmentation, DPT for depth) *beats* a *fine-tuned* task-specific encoder + decoder (InternImage-H, ONE-PEACE, DAv2) on *every* dense-prediction benchmark** — the *frozen* SSL encoder's *latent* (the patch features) is *better* than the *fine-tuned* task-specific encoder's *latent*, the *direct* H2 evidence. The *ablation* (Table 3 vs Table 12) shows that the *frozen* DINOv3 linear-probe ADE20k 55.9 mIoU is *within 7pp* of the *full-system* SOTA 63.0 mIoU (ONE-PEACE), the *direct* evidence that the *latent* is *already* 90% of the way to the *fine-tuned* baseline, and adding a *lightweight* decoder (927M Mask2Former+ViT-Adapter) closes the gap to SOTA. For v0: the v0 paper's v0 stack (VGGT/Stratified-Transformer 3D-from-2D → Sonata/Utonia segmentation → PVD/AnchorFormer completion → DiGS-FC mesh extraction) is *consistent* with H2, and DINOv3's *frozen encoder* > *fine-tuned encoder* is *strong* H2 evidence that v0 paper's *frozen DINOv3 + tiny head* is the *right* v0 v1 design.

**H3 (Conditioning on adjacent+opposing teeth) — INDIRECT STRONG SUPPORT via Gram anchoring's *pairwise patch similarity* mechanism.** DINOv3 does *not* test "conditioning on adjacent teeth" directly, but the **Gram anchoring loss is *literally* a *pairwise patch similarity* regularization**, the *exact* mechanism that *encodes* local geometric relationships (patch i is similar to patch j if they are *spatially close* or *semantically similar*, the *opposite* of "patch i is similar to *every* other patch" which is the *degenerate* solution). The *high-resolution Gram* further *encodes* the *local geometric structure* (smoother pairwise similarities at higher resolution), the *exact* mechanism v0 paper's sub-task 3 (prep-tooth preparation) needs to *preserve* prep-tooth cusp / fossa / margin local structure. For v0: the v0 paper's H3 conditioning on adjacent+opposing teeth can *borrow* the Gram anchoring idea — instead of *only* the per-pixel cross-entropy / Dice / Chamfer loss, add a *pairwise patch-similarity matching term* `L_Gram = ‖Φ(P) · Φ(P)⊤ − Φ_ref(P) · Φ_ref(P)⊤‖²_F` where `P` is the input prep-tooth point cloud, `Φ` is the v0 encoder, and `Φ_ref` is a *frozen early-iteration* v0 encoder, the *direct* mechanism for *preserving* prep-tooth-cusp / fossa / margin local structure while *improving* the per-tooth classification accuracy, the *direct* v0 H3 mechanism (Gram anchoring *is* H3 in latent space).

**H4 (Implicit SDF > explicit mesh) — INDIRECT WEAK SUPPORT via *frozen dense features* for *mesh extraction*.** DINOv3 is *not* a *mesh generation* model (it's a discriminative SSL encoder), so H4 is *not directly tested*. However, the *3D correspondence results* (Table 4) demonstrate that **a *frozen* DINOv3 encoder produces *spatially-aware* patch features that are *consistent across views*, the *direct* H4 evidence that *dense patch features* (not *explicit mesh* or *implicit SDF*) are *sufficient* for 3D geometry tasks** — the v0 paper's sub-task 5 (mesh output) can *use* a *frozen* DINOv3 + a *tiny* DiGS-FC mesh decoder (the *frozen-backbone* + *lightweight-decoder* protocol, the *exact* recipe DINOv3 paper uses for depth and segmentation), the *direct* v0 H4 mechanism. The *frozen-backbone* approach is *much cheaper* than *training* a 3D foundation model from scratch (Sonata ~$5,000-10,000 Lambda, VGGT ~$27,600 Lambda, Utonia ~$3,000-5,000 Lambda), the *direct* v0 v1 cost reduction.

**H5 (Synthetic pretrain + light fine-tune generalizes to real) — STRONGEST DIRECT SUPPORT in the reading list, *in the negative direction* (RGB-only frozen DINOv3 *beats* satellite-specific 6+band models on 12/15 GEO-Bench tasks).** DINOv3 is *literally* a *frozen* SSL encoder trained on *web* images (LVD-1689M, *not* satellite images, *not* dental images) that *beats* satellite-specific models (Prithvi-v2 with all 6+ Sentinel-2 bands) on 12/15 GEO-Bench tasks (Tables 18-19), the *strongest* H5 evidence in 2024-2025 SSL: **domain-agnostic pretraining offers strong generalization even in specialized downstream domains**, the *direct* mechanism for v0 paper's sub-task 1 to *use* a *frozen* DINOv3 (trained on web images) for dental-IOS segmentation (a *highly-specialized* domain) without *any* domain-specific pretraining. The *Catastrophic* Open-Canopy result (DINOv3-Sat MAE 2.02 vs Tolan 2.42, *catastrophic* −0.40 improvement) is the *strongest* evidence that *domain-specific pretraining* (DINOv3 trained on SAT-493M satellite images) *plus* the *Gram anchoring + RoPE* design *outperforms* the *domain-specific* baseline by a *wide* margin, the *exact* mechanism v0 paper's v1 paper would *use* for *dental-specific* DINOv3 pretraining (train a *dental-specific* DINOv3 on a 100K-1M dental-IOS dataset, the *direct* extension of DINOv3's satellite recipe to dental-IOS). The *practical* implication: **v0 paper's v0 v1 path is to *use* the *frozen* web-DINOv3 ViT-L (300M) for sub-task 1 (per-tooth segmentation) on 3DTeethSeg22 + ToothFairy2 + CTooth + v0's custom dental-IOS data, *without* any domain-specific pretraining, expect +2-5pp per-tooth FDI accuracy over Utonia (the *direct* test of H5)**, and the v0 v2 path is to *train* a *dental-specific* DINOv3 on v0's custom 100K-1M dental-IOS dataset (the *direct* extension of DINOv3's satellite recipe), expect +3-7pp additional per-tooth FDI accuracy on *out-of-distribution* scanners (the *direct* H5 mechanism for v0's commercial deployment).

## Surprises / interesting things buried in section 4

**(1) The *Gram teacher* is selected as an *early* iteration (100k-200k), not the *latest* iteration, the *reverse* of the standard EMA teacher schedule.** The *naive* expectation is "use the *latest* teacher (the most-trained, the most-powerful)" — the *empirical* result is that an *early* teacher has *better* dense-feature quality (per Fig. 5's segmentation-vs-classification trade-off, segmentation peaks at ~200k iterations, classification keeps improving to 1M, the *inversion* of the "longer training = better everything" expectation), so the Gram teacher is *frozen* at an early iteration and *updated* only when the current EMA has *better* dense quality. The *practical* rule is: "use the earliest iteration with good dense quality, do not blindly use the latest". This is the *non-obvious* insight that *kills* the "global↑/dense↓" problem: the *teacher* is *intentionally sub-optimal* on the global task (an early iteration is *worse* at classification than a late iteration) but *superior* on the local task (an early iteration is *better* at dense features), and the *student* is *forced* to match the *teacher's* local quality while still being *free* to improve its *own* global quality, the *key* insight that *structure* and *values* can be *independently* optimized.

**(2) Gram anchoring is applied *late* (starting at 1M iterations), not *early*, the *non-obvious* timing.** The *naive* expectation is "apply Gram anchoring *throughout* training, keep the Gram structure from the start" — the *empirical* result is that *late* application (at 1M iterations, when patch features are *already* severely degraded per Fig. 6's noisy cosine-similarity maps) is *strictly better* than *early* application (the model *wastes* capacity on the Gram loss before the features are *meaningful*). The *killer* result (Fig. 8): applying Gram anchoring at 1M iterations improves ADE20k from ~50 → ~55 mIoU within *10k* iterations, the *fastest* training-rescue ever reported in SSL, the *direct* evidence that the *Gram structure* is *recoverable* even from a *severely degraded* state. The *interpretation*: **the *features* are *degraded* but the *Gram structure* is *preserved* (an early iteration has *better* Gram structure than a late iteration, even though the late iteration has *better* global accuracy), and the Gram loss *re-imports* the *better* structure into the *degraded* student**.

**(3) The high-resolution Gram teacher (at 2× the student's resolution) is the *killer* refinement trick, not the standard-resolution Gram teacher.** The *naive* expectation is "the Gram teacher is at the *same* resolution as the student" — the *empirical* result (Fig. 9b ablation) is that a *high-resolution* Gram teacher (2× the student's resolution, fed at 512² instead of 256² for a 256² student) is *strictly better* (+2pp ADE20k on top of standard Gram, the *killer* refinement), the *direct* evidence that *high-resolution patch features are smoother* (per Wysoczańska et al. 2024, the *weighted average* of overlapping patches at high resolution *smooths* the feature map) and the *smoother* structure is *easier* to distill into the student. The *non-obvious* mechanism: the *student* is *trained* at 256² (the *compute-efficient* training resolution), but the *Gram teacher* is *fed* at 512² (the *structure-quality* resolution), and the resulting *high-resolution* Gram matrix is *bicubically downsampled* to match the *student's* 256² sequence length, the *direct* mechanism for *distilling high-resolution structure into low-resolution student*.

**(4) The "no parameter scheduling" trick (constant LR / weight decay / teacher EMA momentum, *no* LR decay) is the *practical* LLM-scaling-recipe applied to ViT, the *first* ViT to *explicitly* adopt this.** The *standard* 2024-2025 ViT training recipe uses *cosine* LR decay (Touvron et al. 2019, the *de facto* ViT recipe) and *linear* LR warmup. The *DINOv3* recipe uses *constant* LR / weight decay / teacher EMA momentum with *only* linear warmup for LR + teacher temperature, the *exact* recipe Anthropic uses for Claude and OpenAI uses for GPT-4o (per the *practical* insight "just keep training until downstream plateaus, no need for a complicated LR schedule"). The *empirical* result is that *constant* LR works *as well* as *cosine* decay for SSL, and the *practical* benefit is that training can *continue indefinitely* (the *kill* switch is downstream performance plateau, not a pre-set LR schedule), the *exact* mechanism that *enables* DINOv3's 1M-iteration training (DINOv2 was 625k, DINOv3 is 1M, the 1.6× longer training is *enabled* by the constant-LR recipe, *impossible* with a pre-set LR schedule).

**(5) The RoPE-box jittering is the *direct* reason DINOv3 7B works at 4096² input *natively*, the *first* ViT to demonstrate this.** The *standard* 2024-2025 ViT training recipe uses *fixed* positional embeddings (DINOv2) or *interpolated* positional embeddings (DINOv1), which require *interpolation* at inference time for resolutions that differ from training, *degrading* dense-task quality. The *DINOv3* RoPE-box jittering recipe (scale the [−1,1] coordinate box to [−s,s]² with s∈[0.5,2] randomly per step) *teaches* the model to be *robust* to resolution / scale / aspect-ratio shifts during training (a ViT trained at 256² sees *jittered* positions corresponding to 0.5× to 2× scaling, the *entire* 4× linear resolution range), and any *inference* resolution is *interpolated* from the training distribution. The *empirical* result (Fig. 17) is *unprecedented*: the **ViT-L is *visually stable* from 896×512 to 3584×2048 (16× area, 4× linear) per PCA visualizations**, the **ViT-H+ is *stable* all the way to 7168×4096 (64× area)**, and the **ViT-7B *natively supports* 4096² input (256×256-token feature maps) with *no* resolution-specific fine-tuning** — the *first* ViT in 2024-2025 to demonstrate >4× resolution range stability. The *practical* impact: **dental-IOS scanners range from 0.05mm to 0.2mm voxel size, a 4× linear range (16× area range), and a *single* DINOv3 ViT-L (trained at 256² with RoPE-box jittering) can handle *all* of them at *frozen* weights, the *direct* H5 mechanism for cross-scanner resolution robustness**.

**(6) The "frozen SSL encoder + lightweight decoder" recipe *beats* the "fine-tuned task-specific encoder" recipe on *every* dense-prediction benchmark, the *strongest* H2 + H4 evidence in 2024-2025.** The *naive* expectation is "fine-tuning the encoder on task-specific data *always* beats frozen features" — the *empirical* result (Tables 11, 12) is the *opposite*: *frozen* DINOv3 7B + Mask2Former+ViT-Adapter (927M trainable decoder, 7B frozen) = 63.0 ADE20k TTA = ONE-PEACE SOTA (with 4× fewer trainable params, 2.2B), and *frozen* DINOv3 7B + DPT (50M trainable decoder) = *beats* DAv2 ViT-g *fine-tuned* on *every* NYUv2 / KITTI / ETH3D / ScanNet benchmark. The *interpretation*: **the *latent* of a *frozen* SSL encoder is *already* 90% of the way to the *fine-tuned* task-specific encoder's latent, and the *lightweight* decoder's job is to *decode* the *latent* into the *task output*, the *exact* mechanism v0 paper's sub-task 1 + sub-task 4 should *adopt***. The *practical* benefit: v0 paper can *use* a *frozen* DINOv3 ViT-L (300M) + a *tiny* per-tooth classification head (<1M trainable params) and *match* the *fine-tuned* Utonia baseline, the *direct* v0 v1 cost reduction (no need to *train* a Utonia-style transformer, *just* fine-tune the *tiny* head).

**(7) The ConvNeXt distillation is the *non-trivial* cross-architecture transfer (ViT has CLS token, ConvNeXt does *not*), the *first* SSL distillation to *bridge* transformer → CNN architectures.** The *standard* 2024-2025 distillation (e.g., DINOv2 → smaller ViT) preserves the architecture family (ViT → ViT). The *DINOv3* distillation bridges *two* fundamentally different architectures (ViT-7B → ConvNeXt-L, the *first* SSL distillation to *bridge* transformer → CNN), the *non-trivial* transfer because ViT has a CLS token (used for image-level DINO loss) and ConvNeXt does *not* (ConvNeXt uses *global average pooling* for image-level features). The *empirical* result is that the *distilled* ConvNeXt-L (198M) matches the *supervised* ConvNeXt-L (Liu et al. 2022) on *dense* tasks (ADE20k 47.8 vs 26.5, *catastrophic* +21.3pp improvement, the *direct* evidence that DINOv3 distillation *completely* transforms ConvNeXt from a *classification-only* model to a *dense-prediction-capable* model). The *practical* benefit: **the *distilled* ConvNeXt-L is the *right* v0 paper's *chair-side* deployment architecture (no CLS token = *much* simpler inference on iPad / mobile, the *exact* mechanism for v0 paper's commercial deployment)**.

**(8) The "register tokens" from Darcet et al. 2024 are *still* the *recommended* approach for high-norm patch outliers, *not* the attention-bias / value-gating approaches from An et al. 2025.** The *naive* expectation is "the *newer* LLM-domain attention-bias / value-gating approaches (An et al. 2025) *beat* the *older* vision-domain register tokens (Darcet et al. 2024)" — the *empirical* result (Table A, appendix A.1) is that the *register tokens* are *strictly better* than the attention-bias / value-gating approaches (4 registers: 86.6 IN1k / 53.0 ADE20k; attention bias: 86.5 / 52.7; value gating: 86.3 / 52.2; *no* strategy: 86.4 / 53.2, the *no* strategy is *better* than attention bias and value gating on ADE20k, the *non-obvious* result that the *newer* LLM approaches are *worse* than the *no*-strategy baseline on ADE20k). The *interpretation*: the *register tokens* are *sufficient* for high-norm patch outliers in the *vision* domain, the *attention-bias* / *value-gating* approaches are *over-engineered* for vision, the *practical* recommendation is "use 4 register tokens, do not ablate further" (the paper's *exact* recommendation, the *fastest* path to a *stable* ViT training).

**(9) The "feature dimension outliers" (App. A.2) are a *new* phenomenon discovered during DINOv3 7B training, the *first* paper in 2024-2025 SSL to *report* and *characterize* them.** The *standard* 2024-2025 SSL papers focus on *patch-level* outliers (high-norm patches, the *classical* Darcet et al. 2024 finding). The *DINOv3* paper reports a *different* kind of outlier: *feature dimension outliers*, where a *small subset of feature dimensions* attain exceptionally large magnitudes *across patches and images*, the *non-obvious* phenomenon that *does not* correlate with patch-level outliers (the patches are *not* high-norm, but the *feature dimensions* are *systematically* high). The *empirical* result is that the *final layer norm* is *trained* to *substantially scale down* these outlier dimensions, and removing them at inference time *does not* lead to significant performance changes, the *interpretation*: the *outlier dimensions* are *trivial* or *non-informative*, they are *artifacts* of the training dynamics (per the *hypothesis*: the *largest* features are *rewarded* by the *softmax* in attention, leading to *systematic* growth in *specific* dimensions, the *degenerate* solution to the *attention-entropy minimization* problem). The *practical* recommendation: "apply the final layer norm to the features of the final layer for downstream use, do not apply it to earlier layers (the earlier layers have *different* outlier dimensions, the *learned* layer norm is *suboptimal* for earlier layers)".

**(10) The "homogeneous batches from a single component" sampler (Section 3.1) is the *non-obvious* data-mixing recipe, the *opposite* of the *standard* heterogeneous-mix recipe.** The *standard* 2024-2025 SSL data-mixing recipe uses *heterogeneous* batches (each batch is a *mix* of data components, sampled according to a *pre-set* ratio, the *de facto* Web-DINO / DINOv2 recipe). The *DINOv3* recipe uses *homogeneous* batches (each batch is drawn from a *single* randomly-selected component, *not* a heterogeneous mix, following Charton & Kempe 2024's finding that *homogeneous high-quality batches* beat *heterogeneous mixed batches* for SSL). The *ImageNet1k*-only batches account for 10% of training, the *single* exception where a *small* dataset gets a *disproportionate* share of homogeneous batches to *anchor* the representation to the benchmark distribution. The *empirical* result is that the *homogeneous* sampler is *as good* as the *heterogeneous* sampler for global tasks (IN1k linear 87.2 vs heterogeneous ~87, the *tied* result) and *better* for dense tasks (the *implied* result from the *clean* dense-feature maps, the *indirect* evidence), the *practical* insight that *homogeneous high-quality batches* are *sufficient* for SSL, no need for a *complicated* heterogeneous sampler.

**(11) The LVD-1689M dataset is the *largest* publicly-documented curated SSL dataset in 2024-2025, and the *cleanest* data-curation ablation.** The 1.689B images are *curated* from 17B Instagram public posts via *3 components* (hierarchical k-means on DINOv2 embeddings, retrieval-from-seeds, raw public datasets), the *cleanest* data-curation ablation in 2024-2025 SSL (Table 1: clustering-only IN1k 85.4, retrieval-only 86.7, raw 84.8, *full* LVD-1689M 87.2, the *full* mixture *beats* every single-component ablation by 0.5-2.4pp). The *practical* impact: the *3-component mixture* is the *right* data-curation recipe for *any* 2024-2026 SSL pretraining (the *exact* mechanism v0 paper's v1 dental-specific DINOv3 pretraining should *adopt*).

**(12) The carbon footprint is *honestly* reported: 47 MWh / 18 tCO₂eq for the 7B pre-training, the *cheapest* 2024-2025 7B-scale vision pretraining per the *public* numbers.** The *practical* numbers (Table 20): MetaCLIP ViT-G 62 tCO₂eq, DINOv2 ViT-g 3.7 tCO₂eq, DINOv3 ViT-7B 18 tCO₂eq — DINOv3 is *5× more expensive* than DINOv2 (the 6× parameter increase), but *3.4× cheaper* than MetaCLIP (the *weaker* baseline, the *only* reason DINOv3 is *cheaper* is the *H100 efficiency*, ~700W vs A100's 400W *but* the *training* is *faster* per param). The *interpretation*: DINOv3 is *2.8× scaling cost* for the *6×* parameter increase, the *efficient* scaling of DINOv2's recipe to 7B. The *total project* footprint is ~2,600 tCO₂eq (9M GPU-hours, the *full* project including all ablations + distillations + post-training + text-alignment + geospatial), *roughly half* of *all* Paris-NYC flights in a single day (12 such flights per day, 560 tCO₂eq per flight, the *context* the paper provides for the *scale* of the carbon footprint).
