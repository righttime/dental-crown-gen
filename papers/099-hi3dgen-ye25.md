## Research question + answer

**RQ:** *How can we generate high-fidelity 3D geometry from a single RGB image where (a) the *fine-grained geometric details* (edges, cavities, sharp boundaries) are preserved in the 3D output (the *primary* failure mode of all 2024-2024 3D-gen models), (b) the training *generalizes* across the synthetic-to-real domain gap (the *secondary* failure mode of all 2024-2024 3D-gen models), (c) the inference is *fast* enough for practical use (sub-minute, not 5-15 min), and (d) the model is *simple* enough to be fine-tuned by a *small* lab (not a Meta + ByteDance + Tencent industrial-scale effort)?*

**Answer:** *Bridge the 2D-to-3D mapping with a 2.5D intermediate representation (normal map), which encodes the *surface orientation* information that is *lost* in RGB (due to lighting, shading, texture) and *retains* the *spatial* information that is *lost* in 3D-latent (due to compression). The Hi3DGen framework is a 2-stage pipeline: **(Stage 1) NiRNE** maps RGB image → normal map with a *dual-stream* regression network (one stream processes the original image for low-frequency generalizability, the other processes a noise-injected version for high-frequency sharpness; the two streams' latent features are concatenated in a ControlNet-style manner and fed to the decoder for the final normal prediction; the noise injection is the *radical* design choice that *borrows* the *sharpness* mechanism from diffusion while *keeping* the *stability* of regression; the *domain-specific training* (real-data first, then synthetic-only fine-tune the noisy stream with the clean stream frozen) is the *key* generalization mechanism). **(Stage 2) NoRLD** maps normal map → 3D geometry with a *normal-regularized latent diffusion* (the normal map is used as the *conditioning* signal for the latent diffusion, and an *online* normal-rendering loss is added to the standard flow-matching loss during training, providing *explicit* 3D normal-map supervision in the *highly-abstract* latent space, the *first* 2025 3D-gen paper to do so). **(Support) DetailVerse** is a *synthesized* 700K-mesh dataset (text → Flux.1-Dev image → Trellis 3D, with rigorous quality filtering via LLaMA-3 + OrientAnything + manual evaluation) that provides the *high-detail* training data that *Objaverse-1.0 / XL* lack (Objaverse is *too simple*, with mean 1,520 / 1,119 sharp edges per model vs DetailVerse's 45,773, a *30×* detail gap). The 2-stage decoupling is *exact* the H1 hypothesis template (image→normal is the *first* stage, normal→3D is the *second* stage, the *intermediate* representation is the *novel* contribution), the normal-rendering loss is the *exact* H4 mechanism (implicit supervision of the *output* 3D normal map = preservation of *fine geometric details*), the NiRNE noise-injection + dual-stream + domain-specific training is the *exact* H2 + H3 mechanism (latent-space *frequency-decoupling* conditioning), and the DetailVerse data is the *exact* H5 mechanism (text-synthesized *high-detail* data = robust generalization across the synthetic-to-real domain gap).*

## Method

### Architecture overview (2-stage image→normal→3D pipeline)

**Hi3DGen is a 2-stage pipeline that *decouples* the image→normal and normal→3D mappings, with the normal map as the *explicit* intermediate representation:**

**Stage 1: NiRNE (Noise-injected Regressive Normal Estimation)**

*Reimplements GenPercept (Xu et al. 2024a) with StableDiffusion 2.1 backbone + dual-stream architecture:*

1. **Clean stream:** VAE encode the input image → SD2.1 UNet encoder (no modification, no noise injection) → latent features. This stream captures the *low-frequency* generalizable information (overall shape, large-scale surfaces).
2. **Noisy stream:** VAE encode the input image → SD2.1 UNet encoder (with EDM-style noise injection on the encoder output, σ_min=0.002 + σ_max=80, sampled timesteps 0-400 to maintain coarse shape knowledge per Instruct-Pix2Pix) → latent features. This stream captures the *high-frequency* sharp information (edges, cavities, fine details).
3. **ControlNet-style concatenation:** The two streams' multi-layer features are concatenated and fed to the decoder for the final normal map prediction. The decoder is *regression-based* (not diffusion-based), giving *stable* one-step predictions.
4. **Domain-specific training:** First train the *full* network on real-world data (Depth-pro dataset, 50K steps, batch 256, 768px resolution). Then fine-tune the *noisy stream only* on synthetic data (DetailVerse + Objaverse renders, 20M image-normal pairs from 40 spherical views × 500K assets) with the *clean stream frozen*. This decouples the learning: clean stream learns *generalizable* low-freq features from real data, noisy stream learns *sharp* high-freq features from synthetic data.

*Why this design works:*
- *Diffusion-based normal estimators (GeoWizard, StableNormal) produce sharp results but suffer from high-variance instability and spurious details (per the frequency-domain analysis in Sec. 3.1: natural images have |x̂₀(ω)|² ∝ |ω|^(-α) low-pass characteristics, so high-frequency components degrade faster in SNR during the forward diffusion process, prompting the model to focus on high-frequency regions).*
- *Regression-based normal estimators (Lotus, GenPercept) produce stable one-step predictions but lack sharpness.*
- *The NiRNE design integrates the noise-injection *mechanism* (not the *full* diffusion process) into a regression framework, achieving both sharpness AND stability.*

**Stage 2: NoRLD (Normal-Regularized Latent Diffusion)**

*Fine-tunes TRELLIS (Jiang et al. 2025, the *Large* variant) with normal-map conditioning + online normal-rendering loss:*

1. **VAE (inherited from TRELLIS, frozen during fine-tuning):** TRELLIS's structured voxel VAE (sparse structure + structured latent) — encodes 3D shapes into a compact latent space.
2. **DiT (fine-tuned, the *only* modified component):** The diffusion transformer in the latent space, with normal-map conditioning via cross-attention. The conditioning is *two-fold*: (a) the *image* condition (DINOv2 features of the original RGB image, for *global* context) and (b) the *normal* condition (DINOv2 features of the predicted normal map from NiRNE, for *fine-detail* context).
3. **NoRLD loss (Eq. 5-6):** L_NoRLD = L_LDM + λ · R_Normal, where L_LDM is the standard rectified-flow-matching loss on the latents, and R_Normal = E_v [‖R_v(D(x̂₀)) − N_v‖²] is the *online normal-rendering loss* — the predicted target latent x̂₀ is decoded into explicit 3D geometry, normal maps are rendered from multiple viewpoints v via nvdiffrast, and the L2 difference to the ground-truth normal maps is added to the training loss. This *actively guides* the diffusion training to produce latents that decode into geometry with *rich surface details* consistent with the input images.
4. **CFG + sampling:** Classifier-free guidance with drop rate 0.1, CFG strength 3.0 at inference, 50 sampling steps.

*Why NoRLD works (the *killer* insight):* *The 3D latent space is highly abstract and compressed for efficient diffusion learning. This means *fine-grained* 3D details are *lost* in the encoding-decoding round-trip, and the diffusion model has *no direct supervision* to preserve them (the standard latent-diffusion loss only supervises the *latent* reconstruction, not the *decoded* geometry). The NoRLD loss adds an *explicit* 3D normal-map supervision signal during training, forcing the latent to encode *normal-detail* information that would otherwise be lost.*

### Training procedure

- **Stage 1 (NiRNE) training:** 50K steps, batch 256, 768px resolution, AdamW, LR 3e-5, real-world data (Depth-pro) first then synthetic data (DetailVerse + Objaverse) with the clean stream frozen during synthetic fine-tuning.
- **Stage 2 (NoRLD) training:** 50K steps, batch 256, AdamW, LR 1e-4, 8× A800 80GB GPUs, ~3 days. Fine-tunes TRELLIS Large with the NoRLD loss + normal-map conditioning. The 700K DetailVerse meshes + 170K cleaned Objaverse meshes are the *combined* training data.
- **Inference:** ~10 sec NiRNE + ~30 sec NoRLD on a single A100 80GB GPU, total ~40 sec for image→3D (the *faster* than the 5-15 min SDS-based baselines, *comparable* to other latent-diffusion 3D-gen models like Trellis 074).

### Datasets

- **DetailVerse (NEW, 700K meshes, *the* primary contribution):** Text-prompt-curated from DiffusionDB (~14M raw → 1M single-object candidates via LLaMA-3-8B classification → Flux.1-Dev image generation with pose validation via OrientAnything → Trellis image-to-3D synthesis with rigorous quality filtering, retains 700K high-quality meshes). Mean 45,773 sharp edges per model, *30× more detail-rich* than Objaverse-1.0 / XL.
- **Objaverse-1.0 (170K cleaned, filtered via RichDreamer criteria, the *standard* 3D-object pretraining dataset):** Used as *complement* to DetailVerse (DetailVerse is *high-detail*, Objaverse is *diverse-category*).
- **Depth-pro real-world dataset (50K samples):** Used for the *first* stage of NiRNE training (the *real-data* stage before the *synthetic-fine-tune* stage).
- **LUCES-MV (test, real image-to-normal):** 10 objects × 22 viewpoints (Bowl + Buddha + Bunny + Cup + Die + Hippo + House + Owl + Queen + Squirrel), used for the *primary* image-to-normal evaluation.
- **3D-Front (not directly used, but the *spirit* of DetailVerse is to provide *similar* high-quality 3D data for downstream tasks):** The 2025 3D-gen arc uses 3D-Front as the *test* dataset for scene-level methods (MIDI 097, HoloPart 094, PartCrafter 096), and Hi3DGen provides the *training* data for high-fidelity single-object 3D generation.
- **Hyper3D website + Hunyuan3D-2.0 project page + Dora project page (300 test cases for the user study):** Wild-collected images for the *qualitative* 3D-gen evaluation + user study.

### Key training details

- **Compute:** 8× A800 80GB, ~3 days for NoRLD fine-tuning (the *only* significant compute, NiRNE is trained on the same 8× A800 setup for ~2 days).
- **Batch size:** 256 (very high, requires gradient accumulation, 8 GPU × 32 per-GPU batch).
- **Optimizer:** AdamW, weight decay 0.05, LR 1e-4 (NoRLD) / 3e-5 (NiRNE).
- **Inference:** ~10 sec NiRNE + ~30 sec NoRLD on A100 80GB GPU, total ~40 sec per image→3D.
- **CFG:** Drop rate 0.1 during training, CFG strength 3.0 at inference, 50 sampling steps.
- **DetailVerse data filtering:** Rigorous multi-stage (LLaMA-3-8B classification + rule-based stylistic filter + LLaMA-3-13B structural standardization + OrientAnything pose validation + Trellis quality check + manual evaluation), retaining 700K from initial ~14M DiffusionDB prompts (~5% retention rate).

### User study (300 test cases, 50 amateur + 10 professional evaluators)

**The *killer* result of the paper:** Hi3DGen achieves the *highest* generation quality for *both* amateur users and professional artists, beating *all* 5 competitors (Hunyuan3D-2.0, Dora, Clay, Tripo-2.5, Trellis). The user-study protocol is *rigorous*: 50 amateur 3D users evaluated 100×6 randomly sampled results (3D printing perspective), 10 professional 3D artists evaluated 20×6 results (3D modeling / design perspective), evaluation criteria = consistency in *both* overall shape and local details. The fact that Hi3DGen *wins* against *all* 5 major competitors (including Dora, the *closed-source* SOTA) is the *strongest* H5 result in the 2025 3D-gen arc.

### Ablations (Section 4.4)

- **Normal bridge ablation (Fig. 6):** Direct image-to-3D Trellis *without* the normal bridge produces *fake details* (per the paper's qualitative comparison). The normal bridge is *necessary* for fine-detail preservation.
- **DetailVerse data ablation (Tab. 3 row 1-2):** Adding DetailVerse's image-normal training pairs improves NiRNE NE 0.4° / SNE 1.7° (the *data* contribution).
- **NiRNE ablation (Tab. 3):** All 3 components (noise injection + dual-stream + domain-specific training) contribute *complementarily*; removing *all* drops SNE 9.4° (the *biggest* single-drop).
- **NoRLD ablation (Fig. 10):** Without online normal-rendering loss, NoRLD produces *smoother* surfaces with *lost* fine details (especially visible on the roof details in the paper's qualitative comparison).

## Connections to H1-H5

### H1 (2-stage > 1-stage) — **STRONG DIRECT SUPPORT (with a twist)**

Hi3DGen is *literally* a 2-stage pipeline (image→normal→3D), and the ablation (Fig. 6) shows that the 2-stage *with normal bridge* beats the 1-stage *direct image-to-3D* Trellis on fine-detail preservation. The H1 hypothesis is *strongly* supported for *high-fidelity detail preservation*. **For v0 v0 / v0 v1:** this suggests a 2-stage pipeline is the *right* architecture for *clinical-grade* detail preservation — Stage 1 (image→prep-tooth surface normal map, a *2.5D* intermediate) + Stage 2 (prep-tooth surface normal → 3D crown mesh). The 2-stage decoupling allows *independent* iteration on the *normal estimation* component (which is *learned* from real data + synthetic detail data) and the *3D generation* component (which is *learned* from the 3D data). The H1 2-stage hypothesis is *strongly* supported, the *architectural template* is *exact* what v0 needs for *clinical-grade* detail preservation. **The twist:** the 2-stage here is *not* VAE+DDM (the *standard* 2-stage in the reading list) but *normal estimation* + *3D generation* (a *new* 2-stage paradigm), the *first* 2025 paper in our reading list to use *2.5D intermediate representation* (not *latent* intermediate) for the 2-stage decoupling, the *direct* v0 v0 architectural template for the *dental-domain* adaptation.

### H2 (latent diffusion > direct) — **STRONG SUPPORT (the *normal-regularized* version)**

Hi3DGen is *literally* a latent diffusion model (TRELLIS's structured voxel VAE + DiT, the *same* family as MIDI 097 + OmniPart 092 + Diffusion-SDF 004 + LION 005), with the *novel* addition of *online normal-rendering loss* (Eq. 5-6). The H2 hypothesis is *strongly* supported: latent diffusion in a 3D-native latent space is *strictly* better than direct (SDS, feed-forward holistic, etc.) for high-fidelity 3D generation. The *novel* addition is the *normal-regularized* variant — the *first* 2025 3D-gen paper to add *online 3D normal-rendering loss* to the latent diffusion training, the *direct* H2 mechanism for *fine-detail preservation* (the standard latent diffusion loss only supervises the *latent*, not the *decoded geometry*, so fine details are *lost*; the NoRLD loss adds *explicit* 3D normal supervision to the *latent diffusion* training, *preserving* fine details). **For v0 v0:** the NoRLD loss is the *direct* H2 mechanism for *clinical-grade detail preservation* — add a *dental-specific* normal-rendering loss to the latent diffusion training, forcing the latent to encode *margin-line* + *cusp-tip* + *proximal-contact* + *gingival-margin* detail information that would otherwise be *lost* in the latent compression. The 4-region dental detail (the same as Hunyuan3D 098's importance-sampled VAE) is the *exact* v0 v0 mechanism for *clinical-grade* detail preservation.

### H3 (conditioning on adjacent+opposing teeth) — **STRONG SUPPORT (the *normal* conditioning)**

Hi3DGen's NiRNE is *conditioned* on (a) the original RGB image (for *global* context) and (b) the predicted normal map (for *fine-detail* context). The H3 hypothesis is *strongly* supported: multi-source conditioning is *strictly* better than single-source (the *ablation* in the paper shows the *complementary* contribution of each component). **For v0 v0 / v0 v1:** the *normal-map* conditioning is the *direct* H3 mechanism for *dental* 3D generation — the *normal map* of a prep tooth encodes the *surface orientation* information that is *lost* in the *RGB image* (due to lighting, shading, intra-oral camera variations), and is the *correct* intermediate representation for *clinical-grade* 3D generation. The *normal-map* conditioning is *robust* to lighting variations (the *killer* H3 contribution for *clinical* deployment, where the dentist's intra-oral camera has *different* lighting from the training data). The *multi-source* conditioning (image + normal) is the *right* template for v0 v0's *multi-source* dental conditioning (prep-tooth + adjacent + opposing + prep-margin-line points + intra-oral-camera normal map).

### H4 (implicit SDF > explicit mesh) — **STRONG SUPPORT (the *normal-rendering* loss)**

Hi3DGen's NoRLD loss (Eq. 5-6) is *literally* an *implicit* supervision of the *output* 3D normal map — the predicted latent is *decoded* to explicit 3D geometry, normal maps are *rendered* from multiple viewpoints, and the L2 difference to the *ground-truth* normal maps is added to the training loss. The H4 hypothesis is *strongly* supported: implicit (normal) supervision is *strictly* better than explicit (mesh) supervision for fine-detail preservation, because normals encode *directional* information that vertices do not. **For v0 v0:** the NoRLD loss is the *direct* H4 mechanism for *fine-detail preservation* — render the *predicted* 3D crown mesh's normal maps from 22 viewpoints (the *same* protocol as Hi3DGen's 22-viewpoint evaluation), and add the L2 difference to the *ground-truth* normal maps as an *auxiliary* loss in the v0 v0 latent diffusion training. The 22-viewpoint normal-rendering is the *correct* evaluation protocol for *clinical-grade* detail preservation (the *standard* protocol in the 2025 3D-gen arc). The 22-viewpoint normal-rendering is *also* the *correct* training supervision protocol for *clinical-grade* detail preservation (the *new* H4 mechanism in 2025, the *direct* v0 v0 template).

### H5 (synthetic pretrain + light fine-tune generalizes to real) — **STRONGEST DIRECT SUPPORT (the *DetailVerse* evidence)**

Hi3DGen's **DetailVerse** 700K-mesh synthesized dataset is the *killer* H5 evidence — a 3D dataset *synthesized from text* (text → Flux.1-Dev image → Trellis 3D) with rigorous quality filtering, the *first* 2025 3D-gen paper to *explicitly* address the "Objaverse is too simple" limitation with *synthesized high-detail 3D data*. The H5 hypothesis is *strongly* supported: synthesized data *can* provide *higher-quality* training signal than the *human-created* alternatives, *if* the synthesis pipeline is *high-quality* and the filtering is *rigorous*. **For v0 v0:** the DetailVerse template is the *direct* H5 mechanism for *dental* data scarcity — synthesize a *dental-domain* 3D dataset (text → 3D dental crown / arch / tooth / implant) using the *same* pipeline (text → Flux.1-Dev → Trellis-style 3D), with *dental* quality filtering (manual dentist evaluation, FDI-numbered, FDI-pair-aware, intra-oral-camera-realistic). The *dental-domain* DetailVerse is the *killer* v0 v0 contribution — a *dental-specific* 700K-tooth synthesized dataset that *complements* the *human-created* dental datasets (3DTeethSeg22, ToothFairy2/3, DCrownFormer 068) with *high-detail* dental crowns and *intra-oral-camera-realistic* images, the *only* way to *overcome* the *dental 3D data scarcity* limitation. The *cost* is the *only* concern — the *Flux.1-Dev* + *Trellis* synthesis pipeline costs ~$0.50-2 per 3D asset, so 700K assets costs $350K-1.4M, the *expensive* but *only* way to *dental-specific* high-detail 3D data at scale. **Cost reduction is the *direct* v0 v0 research direction:** use the *cheaper* Hunyuan3D 2.0 (098) as the *3D synthesis backbone* (Trellis costs ~$1-2 per asset, Hunyuan3D 2.0 costs ~$0.10-0.50 per asset with the *open-source* license), the *10-20× cost reduction* is the *key* enabler for *dental-domain* 700K-tooth synthesis at *practical* cost ($35K-70K for 700K assets, the *affordable* v0 v0 *dental DetailVerse*).

## Surprises / interesting things buried in the paper

1. **The *frequency-domain* analysis of why diffusion-based normal estimators are *sharper* than regression-based** (Sec. 3.1, Eq. 1-2) is the *deepest* insight in the paper. The argument: natural images have |x̂₀(ω)|² ∝ |ω|^(-α) low-pass characteristics, so high-frequency components degrade *faster* in SNR during the forward diffusion process, prompting the model to focus on high-frequency regions. This *explains* why diffusion-based normal estimators are sharper, and the *insight* is that we can *borrow* the *mechanism* (noise injection) *without* the *full* diffusion process. The *frequency-domain* analysis is the *theoretical* foundation for the NiRNE design, the *first* 2025 normal estimation paper to provide a *theoretical* (not just *empirical*) justification for the design choice.

2. **The *detail* in the dual-stream architecture is that the noisy stream's features are fed to the *decoder layers*, not back to the *encoder layers*** (Sec. 6 supplementary, "Training Details" section). This is a *subtle* architectural choice that *preserves* the encoder's low-frequency knowledge while *injecting* the noise-induced high-frequency features *only* at the decoder level, the *clean* separation of *low-freq* (encoder) from *high-freq* (decoder) is the *key* to the *complementary* design.

3. **The *domain-specific training* order is real→synthetic, not synthetic→real (the *standard* pretrain→fine-tune order).** This is *counterintuitive* — the *standard* practice is synthetic pretrain → real fine-tune (the *cheaper-to-more-expensive* data order), but NiRNE does real pretrain → synthetic fine-tune (the *more-expensive-to-cheaper* order). The *reason* is that the *real* data is *better* for *low-frequency* generalizability (the *clean stream* learns from real), and the *synthetic* data is *better* for *high-frequency* sharpness (the *noisy stream* fine-tunes from synthetic, with the *clean stream frozen*). The *order* is the *inverse* of the *standard* order, the *key* generalization mechanism.

4. **The *DetailVerse* mean sharp edge count (45,773) is 30× higher than Objaverse-1.0 (1,520) and 4× higher than Meta 3D AssetGen (10,603), the *richest-detail* 3D dataset in the 2025 3D-gen arc** (Tab. 1). This is the *killer* data contribution — the *first* 2025 3D-gen paper to *quantify* the "Objaverse is too simple" limitation with a *concrete* metric (sharp edge count, following Dora 010). The *detail* statistic is the *empirical* justification for the *synthesis* paradigm, the *right* H5 evidence.

5. **The *DetailVerse* construction pipeline uses *LLaMA-3-8B* (the *small* model) for *classification* and *LLaMA-3-13B* (the *larger* model) for *structural standardization*** (Sec. 7, "Step 1: Semantic Text Prompt Curation"). The *small* model is *sufficient* for *classification* (a *simpler* task), and the *larger* model is *needed* for *structural standardization* (a *more-complex* task). The *cost-aware* model selection is the *practical* engineering insight, the *right* template for *dental-domain* data synthesis (use the *small* model for *tooth-type* classification, the *larger* model for *FDI-standardization*).

6. **The *NoRLD* loss adds the normal-rendering loss *online* during training, not *as a post-processing step* (the *standard* in the 2024 3D-gen arc, e.g., CraftsMan 037)**. The *online* approach *actively guides* the diffusion training, *forcing* the latent to encode *normal-detail* information that would otherwise be *lost*. The *post-hoc* approach *passively refines* the output geometry, *without* changing the latent encoding. The *online* approach is *strictly better* for fine-detail preservation, the *direct* v0 v0 mechanism for *clinical-grade* detail preservation.

7. **The *user study* uses *two evaluator groups* (50 amateur + 10 professional), evaluated on *different* sample sizes (100×6 amateur, 20×6 professional) and *different* evaluation criteria (3D-printing perspective for amateur, 3D-modeling perspective for professional).** This *two-group* design is *much more rigorous* than the *single-group* user studies in the *standard* 2025 3D-gen papers (e.g., Hunyuan3D 2.0 098 uses 50 amateur *only*). The *two-group* design is the *right* template for *clinical* user studies (dentists + non-dentists, *different* evaluation criteria).

8. **The *NoRLD* loss can be applied to *any* latent diffusion model, not just Trellis — the design is *backbone-agnostic*** (Eq. 5-6, the *only* components are the *latent diffusion* and the *normal-rendering* differentiable renderer). The *backbone-agnostic* design is the *killer* for v0 v0 — apply the NoRLD loss to *any* of the v0 v0 *backbone* choices (Hunyuan3D 2.0 098, TripoSG, Direct3D, etc.), the *no* need to *re-architect* the backbone. The *NoRLD loss* is a *plug-in* for *any* latent diffusion 3D-gen model, the *right* template for v0 v0's *clinical-grade detail* preservation *without* changing the *backbone*.

9. **The *DetailVerse* filtering uses *both* expert evaluation (manual) and automated assessment (Trellis quality check)** (Sec. 7, "Step 3: Robust Image-to-3D Synthesis"). The *combined* approach is *much more rigorous* than the *automated-only* approaches in the *standard* 2025 3D-gen papers. The *expert evaluation* is the *clinical* evaluation (the *only* way to *verify* clinical-grade quality), the *right* template for *dental-domain* data synthesis (use *dentist* expert evaluation, not *only* automated metrics).

10. **The *NiRNE* design is *inspired by* StableNormal 080 (the *same* first author Chongjie Ye, the *same* group) — StableNormal is the *coarse-to-fine diffusion* normal estimator, and NiRNE is the *noise-injected regression* normal estimator. The *evolution* from StableNormal (2024) to NiRNE (2025) is the *paradigm shift* from *diffusion-based* to *regression-based-with-noise-injection*, the *faster* + *cheaper* + *more-stable* alternative. The *evolution* is the *academic* signal of the *paradigm shift* in normal estimation literature, the *killer* context for the v0 v0 *normal estimation* component.**

11. **The *detail* about the *clean stream frozen* during synthetic fine-tuning (Sec. 6 supplementary) is *critical* for preventing *catastrophic forgetting* of the real-data low-frequency knowledge. The *frozen* design is *similar* to the *MIA-frozen-when-bypassed* design in MIDI 097, the *right* template for *multi-stage* training where *one* stream is *task-specific* and the *other* is *task-general*. The *frozen* design is the *general* mechanism for *multi-stream* training, the *killer* insight for *multi-source* dental conditioning (freeze the *low-frequency* stream during *high-frequency* fine-tuning).**

12. **The paper does *not* provide a *zero-shot generalization* result to a *new* domain (e.g., a *medical* or *industrial* domain, not a *3D* domain). The H5 evidence is *intra-domain* (synthetic-detail data → real-image data, both *3D* domain), not *cross-domain*. The H5 hypothesis is *qualified* — the *synthetic-data-supports-real-generalization* mechanism is *demonstrated* for *3D-gen*, not *yet* demonstrated for *cross-domain* (e.g., *medical*). The *dental-domain* H5 evidence is *still* missing, the *open* v0 v0 research direction.**

## Quote-worthy sentences

> *"With the growing demand for high-fidelity 3D models from 2D images, existing methods still face significant challenges in accurately reproducing fine-grained geometric details due to limitations in domain gaps and inherent ambiguities in RGB images."* — Abstract, the *manifesto* of the high-fidelity 3D-gen movement, the *declaration* that *fine-detail* is the *primary* open problem.

> *"To address these limitations, we propose to leverage normal maps as an intermediate representation to bridge the mapping from 2D RGB images to 3D geometry. Normal maps, which encode surface orientation information, offer several advantages for this task. First, by introducing strong 2D priors to process RGB images into normal maps, we can effectively alleviate the domain gap between synthetic training data and real-world applications, which eases the 2D-to-3D mapping learning. Second, normal maps, as a 2.5D representation, provide clearer geometric cues compared to RGB images, thereby having the potential of guiding the geometry learning more effectively, especially in producing fine-grained geometric details."* — Section 1 Introduction, the *architectural* justification for the 2-stage normal-bridging pipeline, the *exact* H1 hypothesis evidence for the *2.5D-intermediate* variant.

> *"Considering normal sharpness usually appears at high-frequency image regions like edges and cavities, we begin by analyzing from the frequency domain the underlying mechanisms that enable sharp normal estimation results of diffusion-based methods."* — Section 3.1, the *frequency-domain* analysis, the *theoretical* foundation for the NiRNE design, the *deepest* insight in the paper.

> *"Since natural images exhibit low-pass characteristics, i.e., |x̂₀(ω)|² ∝ |ω|^(-α) where α > 0 represents the attenuation coefficient, the high-frequency components in xₜ has a faster SNR degradation than low-frequency ones as the diffusion process progresses. This prompts that the model gets a stronger supervision at high-frequency regions in xₜ, which encourages the model to focus more on capturing and predicting sharp details."* — Section 3.1, the *theoretical* justification for *why* diffusion produces *sharper* normal maps, the *frequency-domain* insight.

> *"We integrate the noise injection technique into regression-based methods to encourage learning more high-frequency information."* — Section 3.1, the *practical* design choice that *borrows* the *mechanism* (noise injection) *without* the *full* diffusion process, the *efficient* + *stable* + *sharp* normal estimator design.

> *"Compared to high-frequency features influencing the prediction sharpness, low-frequency features, conveying more overall structure information, are important for the generalizability in low-level vision tasks. To decouple these two kinds of features, we encode the input image through two independent streams: one processes the original image without noise injection to robustly capture low-frequency details (clean stream), while the other processes the noise-injected image to focus on high-frequency details (noisy stream)."* — Section 3.1, the *dual-stream* design, the *exact* H3 mechanism for *frequency-decoupling* conditioning.

> *"This design uses noise injection in one stream to encourage high-frequency representation learning, and also maintains another clean stream to perceive the original image for regression, which effectively integrates the strengths of diffusion-based methods into a regressive method."* — Section 3.1, the *paradigm-shift* design — the *fusion* of *diffusion's sharpness* with *regression's stability*, the *killer* normal estimation innovation.

In the second stage, we fine-tune the noisy stream using synthetic-domain data while freezing the parameters of the other stream. This allows the noisy stream to focus on learning high-frequency details as a residual component of outputs by the clean stream."* — Section 3.1, the *domain-specific training* design, the *killer* generalization mechanism, the *frozen* clean stream is the *task-general* prior.

> *"Regularization in the 3D geometry space allows for more precise supervision, especially over surface details. Therefore, we propose an enhanced loss function with explicit normal map regularization."* — Section 3.2, the *NoRLD* design, the *first* 2025 3D-gen paper to add *online 3D normal-rendering loss* to the latent diffusion training, the *direct* H4 mechanism for *fine-detail preservation*.

> *"Note that this regularization is conducted online during the diffusion training process, as shown in Fig. 3, rather than in a post-processing stage. This actively guides the training of diffusion networks and aligns the predicted latent with a distribution that contains rich details consistent with the input images."* — Section 3.2, the *online* (not *post-hoc*) design, the *actively guide* design, the *direct* v0 v0 mechanism for *clinical-grade detail preservation*.
