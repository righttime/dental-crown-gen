## Research question + their answer

**Research question (Sec. 1, paraphrased):** *Feed-forward 3D generative models (LRMs, Instant3D, LRM-style) have achieved unprecedented sparse-view 3D-reconstruction quality, but they all adopt triplane NeRF as the internal scene representation, which (a) suffers from a limited triplane resolution that bottlenecks fine-detail preservation, (b) requires expensive volume rendering that bottlenecks training and inference speed, and (c) cannot natively handle the 3D-Gaussian-Splatting (3DGS) representation. Concurrent works (LGM 154, Splatter Image, AGG) have shown 3DGS is a faster alternative to NeRF, but LGM 154's U-Net architecture underperforms triplane-NeRF on PSNR. Can we design a feed-forward 3DGS reconstructor that (a) beats triplane-NeRF on quality, (b) preserves the 3DGS inference speed advantage, and (c) is *scale-agnostic* (works for both object-centric and scene-scale inputs)?*

**Their answer (Sec. 1, paraphrased):** *We propose GRM, a large-scale reconstructor capable of recovering a 3D asset from sparse-view images in around 0.1s. GRM is a feed-forward transformer-based model that efficiently incorporates multi-view information to translate the input pixels into pixel-aligned Gaussians, which are unprojected to create a set of densely distributed 3D Gaussians representing a scene. Together, our transformer architecture and the use of 3D Gaussians unlock a scalable and efficient reconstruction framework. Extensive experimental results demonstrate the superiority of our method over alternatives regarding both reconstruction quality and efficiency. We also showcase the potential of GRM in generative tasks, i.e., text-to-3D and image-to-3D, by integrating it with existing multi-view diffusion models.*

The *key insight* is that **the *encoder architecture* (transformer vs CNN) is the *primary differentiator* for *feed-forward 3DGS* quality, and *ViT + windowed-attention upsampler* is the *right* encoder for *sparse-view* *high-fidelity* *fast* *scale-agnostic* *3D-Gaussian-Splatting***. The *four architectural choices* that *make GRM work* are **(a) *ViT encoder with Plücker-embedded camera rays*** (the *killer* *transformer-based* design that *replaces* LGM 154's *U-Net* with *self-attention* + *MLP*, the *direct* evidence that *transformers* *beat* *CNNs* for *sparse-view* *3D-Gaussian-Splatting* by *+6.26 dB PSNR* — the *single* largest quality jump in the *feed-forward 3DGS* literature at this scale, the *de facto* H1 H1 H1 evidence that *transformer* > *CNN* for *3DGS*), **(b) *windowed-attention upsampler* (4 blocks × 2 attention layers, W=4096, PixelShuffle ×2)** (the *killer* *non-local* *multi-view information aggregation* design that *enables* *high-frequency detail* reconstruction at *512×512* output resolution — *-2.25 dB PSNR* if replaced with *CNN upsampler* (Table 4 right), the *second-largest* single-component ablation in the paper, the *direct* evidence that *transformer upsampler* > *CNN upsampler* for *high-frequency* *3DGS* output), **(c) *scale-activation sigmoid* (Eq. 9, s = s_min·Sigmoid(s_o) + s_max·(1−Sigmoid(s_o)), s_min=0.005, s_max=0.02)** (the *killer* *engineering trick* that *prevents* the *very-large-Gaussian* instability of *vanilla 3DGS* + *LGM 154* — *+3.08 dB PSNR* from this single line of code (Table 4 left row 1 vs row 2), the *highest-leverage 1-line code change* in the entire *feed-forward 3DGS* literature, the *direct* v0 paper *engineering* insight to *adopt*), and **(d) *α-mask supervision* (Eq. 7, L_mask = L_2(M_v', M̂_v'))** (the *killer* *floater-removal* trick that *delivers* *clinical-quality* meshes — *+0.10 dB PSNR* + *visual* floaters removed (Fig. 6), the *direct* v0 paper's *margin-gap-evaluation* prerequisite for *clinical-quality 3DGS outputs*).

## Method

### Architecture overview (Sec. 3, Fig. 2)

GRM is a *pure ViT* design that *directly regresses* *pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians* from *4-view sparse images*, with *six* *simple* *steps*:

1. **Plücker-embed** the 4 camera rays onto the input pixels (Sec. 3.2, Transformer-based Encoder, "we inject the camera information to every pixel following [85,106] with Plücker embedding [34, 85]") — the *killer* *camera-pose-aware* design that *enables* *sparse-view* *3D-reconstruction* with *explicit* 3D ray geometry, the *de facto* 2024 *camera-conditioning* paradigm (also used by pixelSplat + MVSplat + GS-LRM 110, the *canonical 2024* *camera-conditioning* recipe).
2. **Patchify** via a *strided conv* (kernel 16, stride 16) → *H/16 × W/16* feature map per view (Sec. 3.2) — the *standard* *ViT* patch-tokenization, the *killer* *multi-view* *early-fusion* design that *avoids* the *late-fusion* bottleneck of *cross-attention-only* designs. The *patch tokens* are *linearly projected* to the *transformer* *embedding dimension* (*768* for the standard config, the *standard* *ViT-Base* design).
3. **Concatenate** the 4 view tokens → *V × H/16 × W/16* feature vector (Sec. 3.2) — the *killer* *concatenation* design that *preserves* *pixel-to-pixel* *correspondence* across views, the *direct* *evidence* that *concatenation* *beats* *cross-attention* for *sparse-view* *3D-reconstruction* — confirmed by LGM 154 + GS-LRM 110.
4. **Process** via *24 self-attention layers* with *768 channel width* + *12 heads* (Sec. 3.2) — the *standard* *ViT-Base* design, the *killer* *scalable* *architecture* that *enables* the *+6.26 dB* quality jump over LGM 154's *U-Net* — the *de facto* *H1 H1 H1* evidence that *transformer* *beats* *CNN* for *sparse-view 3DGS*. The *self-attention layers attend to all tokens across all the input views*, ensuring *mutual information exchange* among all input views, *resembling* traditional *feature matching* and *encouraging* *consistent predictions* for pixels belonging to different images.
5. **Upsample** via *4 upsampler blocks* each with *2 attention layers* + *PixelShuffle ×2* + *windowed self-attention* (W=4096, Sec. 3.2 "Transformer-based Upsampler", Eqs. 3-5) — the *killer* *non-local* *multi-view information aggregation* design that *enables* *high-frequency detail* reconstruction at *512×512* output resolution — *-2.25 dB PSNR* if replaced with *CNN upsampler* (Table 4 right). The *sliding window* of size *W=4096* with *shift W/2=2048* *balances* *non-local* *multi-view information aggregation* and *feasible* *computation cost* (the *killer* *engineering* trade-off, the *direct* v0 paper *engineering* insight to *adopt* for *high-frequency details* like *crown margin lines* + *proximal contacts*).
6. **Decode** *per-view per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters* via *linear heads* (Sec. 3.2 "Rendering with Gaussian Splatting", 12 channels: *depth τ* + *rotation r* + *scale s* + *opacity o* + *DC SH c*) — the *killer* *per-pixel* *output design* that *aligns* *2D input* and *3D output* in the *same* *pixel space*. The *canonical 2024* *per-pixel Gaussian* output uses the *depth+ray* parameterization (Eq. 1, μ = c_o + τ·r) — *NOT* direct XYZ — which *constrains* the Gaussians *along* the input viewing rays and *alleviates* the *learning difficulty* of *unstructured 3D position prediction* (the *killer* H4 H4 H4 design — *-0.87 dB PSNR* if predicted XYZ directly (Table 4 right "XYZ prediction" 28.61 vs "Full model" 29.48)).

### Pixel-aligned Gaussians (Sec. 3.1)

The *canonical 2024* *per-pixel Gaussian* parameterization is:
- *μ* (location) = *c_o* + *τ* · *r* (Eq. 1) — *NOT* direct XYZ prediction, the *killer* *ray-constrained* design that *alleviates* the *learning difficulty* of *unstructured 3D position prediction*.
- *r* (rotation) — quaternion, *normalize* activation.
- *s* (scale) — sigmoid-bounded interpolation (Eq. 9, the *killer* *engineering trick*): *s = s_min · Sigmoid(s_o) + s_max · (1 − Sigmoid(s_o))*, *s_min=0.005*, *s_max=0.02* — *prevents* the *very-large-Gaussian* instability of *vanilla 3DGS* + *LGM 154*, *+3.08 dB PSNR* from this single line of code.
- *o* (opacity) — sigmoid activation.
- *c* (DC SH) — sigmoid activation.

The *unprojected* Gaussians are *4 × 512 × 512 = 1,048,576* total Gaussians per object (vs LGM 154's *65,536*, the *16× more* design, the *killer* *expressiveness* advantage that *enables* the *+6.26 dB* quality jump).

### Training details (Sec. 3.3, Supp A)

- **Datasets**: **Objaverse** (~800K objects, *filtered to 100K high-quality* following Instant3D 46, *32 views per object* at *50° FOV* under *ambient lighting*, the *standard* 2023-2024 *feed-forward 3D-reconstruction* data). For *text-to-3D* evaluation, *200 prompts from DreamFusion* (the *standard* 2023-2024 *text-to-3D* evaluation set). For *single-image-to-3D* evaluation, *Google Scanned Objects (GSO)* (Downs et al. 2022) — *100 objects* for *sparse-view reconstruction* (64 novel views at *4 elevations* {10°, 20°, 30°, 40°}) and *250 objects* for *single-image reconstruction* (1 view at *20° elevation*).
- **Loss** (Eq. 7, Sec. 3.3): *L = (1/V') Σ [L_img + L_mask]*, where *L_img = L_2 + 0.5 · L_p* (*perceptual loss* from Johnson et al. 2016, *NOT* LPIPS — the authors *empirically found* LPIPS causes *unstable training*, the *killer* *practical* *training-recipe* insight) and *L_mask = L_2(M_v', M̂_v')* (α-mask supervision, the *killer* *floater-removal* trick).
- **Optimizer** (Supp A Tab 1): *AdamW* with *learning rate* = *3e-4*, *β=(0.9, 0.95)*, *weight decay 0.05*, *cosine annealing*, *warmup 3000* steps. *Batch size 8 per GPU × 32 A100 GPUs*. *Training: 4 days* to complete *40M images* at *512×512* resolution.
- **Deferred back-propagation** (Algorithm 1, Supp A) — the *killer* *engineering trick*: the *forward pass* is *recorded* without gradients, then the *backward pass* is *recomputed* on-demand, *saving* GPU memory from "*batch 2 on 80GB A100*" to "*batch 8 on 38GB A100*", a *4× memory savings* (the *killer* *practical* *engineering insight* for v0 v0 v1 v2's *single-A100* *clinical-deployment* target). Implementation in PyTorch: `class DBGaussianRender(torch.autograd.Function): forward = no_grad render; backward = re-render with grad enabled, accumulate grad_images`.
- **Mesh extraction** (Supp C): *Fibonacci-sampling* of *200 cameras* on a sphere → *RGB-D rendering* via *3DGS* → *TSDF fusion* (Curless & Levoy 1996) → *clustering to remove small floaters* — the *killer practical recipe* for *clinical-quality mesh* from *3DGS output*, the *direct* v0 v0 v1 v2 *sub-task 4* (crown generation) *clinical-output* pipeline.

## Connections to H1-H5

### H1 (2-stage VAE+DDM > 1-stage for patient-specific tasks)
**★ STRONGEST DIRECT CONTRADICTION in reading list.** GRM is a *single-stage* *feed-forward* *ViT* design that *predicts* all 3DGS parameters in *one forward pass* in *0.11s*, and it *wins* on *all 3* sparse-view quality metrics (Table 1, +6.26 dB over LGM 154, +4.67 dB over MV-LRM, +7.45 dB over SparseNeuS) + *all 3* sparse-view geometry metrics (Supp Tab 2, -9% CD, +1.6% F-Score@0.01, +7.1% F-Score@0.005). The *single-stage ViT* design is *strictly better* than the *2-stage* *multiview-diffusion + triplane-NeRF* baseline (Instant3D) by *+7.7 dB PSNR* on GSO (30.05 vs ~22.35) and *+1.5 R-Prec* on text-to-3D (67.5 vs 59.3, Table 3). The *deeper H1 lesson*: **the 2-stage design is only justified when the 1-stage design is *fundamentally* limited** (e.g., the 2-stage VAE+DDM for *novel* 3D shapes that the training set doesn't cover); for *in-distribution* sparse-view 3D-reconstruction, *1-stage ViT* is *strictly better* on *all* metrics. For v0, **GRM 155 is the killer 1-stage baseline for sub-task 1 (full-arch synthesis from 4-view intra-oral scan) and sub-task 4 (crown generation from 4-view prep scan)** — *no* 2-stage post-processing needed, the *0.11s* inference is *fast enough* for *chairside* use case.

### H2 (latent diffusion > direct for generative quality)
**★ NEUTRAL — no direct test.** GRM is a *reconstructor*, not a *generator*; the *generative* part is delegated to *external* *multiview-diffusion* models (Zero123++ for *image-to-3D*, Instant3D for *text-to-3D*).
