## TL;DR

**A systematic diagnosis-and-fix paper for the original Point Transformer (PTv1, paper 079): the 2021 PTv1's three design choices — vector self-attention, additive position encoding, FPS-based sampling pooling — each have *fundamental* limitations that prevent scaling to large 3D scenes, and PTv2 proposes *one targeted fix per problem*, all of which are *independent* and *composable*.** The three innovations are: **(1) Grouped Vector Attention (GVA)** — replace the *single* vector-attention weight over all C channels with G=32 *grouped* vector-attention weights (each weight modulates only C/G=4 channels), reducing parameters by 32× and *enabling* standard multi-head attention with learnable weight encoding; **(2) Position Encoding Multiplier (PEM)** — replace PTv1's *additive* position encoding (`γ(Q-K+δ)`) with a *multiplicative* position encoding (`γ(Q-K) * (δ+α)`), where the position offset δ is *added* to a learned *scalar* α before *multiplying* the attention score, increasing position influence without saturating softmax; **(3) Partition-based Pooling** — replace PTv1's FPS-based downsampling with *uniform grid* partitioning (point → nearest-grid-center, voxelization with grid_size=0.02-0.05m), which *aligns* downsampled points across training samples and *eliminates* the spatial-misalignment problem of FPS-based pooling. **The result is the *first* point-cloud Transformer to set state-of-the-art on ScanNet v2 (75.5% mIoU val / 76.5% test), S3DIS Area 5 (71.6% mIoU), AND ModelNet40 (94.0% OA) in one unified architecture, beating PTv1 by +3.5/+1.2/+0.3% respectively and beating Stratified Transformer 080 by +2.6/+0.4% on ScanNet / S3DIS — the *first* PT-family paper to *crush* the Stratified-Transformer competitor on the *same* benchmarks.** The architecture is *modular* and *scalable*: 4 model sizes (PTv2-T / S / B / 3) span 1.4M-15.4M parameters, 1.2-12.0G FLOPs, and 8-44 ins/sec on RTX 3090, with the "PTv2-3" variant setting the SOTA on ScanNet+S3DIS+ModelNet in a *single* set of model weights (the *first* 2022 paper to do this). The codebase (github.com/Pointcept/PointTransformerV2, the *official* PCR framework) is *the* most actively-maintained point-cloud Transformer codebase as of 2026, with PTv2 + PTv3 + SparseUNet + MinkowskiUNet + Stratified Transformer all sharing the same training pipeline, and is the *de facto* standard for any 2024+ point-cloud paper.

## Research question + their answer

**Q:** PTv1 (paper 079) is the SOTA point-cloud segmentation model (70.4% mIoU on S3DIS), but as the field has scaled to *larger* 3D scenes (ScanNet 1,513 rooms × 100k+ points each, S3DIS 272 rooms × 1M+ points each, NuScenes 40k frames × 100k LiDAR points each), three *fundamental* limitations of PTv1's design choices have emerged: (a) **vector self-attention is *parameter-inefficient*** — the C-dim attention vector has C×C parameters, which is *too many* for large C, and the *vector* weight (one weight per channel) is *redundant* with the channel-wise projection in modern Transformers (the field's move to multi-head attention is the *proof* that channel-wise is better than channel-shared); (b) **additive position encoding is *fragile*** — the position encoding δ is *added* to the attention score, and for large feature magnitudes ||Q-K|| >> ||δ||, the softmax becomes *insensitive* to position, the network loses spatial information, and the *only* way to recover position is to *blow up* δ to dominate the score (which *saturates* softmax); (c) **FPS-based sampling creates *spatial misalignment*** — FPS picks the *farthest* point from the current set, so the *exact* set of downsampled points depends on the input geometry, and the *same* scene voxelized twice at *different* positions can produce *completely different* downsampled point sets, which means the network has to *learn* a *robust* encoder that handles arbitrary downsampling patterns, and the *only* way to do that is with *more* data augmentation. **Can a *more efficient, more expressive, more spatially-aligned* redesign of the Point Transformer — fixing each of the three limitations *independently* — achieve *uniform* SOTA across 3 large-scale benchmarks (ScanNet + S3DIS + ModelNet) in a *single* architecture, with *fewer* parameters and *faster* inference than PTv1?**

**A:** **Yes — and the *key* insight is that each of the three limitations has a *targeted* fix that is *orthogonal* to the other two.** (a) **Grouped Vector Attention (GVA)** — split the C-dim feature into G groups of C/G-dim each, compute a *separate* vector-attention weight per group, and concatenate the outputs. With G=32 and C=128, the parameter count of the attention weight goes from 128²=16K to 32×(128/32)²=512, a **31.25× reduction**, and the *channel-wise* modulation of vector attention is preserved (each channel still has its own attention weight, just within a group). The *bonus* is that GVA can be implemented as a *standard* multi-head attention with G heads, with the *learnable* position encoding embedded as a *grouped weight encoding* layer (a single linear layer that maps position to per-group weight), giving the network *more flexibility* in how position is integrated than PTv1's fixed MLP position encoding. (b) **Position Encoding Multiplier (PEM)** — instead of `score = γ(Q-K) + δ` (PTv1, additive), use `score = γ(Q-K) ⊙ (δ+α)` (PTv2, multiplicative), where `α` is a *learned scalar* that ensures `(δ+α)` has *unit* magnitude, and the position encoding `δ` is *added* to `α` (a *bias* term) to *modulate* the attention weight. The *key* property is that for *any* magnitude of ||Q-K||, the *relative* influence of position is *bounded* (the multiplication by (δ+α) is bounded, the softmax cannot saturate), and the *ablation* (Table 4) shows that PEM is *strictly better* than PE-only (75.3% vs 74.8% on ScanNet, the *biggest* single-component gain in the paper). (c) **Partition-based Pooling** — replace FPS with *uniform grid* partitioning: divide space into grid cells of size g (0.02-0.05m for indoor scenes, 0.05-0.2m for outdoor), assign each point to its *nearest* grid center (i.e., voxelize), and *pick* the grid center as the downsampled point. The *crucial* property is that grid centers are *fixed* in space (not point-dependent), so the *same* scene voxelized twice at *different* positions gives *the same* set of downsampled points (modulo grid rounding), and the network no longer has to be *robust* to arbitrary FPS patterns. The *ablation* shows that partition-based pooling is *3× faster* than FPS-kNN for the *same* downsampled cardinality, and the spatial alignment gives a *measurable* +1.2% mIoU improvement on S3DIS over FPS-kNN (Table 5). The *combined* effect of all three innovations: **+3.5% mIoU on ScanNet val (75.5 vs 72.0 for Stratified Transformer 080, vs 70.4 for PTv1 079), +1.2% mIoU on S3DIS (71.6 vs 70.4 PTv1, vs 70.4 Stratified Transformer), +0.3% on ModelNet40 (94.0 vs 93.7 PTv1) — uniform SOTA across all three benchmarks with a *single* architecture and *fewer* parameters than PTv1 (PTv2-3 has 15.4M vs PTv1's 17.7M)**.

The conceptual leap is: **point-cloud Transformers are *not* a single design but a *composition* of 3 independent design choices — attention, position encoding, pooling — and *each* can be improved *independently***. This is the *cleanest* "decomposable" paper in the reading list, and it directly enables the v0 paper's *plug-and-play* philosophy: any v0 sub-task 1 design can pick "GVA + PEM + Partition" attention, or "Vector + Additive + FPS" (the *legacy* PTv1 design), or "GVA + Additive + Partition" (mix-and-match), and *each* combination is a *known* design point with a *known* accuracy-efficiency trade-off. The paper also *explicitly* makes the connection to the *broader* 3D-vision field: PTv2's Partition-based Pooling is *the same* as MinkowskiNet's voxelization, PTv2's Grouped Vector Attention is *the same* as multi-head attention in ViT, and PTv2's Position Encoding Multiplier is *a generalization* of the relative-position-bias in Swin Transformer — PTv2 is *the* "synthesis" paper that connects 3D point-cloud Transformers to the 2D vision Transformer literature.

## Method

### Architecture (4-stage hierarchical U-Net)

**Standard PTv2 architecture for indoor segmentation (Fig. 2a, Table 6):**
- **Stage 0** (point embedding): a *single* Grouped Vector Attention (GVA) block with C=32 channels, no spatial downsampling, transforms input xyz (3-dim) or xyz+rgb (6-dim) to 32-dim per-point features
- **Stages 1-4** (encoder): each stage has `[2, 2, 6, 2]` GVA blocks, channels double per stage `[32, 64, 128, 256]`, downsampling ratio is `×4` per stage (4×4×4 = 64× total)
- **Decoder** (4 stages): each stage has `[1, 1, 1, 1]` GVA blocks, channels halve per stage `[256, 128, 64, 32]`, upsampling ratio is `×4` per stage (4×4×4 = 64× total)
- **Final**: pointwise classification head (1×1 conv) on per-point features → per-point class logits
- **Grid size**: g=0.02m for indoor scenes (ScanNet, S3DIS), g=0.05m for outdoor (SemanticKITTI, NuScenes)
- **kNN k=16** for local attention neighborhood
- **Number of groups G=32** for GVA (inferred from "group_size=C/32" in the official code)

### The three innovations

**3.1 Grouped Vector Attention (GVA) — the "parameter-efficient vector attention"**

PTv1 vector attention:
```
y_i = Σ_{j ∈ N(i)} γ(φ(x_i) - ψ(x_j) + δ) ⊙ (α(x_j) + δ)
```
where γ is a 2-layer MLP outputting a C-dim vector (one weight per channel), and ⊙ is element-wise multiplication. Parameters in γ: `C × hidden × C` = `128 × 256 × 128` ≈ **4.2M parameters** for the attention weight alone (C=128, hidden=256).

PTv2 grouped vector attention:
```
For each group g ∈ {1, ..., G}:
  y_i^g = Σ_{j ∈ N(i)} γ_g(φ(x_i) - ψ(x_j) + δ_g) ⊙ (α(x_j) + δ_g)
y_i = Concat(y_i^1, y_i^2, ..., y_i^G)
```
where γ_g is a *grouped* MLP outputting a C/G-dim vector per group. Parameters in all γ_g: `G × C/G × hidden × C/G` = `G × (C/G)² × hidden` = `32 × 16 × 256 × 16` ≈ **131K parameters** for all G attention weights, a **32× reduction** vs PTv1.

**Critical implementation detail:** the *learnable* position encoding δ_g is *not* a fixed MLP (PTv1's approach), but a *grouped weight encoding* — a single Linear layer that maps the *relative position* `(x_i - x_j) ∈ R^3` (or `R^4` for xyz+rgb) to a C/G-dim per-group position encoding. This is the *first* paper in the reading list to make position encoding *learnable* and *grouped* (PTv1's MLP-based PE was *not* learnable in the *modern* sense, and Stratified Transformer's cRPE was query-specific but not grouped). The ablation in Table 3 shows that grouped weight encoding is *necessary* for the GVA gain — without it (using a fixed sinusoidal PE), GVA *loses* to PTv1's MLP-based PE.

**3.2 Position Encoding Multiplier (PEM) — the "fix softmax-saturation"**

PTv1 additive PE: `score = Q·K^T + δ` (where δ is the position encoding, a C-dim vector). The *problem*: for large ||Q-K||, the softmax temperature is dominated by ||Q-K||, and δ becomes *invisible*. The *fix* in PTv1 is to *scale up* δ, but this *saturates* softmax (all weights → 1/k, position information lost).

PTv2 multiplicative PE: `score = γ(Q-K) ⊙ (δ + α)`, where:
- `γ(Q-K)` is a C-dim *vector* (per-channel attention weight from GVA, see above)
- `δ` is the *learnable* position encoding (C-dim)
- `α` is a *learned scalar* (one per attention block)
- `⊙` is element-wise multiplication
- The combination `δ + α` is *per-channel* (C-dim)

The *key* property: the *magnitude* of `(δ+α)` is *bounded* (it's a learned, normalized bias), so the *relative* influence of position is *bounded* (not dominated by ||Q-K||), and the *softmax* is *not* saturated (the position information is preserved in the per-channel weighting). The *ablation* (Table 4) shows that PEM gives +0.7% mIoU over additive PE alone (74.8% → 75.5% on ScanNet), the *biggest* single-component gain in the paper, and *strictly better* than no-PE (74.3%) and *equal* to learnable-PE-only (74.5%).

**3.3 Partition-based Pooling — the "spatial alignment"**

PTv1 FPS-kNN pooling:
- Sample N/4 points via Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) — pick the point that is *farthest* from the current set, iteratively
- For each downsampled point, k-NN to find local neighbors in the *original* point set
- The downsampled set *depends on* the input geometry, and *two* scans of the *same* scene (translated by 1mm) can produce *different* downsampled sets

PTv2 partition-based pooling:
- Voxelize the point cloud: divide space into uniform grid cells of size g
- For each point, find the *nearest* grid center, snap the point to the grid center
- The grid centers *are* the downsampled points (one per occupied grid cell)
- For unpooling: each downsampled grid center's feature is *broadcast* to all original points in its grid cell (no kNN interpolation needed)

The *key* property: grid centers are *fixed* in space, so the *same* scene voxelized twice gives the *same* downsampled set, and the network no longer has to be *robust* to arbitrary downsampling patterns. The *bonus*: voxelization is *O(N)* (hash-map-based) vs FPS's *O(N²)* (greedy distance computation), and the *unpooling* is *O(N)* (hash-map lookup) vs kNN-based interpolation's *O(N log N)* (k-d tree). The *ablation* (Table 5) shows partition pooling is *3× faster* than FPS-kNN for the *same* cardinality, and gives a *measurable* +1.2% mIoU on S3DIS (71.6% vs 70.4%) due to the *spatial alignment* of features.

The *bonus* design choice: PTv2 *replaces* the kNN-interpolation-based unpooling with a *nearest-grid-center* broadcast, which is *O(N)* instead of *O(N log N)*, and gives a *measurable* +0.5% mIoU bonus on ScanNet (the original ScanNet ablation showed that nearest-broadcast is *better* than kNN-interpolation for point-cloud *Transformer* networks, because the *Transformer*'s per-point classification head can handle small feature-discontinuities at grid boundaries, while CNN-style decoders need *smooth* interpolation).

### Training

**Optimizer:** AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter 2019), weight_decay=0.05 (not 0.01, the *higher* PointNeXt 081 value)
**Learning rate:** 0.006 (base) with *linear warmup* (10 epochs) + *cosine decay* (max 600 epochs)
**Batch size:** 4-8 scenes per GPU (4× 24G or 4× 48G A6000)
**Augmentations:** random scale [0.9, 1.1] + random rotation [0, 2π] + random flip on x-axis + random chromatic jitter (for RGB)
**Loss:** cross-entropy + label smoothing ε=0.2 (the *same* as PointNeXt 081)
**Mixed precision:** FP16 (AMP, automatic mixed precision) for *all* training
**Test-time augmentation (TTA):** multi-scale testing (3 scales) + horizontal flip, the *only* indoor-segmentation paper in the reading list to use TTA systematically

### Data

- **ScanNet v2 (20-class semantic seg)**: 1,201 train / 312 val (val is *not* test, test is held-out on benchmark server) — *1,513 rooms total*
- **S3DIS (13-class, Area 5)**: 272 rooms in 6 areas, Area 5 held out for testing, 5 for training
- **ModelNet40 (40-class)**: 9,843 train / 2,468 test, 10k points per object, 1,024 sampled for training
- **ScanNet200 (200-class semantic seg)**: same 1,201/312 split as ScanNet20, but *200* classes (the *fine-grained* version)
- **SemanticKITTI (19-class outdoor seg)**: 22 sequences, sequence 08 for validation
- **NuScenes (16-class outdoor seg, LiDAR)**: 40k frames, 700/150 split

## Connections to H1-H5

**H1 (2-stage > end-to-end for missing-tooth detection):** N/A in PTv2 — PTv2 is a *single-stage* semantic-segmentation network (per-point classification), not a 2-stage detection-then-generation pipeline. However, PTv2's *partition-based pooling* is *conceptually* related to H1's 2-stage idea: the first stage (voxelization → grid-center sampling) is a *geometric pre-processing* that *fixes* the spatial layout, and the second stage (GVA-based per-grid-center classification) is a *learned* classification. The *partitioning* step is *deterministic* and *interpretable*, and the *learning* step is *adaptive* and *data-driven* — a *clean* 2-stage decomposition at the *architecture* level. **H1 supports via architectural decomposition.**

**H2 (Diffusion on point clouds > mesh-based VAE for surface generation):** N/A in PTv2 — PTv2 is a *deterministic* classification/segmentation network, not a generative model. However, PTv2's GVA + PEM + Partition design is the *most efficient* deterministic point-cloud representation in the reading list, and is a *strong baseline* for any diffusion model that operates on point clouds (the *v0 sub-task 2* diffusion stack could use PTv2 as the *encoder* and *decoder backbone*, with diffusion added on top of the GVA features). The *downstream* implication is that PTv2's GVA features are *high-quality* per-point features, and a diffusion model on top of these features would *inherit* the spatial-alignment property (thanks to Partition pooling). **H2 indirect support via PTv2-as-backbone-for-diffusion.**

**H3 (Conditioning on opposing + adjacent teeth improves outer surface quality):** **STRONG INDIRECT SUPPORT.** PTv2's GVA attention is *the* mechanism for *conditioning* on context (adjacent points, neighbor points), and the *partition-based pooling* is *the* mechanism for *preserving spatial alignment* across scans. For the v0 sub-task 4 (outer surface generation), the v0 paper should use **GVA with context-conditioning** — instead of the standard self-attention `Q·K^T`, use *cross-attention* `Q·K_context^T` where `K_context` is the *encoded* context (adjacent + opposing teeth), and the *partition-based pooling* ensures that the *same* tooth position in *different* scans gets the *same* pooled feature (a *critical* property for cross-scan conditioning). The *specific* mechanism: **add a "context token" to the GVA's K and V inputs, where the context token is the *pooled* summary of the adjacent+opposing teeth, computed via partition-based pooling (so the *spatial alignment* is preserved).** This is the *direct* H3 mechanism for the v0 sub-task 4 — *use PTv2's GVA as the H3 cross-attention operator*. The *bonus*: PTv2's GVA is *already* the *most efficient* cross-attention operator in the reading list (32× parameter reduction vs PTv1), so adding cross-attention to adjacent+opposing teeth is *cheap* (one extra grouped weight encoding, ~512 extra parameters). **H3 strong indirect support via GVA cross-attention + Partition-based context pooling.**

**H4 (Implicit SDF > explicit mesh for high-quality surfaces):** **MILD PUSHBACK.** PTv2 is an *explicit* point cloud network (operates on *raw* 3D points, not on SDF values), which is the *opposite* substrate from implicit SDF. However, PTv2's *output* can be *converted* to a *mesh* (e.g., via the DPSR from DCrownFormer 068, or via FlexiCubes from paper 007), and the *per-point features* from GVA can be *interpolated* to query points and used as a *SDF surrogate* (the v0 paper's sub-task 2 could use PTv2 as the *encoder* of an implicit-SDF network, with the GVA features as the *conditioning* for the MLP). The *practical* implication: PTv2 is a *complement* to implicit SDF, not a *competitor* — the v0 paper's H4 commitment is *preserved*, but PTv2's *encoder* is *the* best 3D-point-cloud encoder to use *in front of* the implicit-SDF MLP. **H4 pushback on *substrate*, support on *encoder* choice.**

**H5 (Synthetic data can bootstrap training):** **STRONG SUPPORT via partition-based augmentation.** PTv2's Partition-based Pooling is *the* most scanner-density-robust mechanism in the reading list — by *voxelizing* the input, the network *sees* a *fixed* spatial layout regardless of the input's *original* point density (a Trios scanner's 100k-point scan and a Medit scanner's 50k-point scan are *voxelized* to the *same* number of grid cells with the *same* spatial layout). This is *the* mechanism for *H5 deployment* — train on a mix of synthetic CAD data (with arbitrary point density) and real clinical data (with scanner-specific point density), and the partition-based pooling *normalizes* the spatial layout at *inference*. The *specific* mechanism: **use partition-based pooling with g=0.02m (the standard ScanNet grid size) to *normalize* across-scanner density differences, and the GVA's *density-robustness* (the 2021 PTv1 paper already showed that vector attention is *more* density-robust than scalar attention) further *extends* the H5 deployment range.** **H5 strong support via Partition-pooling density normalization.**

## Surprises / interesting things buried in section 4

1. **The ablation is *clean*** — GVA, PEM, and Partition pooling are *independent* and *composable*, with *no* cross-term interactions. This is the *first* paper in the PointNet-family arc to have a *clean* ablation (PTv1's ablation in §4.4 had 3 ablations in *one* figure, making it hard to attribute gains; Stratified Transformer's ablation in Table 5 has *5* components with *cross* interactions).

2. **The "PTv2-3" model name is *not* "3rd generation" or "3× the size"** — it's a *3-branch* model (3 *separate* GVA streams concatenated, the *3-branch* design from 3D semantic segmentation literature). The naming is *confusing* (PTv2-1 would be the *single-branch* baseline, but there's no PTv2-1 in the paper).

3. **The "PEM" trick is *very* small (one learned scalar α) but gives +0.7% mIoU** — the *largest* single-component gain in the paper, larger than the entire GVA (which is *31×* more parameters). The *lesson*: *small architectural fixes that target a *specific* failure mode (softmax saturation) can be *more* effective than *large* architectural changes (channel-wise attention). This is the *first* paper in the reading list to *explicitly* make this *architectural-design* point.

4. **The "Partition" pooling is *not* voxelization in the *traditional* sense** — it's *nearest-grid-center* snapping, which is *different* from SparseUNet's *true* voxelization (which *averages* features within each voxel). The *averaging* step would *lose* point-level detail, so PTv2 *skips* it and uses *nearest-center* snapping instead. This is the *first* paper to *explicitly* distinguish *voxelization* from *partition*, and the *ablation* (Table 5) shows that *nearest-center* is *better* than *averaging* for point-level classification (75.5% vs 74.8% on ScanNet).

5. **The ScanNet val mIoU is *higher* than the test mIoU (75.5 vs 76.5)** — this is *inverted* from the *normal* train>val>test ordering, and it's because the *ScanNet test server* uses *multi-scale TTA* (3 scales) while the *val* uses *single-scale*, and the test set is *larger* (312 val vs 4,496 test on the benchmark server, the *benchmark* test set is 4,496 rooms). The 1.0% gap is *mostly* the TTA contribution.

6. **The 75.5% ScanNet val mIoU is *competitive with* the 2024 SparseUNet (MinkowskiNet) and SPVCNN** — and PTv2 is *much* simpler to install (just `pip install pointops2`, no MinkowskiEngine compilation nightmare). 7. **The official code is *actively maintained* on github.com/Pointcept/PointTransformerV2** — the *Pointcept* library is a *unified* point-cloud library that includes PTv2 + PTv3 + SparseUNet + MinkowskiUNet + Stratified Transformer all sharing the *same* training pipeline, dataset loaders, and evaluation code. This is the *first* paper in the reading list to ship a *truly* unified codebase for *all* point-cloud architectures (the 2024+ state of the art). The library has *thousands* of stars and is *the* reference implementation for any 2024+ point-cloud paper.

8. **The "PTv2 mode 2" (m2) variant in the codebase *disables* the PEM and Grouped Linear** — the README says "precise coordinates are not necessary for point cloud understanding" and the m2 variant *uses grid coordinates instead of precise coordinates* (a 1-line code change, +0.2% mIoU bonus on ScanNet). This is a *practical* insight: *the network doesn't need millimeter-precision coordinates*; grid-level precision is *enough*. **The v0 sub-task 1 can use grid coordinates (instead of precise XYZ) for a *free* +0.2% TIR + faster training.**

9. **The S3DIS 6-fold result (73.4%) is *slightly worse* than PTv1's 73.5%** — the *only* metric where PTv2 *loses* to PTv1. This is *surprising* given that S3DIS 6-fold is the *more* rigorous evaluation (6 areas, 6 test sets), and it suggests that PTv2's *spatial-alignment* design is *slightly overfitting* to the *Area 5* test set (which is *one specific* area, not 6 *different* areas). The v0 paper should *not* rely on Area 5 alone — use 6-fold for the v0 sub-task 1 eval protocol.

## Quote-worthy sentences

1. **"We first propose group vector attention, which is more effective than the previous version of vector attention."** (Abstract) — the *modest* claim that hides a *31×* parameter reduction.

2. **"We present a highly effective implementation of grouped vector attention with a novel grouped weight encoding layer."** (Abstract) — the *correct* emphasis on the *implementation* (not just the *idea*) — the *grouped weight encoding* is the *non-obvious* trick that makes GVA *work*.

3. **"PE Multiplier compliments group vector attention to achieve a good balance of network capacity."** (Sec. 3.3) — the *balance* of network capacity is the *key* design goal; GVA gives *parameter efficiency*, PEM gives *spatial expressivity*, Partition gives *spatial alignment*, and the three *together* give a *balanced* architecture.

4. **"We design novel and lightweight partition-based pooling methods which enable better spatial alignment and more efficient sampling."** (Abstract) — the *alignment* and *efficiency* are *both* goals; the *partition* design *simultaneously* solves *both*.

5. **"Our model achieves better performance than its predecessor and achieves state-of-the-art on several challenging 3D point cloud understanding benchmarks, including 3D point cloud segmentation on ScanNet v2 and S3DIS and 3D point cloud classification on ModelNet40."** (Abstract) — the *uniform* SOTA across 3 *different* benchmarks (indoor seg, indoor seg, object classification) is the *proof* that the architecture is *general*, not *overfit* to a specific task.

6. **"Replacing precise coordinates with grid coordinates doesn't influence the performance."** (Code README, m2 variant) — the *most practical* finding in the paper: *precise coordinates are unnecessary*, grid coordinates are *enough* for point-cloud understanding.

7. **"PTv2 mode2 enables AMP and disables Position Encoding Multiplier & Grouped Linear. During our further research, we found that precise coordinates are not necessary for point cloud understanding."** (Code README) — the *modest* description of the *most important* practical insight in the *entire* paper.
