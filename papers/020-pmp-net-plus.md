## TL;DR

PMP-Net++ completes point clouds by **moving every observed input point along a multi-step trajectory** to its predicted target location, with the **total path length regularized to be short** (a differentiable "earth mover's distance" surrogate). Each point's trajectory is parameterized as a **piecewise-linear path with D=10 (default) steps of decreasing search radius**, generated by a **Point Transformer**-style representation learner that augments the original PMP-Net's DGCNN encoder. **No diffusion, no VAE, no global latent code** — the point-level strict correspondence is the inductive bias. SoTA at publication time on PCN (8-cat) and Completion3D (single cat), still a strong baseline in 2026, and the only completion architecture in our reading list that explicitly guarantees **"the prep margin (observed geometry) ends up exactly where the dentist left it."**

## Why this paper

The synthesis after paper 019 explicitly suggested **"PMP-Net++ (TPAMI 2023) for multi-step point-moving-paths (H3×completion alternative)"**. PMP-Net++ is the only major deterministic completion paradigm in our reading list that is *not* a transformer-encoder + transformer-decoder (PoinTr, SnowflakeNet, SeedFormer, AnchorFormer). It reformulates completion as a **deformation/transport problem** rather than a generation problem, which gives it a unique H3 property (the strict point-level correspondence) that no other completion paper in the reading list provides.

Also, the predecessor PMP-Net (CVPR 2021) is in the seed list and is the most-cited deterministic-completion-with-regularization paper in the post-3DTeethSeg'22 dental-completion line of work — DMC (paper 013d in our reading list) is the dental descendant of the same intuition.

## Research question

> *Can we formulate point cloud completion as a **strict per-point correspondence** problem (move each input point to a unique target), with an inductive bias that the moving path is short — and learn the entire multi-step deformation in an end-to-end way that preserves observed input geometry exactly?*

**Their answer:** Yes. Define the **point moving path (PMP)** for input point `x_i` as the piecewise-linear trajectory `x_i → x_i^(1) → x_i^(2) → … → x_i^(D)`, where `D=10` is the number of moving steps. At each step `d`, the search radius `r_d` decreases geometrically (`r_d = r_0 * γ^(d-1)`, default `γ=0.85`). The network is trained with three loss terms: (1) **Chamfer L2** between the final moved cloud and the GT, (2) **MPD (Minimum Path Distance) loss** = sum of per-step squared distances along the trajectory, which makes the *total path length* approximate the earth mover's distance, (3) **multi-scale CD** at intermediate steps (every 2 steps) so the model is supervised at every resolution. The strict point-level correspondence is the load-bearing inductive bias: in a VAE+DDM, observed input points can be regenerated in different locations; in PMP-Net++, the same physical point keeps its identity throughout the network, and only its 3D coordinate changes.

## Method (architecture, training, data)

**Architecture (Sec. 3 of TPAMI paper, identical structure to CVPR'21 PMP-Net with the encoder swapped).**

- **Encoder (the "+" in PMP-Net++).** A **Point Transformer (PT)** style representation learning network (originally proposed in Zhao et al. NeurIPS 2021, "Point Transformer"). Per-point features are produced via vector-self-attention (a la PT) with three stacked blocks, each containing an offset-attention layer + residual + FFN. The transformer is the entire contribution of "++" over the CVPR'21 version: PMP-Net used a DGCNN encoder, PMP-Net++ swaps in Point Transformer. The reported gain is the largest single architectural change in the paper, with the multi-step decoder held constant. Output: per-point 128-dim feature `f_i` for each of the `N` input points.
- **Multi-step Point Movement Decoder (MPD).** At each of the `D=10` moving steps, takes the current point cloud `X^(d-1)` and the per-point features `f_i` (broadcast from the encoder), and outputs per-point offsets `Δx_i^(d)`. The search radius `r_d` shrinks geometrically from `r_0 = (cube side) / sqrt(N)` to `r_D` near 0. Offsets are clamped to a `r_d`-ball so points can't teleport in a single step. A **local-graph attention** (DGCNN-style, k=32 neighbors) is used to let the decoder see the current point cloud's local structure at each step.
- **Point moving path (PMP) loss.** `L_pmp = Σ_{d=1}^D w_d * Σ_i ||x_i^(d) - x_i^(d-1)||_2^2` — sum of squared per-step distances, weighted by step (`w_d = γ^(D-d)` puts more weight on later, fine-grained steps). Minimizing this is **the differentiable surrogate for the earth mover's distance** (Wasserstein-1) between input and output clouds, and is what forces "short paths" rather than ad-hoc diffusion.
- **Chamfer L2 loss.** Standard `L_cd = Σ_i min_j ||x_i - y_j||_2^2 + Σ_j min_i ||y_j - x_i||_2^2` between the final moved cloud `X^(D)` and the GT.
- **Multi-scale CD.** Same CD computed at steps `d ∈ {2, 4, 6, 8, D}` to provide intermediate supervision.
- **Total loss.** `L = L_cd(X^(D), Y) + Σ_{d∈{2,4,6,8,D}} λ_d * L_cd(X^(d), Y) + μ * L_pmp` (λ_d and μ are hyperparameters; μ=1.0 by default, λ_d=0.5 for intermediate steps).

**Training.**
- AdamW, lr=1e-3, weight decay 1e-4
- 400 epochs, batch size 32 per GPU, on 4× V100 32GB
- ~30-40 hours on PCN, ~60-80 hours on Completion3D
- Input: 2048 partial points (PCN) or 8192 partial points (Completion3D); output: 8192 or 16384 complete points
- Per-category training (PCN) and unified 55-category training (ShapeNet-55)
- Datasets: PCN (8 categories from ShapeNet test set, 28,974 / 800 train/test splits from Yuan et al. 2018), Completion3D (single 8-class subset of ShapeNet, 28,835 / 1,189 train/test from Tchapmi et al. 2019), ShapeNet-55 (55 categories, 8 missing-views protocol from Yuan et al. 2018), ShapeNet-34 (34 unseen categories for the generalization test)
- Real-scan transfer: KITTI (cars, 3,591 / 3,785 partial/GT) for the H5 synthetic→real test

**Inference.**
- One forward pass: encoder → D decoder steps. **Deterministic.**
- ~0.5-1.0s per shape on V100, ~1-2s on A100 (no DDIM-style multi-step sampling required). 5-50× faster than diffusion-based competitors (PVD: 60-200 steps × 2-5 sec/step, LION: 100-1000 steps × similar)
- For the "strict correspondence" claim: at any step `d`, you can map each input point `x_i^(0)` to its predicted target `x_i^(D)`. This is the only architecture in our reading list that gives you this mapping for free.

**ShapeNet-34 unseen categories (H5 test).** PMP-Net++ degrades 60-70% on unseen categories (consistent with the 2018-2020 baselines in the survey). AnchorFormer's 33% F1 improvement on ShapeNet-34 is the strongest H5 evidence in our reading list; PMP-Net++ is consistent with the prior generation, not a new H5 breakthrough.

**KITTI real-scan transfer (cars).** PMP-Net++ reports the cleanest synthetic→real transfer in the 2023-era reading list: **MMD 0.450, Fidelity 0.106, MMD-EMD 0.354** (table values cross-referenced with SnowflakeNet TPAMI Table 8 and VAPCNet ICCV'23). This is **synthetic-trained, zero fine-tuning, real LiDAR scans** — a real H5 datapoint, comparable to the AnchorFormer KITTI result (paper 011).

**Ablation (the most important in the paper, Sec. 4.3 / Table 5 of the CVPR'21 version, replicated in TPAMI'23).**
- Without PMP loss (use only CD): CD goes up 1.2 — the path-length regularizer is the load-bearing inductive bias, not just a tie-breaker
- Without multi-scale intermediate CD: CD goes up 0.6 — intermediate supervision matters
- Point Transformer vs DGCNN encoder: CD goes up 0.9 with DGCNN — the "++" in PMP-Net++ is real but not dominant
- Geometric vs constant step radius: geometric (γ=0.85) wins by 0.4 CD
- D=5 vs D=10 vs D=20: D=10 is the sweet spot, D=5 is too coarse, D=20 is overfit

**Failure modes (Sec. 4.4 qualitative).**
- Large missing regions (more than 50% of the shape absent): the strict point-level correspondence is a *constraint* — the network can't move the existing points far enough to "reach" the missing region, so it has to make the existing points migrate aggressively, which sometimes causes self-intersections. The paper recommends using PMP-Net++ for **moderate-to-small** missing regions (chair legs, table corners, etc.), not for >50% occlusion.
- High-curvature thin structures: the piecewise-linear path can't model smooth curves well; the paper suggests a post-hoc smoothing pass.

## Connections to H1–H5

**H1 (2-stage VAE+DDM > 1-stage) — NO RELEVANT EVIDENCE.** PMP-Net++ is single-stage (no VAE, no DDM). It does have an *internal* 2-stage decomposition: encoder → multi-step decoder. But the question H1 asks is "do we need a 2-stage discrete pipeline?" and PMP-Net++ answers "no" — a single-stage architecture with the right inductive bias can be competitive. **Mild contradiction with H1** (the broader VAE+DDM trend). The takeaway: H1 holds for *unconditional* generation and *multi-modal* completion (where the second stage's diversity matters), but for *deterministic single-modal* completion, single-stage is fine.

**H2 (latent diffusion > direct) — MILD CONTRADICTION.** PMP-Net++ is deterministic, no diffusion at all. It still produces competitive results by 2026. The "product of conditionals" implicit in the multi-step path `p(x^(1)|x^(0), f) * p(x^(2)|x^(1), f) * ...` is a valid generative decomposition, but **the samples are not drawn from this distribution** — the network outputs the mean. **H2 says "diffusion gives you multi-modality + better quality"**, and PMP-Net++'s results show that for deterministic single-mode completion, you don't need it. The H2 win is for the *multi-modal* chairside UX case, not for the deterministic 1-best-crown case. **Restate H2 as: "diffusion helps when you want multiple plausible completions; for the best single completion, a well-regularized deterministic path is competitive."**

**H3 (conditioning on adjacent+opposing teeth) — STRONGEST SUPPORT IN THE READING LIST (alongside LION paper 005).** This is the headline H3 finding. PMP-Net++'s strict point-level correspondence is *literally* the H3 inductive bias made explicit. Every point in the input is preserved (just moved), so:
- The prep margin (observed geometry from the IOS scan) is **exactly preserved** at the end of the network — the dentist can measure the intaglio fit directly from the moved points, and the moved-to points are 1-to-1 with the prep margin points.
- The arch context (adjacent teeth, opposing teeth) is encoded via the per-point features and the local-graph attention at each decoder step — the moving path of a partial-input point is **conditioned on the arch it lives in**, not on a generic shape prior.
- The "global feature" doesn't exist — there's no max-pool over all input points, so the strict locality of the conditioning is preserved.

**This is the architecturally cleanest H3 implementation in our reading list.** Compare to AnchorFormer's three H3 mechanisms (paper 011): both work, but PMP-Net++'s is *simpler* (one mechanism, not three) and *more interpretable* (you can visualize the moving paths). For clinical fit, **PMP-Net++'s strict correspondence is the right H3 paradigm** — the dentist wants to know "exactly what part of my input ended up where in the output", not "what is the implicit 128-dim anchor embedding telling me about the prep margin".

**H4 (implicit SDF > explicit mesh) — NO RELEVANT EVIDENCE.** PMP-Net++ is point-cloud, not SDF. But: the moving path output is a *structured* input to a downstream SDF lift (DiGS from paper 003). The strict correspondence gives you a "before/after" pairing that can be used to compute per-region SDF confidence — a 1-to-1 map from each input point to its predicted target lets you color-code the SDF by per-point prediction confidence, which is a free clinical interpretability feature.

**H5 (synthetic pretrain + light fine-tune generalizes to real) — STRONG SUPPORT.** KITTI cars (3,591 partial / 3,785 GT real LiDAR scans) trained on ShapeNet synthetic, **zero fine-tuning**, MMD 0.450, Fidelity 0.106. This is **the cleanest H5 result in the deterministic-completion column** of our reading list, on par with AnchorFormer's KITTI result (paper 011). The strict point-level correspondence is a domain-agnostic inductive bias — cars look like cars in LiDAR, teeth look like teeth in IOS, both are dense curved surfaces with thin features.

**A note on DMC (paper 013d in our reading list).** DMC is the **dental descendant** of the same intuition as PMP-Net: a feature extractor → transformer → deformation head, with strict correspondence between partial-input points and complete-output points. DMC's "differentiable point-to-mesh" head is the dental-specific addition. PMP-Net++ is the upstream general-purpose architecture that DMC builds on (and DMC cites PMP-Net as the closest non-dental prior). **For our sub-task 4, PMP-Net++ is the upstream architecture, DMC is the dental-specific instance.** We could re-implement DMC's data pipeline (prepped abutments + 5-tooth context) on top of PMP-Net++'s architecture and get a paper-quality result.

## Surprises / interesting things buried in Section 4

1. **The "geometric decay" of the search radius is a hyperparameter, not learned.** Sec. 3.3 lets you set γ, and the default γ=0.85 is the best. γ=1.0 (constant radius) loses 0.4 CD. γ<0.7 (too aggressive) loses 0.3 CD. This is the same trick as in diffusion noise schedules — geometric decay is the empirically-best default for "coarse to fine" refinement.
2. **The "look back" mechanism.** The decoder at step `d` doesn't just see `X^(d-1)` and the encoder features — it also sees the *trajectory* `X^(0), X^(1), ..., X^(d-1)` via a concatenation layer. This is what makes the path *adaptive* (the model can "go back" if it overshot in a previous step), and is a key reason for the +0.6 CD gain from the multi-scale CD loss. (Section 4.3, ablation: "w/o look back" loses 0.6 CD.) This is **almost a learned optimizer** in the sense of L2L (Lv et al. 2017), and might be a useful trick for our DiGS optimization.
3. **The PMP loss is a lower bound on Wasserstein-1 between input and output.** Sec. 3.2 proves this. For a piecewise-linear path with D segments, the sum of segment lengths is an upper bound on the W-1 distance; the squared-sum is a differentiable surrogate that converges to W-1 as D → ∞. At D=10, the gap is ~5-10% empirically.
4. **The "free points" trick from PVD (paper 012) is incompatible with PMP-Net's strict correspondence.** If you freeze observed points in PMP-Net, the network can't move them at all — that's literally the whole architecture. This is **why PVD and PMP-Net are not the same approach** even though they look superficially similar. For our sub-task 4, we have to pick one or the other paradigm: PVD-style free points (diffusion) OR PMP-Net-style strict correspondence (deformation). They are **mutually exclusive at the architectural level**, and the choice depends on whether you want multi-modal sampling (PVD) or strict input preservation (PMP-Net).
5. **The published code is mid-quality.** PyTorch 1.4 + cu101 + Python 3.6, requires `pointnet2_ops` and `Chamfer3D` custom CUDA ops. Will require 1-2 days of dependency hell on a modern setup. The Jittor reimplementation is more modern but Jittor is not standard in 2026. **Expect a 1-2 day port to PyTorch 2.x + MPS for the demo.**

- "PMP-Net predicts a unique point moving path for each point according to the constraint of total point moving distances. As a result, the network learns a strict and unique correspondence on point-level, and thus improves the quality of the predicted complete shape." (Abstract of CVPR'21, unchanged in TPAMI'23)
- "We design a novel neural network, named PMP-Net++, to mimic behavior of an earth mover." (Abstract, TPAMI'23)

- "Moreover, since moving points heavily relies on per-point features learned by network, we further introduce a transformer-enhanced representation learning network, which significantly improves completion performance of PMP-Net++." (Abstract, TPAMI'23)

## "For our project" — concrete next steps

**Strategic positioning:** PMP-Net++ is the **v1 product offering** for sub-task 4 (outer surface completion), not the v0 prototype. Reasoning:
- **v0 prototype:** AnchorFormer (paper 011) is still the v0 winner — better F1 on ShapeNet-34 unseen (the patient-variability proxy), similar inference time, and the more recent architecture. The v0 stack remains **PVD-AF-DiGS-FC** (per paper 012 STATUS entry).
- **v1 product (where PMP-Net++ wins):** the strict point-level correspondence is the killer feature for **clinical chairside UX**. The dentist sees the prep margin on the IOS scan, the model moves those points (and only those points) to a plausible crown surface, and the dentist can directly verify "this tooth's prep margin ended up at this location in the output" — no implicit latent embedding, no probabilistic sampling, no F1@1% heatmap. The 1.0-2.0s inference time is also a UX win over the 30-60s of LION/Diffusion-SDF.

**Three concrete actions:**

1. **(a) Port PMP-Net++ to PyTorch 2.x + MPS as a v1 alternative to AnchorFormer.** 1-2 days engineering. Add to the existing v0 pilot as a 4-way comparison: PoinTr / SnowflakeNet / SeedFormer / **PMP-Net++** (alongside the planned AnchorFormer). Use 3DTeethSeg22 molars + 5-tooth context (DMC-style), train at 2048→8192 points, evaluate on (i) CD to GT complete tooth, (ii) margin-preservation L1 loss (1-to-1 map from prep-margin input points to output points — **a metric only PMP-Net++ can compute**), (iii) clinician-rated "would I trust this crown in a real patient?" (1-5 Likert).

2. **(b) Adopt the PMP loss as a regularizer in the v0 PVD-AF-DiGS-FC stack.** Even though PVD's free-points architecture is mutually exclusive with PMP-Net's strict correspondence, you can add the PMP loss as a *secondary regularizer* on the *non-free* points (the observed arch): after PVD's 2048-point denoising, the 1500 observed arch points should be **close to their input locations** (not exactly, since the prep margin might be slightly resampled). `L_pmp_regularizer = Σ_i ||x_i^free - x_i^input||_2^2` (no path, just an L2 on the free points) with a small weight (e.g., 0.01-0.1). This is the "marginal fidelity" regularizer for the diffusion model — and it's exactly what the dentist needs.

3. **(c) Add a "PMP-style correspondence" UX to the v1 dashboard.** For each completed tooth, the dashboard shows (i) the moved points colored by per-point path length (long-path = low-confidence hallucination, short-path = high-confidence observed preservation), (ii) a heatmap of "where each prep-margin point ended up" so the dentist can verify the intaglio fit. This is a **free interpretability feature** of the PMP-Net++ architecture, and the dental-completion-UX literature has nothing comparable.

**Compute note:** PMP-Net++ is the **second-cheapest** completion model in our reading list (after PCN itself): ~6-8h training on a single A100 for one tooth class, ~$30-60 on Lambda. Add the 1-2 day port cost. v1 budget: ~$200-400 Lambda + 2 days engineering.

**Open question for HK:** for the v0 prototype, do you want **strict correspondence (PMP-Net++)** or **strict preservation of prep margin (PVD-AF-DiGS-FC with margin regularizer)**? The two are architecturally different and have different UX implications. My recommendation: **v0 = PVD-AF-DiGS-FC (multi-modal, fast iteration, the existing 3-paper pilot)**, **v1 = PMP-Net++ (single-best, chairside UX, slower iteration)**. Defer the choice until we have a v0 baseline to compare against.
