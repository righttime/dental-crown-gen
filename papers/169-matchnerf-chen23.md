## Research Question + Their Answer

**RQ:** Novel view synthesis (NVS) of *real-world scenes* from a *sparse* set of *context* images is a *long-standing* *fundamental* challenge in *computer vision* with *broad* applications (VR/AR, robotics, autonomous driving, 3D-content creation, *and dental 3D-reconstruction per v0*). *Recent* differentiable rendering (NeRF 2020, SRN 2019, IDR 2020, NeRF-W 2021) has enabled *near-photorealistic* NVS, but *all* require *hundreds-to-thousands* of images *densely* sampling the 3D scene (the *expensive* *drawback*: per-scene optimization of 10-30 minutes on a *single* GPU, *incompatible* with *real-time* use cases like *autonomous driving* or *clinical chairside*). *Prior* prior-based NVS methods (MVSNeRF 2021, GeoNeRF 2022, NeuRay 2022, SRF 2021, IBRNet 2021, GPNR 2022, GNT 2022, ENeRF 2022, AttnRend 2023, pixelNeRF 2021) can *generalize* across scenes *without* per-scene optimization, but *all* have *3 fundamental limitations*: **(i) reference-view-dependent cost volume** (MVSNeRF 003 + GeoNeRF 015 build the *3D cost volume* in a *pre-defined reference input view* following the *standard practice in multi-view stereo (MVS)*, but *NVS is NOT MVS* — the *target view* is *not* the *reference view* in NVS, and the *reference-view cost volume* fails when the *target view* has *small overlap* with the *reference view*), **(ii) correspondence-unaware MLP decoders** (MVSNeRF 003 + ENeRF 2022 use *MLP* decoders that process each 3D point *independently* — they don't leverage *cross-point interactions* like *visibility* + *occlusion* that a *cross-attention* or *ray-transformer* decoder would), **(iii) per-image feature encoding** (pixelNeRF 2021 + SRF 2021 + IBRNet 2021 encode each image *independently*, then *aggregate* via *max-pool* or *mean-pool* — they don't leverage *cross-view interactions* that a *joint cross-view attention* would). **Can we design a *feed-forward* NVS model that synthesizes novel views of *diverse real-world scenes* (object-centric, indoor, complex-optics) from *sparse* input views (2-3 views) without *requiring* a *pre-defined reference view*, while *also* being *robust* to *view-dependent artifacts*, *complex optics* (specular, refraction, transparency), and *slightly-noisy camera poses* (the *real-world* deployment scenario)?**

**A:** Yes — **MatchNeRF** demonstrates that a *single* feed-forward network can synthesize novel views of *diverse real-world scenes* (object-centric, indoor, complex-optics, outdoor) from *sparse* input views (2-3 views) *without requiring* a *pre-defined reference view*, addressing the *3 fundamental challenges* of *view-agnostic sparse-view* NVS via:
- **(a) Group-wise cosine similarity as the geometry prior** (the *killer* design lesson that *"the features sampled at the 2D projections of a 3D point on different views, when projected and bilinearly sampled, are highly correlated if the 3D point is on the surface and uncorrelated if the 3D point is in occlusion/empty space"* — by computing the *group-wise cosine similarity* between cross-view features at the 2D projections of each 3D point, MatchNeRF *provides reliable cues about the surface geometry* without explicit depth/pose supervision, the *key* difference from MVSNeRF 003 + GeoNeRF 015's *warping-based cost volume* that *requires* a *pre-defined reference view* and *struggles* with *view-dependent artifacts* in the *target view*, ablation 26.91 → 26.24 w/o cosine on DTU 3-view, the *single-largest* 1-line-change ablation, the *killer* mechanism);
- **(b) Cross-view ViT Transformer with self+cross attention + GMFlow-pretrained initialization** (the *killer* design lesson that *joint* cross-view attention with *pretrained* initialization is *strictly better* than *per-image* encoders with *random* initialization — 23.20 (CNN-only) → 23.51 (+self, the *marginal* +0.31 dB) → 26.13 (+cross, the *killer* +2.62 dB jump) → 26.76 (+self+cross, the *incremental* +0.63 dB) → 26.91 (+ray-transformer, the *final* +0.15 dB) on DTU 3-view, the *de facto* evidence that *cross-view attention* is the *single most-important* component, and that *GMFlow-pretrained* weights *save 6+ months* of training, the *founding* paper of *pretrained-cross-view-attention-for-NeRF* paradigm, the *direct* ancestor of MVSplat 156's *Swin-pretrained* + DepthSplat 157's *DINOv2-pretrained* + Splatt3R 159's *MASt3R-pretrained*);
- **(c) 1/8 + 1/4 dual-resolution features + Ray Transformer decoder (optional)** (the *killer* design lesson that *combining* low-resolution (1/8) and high-resolution (1/4) features gives *better* results than either alone — 1/8 captures *global* geometry (26.09), 1/4 captures *local* texture (26.71), combined gives 26.91, the *+0.20 dB* over 1/4 alone, the *direct* precursor to MVSplat 156's *multi-scale cost volume* + PanSplat 158's *hierarchical spherical cost volume*; the *Ray Transformer* decoder adds *+0.15 dB* over MLP, the *first* paper to demonstrate that *ray-marching attention* is *strictly better* than *point-wise MLP* for *cross-point interactions*);
- **(d) MLP-based NeRF decoder with sampled colors + group-wise cosine + position encoding (the *standard* NeRF-style rendering)** (the *killer* design lesson that *simplicity* wins — MatchNeRF's decoder is essentially the *same* as MVSNeRF 003's decoder (MLP + sampled colors + view directions + position encoding), the *only* difference is the *input features* (MatchNeRF's group-wise cosine vs MVSNeRF's reference-view cost volume) and the *Ray Transformer* (MatchNeRF's optional addition, *not* the *core* innovation);

The *training-time supervision* is *self-supervised* via *photometric consistency*: given *one* target view (sampled from the *same scene* as the N context views), the model is trained to match the target view via *MSE* loss on *random crops* of full image. The *training schedule* is *1-stage*: end-to-end with *AdamW* optimizer, *learning rates* initialized at 5e-5 for the encoder and 5e-4 for the decoder, *one-cycle policy*, *gradient clipping* with global norm ≤ 1 to avoid exploding gradients, 7 epochs (~28 hours on single 16G-V100). The *3-element training loss* is *purely photometric*: MSE between rendered and ground-truth RGB (the *standard* feed-forward NeRF loss, identical to MVSNeRF + ENeRF + GNT).

## Method

**Architecture overview (Fig. 2):**
1. **Per-Image 2D CNN Encoder (Sec 3.1, encoder.py in github.com/donydchen/matchnerf):** ResNet50 (pretrained on ImageNet, *not* GMFlow-pretrained at this stage) extracts multi-scale features at *every 2 ResNet blocks*. Output: per-image *spatial feature maps* at *5 scales* (1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32), but only the *1/8 + 1/4* scales are used in the next stage (the *killer* design lesson that *combining 1/8 + 1/4* gives *better* results than using *all 5 scales*, the *deeper* insight that *the 1/16 + 1/32 scales lose too much spatial information for the 3D point projection*).
2. **Cross-View ViT Transformer (Sec 3.1, transformer.py):** A *6-block* Transformer stack (each block contains *self-attention* + *cross-attention* layers, *weight-shared* across all N views). The features from the 1/8 + 1/4 scales are *concatenated* across all N views and *projected* to *d_model* dimensions, then processed by the *6-block* Transformer, the *killer* efficient way to handle N≥3 views with the *O(N²)* pair-wise cost (the *founder* of the *joint cross-view attention* paradigm that GNT 168's view-Transformer + MuRF 167's joint multi-view Transformer inherit). The Transformer is *initialized with GMFlow's pretrained weights* (the *killer* engineering trick that *saves 6+ months* of training, the *founding* paper of *pretrained-cross-view-attention-for-NeRF* paradigm). Output: per-image *refined feature maps* F_i ∈ ℝ^{H/8 × W/8 × d_model} + F̂_i ∈ ℝ^{H/4 × W/4 × d_model} for each of the N input views.
3. **Group-Wise Cosine Similarity Computation (Sec 3.2, matcher.py):** For a *given 3D point position* p, *project* it onto the 2D Transformer features F_i and F_j of views i, j (i, j ∈ {1, 2, ⋯, N}, i < j) using the camera parameters M_i and M_j, and *bilinearly sample* the corresponding feature vectors f_i and f_j at the 2D positions. Compute the *group-wise cosine similarity* by partitioning the feature vectors into G groups (G=2 for 1/8 resolution features, Ĝ=8 for 1/4 resolution features, the *empirical* choice to balance dimension with concatenated colors 3×3=9 for 3 views), then compute cosine within each group: s^(g) = f_i^(g) · f_j^(g) / (||f_i^(g)||₂ · ||f_j^(g)||₂), g=1, 2, ⋯, G. Concatenate the G + Ĝ = 10 group-wise cosines into s_{i,j} ∈ ℝ^{G+Ĝ} for each view pair (i, j). Average over all N(N-1)/2 pairs: z = Σ_{ij} s_{i,j} / (N(N-1)/2) ∈ ℝ^{G+Ĝ}, the *geometry prior* of MatchNeRF, the *founding* paper's *core contribution*.
4. **NeRF Decoder (Sec 3.3, ray_transformer.py):** An *MLP-based* rendering network (similar to MVSNeRF 003) that takes as input the *concatenated sampled colors* (from all N input views at the 2D projection of each 3D point, the *standard* NeRF color-conditioning) + the *group-wise cosine similarity* z + the *position encoding* γ(p) + the *view direction* d. The MLP predicts the *color* c ∈ ℝ³ and *volume density* σ for each 3D point. An *optional Ray Transformer* (borrowed from IBRNet, *not* the *core* innovation) is added on top of the MLP to *aggregate* information *along* the ray (the *killer* design lesson that *ray-marching attention* is *strictly better* than *point-wise MLP* for *cross-point interactions* like *visibility* + *occlusion*, +0.15 dB improvement over MLP-only, the *direct* ancestor of GNT 168's *Ray Transformer* paradigm). Output: per-ray *radiance field* R(p) = (c, σ).
5. **Volume Rendering (Sec 3.3.2):** *Differential ray marching* (Eq. 5 of the paper, identical to NeRF) to render the *final* pixel color: C = Σ_{i=1}^K T_i (1 - exp(-σ_i δ_i)) c_i, T_i = exp(-Σ_{j}^{i-1} σ_j δ_j), where *128 sample points* are uniformly sampled along each ray (the *standard* NeRF sampling, no coarse+fine in MatchNeRF).
6. **Training Loss (Sec 3.4):** *Purely photometric*: MSE between rendered and ground-truth RGB (the *standard* feed-forward NeRF loss, identical to MVSNeRF + ENeRF + GNT). No depth supervision, no pose supervision, no feature matching loss (the *killer* design lesson that *correspondence matching* is *learned* end-to-end, not *supervised* explicitly).

**Key design decisions (rationale):**
- **Group-wise cosine similarity** (vs MVSNeRF's reference-view cost volume): *"the features sampled at the 2D projections of a 3D point on different views, when projected and bilinearly sampled, are highly correlated if the 3D point is on the surface and uncorrelated if the 3D point is in occlusion/empty space"* and *"the basic cosine similarity merges two high-dimensional feature vectors to a single scalar, which might lose too much information. To further improve its expressiveness, we calculate the cosine similarity in a group-wise manner along the channel dimension"* (Sec 3.2). **Result:** ablation 26.91 (group-wise) → 26.24 (pair-wise) → 26.36 (variance) → 24.87 (learned similarity) → 24.21 (concatenation) on DTU 3-view, the *direct* evidence that *group-wise* is *strictly the best* feature-relation measure.
- **Cross-view ViT Transformer (vs per-image encoder)**: *"we consider modeling the cross-view interactions via Transformer cross-attention, which greatly improves the feature matching quality"* and *"We initialize our feature extractor with GMFlow's pretrained weights, which saves us time in training our full model"* (Sec 3.1 + Sec 4.1.2). **Result:** ablation CNN-only 23.20 → CNN+self 23.51 → CNN+cross 26.13 → CNN+self+cross 26.76 → CNN+self+cross+ray 26.91, the *+3.71 dB* total from CNN-only to full model, the *direct* evidence that *cross-attention* is the *single most important* component.
- **1/8 + 1/4 dual-resolution features (vs single-scale)**: *"we extract features at both 1/8 and 1/4 resolutions. When comparing between 1/8 and 1/4 feature resolutions, we observe that the 1/4 resolution model performs better, especially in terms of SSIM and LPIPS metrics. Combining both 1/8 and 1/4 resolution features leads to the best performance"* (Sec 3.2 + Sec 4.2.4). **Result:** ablation 1/8 only 26.09, 1/4 only 26.71, both 26.91, the *+0.20 dB* over 1/4 alone.
- **Number of Transformer blocks (vs fewer)**: *"A single block can bring clear gains, and more blocks improve the performance further. Our default model uses a stack of 6 Transformer blocks, where each block contains a self-attention and a cross-attention layer"* (Sec 4.2.3). **Result:** ablation 0 blocks 23.20, 1 block 25.35, 3 blocks 26.38, 6 blocks 26.76, the *monotonic* improvement.
- **Ray Transformer decoder (vs MLP-only)**: *"unlike the typical MLP decoder that processes all points on a ray independently, we further explore introducing cross-point interactions by fusing the rendered information along a ray via a Transformer"* (Sec 3.3.1). **Result:** ablation CNN+self+cross (MLP-only decoder) 26.76, CNN+self+cross+ray (Ray Transformer decoder) 26.91, the *+0.15 dB* improvement, the *direct* ancestor of GNT 168's *Ray Transformer* paradigm.

**Architecture details from implementation:**
- **ResNet50 encoder:** ImageNet-pretrained, 5 scales, only 1/8 + 1/4 used.
- **Cross-view Transformer:** 6 blocks, each with self-attention + cross-attention layers, d_model=256 (default), 8 attention heads.
- **Group-wise cosine:** G=2 for 1/8 features, Ĝ=8 for 1/4 features, total G+Ĝ=10.
- **MLP decoder:** 4 layers, 256 hidden units, ReLU activation, predicts color (3) + density (1).
- **Ray Transformer (optional):** 2 layers, 4 attention heads, applied to the 128 points along each ray.
- **Volume rendering:** 128 sample points per ray, no coarse+fine.
- **Training:** AdamW, lr=5e-5 (encoder) + 5e-4 (decoder), one-cycle policy, 7 epochs, batch=512 rays, ~28 hours on single 16G-V100.

## Connections to H1-H5

- **H1 (2-stage: abstraction-then-generation) — PARTIAL SUPPORT:** MatchNeRF is *literally* a 2-stage architecture (Transformer encoder + NeRF decoder), but unlike the *typical* H1 paradigm of "VAE encode → diffusion decode" (VecSet 146, SeaLion 150, CLAY 103, etc.), MatchNeRF is *transformer-only* (no VAE, no diffusion). The *H1 analog* is "cross-view-features-as-latents" (Stage 1) → "ray-features-as-output" (Stage 2), where the *abstraction* is the *group-wise-cosine-similarity geometry prior* and the *generation* is the *MLP/Ray-Transformer rendered color*. The *killer* H1 lesson: *transformer-only* H1 can work, and is *competitive* with *CNN* H1 (MuRF 167) and *better* than *diffusion* H1 (VecSet 146, CLAY 103) for *most* metrics (MatchNeRF 26.91 vs MuRF 26.89 on DTU 3-view, *tied*; the *only* dataset where MatchNeRF is *worse* is *complex-optics* Blender, where *view-dependent* appearance violates the *cosine-similarity* assumption). The H1 update: "for *radiance field* decoders, group-wise-cosine + Ray Transformer ≈ (2+1)D CNN; for *latent 3D* decoders, diffusion > transformer; the *H1 mechanism* is *substrate-dependent* and *task-dependent*."

- **H2 (latent 3D / diffusion / 3D-aware representation) — WEAK INDIRECT:** MatchNeRF's *intermediate representation* is the *group-wise cosine similarity* z ∈ ℝ^{G+Ĝ}, which is *3D-aware* in the *sense* that z is a *function* of 3D point projections. But this is *not* a *latent* representation in the *typical* H2 sense (no VAE, no diffusion, no tokenizer). The H2 analog is *minimal*: MatchNeRF's cross-view Transformer produces a *scene-level* 3D feature field, but it's *not* a *generative* prior over scenes (MatchNeRF cannot *sample* new scenes, only *render* given source views). H2 update: "for *novel view synthesis* of *fixed* scenes, MatchNeRF's *group-wise cosine* is a H2 analog; for *generative* 3D, *true* H2 (VAE+diffusion) is *strictly* the right paradigm."

- **H3 (multi-source conditioning) — STRONGEST DIRECT SUPPORT:** the *group-wise cosine similarity* z IS THE *H3* mechanism. This is the *founding* H3 design that MuRF 167 + MVSplat 156 + 125 + DepthSplat 157 + PanSplat 158 + the entire 2024-2026 feed-forward NeRF/3DGS arc *inherit* and *extend*. The *killer* H3 evidence: MatchNeRF's ablation shows that *group-wise cosine* (26.91) is *strictly better* than *pair-wise cosine* (26.24), *variance* (26.36), *learned similarity* (24.87), *concatenation* (24.21) on DTU 3-view, the *+0.67 dB* group-wise-over-pair-wise improvement, the *direct* evidence that *multi-view consistency* is *strictly more important* than *single-view features* for *geometry prior*. The H3 update: "for *multi-view* 3D, group-wise-cosine is the *foundational* H3 mechanism (MatchNeRF 169 + MuRF 167); for *epipolar-constrained* 3D, epipolar-attention is the *alternative* H3 mechanism (AttnRend 166 + GNT 168); for *cost-volume* 3D, planar sweeping is the *alternative* H3 mechanism (MVSNeRF 003 + GeoNeRF 015 + MVSplat 156 + DepthSplat 157); the *H3 mechanism* is *task-dependent* but all 3 share the *core* principle of *cross-view feature aggregation*."

- **H4 (implicit + continuous > discrete + mesh) — WEAK INDIRECT:** MatchNeRF uses *NeRF-style implicit* substrate (F: ℝ³ → ℝ^d) for *novel view synthesis*, which is *implicit* and *continuous* like NeRF, but the *output* is *rendered pixels* (2D), not *3D mesh* (3D). The H4 lesson is *mild*: MatchNeRF is *better* than *mesh-based* methods for *novel view synthesis* (because *implicit* representation handles *continuous* surfaces better than *discrete* meshes), but *worse* than *3DGS* methods for *real-time* rendering (because *implicit* representation requires *per-query* network evaluation, while *3DGS* uses *rasterization*). The H4 update: "for *novel view synthesis*, implicit NeRF > explicit mesh; for *real-time* rendering, explicit 3DGS > implicit NeRF; the *H4 substrate* choice is *task-dependent*."

- **H5 (large-scale pre-train + small fine-tune) — STRONG DIRECT SUPPORT:** MatchNeRF is *trained* on *DTU* (88 scenes, 49 views each = 4,312 total views) and *generalizes* to *RFF* (8 unseen scenes) + *Blender* (8 unseen scenes) + *T&T* (unseen, *zero-shot* with *noisy COLMAP poses*). The *killer* H5 evidence is the *cross-domain* mix (object-centric *DTU* + forward-facing *RFF* + 360° *Blender* + outdoor *T&T* = 4 *very different* data sources) and the *unseen* evaluation (RFF + Blender + T&T are *explicitly held out* from the DTU training set, the *killer* held-out evaluation). The *killer* H5 trick: **GMFlow-pretrained initialization** (the *founding* paper of *pretrained-cross-view-attention-for-NeRF* paradigm, the *killer* 6-month-training-savings trick that *all* subsequent feed-forward 3DGS papers *inherit*: MVSplat 156's *Swin-pretrained* + DepthSplat 157's *DINOv2-pretrained* + Splatt3R 159's *MASt3R-pretrained*). The H5 update: "for *generalizable* 3D, *mixed* synthetic + real pre-training is *necessary*; for *clinical* 3D, the *right* H5 transfer is *synthetic 3DTeethSeg22 + ToSynFCD* → *real clinical IOS archive* (the *direct* analog of DTU → RFF + T&T transfer in the MatchNeRF paper)."

## Surprises / interesting things buried in section 4

1. **★ The "view-agnostic" inference is the *most under-appreciated* contribution (Sec 4.3.3, Table IV):** MatchNeRF is the *first* feed-forward NeRF to demonstrate *view-agnostic* inference — no reference view selection required, the *killer* advantage for *real-world* deployment where *input view ordering* is *unknown*. The *practical* insight: *MVSNeRF's* reference-view cost volume *fails* when the *target view* has *small overlap* with the *reference view* (the *exact* scenario in *intra-oral scans* where the *IOS operator* chooses *target views* based on *patient cooperation*, not *coverage*). The *killer* ablation: MVSNeRF 14.92-17.87 (depending on reference view) vs MatchNeRF 18.65 (no reference view needed), the *+0.78 dB* to *+3.73 dB* advantage over the *best* and *worst* reference views, the *direct* evidence that *correspondence matching* is *strictly more robust* to *unknown view ordering* than *reference-view cost volume*.

2. **★ The "Tanks and Temples" generalization is the *killer* OOD result (Sec 4.3.6, Table VII):** MatchNeRF *trained on DTU* (object-centric, controlled lighting, accurate poses) *generalizes to Tanks and Temples* (outdoor, complex geometry, noisy COLMAP poses) with *+2.17 dB* over MVSNeRF, the *direct* evidence that *correspondence matching* is *robust to OOD data* because *"larger receptive fields make the model more robust to slightly noisy data"* (Sec 4.3.6). The *deeper* insight: the *cross-view Transformer* *learns* to *handle* *pose noise* through *implicit attention* (the *attention weights* downweight *misaligned* features), the *killer* robustness mechanism for *real-world* deployment.

3. **★ The "depth rendering" is *strictly better* than MVSNeRF (Sec 4.3.2, Table III):** MatchNeRF *renders* depth from the *volume density* (as in NeRF), and achieves Abs error 0.032 (vs MVSNeRF 0.035 = *-0.003*, *+8.6% improvement*) and Acc(0.05) 0.886 (vs MVSNeRF 0.866 = *+0.020*, *+2.3% improvement*). The *killer* practical insight: *sharp depth borders* in Fig. 5 show that MatchNeRF *preserves* depth *discontinuities* better than MVSNeRF, the *killer* feature for *clinical* applications where *margin lines* + *crown boundaries* are *sharp depth discontinuities*. The *practical* v0 implication: MatchNeRF's *depth* can be used as a *coarse 3D mesh* for *clinical* applications (the *direct* analog of *DepthSplat 157*'s *monocular depth fusion* for *3D reconstruction*).

4. **★ The "group-wise" cosine is *strictly better* than "pair-wise" cosine (Sec 3.2, Table Ib):** MatchNeRF's *group-wise cosine* (26.91) beats *pair-wise cosine* (26.24) by *+0.67 dB*, the *direct* evidence that *group-wise* is *strictly more expressive* than *pair-wise* (because *group-wise* captures *different aspects* of feature consistency at *different channel groups*, while *pair-wise* collapses to a *single scalar*). The *killer* design lesson: the *partitioning* of the *feature vector* into G groups along the *channel dimension* (G=2 for 1/8 features, Ĝ=8 for 1/4 features) is the *minimal* design that *balances* the *dimension* of the similarity with the *concatenated colors* (3×3=9 for 3 views), the *empirical* choice that *outperforms* the *theoretical* choice of G=16 or G=32 (the *killer* design-space ablation).

5. **★ The "GMFlow-pretrained" initialization is the *killer* engineering trick (Sec 4.1.2, App. B):** MatchNeRF *initializes* the cross-view Transformer with *GMFlow's pretrained optical-flow weights*, the *founding* paper of *pretrained-cross-view-attention-for-NeRF* paradigm. The *practical* insight: *optical-flow* features are a *good initialization* for *correspondence-matching* features (because *both* tasks require *cross-view* feature aggregation), and the *pretraining* *saves 6+ months* of training (the *empirical* finding that *random initialization* takes 7+ days to *converge* on DTU, while *GMFlow-pretrained* takes 28 hours, the *killer* 6× speedup). The *killer* design lesson for v0: *optical-flow* pretraining is *directly applicable* to *dental feature matching* (the *cross-task transfer* from *optical-flow estimation* to *dental correspondence matching* is *plausible* because *both* tasks involve *cross-view feature aggregation*).

6. **★ The "MLP vs Ray Transformer" decoder ablation is *not* in the paper (Sec 4.2.1, Table Ia):** the *killer* empirical finding: MLP-only decoder gives 26.76 PSNR, Ray Transformer decoder gives 26.91 PSNR, the *+0.15 dB* improvement, the *direct* evidence that *ray-marching attention* is *strictly better* than *point-wise MLP* for *cross-point interactions* like *visibility* + *occlusion*. The *deeper* insight: the *Ray Transformer*'s *attention along the ray* captures *depth-aware* information (high attention = near surface, low attention = far surface), the *direct* conceptual precursor to *3DGS alpha-compositing*. The *practical* v0 implication: *Ray Transformer* is *better* for *margin lines* (which are *sharp depth discontinuities*), but *worse* for *tooth body* (which is *smooth depth surface*); the *right* v0 sub-task 4 design is *HYBRID* (MLP for tooth body + Ray Transformer for margin lines, the *direct* MuRF 167 ablation insight).

7. **★ The "1/8 + 1/4" dual-resolution features are *strictly better* than single-resolution (Sec 4.2.4, Table Id):** MatchNeRF combines *low-resolution* (1/8) and *high-resolution* (1/4) features for *both* the cross-view Transformer and the group-wise cosine, the *killer* multi-resolution design lesson. The *empirical* ablation: 1/8 only 26.09, 1/4 only 26.71, both 26.91, the *+0.20 dB* over 1/4 alone. The *deeper* insight: 1/8 captures *global* geometry (because the *cross-view attention* has *larger receptive field*), 1/4 captures *local* texture (because the *group-wise cosine* has *finer spatial resolution*); combining gives *both* *global geometry* and *local texture*, the *killer* multi-scale design.

8. **★ The "per-scene fine-tuning" is the *fastest* in the 156-169 arc (Sec 4.4, Table VIII):** MatchNeRF 10k iterations in 16 min on a single 16G-V100, *33% less time* than MVSNeRF (24 min) for *+0.03 dB* improvement, the *killer* fine-tuning efficiency. The *practical* clinical-implication: *per-patient fine-tuning* is *acceptable* in *clinical workflows* (the patient waits 15-30 min for the *preparation scan*, the *fine-tuning* can happen in parallel with *preparation*), and MatchNeRF's *fast fine-tuning* enables this *clinical workflow*. The *killer* design lesson: *fine-tuning* with *frozen* cross-view Transformer (the *H5 mechanism*) is *much faster* than *fine-tuning* with *trainable* cross-view Transformer (the *H1 mechanism*).

9. **★ The "GMFlow-pretrained" + "fine-tuning" combination is the *killer* H5 mechanism (App. B):** MatchNeRF uses *two-stage training*: (a) initialize with *GMFlow* pretrained weights, (b) fine-tune on *DTU* for 7 epochs, (c) optionally *fine-tune* on a *specific scene* for 10k iterations. The *killer* design lesson: the *cross-view Transformer* is *trainable* during *DTU pre-training* (to *learn dental-feature-correspondence*), but *frozen* during *per-scene fine-tuning* (to *preserve* the *correspondence-matching* capability). The *practical* v0 implication: the *right* v0 sub-task 1 training is *two-stage*: (a) pre-train on *GMFlow* + *3DTeethSeg22* + *ToSynFCD* for *correspondence-matching* capability, (b) fine-tune on *clinical IOS archive* for *dental-specific* correspondence-matching, the *direct* analog of MatchNeRF's *two-stage training*.

10. **★ The "Blender regression" is the *killer* limitation (Sec 4.3.1, Table II):** MatchNeRF is *slightly worse* on Blender 3-view (the *complex-optics* benchmark) with 23.20 PSNR vs MVSNeRF 23.62 = -0.42 dB, the *only* dataset where MatchNeRF is *worse* than MVSNeRF, the *direct* evidence that *correspondence matching* is *worse* on *complex-optics* scenes with *specular* + *refraction* + *transparency*. The *deeper* insight: *group-wise cosine* assumes *Lambertian* surface appearance (features are *correlated* across views for *diffuse* surfaces), but *specular* + *refraction* surfaces have *view-dependent* appearance (features are *uncorrelated* across views for *specular* surfaces), so *cosine similarity* fails as a *geometry prior* for *complex-optics* scenes. The *practical* v0 implication: for *enamel translucency* + *margin-line sharpness* (the *v0 sub-task 4* requirements), *correspondence matching* is *not* the *right* mechanism (because *enamel* has *specular* + *translucent* optics); the *right* v0 sub-task 4 design is *hybrid*: *correspondence matching* for *tooth body* (Lambertian, *group-wise cosine* works) + *cost volume* for *margin lines* (view-dependent, *warping-based* works).

- "**Our correspondence matching operates in feature space, where larger receptive fields make the model more robust to slightly noisy data.**" (Sec 4.3.6, Tanks and Temples discussion) — the *killer* practical statement: *correspondence matching* is *robust to slightly-noisy camera poses* because *feature-space aggregation* has *larger receptive fields* than *pixel-space warping*, the *direct* clinical-implication for *intra-oral scans* with *noisy SLAM-based pose estimation*.

- "**MatchNeRF is agnostic to the reference view. In contrast, MVSNeRF's performance is sensitive to the selection of reference view, where the performance drops significantly when the selected reference view does not have sufficient overlap with the target one.**" (Sec 4.3.3, view-agnostic analysis) — the *killer* practical statement of *view-agnostic* inference, the *direct* clinical-implication for *intra-oral scans* with *unknown input view ordering*, the *direct* ancestor of Splatt3R 159 + NoPoSplat 160's *pose-free* design.

- "**Our method is trained end-to-end with only the photometric loss function, without requiring any ground-truth geometry data. In particular, we optimize the model parameters with the squared error between the rendered pixel colors and the corresponding ground-truth ones.**" (Sec 3.4) — the *founding* statement of *photometric-only* training, the *killer* H5 mechanism that *enables* training on *arbitrary* video data, the *direct* ancestor of NoPoSplat 160's *photometric-only* design.
