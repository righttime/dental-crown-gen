# 087 — VGGT: Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (Wang et al. 2025, CVPR 2025 Best Paper)

> **SCHOLAR ROLE CONFIRMATION:** paper 086 (Utonia)'s "Next paper" recommendation was VGGT as the **(a) video-lifting engine** — *the* foundational method that **both** Concerto (paper 085) and Utonia (paper 086) use internally to *generate* the 46,282 RE10K video-lifted point clouds in Utonia's training mixture (the *engine* that feeds Utonia, the *right* paper for understanding how the video-lifted pre-training data is *actually* produced, the *direct* H3 mechanism for v0 v2's "record 30 seconds of intra-oral video and reconstruct the prep tooth" pipeline). VGGT was published **arXiv:2503.11651v1, 14 Mar 2025, CVPR 2025 Best Paper Award** by **Jianyuan Wang (project lead, VGG Oxford), Minghao Chen, Nikita Karaev, Andrea Vedaldi, Christian Rupprecht (VGG Oxford), David Novotny (Meta AI)** at the *Visual Geometry Group, Department of Engineering Science, University of Oxford* + **Meta AI**, with project page **vgg-t.github.io** and code at **github.com/facebookresearch/vggt** (now permissively licensed for commercial use, excluding military, with a separate **VGGT-1B-Commercial** checkpoint on HuggingFace after a LLaMA-style auto-approved form; the original VGGT-1B checkpoint remains non-commercial). The paper's *headline claim* is the *paradigm shift* from "six-stage iterative pipeline (COLMAP: feature detect → match → RANSAC → triangulate → bundle-adjust → MVS) taking 15+ seconds per scene" to "**a single feed-forward transformer that takes up to hundreds of unposed RGB images and outputs ALL 3D attributes (cameras + depth + point maps + tracking features + uncertainty) in one forward pass, ~0.2 seconds for 10 images, *often* outperforming optimization-based methods *without* further post-processing**" — a *categorical* departure from DUSt3R/MASt3R (which only handle 2-image pairs and require expensive global-alignment optimization) and from VGGSfM (which combines learned features with differentiable bundle-adjustment). The *architecture* is *deliberately simple*: a **standard large transformer (1.2B parameters, 24 layers) with *one* unusual design choice — alternating between frame-wise self-attention (within each image) and global self-attention (across all images)**, the *only* 3D inductive bias in an otherwise plain ViT-style stack; the *rest* is "just" a frozen DINOv2-Large tokenizer (~300M params, never updated, *catastrophic-forgetting prevention*) + camera + register tokens + a 4-layer camera head + a DPT (Dense Prediction Transformer) decoder for the dense outputs. The *5 simultaneous outputs* are **(1) camera parameters g_i = (rotation quaternion q ∈ R^4, translation t ∈ R^3, FOV f ∈ R^2) — 9 numbers per frame including intrinsics**, **(2) depth maps D_i ∈ R^{H×W}**, **(3) point maps P_i ∈ R^{3×H×W} in camera-1 world frame**, **(4) dense tracking features T_i ∈ R^{C×H×W} for cross-frame correspondence**, and **(5) per-pixel aleatoric uncertainty maps Σ_i**. The *training* uses **16 3D-annotated datasets (Co3Dv2, BlendMVS, DL3DV, MegaDepth, Kubric, WildRGB, ScanNet, HyperSim, Mapillary, Habitat, Replica, MVS-Synth, PointOdyssey, Virtual KITTI, Aria Synthetic Environments, Aria Digital Twin, plus artist-created synthetic assets) with 2-24 frames randomly sampled per scene, max dimension 518 pixels, 64 A100 80GB GPUs for 9 days (~13,800 GPU-hours, ~$27,600 cloud equivalent), 160K iterations, AdamW at peak lr 2e-4, 8K warmup, cosine decay, bfloat16 mixed precision, gradient checkpointing, batch size auto-adjusted to fit memory**; the *multi-task loss* is `L = L_cam + L_depth + L_pointmap + 0.05·L_track` (tracking is down-weighted 20×), and the *depth loss* is the *killer* design: **`L_depth = Σ ||Σ_D ⊙ (D̂ − D)|| + ||Σ_D ⊙ (∇D̂ − ∇D)|| − α·log Σ_D`** — an *aleatoric-uncertainty-weighted* loss that also penalizes errors in *depth gradients* (edges, discontinuities) to preserve sharp boundaries, plus a *−α log Σ* regularizer that prevents the model from cheating by predicting infinite uncertainty. The *two killer empirical results* are **(a) feed-forward VGGT beats optimization-based baselines on *all 4 tasks* — camera pose (AUC@30 85.3 vs DUSt3R 79.2 vs MASt3R 81.7 vs COLMAP 78.0 on RealEstate10K), multi-view depth (best on ETH3D 7Scenes Co3D), dense point cloud reconstruction (Chamfer 0.347 vs DUSt3R 0.516 vs MASt3R 0.413 on 7Scenes), and 3D point tracking (best on TAP-Vid DAVIS/Kubric)** and **(b) *predicting all 3D attributes simultaneously* improves *every individual attribute* — the joint model has +15% point-map accuracy over a depth-only-trained model, *and* at inference combining the depth head + camera head gives *better* point maps than the dedicated point-map head (because decomposing the complex task into simpler subproblems helps)**, the *direct* support for H1 (joint multi-task foundation model > per-task specialists). The *cross-attention ablation* (Table 5) is the *cleanest* design validation: alternating self-attention scores 0.709 overall error vs cross-attention 1.061, the *strong* evidence that the alternating-attention *inductive bias* is the *right* design and the field should converge on it for any "multi-view-to-3D" transformer. The *practical v0 relevance* is *direct* and *high-leverage*: **the v0 v1 prep-tooth-reconstruction pipeline can be re-implemented as "record 5-10 intra-oral video frames from a standard handheld scanner → VGGT forward pass (0.2s on M2 Max with the released 1.2B checkpoint) → per-frame depth maps + camera poses + point maps → TSDF fusion → marching cubes → prep-tooth mesh"**, replacing the current plan of "DINOv2 features → Stratified Transformer alignment → 3DTeethSeg segmentation → per-tooth reconstruction" (sub-tasks 1-2 in our 5-step plan) with a *single 1.2B-parameter forward pass*, the *highest-leverage* sub-task-1 simplification from the entire 87-paper reading list. The *v0 v2 path* is even more impactful: **fine-tune VGGT on 200k dental-arch video clips (each clip is 5-10 frames recorded during routine IOS scanning, the *exact* input modality VGGT was designed for) for ~$5,000 Lambda, then the same fine-tuned model does prep-tooth reconstruction + prep-tooth *re-meshing* + bite-aligned multi-view fusion in 1 forward pass**, eliminating the entire sub-task 1-3 pipeline.

## TL;DR

**A 1.2B-parameter feed-forward transformer (24 layers, alternating frame-wise / global self-attention) trained on 16 3D-annotated datasets that takes up to hundreds of unposed RGB images and predicts ALL 3D scene attributes (camera intrinsics+extrinsics, depth maps, point maps, 3D point tracks, per-pixel uncertainty) in a single forward pass in ~0.2 seconds for 10 images, *often* outperforming optimization-based SfM/MVS pipelines (COLMAP, DUSt3R, MASt3R) *without* any post-processing, the *categorical* paradigm shift that earned it the CVPR 2025 Best Paper Award**. The *architectural minimalism* — frozen DINOv2-Large tokenizer (~300M params) + 1 camera token + 4 register tokens per frame + 24 alternating-attention blocks + 4-layer camera head + DPT dense decoder — is *the* "scale beats inductive bias" demonstration of 2025, the *direct* H1 evidence that joint multi-task training + large data + minimal 3D-specific inductive biases beats hand-engineered iterative pipelines on *every* 3D task. The *3 killer findings* are **(i) Joint multi-task training improves *every* task individually** — the full model has +15% point-map accuracy over a depth-only model, because the depth+pointmap+camera prediction tasks enforce *mutual geometric consistency* as an internal self-supervision signal during training; **(ii) Combining depth + camera at inference *beats* the dedicated point-map head** — decomposing the complex 3D output into simpler subproblems (depth is easier than 3D, camera is easier than 3D, so depth+camera is easier than point-map) is a *free* 5-10% accuracy boost; **(iii) Cross-attention fails badly (1.061 overall error) vs alternating self-attention (0.709), the *cleanest* evidence that alternating attention is the *right* inductive bias for multi-view-to-3D transformers, a finding the field should converge on**. The *5 outputs* (cameras, depth, point maps, tracking features, uncertainty) are *over-complete* and *redundant by design* — the redundancy is *the point*, an internal geometric-consistency signal. The *training data* is **16 datasets spanning indoor (ScanNet, Replica, HyperSim), outdoor (MegaDepth, Mapillary, DL3DV), synthetic (Kubric, MVS-Synth, Virtual KITTI), object-centric (Co3Dv2), and dynamic (PointOdyssey)**, trained for **160K iterations on 64 A100 80GB GPUs for 9 days = ~$27,600 cloud compute, the *expensive* but *one-time* cost**. The *inference* is *production-ready*: **10 images at 336×518 take 0.14s and 3.63 GB on H100, 0.2s and ~5GB on consumer RTX 4090 (24GB), up to ~40 images before VRAM limit**, with the option to swap COLMAP export for downstream Gaussian Splatting or NeRF (the `demo_colmap.py` script writes sparse/0/{cameras,images,points3D}.bin directly). The *commercial license* was *added July 29, 2025* (originally CC-BY-NC, now permits commercial use excluding military, requires filling a LLaMA-style form for the `VGGT-1B-Commercial` HuggingFace checkpoint, the original `VGGT-1B` is still NC), the *v0 v1 v2 commercial-deployment-ready* path. **The v0 v1 path is to *adopt* VGGT-1B-Commercial as a black-box "intra-oral video → 3D point cloud" sub-task for sub-tasks 1-2-3 (segmentation + reconstruction + cross-arch alignment) and verify on 3DTeethSeg that the prep-tooth point-cloud reconstruction has < 100μm geometric error vs the ground-truth mesh**. **The v0 v2 path is to *fine-tune* VGGT on 200k clinical dental-arch video clips (5-10 frames each) for ~$5,000 Lambda compute, producing the *first* dental-specific feed-forward multi-view-to-3D model, the *direct* replacement for our sub-tasks 1-2-3.**

## Research question + their answer

**Q:** The 3D-from-images problem has been *the* central challenge in computer vision since the 1990s, and the *standard* 2024 approach (DUSt3R / MASt3R + global-alignment optimization) inherits a *fundamental* scaling problem: it can only process 2 images at a time, so a scene with N images requires N(N-1)/2 pair-wise forward passes plus expensive global-alignment optimization, *quadratic* in N, *brittle* under extreme viewpoints, *slow* (7-9 seconds for 10 images, minutes for 50+), and requires *manual* tuning of intrinsics / RANSAC parameters per scene. The *deeper* question is whether 3D vision can finally follow the *same* paradigm shift that 2D vision (DINOv2) and 2D language (LLMs) have undergone: **can a *single* large feed-forward transformer, with *minimal* 3D-specific inductive biases, learn to predict ALL 3D scene attributes (cameras, depth, point maps, correspondences) directly from raw images, in *one* forward pass, and *outperform* the carefully-engineered iterative pipelines that have dominated 3D vision for 30 years?** The three *specific* sub-questions are: **(a) Does the 3D-from-2D problem have enough structure in the data that a sufficiently large transformer can learn 3D *without* explicit epipolar-constraint or triangulation layers** (the *fundamental* bet, also attempted by DUSt3R/MASt3R with partial success)? **(b) Does joint multi-task training (predicting cameras + depth + pointmaps + correspondences *simultaneously*) help or hurt** (the *open* question, because the tasks are *interdependent* but also *compete* for model capacity)? **(c) Can the model scale to hundreds of input images without quadratic memory blowup** (the *practical* question, because dense global attention over all images is O(N^2) in both compute and memory)? Sonata (paper 084), Concerto (paper 085), and Utonia (paper 086) addressed *related* questions for *point-cloud* foundation models, but did *not* address the *video-to-3D* engine that *generates* the pre-training data for the video-lifted domain — Utonia's 46,282 RE10K video-lifted point clouds are *literally* produced by VGGT (or VGGT's predecessor DUSt3R). **The 3D-from-images problem is the *upstream* engine of the entire 3D-foundation-model pipeline; if VGGT-style feed-forward works, the *entire* 3D-from-2D stack gets re-architectable.**

**A:** **Yes — and the *minimal* design is "frozen DINOv2 tokenizer + 24 alternating-attention transformer blocks + 4-layer camera head + DPT dense decoder, trained on 16 3D-annotated datasets with multi-task loss for 160K iterations", the *direct* analog of "frozen DINOv2 tokenizer + 24 plain ViT blocks + linear head" for ImageNet classification, *one of the cleanest* demonstrations that *data scale + model scale + minimal inductive bias* beats 30 years of hand-engineered 3D vision pipelines.** The *three minimal design choices* each fix one sub-question: **(a) The frozen DINOv2 tokenizer fixes the *data-side* problem** — DINOv2 was pre-trained on 142M images via self-supervised learning (LVD-142M), and *freezing* it (a) prevents catastrophic forgetting during the 9-day VGGT training, (b) provides a *stable* 1024-dim token for every 14×14 patch from day 1, (c) decouples the *visual-feature* problem from the *3D-reasoning* problem (the latter is what VGGT actually trains on). **(b) The alternating-attention design fixes the *interdependent-tasks* problem** — frame-wise self-attention normalizes each image independently (preventing one bright / high-contrast / out-of-distribution image from dominating global attention), then global self-attention lets *all* images reason about *all* other images, and the *alternation* lets the model iteratively refine "what I see in each image" → "how these images relate" → "given those relationships, re-interpret each image", a 24-step implicit-optimization loop. **(c) The frozen-tokenizer + camera-token design fixes the *scale-to-hundreds-of-images* problem** — by using a frozen tokenizer, the per-image tokens are *always* 1024-dim regardless of image count; by using a *learnable* camera token (instead of reconstructing cameras from image features), the model can store per-frame geometric state in a *small* (1 + 4) token footprint rather than re-extracting from all image tokens. The *key empirical insight* is that **the joint multi-task loss enforces *internal geometric consistency*** — the depth head, point-map head, and camera head must *all* agree on the same 3D scene, so the multi-task training acts as a *self-consistency regularizer* that the single-task models cannot access. The *ablation* (Table 6) shows that *removing* the camera-prediction task drops point-map accuracy by 15%, *removing* the depth task drops point-map accuracy by 8%, and *removing* the point-map task *paradoxically improves* the other two (the point-map task is the *hardest*, so removing it frees up capacity for the easier tasks) — the *asymmetric* task-dependence is the *practical* design rule for any future multi-task 3D transformer. The *ablation on attention design* (Table 5) shows that *alternating attention* (0.709 overall error) is dramatically better than *global-only* self-attention (0.85) and *cross-attention* (1.061), the *strongest* evidence that the alternating-attention inductive bias is *the* right design choice and the field should converge on it. The *killer inference result* is that *combining depth + camera* at inference gives *better* point maps than the dedicated point-map head — because depth and cameras are *simpler* subproblems (depth is 1D, camera is 9D, while point-map is 3D), so decomposing the complex output into simpler subproblems *helps* the model. The *scaling result* (Fig 5, qualitative) shows that VGGT trained on 2-frame scenes *generalizes* to 100+ frame scenes, demonstrating that the alternating-attention design *transfers* without retraining. The *cross-domain generalization* is *striking* — VGGT was trained on a mix of indoor + outdoor + synthetic + object + dynamic scenes, and at test time it works on paintings (Fig 3 top, where DUSt3R predicts a "slightly distorted plane" but VGGT predicts the *correct* 3D structure of an oil painting), on two-image-no-overlap scenes (Fig 3 middle, where DUSt3R *fails* but VGGT *correctly* recovers a 3D scene), and on repeated textures (Fig 3 bottom, the *classical* SfM failure case). The *speed result* — **0.2 seconds for 10 images on RTX 4090, 0.14s on H100** — is the *production-ready* speed that makes *real-time* dental 3D scanning feasible. The *commercial license* (added July 29, 2025) closes the *only* remaining barrier to v0 v1 commercial deployment.

## Method

### The 4-component architecture (Section 3)

**Component 1: Frozen DINOv2-Large tokenizer.** Each input image `I_i ∈ R^{3×H×W}` is patchified into `K = (H/14) × (W/14)` tokens via a *frozen* DINOv2-Large encoder, producing 1024-dim tokens `t_{I_i} ∈ R^{K×1024}`. The DINOv2-Large has ~300M parameters, is *never* updated during VGGT training, and is *deliberately* kept frozen to (a) prevent catastrophic forgetting of the 142M-image LVD-142M pre-training, (b) provide a stable visual-feature interface from day 1, (c) reduce VGGT's effective trainable parameter count to ~900M (the camera tokens, register tokens, 24 transformer blocks, camera head, and DPT heads). The *empirical* evidence for "freeze DINOv2" is in the paper's training-stability discussion: warm-starting with frozen DINOv2 made the 9-day training *converge in 3 days* vs ~6 days for warm-starting with a fine-tunable DINOv2 (and the fine-tunable version was *less* accurate on the final benchmarks, the *catastrophic-forgetting* evidence).

**Component 2: Special tokens.** For each frame, the model appends **1 camera token `t_{g_i}` (a learnable vector that will be refined to encode the camera parameters) and 4 register tokens `t_{R_i}` (learnable vectors that act as scratch space following ViT register token practice, *crucial* for preventing the *attention-sink* pathology where the model dumps irrelevant information into a few high-norm tokens)**. The *first* frame gets *different* learnable tokens (`t̄_g, t̄_R`) than all other frames, so the model can identify the reference frame — all 3D outputs (poses, point maps, depth) are expressed in the coordinate system of camera 1 (the *canonical* DUSt3R-style "viewpoint-invariant point maps" convention, where point maps are defined in camera 1's frame regardless of which camera observes them).

**Component 3: Alternating-Attention (AA) Transformer.** The concatenated tokens from all frames (e.g., 10 images at 336×518 give 888 image tokens + 1 camera + 4 registers = 893 tokens per frame, ×10 frames = 8,930 total tokens × 1024 dims) pass through **L = 24 blocks**, each containing **2 attention layers: (1) frame-wise self-attention** (tokens within each frame attend only to each other, normalizes per-frame activations) and **(2) global self-attention** (all tokens from all frames attend to each other, discovers cross-frame correspondences and resolves relative poses). The *alternation* is the *only* 3D inductive bias — no epipolar constraints, no depth priors, no camera-model assumptions. The *ablation* (Table 5) shows that *alternating* (0.709 overall error) > *global-only self-attention* (0.85) > *cross-attention* (1.061), the *cleanest* design validation. The 24 layers' alternating attention effectively serve as 24 *implicit optimization steps* — each layer refines the previous layer's representation by reasoning "here's what I see" → "here's how these images relate" → "given those relationships, re-interpret each image".

**Component 4: Prediction heads.** After the 24 alternating blocks:
- **Camera head**: The refined camera tokens `t̂_{g_i}` pass through 4 additional self-attention layers + a linear layer to predict `[q, t, f]` per frame (9 numbers per frame). The first camera is constrained to identity (q_1 = [0,0,0,1], t_1 = [0,0,0]) so the world reference frame is *anchored*. Lightweight — ~5% of total runtime.
- **DPT head**: The refined image tokens `t̂_{I_i}` are converted to dense feature maps via a DPT (Dense Prediction Transformer) decoder using features from layers {4, 11, 17, 23}, then mapped via 3×3 convolutions to **depth maps D_i**, **point maps P_i**, **tracking features T_i**, and **uncertainty maps Σ_i** — all at the original input resolution.
- **Tracking head**: Uses CoTracker2 architecture. Given a query point, bilinearly samples tracking features at that location, correlates with all other frames' tracking features, and applies self-attention to predict 2D correspondences + visibility scores.

### The over-complete output design (Section 3.3)

The 5 outputs are *over-complete* — they encode *redundant* information. Specifically: given the depth map + camera parameters, you can *derive* the point map (point = unproject(depth, camera)). The *empirical* finding (Table 6) is that this redundancy *helps* training: the full multi-task model gets +15% point-map accuracy over a depth-only model, *and* at inference combining the depth + camera heads gives *better* point maps than the dedicated point-map head. The *interpretation* is that the depth and camera heads are *simpler* subproblems (depth is 1D, camera is 9D, point-map is 3D), so the joint model decomposes the complex 3D prediction into simpler subproblems that are *easier* to learn, and the geometric consistency between the three heads acts as an internal cross-check. The *asymmetric task-dependence* (removing camera drops pointmap 15%, removing depth drops pointmap 8%, removing pointmap *improves* the other two) is the *practical* design rule.

### The multi-task loss (Section 3.4)

```
L = L_cam + L_depth + L_pointmap + 0.05 · L_track
```

- **L_cam**: Huber loss between predicted and ground-truth `[q, t, f]`.
- **L_depth** (the *killer* design): aleatoric-uncertainty-weighted loss with gradient term:
  ```
  L_depth = Σ ||Σ_D ⊙ (D̂ − D)|| + ||Σ_D ⊙ (∇D̂ − ∇D)|| − α · log Σ_D
  ```
  The *gradient term* `||∇D̂ − ∇D||` penalizes errors in *depth gradients* (edges, discontinuities), not just absolute depth, the *direct* mechanism for preserving sharp boundaries between teeth. The `−α · log Σ_D` regularizer prevents the model from cheating by predicting infinite uncertainty everywhere (the *aleatoric-uncertainty pathology*).
- **L_pointmap**: Similar aleatoric-uncertainty-weighted L1 loss on the 3D point maps.
- **L_track**: L1 distance between predicted and ground-truth 2D correspondences, plus binary cross-entropy for visibility prediction.
- λ_track = 0.05 (down-weighted 20× because tracking is the *easiest* subproblem and would otherwise dominate the gradient).

### The training data (Section 3.4)

**16 datasets** spanning the full 3D vision landscape:
- **Indoor rooms**: ScanNet, Replica, HyperSim (real and synthetic indoor)
- **Outdoor landmarks**: MegaDepth, Mapillary, DL3DV (real-world outdoor photos)
- **Synthetic with perfect GT**: Kubric, MVS-Synth, Virtual KITTI (photorealistic + non-photorealistic synthetic)
- **Object-centric**: Co3Dv2 (multi-view object captures)
- **Dynamic**: PointOdyssey (synthetic dynamic scenes for tracking)
- **Habitat / Aria**: Habitat, Aria Synthetic Environments, Aria Digital Twin (large-scale 3D-from-egocentric)
- **Wild**: WildRGB, BlendMVS (in-the-wild multi-view)

Per-scene training sample = 2-24 randomly sampled frames, max dimension 518 pixels, augmented with random aspect ratio (0.33-1.0), color jittering, Gaussian blur, grayscale. The *aggressive color augmentation* is the *killer* detail — each frame within the same scene gets *independent* color jittering, forcing the model to be *robust* to varying lighting and preventing it from using color consistency as a shortcut for matching (the *cross-modal* insight from Concerto/Utonia, *first* applied to 3D-from-2D in VGGT).

### The training schedule (Section 3.4)

- Optimizer: AdamW, peak lr 2e-4, 8K warmup, cosine decay
- Duration: 160K iterations on 64 A100 80GB GPUs over 9 days = ~13,800 GPU-hours = ~$27,600 cloud compute (at $2/GPU-hr A100 pricing)
- Precision: bfloat16 mixed precision with gradient checkpointing (to fit 1.2B params + activations)
- Batch: 2-24 frames per scene, max dimension 518 pixels

### The ground-truth normalization

Scenes are normalized by expressing everything in camera 1's coordinate frame, then scaling so the average point-to-origin distance is 1.0. **Unlike DUSt3R, VGGT does *not* normalize its predictions at inference** — it learns to output the correct *scale* directly, the *direct* mechanism for "metric 3D reconstruction from unposed images" without a separate scale-recovery step.

## Connections to H1-H5

**H1 (Joint foundation model > per-task specialists) — STRONGEST support in the 86→87 reading list.** VGGT is the *cleanest* demonstration: predicting cameras + depth + pointmaps + tracking *jointly* improves *every individual task* (+15% on pointmaps over depth-only), the *direct* evidence that the multi-task foundation-model paradigm (à la LLM, DINOv2, Sonata/Concerto/Utonia) is the *right* architecture for 3D. **Implication for v0:** the v0 architecture should be *one* multi-task model that predicts tooth segmentation + tooth depth + tooth pose + tooth surface *jointly*, not a pipeline of 5 specialist models. **Concrete v0 v1 action:** design sub-task 1 (tooth segmentation) as a multi-task model that predicts (tooth segmentation + tooth centroid + tooth depth + tooth pose) jointly, the *direct* VGGT analog for dental.

**H2 (Diffusion) — PARTIAL CONTRADICTION.** VGGT's *single forward pass* is the *opposite* of diffusion's *iterative denoising* — VGGT argues that with enough data + a big enough model, *one* feed-forward pass is enough to produce high-quality 3D, *no* iterative refinement needed. **However**, the v0 v2 sub-task 4 (crown generation) may *still* benefit from diffusion (because generation is *one-to-many*, the *natural* setting for diffusion's multi-modal sampling), while sub-tasks 1-3 (reconstruction) are *one-to-one* and benefit from VGGT-style feed-forward. **Implication for v0:** **use VGGT for sub-tasks 1-3 (reconstruction), use diffusion for sub-task 4 (crown generation)**, the *hybrid* architecture that combines the strengths of both paradigms. **Concrete v0 v1 action:** pilot VGGT for sub-task 1-3 (intra-oral video → 3D tooth point cloud), keep the existing Diffusion-SDF (paper 004) plan for sub-task 4 (crown generation).

**H3 (Conditioning on context) — STRONG support.** VGGT's *alternating global self-attention* is the *H3 mechanism*: every frame attends to every other frame, so the model reasons about *cross-frame context* (the prep tooth's context = adjacent teeth, opposing teeth, gum, etc.) in *every* attention block. The *qualitative* result (Fig 3 middle, two-image-no-overlap) shows that VGGT correctly recovers 3D from a *single* cross-image context clue, the *strong* evidence that the *global* attention is the *right* mechanism for cross-frame reasoning. **Implication for v0 v1:** sub-task 1 should condition each tooth's segmentation on *all other teeth* (the *exact* analog of VGGT's global attention), not on *per-tooth* features (the *opposite* of the per-tooth 3DTeethSeg baseline). **Concrete v0 v1 action:** implement the sub-task 1 model as a cross-attention transformer where each tooth's features attend to all other teeth's features, the *direct* VGGT analog (paper 029 TSegLab's GNN is the *graph-attention* version of this idea; VGGT's *full-attention* version is the *correct* generalization).

**H4 (Implicit SDF > explicit mesh) — PARTIAL CONTRADICTION.** VGGT produces *explicit* 3D representations (point maps, depth maps, cameras) — the *opposite* of DeepSDF/DiGS (papers 002-003) which produce *implicit* SDFs. The *argument* for VGGT's explicit output is that explicit point clouds are *easier to inspect, edit, and convert to meshes* (marching cubes, TSDF fusion, Gaussian splatting) than implicit SDFs, and the *production pipeline* (CAD software, 3D printing) is *all* explicit-mesh. **However**, VGGT's *internal* representation is *implicit* — the camera + depth heads learn implicit 3D reasoning that gets *decoded* into explicit outputs, the *hybrid* implicit-explicit paradigm. **Implication for v0 v1:** **for sub-task 4 (crown generation), keep the implicit-SDF + diffusion stack from Diffusion-SDF (paper 004) because the *inner surface* of a crown needs sub-50μm precision and SDFs are the best representation for that; for sub-tasks 1-3 (reconstruction), use VGGT's explicit point cloud output because the *outer surface* of a prep tooth only needs ~100μm precision and explicit point clouds are easier to integrate with the existing pipeline.**

**H5 (Cross-input-source) — STRONG support.** VGGT was *designed* for cross-domain generalization (16 training datasets spanning indoor, outdoor, synthetic, object, dynamic, real, photorealistic, non-photorealistic), the *direct* analog of v0 v2's "cross-IOS-scanner" deployment problem. The *cross-domain emergence* (Fig 3) — VGGT works on paintings, on no-overlap image pairs, on repeated textures, on synthetic and real scenes — is the *strongest* cross-domain evidence in the 3D vision literature. **Implication for v0 v2:** **fine-tune VGGT on a mixture of clinical dental videos + CBCT surfaces + intra-oral photos + dental CAD models, $5,000 Lambda compute, target < 100μm geometric error on 3DTeethSeg test, target generalization across at least 3 IOS scanner brands (3Shape Trios, iTero, Medit) and 2 photo sources (clinical + patient-selfie)**. **Concrete v0 v2 action:** (a) collect 200k dental-arch video clips from clinical partners (5-10 frames per clip, 30 seconds each), (b) fine-tune VGGT-1B-Commercial for ~3 days on 4× H100 GPUs, (c) evaluate on 3DTeethSeg test + clinical test, (d) deploy as the *default* "intra-oral video → 3D tooth point cloud" service for v0 v2.

## Surprises / interesting things buried in section 4

1. **The *frozen* DINOv2 tokenizer is 30% of total parameters** (~300M of 1.2B) and *never* updates during training. The *practical* implication is that any future fine-tuning (including dental fine-tuning) can *also* keep DINOv2 frozen, reducing trainable params to ~900M and effective VRAM by ~30%. The *theoretical* implication is that DINOv2's 142M-image pre-training is *sufficient* as a visual-feature interface, and the 3D reasoning is *fully* learned from the 16 3D datasets — a clean separation of "perception" and "reasoning".

2. **Combining depth + camera at inference *beats* the dedicated point-map head.** The intuitive assumption is that a dedicated head trained to predict point maps should be *better* than reconstructing point maps from depth + camera. The empirical result is the *opposite* — the dedicated head is 5-10% *worse*. The explanation is that depth (1D) and camera (9D) are *simpler* subproblems than pointmap (3D), so the model can solve them *more accurately*, and the geometric composition (unproject(depth, camera) = pointmap) is *trivial* relative to the head's residual error. **For v0: when we need 3D positions, prefer the depth+camera heads, not the position head.**

3. **The aggressive color jittering is the *key* robustness trick.** Each frame within a scene gets *independent* random color jittering during training, forcing the model to find *geometric* (not photometric) correspondences. **For v0 v1: when fine-tuning VGGT on dental videos, use *extreme* color jittering (per-frame independent) to force the model to be robust to the *wildly* varying lighting in dental clinics** (LED ring light vs natural light vs headlamp, all present in a single 30-second scan).

4. **The depth loss's gradient term `||∇D̂ − ∇D||` is the *killer* design for sharp boundaries.** The loss penalizes errors in *depth gradients* (edges, discontinuities) in addition to absolute depth, the *direct* mechanism for preserving sharp boundaries between teeth. **For v0 v1: when training a custom depth model for the prep tooth, use the *same* gradient term to preserve the sharp boundary at the prep margin (the *clinically critical* interface between the prep tooth and the crown).**

5. **The register tokens (4 per frame) are *crucial* for preventing the attention-sink pathology.** The first version of VGGT *without* register tokens exhibited a "few high-norm tokens dump all the irrelevant information" pathology (known from the DINOv2 register-tokens paper, paper 053 in the reading list). The 4 register tokens per frame *absorb* the high-norm attention, letting the image tokens stay clean. **For v0 v1: any custom multi-view transformer we build for sub-task 1 should use register tokens, the *minimal* change to avoid the attention-sink pathology.**

6. **The first-frame-different learnable tokens are a *clever* design for reference-frame identification.** The model needs to know which frame is the *reference* (camera 1's coordinate frame is the world frame), so the first frame gets *different* learnable camera + register tokens than all other frames. This is a *soft* version of "prepend a reference-token to the first frame" and lets the model learn the *identity* of the reference frame implicitly. **For v0 v1: when building a dental analog, the *first* intra-oral video frame (the buccal view) should be the reference frame, and we should *pretrain* with a special "first-frame" token embedding.**

7. **VGGT-Omega (May 2026) fixed an implementation issue that *doubles* the number of input frames.** The original VGGT had a memory leak that kept redundant intermediate tensors in memory; VGGT-Omega (released May 15-18, 2026) fixes the leak, enabling 2-3× more input frames on the same GPU memory budget. **For v0 v1: use VGGT-Omega for production deployment, not the original VGGT-1B, because dental scans can have 50+ frames per arch (upper + lower + buccal + lingual + occlusal).**

8. **The commercial license (VGGT-1B-Commercial) is *almost* the original license** (excludes only military use) but requires filling a LLaMA-style auto-approved form. **For v0 v1 commercial deployment, use VGGT-1B-Commercial, not the original VGGT-1B** (which is CC-BY-NC and would block commercial use).
