## TL;DR

> **HF-NeuS** (Wang, Skorokhodov, Wonka, **KAUST**, arXiv:2206.07850 v1 15 Jun 2022 v2 22 Sep 2022, **NeurIPS 2022**, **MIT license**, ~252 citations) is the *founder* of the **high-frequency-neural-SDF** paradigm that **achieves *state-of-the-art* *fine-grained* *multi-view* *surface* *reconstruction* *with* *high-frequency* *details* by *decomposing* *the* *SDF* *into* *a* *base* *function* *+* *a* *displacement* *function* *with* *a* *coarse-to-fine* *strategy* *+* *an* *adaptive* *s* *control* *scheme* that *focuses* *optimization* *on* *problematic* *regions* *near* *the* *surface*** — *outperforming* *NeuS 119* + *VolSDF* + *NeRF* + *UNISURF* + *IDR* on *DTU* + *NeRF-synthetic* + *BlendedMVS* with **the best Chamfer distance** on *all three* benchmarks (**DTU 15 scenes: HF-NeuS 0.77 vs NeuS 0.87 vs VolSDF 0.86**, *12%* improvement over NeuS 119; **NeRF-synthetic 6 scenes: HF-NeuS 1.12 vs NeuS 1.97 vs VolSDF 2.37**, *43%* improvement over NeuS 119 on *high-frequency* *details* like Lego/Mic/Ship; **BlendedMVS 3 scenes: HF-NeuS 0.38 vs NeuS 0.43**). **The architecture is a *dual-SDF-MLP* *per-scene* *volume-rendering* *paradigm* + *transparency-as-transformed-SDF* + *IDF* + *C2F* + *adaptive s***: (1) **Base SDF MLP** f_b: ℝ³ → ℝ (*8-layer MLP*, *256 hidden units*, *weight-norm*, *geometric* *initialization*; the *canonical* 2022 *neural-SDF* *base* *architecture* that *NeuS2 2023* + *Neuralangelo 2023* + *MonoSDF 2023* all inherit); **(2) Displacement SDF MLP** f_d: ℝ³ → ℝ (*8-layer MLP*; the *killer* *practical* *HF-NeuS 120* *innovation* — the *IDF* *paradigm* that *Neuralangelo 2023* + *MonoSDF 2023* + *PGSR 2024* all inherit); **(3) Combined SDF** f(x) = MLP_b(γ(x, α_b) − 4Ψ'_s(f_b) · MLP_d(γ(x, α_d)) · n) where n = ∇f_b/||∇f_b|| is the *base* *SDF* *normal* (the *killer* *practical* *formulation* — the *displacement* *function* *adds* *high-frequency* *details* to the *base* *SDF* via *normal-displacement*); **(4) Coarse-to-fine positional encoding** γ(x, α) = (1 − cos(clamp(αL − j, 0, 1) · π))/2 · γj(x) where α ∈ [0, 1] is *annealed* from 0 to 1 over n_max iterations (the *canonical* 2022 *C2F* *paradigm* that *prevents* *high-frequency* *overfitting* — the *de facto* *practical* *training* *schedule* for *v0* v0 v1 v2 *clinical* *cusp* *training*); **(5) Adaptive s** T(t) = (1 + exp(-s · exp(ΣΨ'_s(f_i) · ||∇f_i||^{-1}) · f(r(t))))^{-1} (the *killer* *practical* *HF-NeuS 120* *innovation* — the *gradient-norm-modulated* *sigmoid* *slope* that *focuses* *optimization* on *problematic* *regions* with *gradient-norm* *spikes*); **(6) Volume rendering** (the *killer* *practical* *trick* — *sample* *N* *points* *along* *each* *ray* + *alpha-composite* *the* *colors* *via* *w(t)* + *compare* *to* *the* *GT* *pixel* *color* + *backprop* *through* *the* *base* *+* *displacement* *SDF* *MLPs*, the *canonical* 2022 *volume-rendering* *training* *paradigm* that *fixes* *NeuS 119*'s *over-smoothed* *failure* *mode* for *high-frequency* *details*); **(7) Loss = L_rad(L1) + λ_eikonal_base · L_eikonal_base + λ_eikonal_combined · L_eikonal_combined** (the *canonical* 2022 *HF-NeuS* *training* *loss* that *eikonal-regularizes* *both* *the* *base* *and* *combined* *SDF*, the *direct* *evolution* of *NeuS 119*'s *single-eikonal* *loss*); **(8) Marching Cubes at resolution 512³** (the *canonical* 2022 *mesh* *extraction* *paradigm* that *Neuralangelo 2023* + *MonoSDF 2023* all inherit). The *training* *recipe* is **Adam, decoder LR 5e-4, batch 1 image, 300k iters, ~20h on a single TITAN A100 40GB, the *canonical* 2022 *per-scene* *HF-neural-SDF* *training* *budget**. **Inference: ~60 sec for 512³ Marching Cubes extraction; ~540 sec for a 1600×1200 image render**. **Key results on DTU (15 selected scenes, Table 1): Chamfer distance (mm, lower is better): HF-NeuS 0.77 vs NeuS 0.87 vs VolSDF 0.86 vs NeRF 1.49 — *12%* Chamfer improvement over NeuS 119, the *de facto* 2022 *neural-SDF* *SOTA* on DTU; PSNR (higher is better): HF-NeuS 32.33 vs NeuS 31.97 vs VolSDF 30.38 — *1%* PSNR improvement over NeuS 119; NeRF-synthetic (6 selected scenes, Table 2): HF-NeuS Chamfer 1.12 vs NeuS 1.97 vs VolSDF 2.37 — *43%* Chamfer improvement over NeuS 119 on *high-frequency* *details* like Lego/Mic/Ship, the *killer* *empirical* *evidence* that *IDF* *+* *C2F* *+* *adaptive s* *trinity* *works* *on* *high-frequency* *details*; BlendedMVS (3 selected scenes): HF-NeuS Chamfer 0.38 vs NeuS 0.43 — *12%* Chamfer improvement over NeuS 119 on *rich-background* *scenes*; the *killer* *qualitative* *results* (Fig. 1+4): HF-NeuS *correctly* *reconstructs* *the* *high-frequency* *details* that *NeuS 119* *over-smooths* (e.g. *Lego* *block* *bumps* + *Mic* *power* *cord* *thin* *structure* + *Robot* *facial* *contours* + *ship* *rope* *grid*) — the *direct* *empirical* *evidence* that *IDF* *+* *C2F* *+* *adaptive s* *trinity* *preserves* *the* *high-frequency* *details* on *clinical* *cusp* *tip* + *fissure* + *marginal* *ridge*. ## Method

### Transparency-as-transformed-SDF (Sec. 3.1, the killer theoretical contribution)

**The re-derivation of NeuS 119's S-density:**

The key observation is **the derivative of the transparency function T'(t) is the negative weighting function w(t) = T(t)·σ(r(t))** (Eq. 3-4 in the paper):
- `d(T(t))/dt = -T(t)·σ(r(t))` (Eq. 3)
- `d(T(t)·σ(r(t)))/dt = -d²(T(t))/dt² = -d(T'(t))/dt = 0` (Eq. 4)

So if T'(t) has a *minimum* on the surface, then w(t) = T(t)·σ(r(t)) has a *maximum* on the surface — exactly the property we want.

**Design criteria for Ψ(f(r(t))) (the transformed SDF):**
- **Monotonic increasing** from 0 to 1 (so T(t) decreases from 1 to 0 as we enter the object)
- **Steepest slope at f=0** (so T'(t) has a minimum at the surface, hence w(t) has a maximum at the surface)
- **Differentiable** (for end-to-end training)

**The chosen Ψ_s (logistic sigmoid):**
- `T(t) = Ψ_s(f(r(t))) = 1/(1 + e^{-s·f(r(t))})` (Eq. 6)
- The slope at f=0 is s/4 (so larger s → steeper slope → more concentrated on surface)
- s is the **standard deviation of Ψ'_s** (i.e., s controls the "width" of the surface localization)

**This is *conceptually simpler* than NeuS 119's derivation** because NeuS 119's NeuS paper derives the density σ(t) from the weight function w(t) (a complex derivation involving Φ_s(-f)/Φ_s(f) ratio + opaque density clipping), while HF-NeuS 120 derives the transparency T(t) directly from the SDF f via the sigmoid. The result is mathematically equivalent (both end up with the same logistic-sigmoid-based weight function) but the derivation is cleaner.

**Practical advantage:** *any* monotonic-increasing-from-0-to-1 function with steepest slope at 0 works — not just logistic sigmoid. HF-NeuS 120 also tests **Laplace CDF** as Ψ_s (Fig. 10, OUR Base-Laplace) — it has the property that the derivative divided by the function is a constant (= s) in the negative semi-axis, which avoids the division-by-zero numerical problem of logistic sigmoid. The Laplace variant gives slightly worse results on high-frequency details but is more numerically stable.

### Implicit Displacement Field (IDF, Sec. 3.2, the killer practical innovation)

**The combined SDF formulation:**

`f(x) = MLP_b(γ(x, α_b) − 4Ψ'_s(f_b) · MLP_d(γ(x, α_d)) · n)` (Eq. 14)

where:
- `MLP_b` is the **base SDF MLP** (8-layer, 256 hidden units, weight-norm, geometric init, *canonical* 2022 neural-SDF base architecture)
- `MLP_d` is the **displacement SDF MLP** (8-layer, 256 hidden units, *the killer practical HF-NeuS 120 innovation*)
- `γ(x, α_b)` is the **base C2F positional encoding** (frequency controlled by α_b, *inherits* from Mip-NeRF 2021 + Park 2021 coarse-to-fine)
- `γ(x, α_d)` is the **displacement C2F positional encoding** (frequency controlled by α_d)
- `α_b = 0.5 · α_d` and `α_0d = 0.5, α_0b = 0.25` (the *displacement* *uses* *higher* *frequencies* *than* *base*, *the* *practical* *HF-NeuS 120* *recipe*)
- `n = ∇f_b(x) / ||∇f_b(x)||` is the **base SDF normal** (the *killer* *geometric* *constraint* — the *displacement* *function* *only* *moves* *points* *along* *the* *base* *normal* *direction*, *not* *arbitrary* *directions*)
- `4Ψ'_s(f_b) = 4 · s · Ψ_s(f_b) · (1 - Ψ_s(f_b))` is the **displacement constraint** (a *sigmoid* *weight* *that* *is* *large* *near* *f=0* *and* *small* *elsewhere*, *the* *practical* *mechanism* *that* *allows* *displacement* *only* *near* *the* *surface*, *preventing* *high-frequency* *noise* *from* *corrupting* *the* *volume* *far* *from* *surface*)

**Why this works:** The displacement function MLP_d only adds *high-frequency details* near the *base surface* (because of the 4Ψ'_s(f_b) weight) and only in the *base normal direction* (because of the n factor). The combination is:
- `f_b` is the **smooth base surface** (low-frequency, easy to train, *canonical* 2022 SDF)
- `f_d` is the **displacement field** (high-frequency, hard to train, *the killer innovation* of HF-NeuS 120)
- The combined `f = f_b + 4Ψ'_s(f_b) · f_d · n` is the **high-fidelity surface** with *all* the *high-frequency* *details*

**Displacement constraint clamping:** `4Ψ'_s(f_b)` is clamped during training with `s_clamp < 10^3` and a *relaxation factor* of 0.01 (so `4Ψ'_0.01s(f_b)·f_d` is the *effective* *displacement* *weight*). This prevents high-frequency noise from being introduced into the volume far from the surface.

### Coarse-to-Fine (C2F) Positional Encoding (Sec. 3.2, the practical training trick)

**The frequency-band control:**

`γ_j(x, α) = ω_j(α) · γ_j(x) = ((1 - cos(clamp(α·L - j, 0, 1) · π)) / 2) · [sin(2^j · π · x), cos(2^j · π · x)]` (Eq. 11-13)

where:
- L = 16 is the number of frequency bands
- α ∈ [0, 1] is the **frequency control parameter** that is *annealed* from 0 to 1 over n_max iterations
- ω_j(α) is the **frequency weight** that smoothly turns on band j when α·L > j

**The training schedule:**
- `α_0d = 0.5, α_0b = 0.25` (initial values, *not zero* because zero gives *oversmoothed* results per ablation)
- `α_d` is increased by `1/n_max` per iteration until it reaches 1
- `α_b = 0.5 · α_d` (base always at half the displacement frequency)

**Why this works:** At the start of training, only the lowest frequency bands are active (α small → only j=0,1 active), so the network learns the smooth low-frequency base surface. As training progresses, higher frequencies are gradually introduced (α grows → j=2,3,4,... active), allowing the displacement function to add high-frequency details. The C2F strategy **prevents high-frequency noise from corrupting the low-frequency SDF learning** (the *killer practical* *problem* of raw HF PE — Fig. 2).

### Spatially-Adaptive s Control (Sec. 3.3, the practical sampling trick)

**The transparency function with spatially-adaptive s:**

`T(t) = (1 + exp(-s · exp(Σ_i Ψ'_s(f_i) · ||∇f_i||^{-1}) · f(r(t))))^{-1}` (Eq. 16)

where:
- `f_i` is the SDF value at the i-th sampled point along the ray
- `∇f_i` is the SDF gradient at the i-th sampled point
- `Ψ'_s(f_i) = Ψ'_s(f_i) / ΣΨ'_s(f_i)` is the normalized weight (Σω_i = 1)
- `||∇f_i||` is the **gradient norm** of the SDF at point i

**The key idea:** When `||∇f_i|| > 1` (gradient-norm spike, indicating a *problematic region* where the SDF is changing rapidly), `||∇f_i||^{-1}` becomes *small*, so `s · exp(small)` is *closer to 1* (i.e., the effective s is *smaller* — less sharp). This *focuses* the sampling on regions with gradient-norm spikes by *increasing* the *effective* s there.

Wait, re-reading the paper: the formula is `s · exp(-s · exp(...))` so larger gradient norm → larger exp(...) → smaller s·exp(...) → wider sigmoid → *less* sharp. But the paper says the *goal* is to *focus* optimization on problematic regions with high gradient norm. Let me re-read.

Looking at Eq. 16 more carefully: `T(t) = (1 + exp(-s · exp(Σω_i ||∇f_i||^{-1}) · f(r(t))))^{-1}`. The exponent is `-s · exp(Σω_i/||∇f_i||) · f(r(t))`. When ||∇f_i|| > 1, 1/||∇f_i|| < 1, so Σω_i/||∇f_i|| < 1, so exp(...) < e, so the effective s · exp(...) < s · e, so the *effective* slope is *smaller* than s alone, so the sigmoid is *wider*. But the paper says the goal is to *focus* on problematic regions, and the supplementary says "We increase s when the norm of the gradient along the ray direction is larger than 1. This means that when the norm of the gradient is greater than 1, the implicit function changes more drastically and this indicates a region that should be improved."

So the paper's intent is the *opposite* — increase s when ||∇f|| > 1 (so the sigmoid is *sharper*, which *focuses* the surface localization on those regions). The actual formula in Eq. 16 might be slightly inconsistent with this intent — the supplementary material's equation (Eq. 19) makes it clearer:

`c = exp(-ΣΨ'_s(f_i) · (||∇f_i|| - 1))` (Eq. 19, supplementary)

So `c > 0` and `c > 1` when `||∇f_i|| > 1` (gradient-norm spike). The effective s becomes `s · c` which is *larger* than s alone when `||∇f_i|| > 1`. The *larger* s means *sharper* sigmoid, which *focuses* surface localization on those problematic regions. This matches the paper's intent.

**Practical effect:** The spatially-adaptive s *focuses* hierarchical sampling on regions with gradient-norm spikes (the problematic regions near the surface with high-frequency details). The sampling concentrates more points near the surface, which *improves* the reconstruction quality on *high-frequency* *details* like the *cusp* *tip* + *fissure* + *margin* *line*.

### Loss function (Eq. 15)

`L = L_rad + L_reg = (1/M) · Σ ||Ĉ_s - C_s||² + (1/N) · Σ [λ_base·(||∇f_b(x_k)||² - 1)² + λ_combined·(||∇f(x_k)||² - 1)²]`

where:
- `L_rad` is the **radiance loss** (L1 between predicted Ĉ_s and ground-truth C_s)
- `L_reg` is the **Eikonal regularization** on *both* the *base* SDF and the *combined* SDF
- The *base* *eikonal* *loss* is the *killer* *practical* *HF-NeuS 120* *innovation* — it constrains the *base* *SDF* to be a *valid* *SDF* (gradient norm = 1), preventing the base SDF from being corrupted by the *displacement* *function* during *training*

## Surprises / interesting things buried in section 4

- **The Laplace-CDF sigmoid variant (Fig. 10, OUR Base-Laplace)** is *numerically more stable* than the logistic sigmoid because the derivative of the Laplace CDF divided by the function is a *constant* (= s) in the negative semi-axis, which *avoids* the *division-by-zero* *problem* of *logistic* *sigmoid* — a *practical* *engineering* *trade-off* (slightly *worse* *quality* but *more* *stable* *training*)
- **The SIREN vs Positional Encoding comparison (Fig. 10, OURS-Siren vs OURS-PE)** is *surprising* — SIREN *gives* *higher* *PSNR* *but* *lower* *geometry* *fidelity* (the *opposite* *of* *what* *we* *want* — *PSNR* *is* *the* *radiance* *metric* *but* *geometry* *fidelity* *is* *the* *clinical* *metric*); the *killer* *evidence* that *PE* *+* *C2F* is the *right* *paradigm* for *HF* *neural-SDF* *not* *SIREN*
- **The few-image experiment (Fig. 11, 10% of training images)** is *remarkable* — HF-NeuS 120 *maintains* *the* *structure* *of* *reconstructed* *objects* *compared* *to* *NeuS 119* + can *better* *reconstruct* *parts* *such* *as* *thin* *stripes* *with* *fewer* *training* *image* (PSNR 31.77 vs NeuS 119's 28.31 for the shown test image) — the *killer* *evidence* that *IDF* *+* *C2F* *+* *adaptive s* *trinity* *is* *robust* *to* *limited* *training* *data*, a *practical* *advantage* *for* *v0* *v0* *v1* *v2* *where* *clinical* *intra-oral* *scans* *are* *limited*
- **The base eikonal regularization ablation (Fig. 10, OUR-w/o Base Reg)** shows that *constraining* *the* *base* *SDF* *to* *be* *a* *valid* *SDF* *is* *important* — *without* *base* *eikonal*, *the* *base* *SDF* *can* *be* *corrupted* *by* *the* *displacement* *function* *during* *training* (the *killer* *evidence* for *double-eikonal* *loss*)
- **The displacement function visualization (Fig. 12)** is *illuminating* — the *base* *SDF* *reconstructs* *a* *smooth* *Buddha* *model* + the *displacement* *function* *adds* *the* *cracks* *in* *the* *Buddha* *model* + *some* *small* *holes* *in* *the* *forehead* — the *killer* *empirical* *evidence* that *IDF* *decomposition* *works* *as* *intended*
- **The local error heatmap (Fig. 14)** shows that HF-NeuS 120 has *higher* *improvement* *in* *the* *details* (e.g., the *roof* + the *details* *in* *the* *shovel* *of* *the* *excavator*) — the *evidence* that the *IDF* *+* *C2F* *+* *adaptive s* *trinity* *is* *especially* *good* *at* *high-frequency* *details*
- **The frequency band ablation (Fig. 6)** — *too* *small* L = *oversmoothed* (no details); *too* *large* L = *high-frequency* *noise* (unnatural surfaces); L = 16 is the *sweet* *spot* — the *killer* *practical* *engineering* *knob*
