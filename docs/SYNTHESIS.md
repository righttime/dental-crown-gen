## Three mega-insights

### 1. H3 (conditioning) > H2 (diffusion) in terms of architectural leverage

The single largest performance gain in our reading list is **+0.133 F1** on ShapeNet-34 unseen categories, from AnchorFormer (paper 011). This is achieved by a **deterministic** architecture (no diffusion, no GAN) using three independent H3 mechanisms:

- Learned per-instance anchor positions
- Learned offset scattering into unobserved regions
- Global-α / per-point-local-β modulation (FILM/AdaGN operator)

Compare to LION's +6 1-NNA points from adding diffusion on top of VAE. **The conditioning mechanism is the bigger lever.**

**Implication:** for the v0 prototype, prioritizing the best H3 implementation (AnchorFormer) over the best H2 implementation (LION or Diffusion-SDF) is the higher-leverage choice.

### 2. H4 is won — but the mesh extractor is the real bottleneck

The implicit-SDF substrate wins on every field-quality metric (DiGS IoU 0.939, DeepSDF watertight surfaces, Diffusion-SDF thin-structure recovery). **But the *output mesh* quality is determined by the mesh extractor, not the field.**

- FlexiCubes (007) is the only extractor that survives clinical-quality regularizers (margin preservation, occlusal convexity, developability). DMTet gets +1.5 CD penalty with a regularizer; MC gets +2.23.
- FlexiCubes is the only extractor that hits SoTA on **all three** test regimes (analytical normals, GET3D generation quality, nvdiffrec NeRF reconstruction).
- DiGS w/o normals *beats* DiGS w/ normals — for printable crowns, **train without normals** even if we had them.

**Implication:** the v0 stack must use DiGS as the field, FlexiCubes as the extractor, and add a margin-preservation regularizer to the loss. Add trimesh/PyMeshFix for self-intersection repair before 3D printing.

## Reusable trick: PVD's "free points" (paper 012, Eq. 10)

The cleanest H3 conditioning template in the reading list, and architecturally faithful to the problem:

1. Hold the existing 22-30 teeth's point coordinates fixed as `z₀` for all timesteps
2. Concatenate `z₀` with the noisy free points `x̃ₜ` and feed to PVD's PVCNN
3. **Mask the L2 loss to the free points only**
4. At inference, only update the `x̃` part; replace the `z₀` part with the original

This is the same training architecture as unconditional PVD, just a loss-mask swap. PVD's "van vs. sedan" Fig. 5 demonstrates that the free points sample multi-modal completions from the same trained network — the dentist can sample τ times and pick the best crown, with τ being a meaningful UX knob.

For comparison, LION's AdaGN conditioning requires a separate encoder + cross-attention setup, ~10× more code, and ~3× the compute. The PVD free-points approach is the right v0; AdaGN can be added in v1 if multi-modal sampling becomes a product requirement.

## v0 decisions locked (HK, 2026-06-06)

1. **Conditioning: PVD free-points** — simple ($50-200), architecturally faithful H3 (mask L2 to free points only), no encoder required.
2. **Intaglio surface: geometric offset** — clinical <50μm margin gap is the threshold; learned DiGS inner surface unlikely to beat a geometric pipeline. Decided: keep intaglio as a deterministic offset from the prep boundary.
3. **Phase: v0 deterministic first, v1 diffusion later** — ship AnchorFormer v0 fast, add LION/Diffusion-SDF in v1 if multi-modal sampling becomes a product requirement.
