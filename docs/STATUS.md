- **Week of 2026-06-06:** read paper 002 — *DeepSDF: Learning Continuous Signed Distance Functions for Shape Representation* (Park, Florence, Straub, Newcombe, Lovegrove, CVPR 2019). Key insight: **strong support for H4** (implicit SDF > explicit mesh). An 8-layer MLP with a per-shape latent code learned via the **auto-decoder** trick (no encoder — codes and decoder weights jointly optimized with a Gaussian prior on the codes) hits SoTA on ShapeNet reconstruction and partial-depth completion at 7.4 MB with watertight surfaces and analytic normals. The auto-decoder formulation is directly extensible to our setting: condition `f_θ(z, x)` on a context feature from adjacent + opposing teeth (H3) and infer the missing tooth's code at inference time via MAP. The L1-clamp loss, layer-4 skip connection, and code-LR-100×-decoder-LR ratio are the four tricks worth adopting verbatim. Concrete action: clone the official PyTorch repo, run the pipeline end-to-end on a toy crown dataset, and queue **DiGS** (CVPR 2022) as paper 003 — it directly fixes DeepSDF's main weaknesses (slow inference, thin-structure failure). Note in `papers/002-deepsdf.md`.
- **Week of 2026-06-06 (continued):** read paper 003 — *DiGS: Divergence Guided Shape Implicit Neural Representation for Unoriented Point Clouds* (Ben-Shabat, Hewa Koneputugodage, Gould, CVPR 2022). Key insight: **strongest support yet for H4**, *and* the cleanest path to H3 we've seen. DiGS is a SIREN (sinusoidal-activation MLP, not ReLU) trained with a **soft Laplacian / divergence penalty** on the learned SDF — no normal vectors, no sign labels, no normal-estimation pre-processing needed. The geometric insight is that the gradient field of a true SDF is **incompressible** (low divergence nearly everywhere), and penalizing `|∇·∇Φ|` acts as the canonical **Dirichlet-energy smoothness prior** (Sec. 5.3 derives this formally). The architectural price is a non-negotiable `C²` activation, hence SIREN. Pairs with a **geometric initialization** that pre-shapes the field to a sphere, plus a multi-frequency variant (MFGI) that bakes in high-frequency capacity without losing the geometric structure. SoTA on SRB and ShapeNet among **unoriented** methods (Table 2: dC 0.19 vs. SIREN-wo-n's 0.42; Table 3: IoU 0.939 on 260 shapes, beating every normal-supervised method). **The crown jewel for us: the auto-decoder extension to DFAUST (Sec. 6.2) is exactly the conditional generation pipeline we want for H3** — learn one code per training tooth, MAP-infer at test time given the context teeth's SDF samples. **Critical empirical finding: DiGS w/o normals *beats* DiGS w/ normals on ShapeNet mean IoU** (0.939 vs. 0.920) because normals push the network to fit internal structure and create ghost geometry — for printable crowns, this means we should deliberately train without normals even if we had them. **Concrete action: promote DiGS to the default backbone for sub-tasks 3–4, drop DeepSDF from the plan, and queue a diffusion-on-implicit-fields paper (DiffusionSDF / LION / CIGS) as paper 004** to add the H2 (diffusion) layer on top of this DiGS + auto-decoder base. Also open question for HK: keep the inner surface (sub-task 3) as a deterministic offset rather than DiGS-generated, since clinical fit needs < 50μm margin gap and learned inner surfaces are unlikely to beat a geometric pipeline. Note in `papers/003-digs.md`.
- **Week of 2026-06-06 (paper 004):** read paper 004 — *Diffusion-SDF: Conditional Generative Modeling of Signed Distance Functions* (Chou, Bahat, Heide, ICCV 2023). Key insight: **first paper to put a diffusion model over a 1D latent that parameterizes a neural SDF**, giving us clean, multi-modal 3D shape completion conditioned on partial point clouds, 2D images, or noisy real scans. The two-stage architecture is the same pattern as LION (paper 005): **(1) train an "SDF-VAE"** (GenSDF backbone + 5-layer VAE over 3 plane features, 1D latent of dim 128) to compress thousands of SDFs into a 1D latent space, then **(2) train a 6-block DALL·E-2-style attention-based diffusion model** over those 1D latents with **cross-attention conditioning** on encoder features (PointNet for points, ResNet-18 for images). The trick that unlocks geometric detail beyond what the latent space alone can express is the **end-to-end fine-tune**: re-run each diffusion sample through the SDF decoder and add the L1 SDF loss on top of the diffusion MSE. SoTA on Acronym unconditional generation and on partial point-cloud completion across Chair/Couch/106-class (Table 1–2). **Strong support for H1** (2-stage VAE-then-DDM is correct), **strong support for H2** (diffusion on implicit fields produces clean thin structures), **mild support for H3** (cross-attention conditioning is the right mechanism, but the paper's conditioning is on single partial point clouds, not on adjacent+opposing teeth). **H4 reaffirmed**: the SDF latent is the right data substrate for the diffusion prior when clinical fit is the metric. **Critical contrast with paper 002/003 (DeepSDF/DiGS):** the auto-decoder (one code per shape) catastrophically fails on multi-class (CD 17.83 vs. Diffusion-SDF's 0.607, supplement Table 2) — 128 dims can't carry both "category" and "instance" info. The shared SDF-VAE backbone sidesteps this by baking category/pose into the weights and letting the 1D latent carry only per-instance variation. **Concrete action:** Diffusion-SDF is now the H2 × H4 candidate, paired with DiGS (paper 003) as the H4 reconstruction backbone. The LION (paper 005) point-cloud path is the H2 × H4' alternative — pilot both next. **Compute note:** Diffusion-SDF trains on A100 40GB; the SDF-VAE alone is expensive. Note in `papers/004-diffusion-sdf.md`.
- **Week of 2026-06-06 (paper 005):** read paper 005 — *LION: Latent Point Diffusion Models for 3D Shape Generation* (Zeng, Vahdat, Williams, Gojcic, Litany, Fidler, Kreis — NVIDIA Toronto / U. of Toronto, NeurIPS 2022). Key insight: **LION is the direct alternative to Diffusion-SDF (paper 004): same "VAE + latent DDM" two-stage pattern, but the latent is *point-structured* (`h0 ∈ ℝ^((3+1)×2048)`) instead of a 1D vector that parameterizes a neural SDF.** Architecture: (1) hierarchical point-cloud VAE with **PVCNN** encoder/decoder, global shape latent `z0 ∈ ℝ^128` + point-structured latent `h0`, trained with **aggressive KL annealing** (1e-7 → 0.5 linearly over 8000 epochs, β-schedule from Vahdat et al. 2021); (2) two latent DDMs — small ResNet-FC for `z0`, **conditional PVCNN DDM `ϵψ(h_t, z0, t)` for `h0` with `z0`-conditioning via AdaGN** (the same trick as Stable Diffusion's text conditioning); (3) **diffuse-denoise trick** (encode → forward-diffuse `τ` steps in prior → reverse-denoise → decode) as the inference mechanism for partial/noisy/voxelized inputs. SoTA on ShapeNet airplane/chair/car (1-NNA-CD 67.41/53.70/53.41, vs. PVD's 73.82/56.26/54.55) and 13-class ShapeNet-vol (1-NNA-CD 51.85 vs. PVD's 58.65) with no class conditioning. **The 13-class result is the cleanest empirical support for H3 we've seen: a hierarchical latent where `z0` captures global shape and `h0` captures per-tooth details is sufficient to disambiguate across diverse categories without explicit class labels.** **Strong support for H1** (2-stage architecture), **strong support for H2** (latent diffusion > direct diffusion, by 6+ 1-NNA points on the 13-class setting), **strongest architectural support for H3** (the `ϵψ(h_t, z0, t)` conditional DDM with AdaGN conditioning is *exactly* the template for conditioning on adjacent+opposing teeth, and the diffuse-denoise trick is *exactly* the inference algorithm for "partial scan → complete missing tooth"). **Mild contradiction with H4** (LION is explicit point cloud, not implicit SDF — but the H2-vs-VAE half of H2 is supported; the question is just "SDF latent vs. point cloud latent"). **Strong support for H5** (trained entirely on synthetic ShapeNet). **The big new architectural idea: diffuse-denoise with a `τ` knob gives the dentist a meaningful UX** — "τ=10: respect the prep margin strictly, low hallucination" vs. "τ=200: ignore the prep margin, generate a beautiful occlusal surface" — and the LION paper's voxel-guided synthesis (Fig. 12-13) is a direct proof-of-concept. **Concrete action: this is the H3 implementation template** (a) VAE on complete arches from 3DTeethSeg22, (b) encoder fine-tuned to map partial arches (one tooth missing) to meaningful `z0`, (c) tooth DDM conditioned on `z0` via AdaGN, (d) diffuse-denoise at inference with dentist-controlled τ. **Compute note:** 110 V100-hours for the VAE alone, 440 for the DDMs = 550 V100-hours per single-class model. ~$1,500 on Lambda for the pilot. **Decision gate for next week: pilot both LION and Diffusion-SDF (papers 004/005) on a single tooth class with ~100 training meshes** — pick the one that produces better printable meshes (chamfer to GT + visual smoothness of occlusal surface). This is the single most important architecture decision for the next quarter. Also queued: paper 006 = either "Score-Based Diffusion on Neural Fields" (skip-the-VAE path) or "Neural Dual Contouring" (clinical-grade surface reconstruction). Note in `papers/005-lion.md`.
- **Week of 2026-06-06 (paper 006):** read paper 006 — *Neural Dual Contouring* (Chen, Tagliasacchi, Funkhouser, Zhang — SFU / Google / Princeton, SIGGRAPH 2022). Key insight: **NDC is the surface-reconstruction half of the pipeline we've been building** — given the implicit field (Diffusion-SDF) or point cloud (LION) coming out of our generative model, NDC turns it into a printable mesh with **sharp cusps and smooth outer surface** in a single feed-forward pass (0.5s/shape on V100). The architecture is a 3D U-Net that predicts per-vertex offsets and per-edge existence flags for every voxel cell, supervised against the output of running Dual Contouring offline on a high-resolution grid (the "soft teacher" trick). Three variants: NDC for SDF / binary occupancy, UNDC for unsigned-distance / point clouds, NDCx (heavier backbone) for higher fidelity. The UNDC variant is critical for us because patient IOS scans come in as raw point clouds, not SDFs. **Strong support for H1** (2-stage: generator + learned mesh extractor), **refines H4** (the implicit field is the substrate, but the mesh extractor is what determines final quality — NDC and FlexiCubes are both arguments for "use a *learned* extractor, not MC"). **The big architectural idea: per-cell edge-existence flags** are predicted by the 3D U-Net, so the connectivity is *adaptive* (more edges in high-detail regions, fewer in flat regions), unlike MC which is fixed by the grid resolution. **Concrete action:** (a) adopt NDC as the default mesh-extractor for the v0 prototype, (b) run NDC on 3DTeethSeg22 as a sanity check (sharp cusps? clean margins? < 50μm intaglio smoothness?), (c) pilot NDC vs MC on the same Diffusion-SDF outputs as a 1-week experiment, (d) bookmark **SDC (CVPR 2024)** for the end-to-end fine-tuning story. **Note: paper 006 was committed in a separate batch by an earlier scholar run**; this entry documents it for completeness. Note in `papers/006-neural-dual-contouring.md`.
- **Week of 2026-06-06 (paper 007):** read paper 007 — *FlexiCubes: Flexible Isosurface Extraction for Gradient-Based Mesh Optimization* (Shen, Munkberg, Hasselgren, Yin, Wang, Chen, Gojcic, Fidler, Sharp, Gao — NVIDIA Toronto / U. of Toronto / Vector Institute, SIGGRAPH 2023). Key insight: **FlexiCubes is the *v1 mesh extractor* for our pipeline** — the direct successor to NDC (paper 006) and the **first isosurface extractor to satisfy both *Grad* (differentiable optimization) and *Flexible* (sharp features) simultaneously**, a property the paper's Table 1 taxonomy marks with a checkbox that no prior method has. Built on **Dual Marching Cubes** (Nielson 2004, an obscure but manifoldness-guaranteeing variant of Dual Contouring) with **four sets of learnable, differentiable parameters per cell**: interpolation weights `α ∈ R⁸` for edge crossings, dual-vertex weights `β ∈ R¹²` for positioning inside the primal face, quad-split weights `γ ∈ R` for triangulation, and grid-vertex deformations `δ ∈ R³` from DMTet. All parameters are passed through `tanh(·)+1` to keep them positive and bounded, and the dual vertex is computed as a *convex combination* of primal-face crossings — so gradients are well-behaved and the output vertex is guaranteed to stay in the cell. **Killer result for us: FlexiCubes is the *only* mesh extractor that survives an equilateral-triangle regularizer without geometric collapse** (Table 4: CD 5.46 with reg vs 4.87 baseline, while DMTet goes 5.17 → 6.69 and MC goes 6.33 → 8.56). This means we can add a **clinical-quality regularizer** (margin preservation, occlusal convexity, developability) without losing occlusal anatomy. **Strong support for H1** (2-stage: generator + learned extractor is the only paradigm that works), **mild contradiction with H2** (mesh-based GET3D with FlexiCubes beats DMTet by 4–5 FID, narrowing the case for point-cloud diffusion — restate H2 as "diffusion on implicit fields > mesh-based VAE"), **refines H4** (the implicit field is the substrate, the *mesh extractor* determines final quality — use DiGS as the field, FlexiCubes as the extractor), **mild support for H5** (synthetic CAD pretraining + light fine-tune on patient data, as the paper's nvdiffrec-from-synthetic-then-finetune pattern demonstrates). **Quantitative wins:** 34.87% inaccurate-normals at 64³ vs DMTet's 48.66% (Table 2); FID 17.51 vs 22.41 on ShapeNet chairs in GET3D (Table 6); CD 0.45 vs 4.51 in nvdiffrec on the NeRF-synthetic Chair scene (Table 5). **Caveats:** ~5× more memory than MC at 64³ (116 MB) — budget 64³ for v0, 96³ for v1; rare self-intersections require a trimesh / PyMeshFix post-processing pass before 3D printing; the extractor is not globally continuous (mesh jumps when isosurface slips over a grid vertex) but stochastic optimization (Adam) doesn't care. **Concrete action: adopt FlexiCubes as the default mesh-extractor for v1, replacing NDC** — the 4–5 point FID improvement and 10× CD improvement are decisive, and the drop-in pattern is well-validated in the GET3D + nvdiffrec experiments. Add a **margin-preservation regularizer** to the diffusion model's training loss (FlexiCubes is the *only* mesh extractor that can take a regularizer without geometric collapse). The **final v0 architecture** is now: Diffusion-SDF (paper 004) for the generator + DiGS (paper 003) as the implicit-field backbone + FlexiCubes (this paper) at 64³ as the mesh extractor + trimesh/PyMeshFix for self-intersection repair. Total compute budget ~$2,000 on Lambda for the v0 pilot. Note in `papers/007-flexicubes.md`.
- **Week of 2026-06-06 (paper 008):** read paper 008 — *PoinTr: Diverse Point Cloud Completion with Geometry-Aware Transformers* (Yu, Rao, Wang, Liu, Lu, Zhou — Tsinghua, ICCV 2021, arXiv:2108.08839). Key insight: **PoinTr is the v0 completion backbone for sub-task 4 (outer surface)** — the cleanest *single-pass* point cloud completion architecture in the literature, and the one whose **dynamic Query Generator is the most direct implementation of H3** (conditioning on adjacent + opposing teeth) that we've seen so far. The architecture: (1) FPS-downsample the partial point cloud to 128 center points, (2) extract DGCNN local features (channels 3→8→32→64→64→128) and add position embeddings to form "point proxies", (3) run a 6-layer transformer encoder + 8-layer decoder, (4) the Query Generator produces M=96/224 *dynamic* queries from the encoder's max-pooled global feature, (5) the decoder's predicted proxies are then upsampled via a FoldingNet head to the full point cloud. The **Geometry-Aware Transformer Block** is a one-line DGCNN-style kNN attention augmentation that injects spatial inductive bias into self-attention — and the ablation (Table 4) shows it's most useful in the *first* block only. Results: **CD-Avg 1.09 (×10³) on ShapeNet-55 vs GRNet's 1.97 (-45%)**, **F1@1% 0.464 vs 0.238 (+95%)** — the largest single improvement over a strong baseline in the table. On the ShapeNet-34 unseen-category generalization test, PoinTr degrades 67% (1.07→1.84) vs GRNet's 72% — meaningful for our patient-variability story. On KITTI (real LiDAR cars), the synthetic-trained model fine-tunes and transfers — **the cleanest H5 precedent in the reading list.** **Hypothesis connections: H3 STRONG support** (the query generator is literally the H3 conditioning mechanism — encoder sees the partial arch, decoder queries are dynamically generated from encoder output, decoder produces the missing tooth's points), **H5 STRONG support** (synthetic-only training, fine-tune to real scans), **H1 mild support** (encoder-decoder cleanly separates understanding from generation, so the front-end detector can be swapped without retraining), **H2 mild contradiction** (PoinTr is single-pass, no diffusion — we lose the multi-modal sampling that LION/Diffusion-SDF give us), **H4 mild contradiction** (PoinTr is the *most explicit* representation in our reading list — point cloud, no SDF — but it has a clean role as the completion backbone upstream of DiGS (paper 003) and FlexiCubes (paper 007)). **Concrete action: (a) adopt PoinTr as the v0 prototype for sub-task 4 outer surface**, (b) build a **synthetic 10,000-arch dataset** (Bézier arch prior + CAD-library per-tooth shapes + random masking of 1–3 teeth) and PoinTr is by far the cheapest to train in our reading list — ~$50 on Lambda for the PCN/ShapeNet-55 fine-tune** vs LION's $1,500 or Diffusion-SDF's $2,000, so this is the right v0 to ship. **Open - **Week of 2026-06-06 (paper 009):** read paper 009 — *SnowflakeNet: Point Cloud Completion by Snowflake Point Deconvolution with Skip-Transformer* (Xiang, Wen, Liu, Cao, Wan, Zheng, Han — Tsinghua / Kuaishou Y-tech / Wayne State, ICCV 2021 **Oral**, arXiv:2108.04444; extended to TPAMI 2023). Key insight: **SnowflakeNet is the strongest v0 candidate for sub-task 4 yet** — it directly addresses the "cusps + fissures + sharp features" requirement that PoinTr (paper 008) can't, and does it with the simplest possible pipeline: a **3-layer Snowflake Point Deconvolution (SPD)** stack that grows a 512-point seed into a 16,384-point complete shape through rooted-tree splitting (r₁=1 refine, r₂=4, r₃=8), with a **skip-transformer** between layers that uses the previous layer's displacement features as kNN-restricted attention context for the current layer's splitting. The kernel interpretation is the conceptual hook: each parent's 128-dim feature is a vector of "shape characteristic activations" (learned kernels K_m) and each child point is the activation-weighted sum of kernel rows — a 1D deconvolution applied independently per point. Results: **PCN CD 7.21 (×10³) vs NSFA 8.06 (-10.5%)**, **Completion3D CD 7.60 (×10⁴) vs PMP-Net 9.23 (-17.3%)** — and on PCN it **beats PoinTr's 8.38**, which is the closest direct comparison in our reading list. Skip-transformer ablation: Full 8.48 vs Self-att 8.89 vs No-att 9.30 vs No-connect 9.39 — the cross-layer attention is the unique contribution, the in-layer self-attention variant (closer to PoinTr) loses 0.41 CD. **Hypothesis connections: H1 mild support** (internal coarse+fine 2-stage via seed-then-grow), **H2 mild contradiction** (deterministic single-pass, not diffusion — but the SPD product-of-conditionals is a clean alternative generative decomposition), **H3 STRONG support** (skip-transformer *is* the H3 conditioning mechanism, just over parent-child neighbors — extend to FDI-aligned neighbor teeth for our use case), **H4 REFINES** (implicit-SDF wins for the *field*; SPD-style local generation wins for the *surface quality* — our DiGS+FlexiCubes stack could inherit SPD's skip-transformer as a "neighborhood-conditioned SIREN update"), **H5 strong support** (synthetic-only pretraining, KITTI transfer in TPAMI extension). **Concrete action: (a) promote SnowflakeNet to v0 pilot alongside PoinTr** — both are <$50 compute on Lambda and complete in a week, (b) bump kNN-k from 8 to 16–24 for tooth-scale data (default k is too local for the occlusal surface), (c) add a 4-term multi-resolution CD loss to the v0 pipeline (the trick that makes SPD train stably), (d) inject a second attention head into the skip-transformer that conditions on adjacent + opposing teeth (the H3 extension), (e) visualize what the SPD kernels learn on tooth data — if they correspond to cusps / fossae / ridges, that's a strong clinical-interpretability win. **Compute note: ~$60 for a v0 pilot (6h synthetic + 2h fine-tune + 1h mesh extraction) — the cheapest full stack in the reading list.** **Open question for HK: deterministic SnowflakeNet vs multi-modal LION/Diffusion-SDF for the v1 product?** Recommendation: pilot both, in parallel, on the same 100-tooth subset. **Next paper to read: SeedFormer (ICCV 2022, arXiv:2207.10399) — the direct successor to SnowflakeNet with patch-based self-attention and Voxel-Set-Attention; or SVDFormer (ICCV 2023) for a 2023-era point of comparison.** Note in `papers/009-snowflakenet.md`.
- **Week of 2026-06-06 (paper 010):** read paper 010 — *SeedFormer: Patch Seeds based Point Cloud Completion with Upsample Transformer* (Zhou, Cao, Chu, Zhu, Lu, Tai, Wang — Nanjing U. + Tencent Youtu Lab, ECCV 2022, arXiv:2207.10315). Key insight: **SeedFormer is the most parameter-efficient (3.20M params, 23× smaller than GRNet) and the strongest H3-supporting completion architecture we've seen, with the cleanest architectural idea in the reading list: Patch Seeds — preserve local regional features (256 seeds × 128 channels) instead of max-pooling to one global vector, and propagate those regional features to every upsample layer via inverse-distance interpolation + a regional positional encoding `θ(s_i − s_j)` in the transformer.** Combined with the **Upsample Transformer** (each new point = self-attention-weighted average of kNN-neighbor features, with r_l separate kernels per layer — a direct cousin of SnowflakeNet's per-point 1D deconv), SeedFormer sets a new SoTA on **PCN (CD-Avg 6.74 vs SnowflakeNet 7.21, −6.5%)**, on **ShapeNet-55 (CD-Avg 0.92 vs PoinTr 1.09, −15.6%)**, and on **ShapeNet-34 unseen categories (CD-Avg 1.34 vs PoinTr 2.05, −34.6% on generalization — the strongest evidence for H5 in the reading list)**.
